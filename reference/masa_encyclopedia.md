# Māsa Viśiṣṭata, from the Encyclopedia: Caitra, Vaiśākha, Jyeṣṭha

Sourced only from the shared encyclopedia document. Where the encyclopedia gives a verse, it is quoted. Where it does not, this is stated. The encyclopedia's own cautions are kept as written in substance. Its "deeper meaning" paragraphs are its own editorial interpretation, not scripture.

Calendar note (encyclopedia): Months are lunar. In an amānta calendar a month ends at amāvāsyā; in a pūrṇimānta calendar it ends at pūrṇimā. A dark-half observance may therefore carry a different month name in northern and southern pañcāṅgas. Exact dates, parāṇa and a tithi spanning two days must come from a competent local pañcāṅga. A Gregorian date must never be hard-coded.

Source labels used by the encyclopedia: A, direct primary source. B, primary narrative with later festival linkage. C, ritual digest or established regional tradition, not an ancient Purāṇic command. D, attribution not verified, not to be published as scripture.

## Caitra Māsam (चैत्र)

### 1. Ugādi
- Date rule: Caitra śukla pratipadā, in the southern amānta tradition. Maharashtra keeps the same tithi as Guḍī Pāḍvā. Other communities begin the year by other calendars. (Source: encyclopedia, Vol. 1 Caitra, item 1)
- Significance: Calendrical and regional new year. It opens the month and a new named saṃvatsara in Telugu and Kannada regions. Observance: oil bath, doorway decoration, home pūjā, hearing the pañcāṅga, festive food, elders' blessings, one realistic dhārmic resolve. The Telugu pacchaḍi of several tastes is a regional interpretation.
- Verse: The encyclopedia gives a familiar lamp-prayer as an opening prayer, not as an Ugādi verse of any Purāṇa.
  - śubhaṃ karoti kalyāṇam ārogyaṃ dhanasampadaḥ | śatrubuddhivināśāya dīpajyotir namo 'stu te ||
  - शुभं करोति कल्याणम् आरोग्यं धनसम्पदः । शत्रुबुद्धिविनाशाय दीपज्योतिर्नमोऽस्तु ते ॥
- Caveat and audit flag: The popular claim that Brahmā began creation on this day, cited to the Brahma Purāṇa, is not treated as verified, because no exact chapter and verse is established from a reliable edition. It may be kept only as "traditional attribution, textual locus under verification." Source status is pañcāṅga and nibandha practice. Verse for the creation claim: verse not given in the encyclopedia.

### 2. Vasanta Navarātri
- Date rule: The nine nights beginning Caitra śukla pratipadā.
- Significance: Vyāsa tells Janamejaya that spring and autumn are difficult seasons for embodied beings, so the wise worship Caṇḍikā in Caitra and Āśvina. The chapter describes preparing the place, installing the Goddess or kalaśa, disciplined food, recitation, homa, feeding and kumārī-pūjā. Many homes end the period with Rāma Navamī.
- Scripture: Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Skandha 3, Chapter 26, especially verses 3–7.
  - vasante ca prakartavyaṃ tathaiva premapūrvakam | dvāv ṛtū yamadaṃṣṭrākhyau nūnaṃ sarvajaneṣu vai ||
  - वसन्ते च प्रकर्तव्यं तथैव प्रेमपूर्वकम् । द्वावृतू यमदंष्ट्राख्यौ नूनं सर्वजनेषु वै ॥
  - tasmāt tatra prakartavyaṃ caṇḍikāpūjanaṃ budhaiḥ | caitrāśvine śubhe māse bhaktipūrvaṃ narādhipa ||
  - तस्मात्तत्र प्रकर्तव्यं चण्डिकापूजनं बुधैः । चैत्राश्विने शुभे मासे भक्तिपूर्वं नराधिप ॥
  - The encyclopedia does not assign these two verses to individual verse numbers within 3–7.
- Caveat: Communities differ in the nine forms, readings and colours. Fasting must not become self-harm. (Source: Sanskrit text as consulted through anantasatsang.org/scripture/radha-bhakti/devi-bhagavata/third-skandha-chapter-026)

### 3. Matsya Jayantī
- Date rule: Commonly Caitra śukla tṛtīyā in many calendars; confirm locally.
- Significance: King Satyavrata finds a small fish in his palms while offering water. It grows beyond every vessel and is revealed as Bhagavān. He is warned of a dissolution and preserves sages and seeds in a boat guided by Matsya. He later becomes Vaivasvata Manu. Observance: Matsya or Viṣṇu pūjā, reading Bhāgavata 8.24, respectful offering of water, care for living waters.
- Scripture: Śrīmad Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Skandha 8, Chapter 24. The encyclopedia does not give the verse number for the line below.
  - satyavrato 'ñjaligatāṃ saha toyena bhārata | utsasarja nadītoye śapharīṃ draviḍeśvaraḥ ||
  - सत्यव्रतोऽञ्जलिगतां सह तोयेन भारत । उत्ससर्ज नदीतोये शफरीं द्रविडेश्वरः ॥
- Caveat: The Bhāgavata supplies the narrative. The annual Jayantī date and the vrata procedure belong to later calendrical and ritual tradition and must be documented separately. (Source: vedabase.io/en/library/sb/8/24/)

### 4. Gaurī Tṛtīyā, Gangaur and Saubhāgya Gaurī
- Date rule: Several Gaurī observances fall on different tṛtīyās. Caitra customs are strongly regional.
- Significance: Gaurī is worshipped for well-being, marital harmony, fertility and inner strength. Gangaur in Rajasthan honours Gaṇa (Śiva) and Gaurī. Saubhāgya Gaurī Vratam in parts of the South is related but not necessarily identical.
- Verse for any Caitra Gaurī vrata: verse not given in the encyclopedia. It supplies only a devotional invocation, stated not to prove any vrata procedure.
  - sarvamaṅgalamāṅgalye śive sarvārthasādhike | śaraṇye tryambake gauri nārāyaṇi namo 'stu te ||
  - सर्वमङ्गलमाङ्गल्ये शिवे सर्वार्थसाधिके । शरण्ये त्र्यम्बके गौरि नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥
- Caveat and audit flag: Matsya Purāṇa Chapter 62 (Ananta-tṛtīyā or Lalitā-Gaurī) does not by itself prove every Caitra Gaurī observance. Published studies place that vrata in Bhādrapada, Vaiśākha or Mārgaśīrṣa, not automatically Caitra. Regional vrata-kathās must come from a traceable regional paddhati, temple publication or stable oral lineage, not from one blended story. Research status: keep three separate records (Gangaur, Caitra regional Gaurī Tṛtīyā, Matsya Purāṇa Ananta/Lalitā-Gaurī) and link them without merging. (Source: wisdomlib.org/hinduism/essay/matsya-purana-critical-study/d/doc628463.html)

### 5. Śrī Rāma Navamī
- Date rule: Caitra śukla navamī.
- Significance: The birth of Rāma to Kausalyā after Daśaratha's sacrifice. Observance: reading the birth sarga, worship of Rāma with Sītā, Lakṣmaṇa and Hanumān, Rāma-nāma, a fast until midday in some communities (health and family guidance first), and Sītā-Rāma Kalyāṇam in many Telugu temples.
- Scripture: Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa 18.8–11.
  - tato yajñe samāpte tu ṛtūnāṃ ṣaṭ samatyayuḥ | tataś ca dvādaśe māse caitre nāvamike tithau ||
  - ततो यज्ञे समाप्ते तु ऋतूनां षट् समत्ययुः । ततश्च द्वादशे मासे चैत्रे नावमिके तिथौ ॥
  - prodyamāne jagannāthaṃ sarvalokanamaskṛtam | kausalyā janayad rāmaṃ sarvalakṣaṇasaṃyutam ||
  - प्रोद्यमाने जगन्नाथं सर्वलोकनमस्कृतम् । कौसल्याजनयद्रामं सर्वलक्षणसंयुतम् ॥
- Caveat: Vālmīki supplies the birth narrative and the Caitra navamī setting. Fast rules, festival procedure and temple weddings come from later ritual literature and sampradāyas and are not to be attributed to this sarga. (Source: sanskritdocuments.org/sites/valmikiramayan/baala/sarga18/balasans18.htm)

### 6. Kāmada Ekādaśī
- Date rule: Caitra śukla ekādaśī.
- Significance: The Gandharva Lalita is cursed by King Puṇḍarīka into a rākṣasa form after his song falters. His wife Lalitā follows him, is directed by a sage to keep Kāmada Ekādaśī, and gives the merit to him; the curse is lifted. The teaching passes from Kṛṣṇa to Yudhiṣṭhira, from Vasiṣṭha to Dilīpa. Observance: Ekādaśī discipline by health, age and family tradition, Viṣṇu worship, restraint of anger and false speech, Dvādaśī pāraṇa at the local time.
- Scripture: Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa, Chapter 47. Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat: Names and details should be checked against the Sanskrit or a reputable complete translation when localized. (Source: wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365624.html)

### 7. Caitra Pūrṇimā and Hanumān observances
- Date rule: Hanumān Jayantī on Caitra pūrṇimā in North Indian practice. In Telugu practice a 41-day Hanumān dīkṣā commonly begins on Caitra pūrṇimā and ends on Vaiśākha kṛṣṇa daśamī. Karnataka and Tamil traditions may differ.
- Significance: Jāmbavān reminds Hanumān of his forgotten strength before the leap to Laṅkā; Hanumān declares his descent from the Wind. The dīkṣā may include daily worship, Hanumān Cālīsā, dietary restraint, brahmacarya, temple visits and service. Rules differ by temple and guru.
- Scripture: Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa, Sarga 66. The encyclopedia gives no verse number in the Caitra volume; the Vaiśākha volume gives 4.66.7.
  - tasyāhaṃ śīghravegasya śīghragasya mahātmanaḥ | mārutasyaurasaḥ putraḥ plavane nāsti me samaḥ ||
  - तस्याहं शीघ्रवेगस्य शीघ्रगस्य महात्मनः । मारुतस्यौरसः पुत्रः प्लवने नास्ति मे समः ॥
- Caveat: Do not present Caitra pūrṇimā as the universal date of Hanumān's birth. Do not present the 41-day discipline as a one-day fast. Both are authentic in their own regional frames. A national app should show the user's tradition and region. (Source: sanskritdocuments.org/mirrors/ramayana/utf/4_kishkindhakanda_66.html)

### 8. Citra Paurṇamī and Citragupta
- Date rule: The full moon in the Tamil solar month Cittirai. Not every lunar Caitra pūrṇimā. Many North Indian traditions worship Citragupta on Yama Dvitīyā in Kārttika.
- Significance: Citragupta is remembered as the divine recorder in Yama's administration. The devotional emphasis is honest self-accounting, restitution, charity or correction.
- Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat: The lunar month Caitra and the Tamil solar month Cittirai overlap only partly. Origin stories vary and need separate source records. The encyclopedia calls this entry intentionally conservative and asks for a later regional-source appendix comparing Tamil temple publications, Dharmaśāstra and nibandha references, and the Kārttika tradition.

### Caitra: encyclopedia audit summary
Not presented as settled: an exact Brahma Purāṇa locus for creation beginning on Ugādi; a single pan-Indian Purāṇic source for Saubhāgya Gaurī or Gangaur; one universal Hanumān birth date; one Citragupta origin story or an equation of Tamil Citra Paurṇamī with lunar Caitra pūrṇimā. Dates and ritual rules are to be reviewed by a qualified pañcāṅga or sampradāya specialist.

## Vaiśākha Māsam (वैशाख)

### 1. Akṣaya Tṛtīyā
- Date rule: Vaiśākha śukla tṛtīyā.
- Significance: A day of worship, bathing, charity and beginning lasting work; Viṣṇu, Lakṣmī or Paraśurāma are honoured in several regions. Observance: morning bath, worship, gifts of water, food, grain, clothing, sandals, umbrellas, remembrance of ancestors where prescribed. Dāna is the strongest pan-Indian theme.
- Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat and audit flag: Popular lists attach to this day the beginning of Tretā-yuga, Paraśurāma's birth, Gaṅgā's descent, Draupadī's inexhaustible vessel and Vyāsa beginning the Mahābhārata. These come from different narrative streams, and no single Purāṇa passage was verified for all of them. The Akṣaya-pātra episode (Mahābhārata, Vana-parvan) does not prove the festival date. Buying gold is a modern commercial emphasis. Bengal keeps Hālkhatā, Odisha begins chariot construction; customs vary. The vrata procedure should be sourced to a named nibandha or paddhati.

### 2. Paraśurāma Jayantī
- Date rule: Commonly Vaiśākha śukla tṛtīyā, often with Akṣaya Tṛtīyā; sunset and tithi rules vary.
- Significance: Paraśurāma, son of Jamadagni and Reṇukā, defeats Kārtavīryārjuna and later campaigns against oppressive rulers after his father's murder. Observance: Viṣṇu or Paraśurāma pūjā, reading Bhāgavata 9.15–16, fast or sāttvika diet, charity.
- Scripture: Śrīmad Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.15–16. Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat: The festival prescription is not verified; the birth-tithi observance is later calendrical and sectarian practice and is not to be inferred from Bhāgavata 9.15. "He eliminated every kṣatriya" is an oversimplification. (Source: vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/15/ and vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/16/)

### 3. Sītā Navamī / Jānakī Navamī
- Date rule: Vaiśākha śukla navamī in many North Indian Vaiṣṇava calendars; not universal.
- Significance: King Janaka tells Viśvāmitra how he found Sītā in the furrow of a sacrificial field and raised her. Observance: Rāma–Sītā worship, reading her appearance and marriage narratives, fast by capacity, gifts of food or support for women and children.
- Scripture: Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa 1.66.13–15.
  - atha me kṛṣataḥ kṣetraṃ lāṅgalād utthitā mama | kṣetraṃ śodhayatā labdhā nāmnā sīteti viśrutā ||
  - अथ मे कृषतः क्षेत्रं लाङ्गलादुत्थिता मम । क्षेत्रं शोधयता लब्धा नाम्ना सीतेति विश्रुता ॥
  - The encyclopedia cites this as 1.66.13–14, "continuous sense," and gives the Devanāgarī only. The IAST above is a transliteration of that Devanāgarī line.
- Caveat: The passage verifies the furrow narrative, not the Jayantī tithi, her birth hour or any ritual method. (Source: vidhyamitra.com/valmiki-ramayana/bala-kanda/66)

### 4. Gaṅgā Saptamī / Jahnu Saptamī
- Date rule: Vaiśākha śukla saptamī in many northern calendars.
- Significance: Gaṅgā's re-emergence from the sage Jahnu, hence the name Jāhnavī. Observance: safe bathing, Gaṅgā worship, water offering, lamp and flowers, reading the descent narrative, water charity, river care.
- Scripture: Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa, sargas 38–44. Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat: The named annual vrata and tithi are not established by that passage. Ritual details need later vrata or nibandha sources. Gaṅgā Saptamī (Jahnu, re-emergence) and Gaṅgā Daśaharā (descent, Jyeṣṭha śukla daśamī) are related but not interchangeable. (Source: sanskritdocuments.org/sites/valmikiramayan/)

### 5. Nṛsiṁha Jayantī
- Date rule: Vaiśākha śukla caturdaśī, with the evening twilight observance central in many Vaiṣṇava traditions.
- Significance: Śuka tells Parīkṣit of Prahlāda, persecuted by Hiraṇyakaśipu. Nṛsiṁha appears from the pillar at twilight, on a threshold, neither human nor animal. Observance: day fast by health and lineage, evening pūjā, recitation of Bhāgavata 7.8 and Prahlāda's prayers in 7.9, pāraṇa next day.
- Scripture: Śrīmad Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.2–10, especially 7.8. Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.16–20 holds another Prahlāda cycle.
  - satyaṃ vidhātuṃ nijabhṛtyabhāṣitaṃ vyāptiṃ ca bhūteṣv akhileṣu cātmanaḥ | adṛśyatātyadbhutarūpam udvahan stambhe sabhāyāṃ na mṛgaṃ na mānuṣam || (Bhāgavata 7.8.17)
  - सत्यं विधातुं निजभृत्यभाषितं व्याप्तिं च भूतेष्वखिलेषु चात्मनः । अदृश्यतात्यद्भुतरूपमुद्वहन् स्तम्भे सभायां न मृगं न मानुषम् ॥
  - The IAST is a transliteration of the encyclopedia's Devanāgarī line.
- Caveat: Fast rules, tithi rules and offerings are later sectarian and nibandha practice and must follow a qualified pañcāṅga. (Source: vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/8/17/ and vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/)

### 6. Mohinī Ekādaśī
- Date rule: Vaiśākha śukla ekādaśī.
- Significance: Dhṛṣṭabuddhi, a merchant's son who wastes his life in crime, is directed by the sage Kauṇḍinya to keep this Ekādaśī and is restored. Observance: Viṣṇu worship, restraint by ability, scripture listening, wakeful devotion where customary, correct Dvādaśī pāraṇa. Medical need overrides austerity.
- Scripture: Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa, chapter 49. Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat and audit flag: One sentence in the available English translation appears to call it a dark-half Ekādaśī, though the opening question and title specify the bright half. Consult the Sanskrit and the pañcāṅga and do not reproduce the apparent inconsistency. This legend is not the Mohinī form of Viṣṇu at the churning. (Source: wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365626.html)

### 7. Kūrma Jayantī
- Date rule: Commonly Vaiśākha pūrṇimā.
- Significance: Viṣṇu as a tortoise supports Mount Mandara during the churning of the ocean. Observance: Viṣṇu or Kūrma worship, fast or sāttvika food, reading the churning narrative, charity. Temple procedures differ, especially at Śrīkūrmam.
- Scripture: Śrīmad Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.5–12, especially 8.6–8; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.9. Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat: The annual Jayantī tithi is not derived from those chapters. The churning is not to be presented as a literal scientific account. (Source: vedabase.io/en/library/sb/8/)

### 8. Vaiśākha Pūrṇimā / Buddha Pūrṇimā / Vesak
- Date rule: Full moon of Vaiśākha. Hindu calendars may also mark Vaiśākha snāna completion or Kūrma Jayantī on the same day.
- Significance: For Buddhists, Vesak commemorates the Buddha's birth, awakening and final passing; emphases vary by country and school. Observance: temple visits, precepts, meditation, chanting, lamps, generosity, service.
- Scripture: Pāli Mahāparinibbāna Sutta (Dīgha Nikāya 16) is a primary source for the final journey and passing, not for all three events. Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat: This is a Buddhist observance and needs no Hindu Purāṇic pedigree. Hindu pūjā rules must not be imported into Buddhist practice. It is inaccurate to say all Buddhists keep all three events on the same day in the same way. The United Nations recognition is institutional background, not a scriptural source. (Source: suttacentral.net/dn16 ; un.org/en/observances/vesak-day)

### 9. Śaṅkara Jayantī
- Date rule: Usually Vaiśākha śukla pañcamī in Smārta institutions.
- Significance: Honours Ādi Śaṅkarācārya, systematizer of Advaita Vedānta. Observance: guru-pūjā, study of texts attributed to him, lectures on Advaita, service, by the calendar of the relevant maṭha.
- Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat: Source is later hagiographical and monastic tradition. The Śaṅkara-vijaya works were composed long after his life and differ. It is not a Purāṇic festival. His exact dates are debated. His securely attributed works are better evidence than the biographies. One Śaṅkara-vijaya must not be reported as uncontested history.

### 10. Rāmānuja Jayantī / Tirunakṣatram
- Date rule: Usually Caitra (Chittirai) by regional solar-lunar reckoning, often around Tiruvādirai (Ārdrā). Some pan-Indian lists place it in Vaiśākha. Use the calendar of the Śrīvaiṣṇava institution concerned.
- Significance: Rāmānuja articulated Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta. Observance: temple procession and tirumañjanam, guru-paramparā recitation, study of Śrī-bhāṣya and Gītā-bhāṣya, feeding devotees.
- Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat: Source is Śrīvaiṣṇava hagiography (Divyasūri-carita, Yatirāja-vaibhava, guru-paramparā works), not Purāṇa. Details and chronology differ. A civil date or a generalized Vaiśākha label must not override the temple's solar month and birth-star rule. Note that the encyclopedia thus places this observance in both Caitra and Vaiśākha, and this file lists it under Vaiśākha because the encyclopedia does.

### 11. Telugu Hanumān Jayantī and the end of the 41-day dīkṣā
- Date rule: Vaiśākha kṛṣṇa daśamī, on which the 41-day dīkṣā begun on Caitra pūrṇimā concludes. This differs from North India and from Karnataka and Tamil calendars.
- Significance: Strength remembered and placed in the service of Rāma. On the concluding day: Hanumān worship, Sundara-kāṇḍa or name recitation, offering, charity. Dīkṣā rules belong to the initiating guru or temple.
- Scripture: Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Kiṣkindhā 4.66.7.
  - tasyāhaṃ śīghravegasya śīghragasya mahātmanaḥ | mārutasyaurasaḥ putraḥ plavane nāsti me samaḥ ||
  - तस्याहं शीघ्रवेगस्य शीघ्रगस्य महात्मनः । मारुतस्यौरसः पुत्रः प्लवने नास्ति मे समः ॥
- Caveat: Regional sampradāya practice. The 41-day calendar is not claimed as a Vālmīki prescription. There is no single pan-Indian Hanumān birthday.

## Jyeṣṭha Māsam (ज्येष्ठ)

### 1. Aparā Ekādaśī
- Date rule: Jyeṣṭha kṛṣṇa ekādaśī in the amānta naming of many southern calendars; often called Vaiśākha kṛṣṇa ekādaśī in pūrṇimānta reckoning.
- Significance: Yudhiṣṭhira asks Kṛṣṇa about the dark-half Ekādaśī. The chapter praises Aparā as a discipline addressing grave moral failure. Observance: Viṣṇu worship, fast or suitable restraint, truthful conduct, charity, vigil where customary, Dvādaśī pāraṇa.
- Scripture: Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa, chapter 50. Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat: The catalogue of sins is theological rhetoric praising repentance, not permission to do harm and erase consequences. Fasting does not remove legal, relational or ethical responsibility. Consult the Sanskrit edition for exact wording. (Source: wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365627.html)

### 2. Śani Jayantī
- Date rule: Commonly Jyeṣṭha amāvāsyā in North and parts of West India; southern Śani traditions may emphasize other days.
- Significance: Śani personifies the consequences of action, constraint, time and impartial judgment. Observance: Śani or Navagraha worship, sesame-oil lamp where permitted, recitation, helping vulnerable people, honest repayment of debts. Never pour oil where a temple forbids it.
- Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat and audit flag: Śani is securely present in Purāṇic and jyotiṣa tradition, with stories in texts such as the Skanda Purāṇa. No early Purāṇic passage was verified prescribing Jyeṣṭha amāvāsyā as his birthday. The date and the oil and black-sesame rites are later regional or temple practice unless a precise locus is supplied. Śani is not an evil planet who can be bribed.

### 3. Vaṭa Sāvitrī Vrat / Vaṭa Pūrṇimā
- Date rule: Jyeṣṭha amāvāsyā in North Indian practice (Vaṭa Sāvitrī); Jyeṣṭha pūrṇimā in Maharashtra, Gujarat and parts of the south (Vaṭa Pūrṇimā).
- Significance: Sāvitrī chooses Satyavān despite Nārada's prediction of his death, follows Yama, and wins Satyavān's life by her boons. Observance: bath, saṅkalpa, Sāvitrī–Satyavān worship, circumambulation or thread around a banyan where customary, hearing the story, fast by health, prayers for family well-being. The rite must never impose fasting or marital hierarchy.
- Scripture: Mahābhārata, Vana-parvan, Pativratā-māhātmya, critical-edition chapters 277–283 (K. M. Ganguli sections 293–299). Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat: The epic does not establish the banyan ritual or the regional dates; later vrata texts and custom govern. Reducing Sāvitrī to a wife who merely fasts erases her agency. (Source: sacred-texts.com/hin/m03/index.htm)

### 4. Gaṅgā Daśaharā
- Date rule: Jyeṣṭha śukla daśamī, often the close of a ten-day period beginning on śukla pratipadā. The name is understood as removal of ten kinds of fault; the lists vary.
- Significance: Bhagīratha brings Gaṅgā down for Sagara's sons; Śiva receives her in his hair. Observance: safe bathing, water offering, prayer, charity of drinking water and cooling necessities, lamps where lawful, reading the descent, river-cleaning service.
- Scripture: Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa 38–44; Purāṇic tīrtha-māhātmyas also praise Gaṅgā. Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat: The ten-day vrata and lists of ten sins belong to later sources and should be cited separately. Ritual reverence never licenses plastic, cloth, chemical colour or other pollutants in a river.

### 5. Nirjalā / Bhīmasenī Ekādaśī
- Date rule: Jyeṣṭha śukla ekādaśī.
- Significance: Bhīma, unable to keep the twice-monthly fast, is given by Vyāsa one rigorous Jyeṣṭha fast, traditionally without water, ending on Dvādaśī. Observance: Viṣṇu worship, controlled fast, vigil, water-pot or food charity in some traditions, timely pāraṇa.
- Scripture: Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa, chapter 51. Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat: A waterless fast can be dangerous in Jyeṣṭha heat. Children, pregnant or nursing people, elders, people with diabetes, kidney or other conditions, people on medication and outdoor workers should follow medical advice and an adapted vrata. Devotion is not measured by dehydration. No one is to be pressed to abstain from water. One Nirjalā does not automatically cancel omitted observances regardless of conduct. (Source: wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365628.html)

### 6. Mahesh Navamī
- Date rule: Jyeṣṭha śukla navamī, important to the Māheśvarī community.
- Significance: A community-origin legend of youths cursed after disturbing sages, then renewed by the grace of Śiva and Pārvatī. Observance: Śiva–Pārvatī pūjā, procession, gathering, service, reflection on ethical livelihood.
- Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat and audit flag: No verified Purāṇic locus. Published retellings differ. Present it as Māheśvarī oral and community heritage, not an ancient Purāṇa chapter. Do not harmonize clan lists or details without naming the community source.

### 7. Jagannātha Snāna Yātrā / Snāna Pūrṇimā
- Date rule: Jyeṣṭha pūrṇimā. The anasāra period follows, before Ratha Yātrā.
- Significance: Jagannātha, Balabhadra and Subhadrā are bathed publicly at Purī. Observance: darśana, name and kīrtana, simple offering, temple support and food service. Home observance should be modest.
- Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat: Source is temple and regional tradition: the Puruṣottama-kṣetra-māhātmya of the Skanda Purāṇa and Odia chronicles such as the Mādalā Pāñji. Living procedure is governed by the Purī temple's record and hereditary servitors, not a generic home manual. It is an independent major festival and not merely preparation for Ratha Yātrā.

### 8. Nārada Jayantī
- Date rule: Many northern calendars place it the day after Vaiśākha pūrṇimā (Jyeṣṭha kṛṣṇa pratipadā in pūrṇimānta reckoning). Some sources use another tithi. Follow the local pañcāṅga.
- Significance: Nārada urges the dissatisfied Vyāsa to speak of devotion (Bhāgavata 1.5) and recounts how listening to sages in a former life transformed him (1.6). Observance: Nārāyaṇa worship, kīrtana, reading Bhāgavata 1.5–6, honouring music and truthful speech, service to teachers.
- Scripture: Śrīmad Bhāgavata 1.5–6. Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat: No primary passage was verified as prescribing this Jayantī tithi. Nārada is not merely a comic troublemaker. (Source: vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/5/ and vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/6/)

### 9. Dhumāvatī Jayantī
- Date rule: Commonly Jyeṣṭha śukla aṣṭamī in some Śākta and Tantric lineages.
- Significance: Dhumāvatī is one of the Daśa Mahāvidyās. Her widow and smoky imagery confronts loss and disappointment. Public treatment should be respectful, not sensational.
- Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat: No Purāṇic birth-tithi prescription was verified. Worship is Tantric and lineage-governed. Do not publish mantras or advanced rites from unsourced websites.

### 10. Jāmāi Ṣaṣṭhī
- Date rule: Jyeṣṭha śukla ṣaṣṭhī in Bengal.
- Significance: The mother-in-law welcomes and feeds the son-in-law and the family invokes Ṣaṣṭhī for welfare and continuity. Should not become coercive gift exchange or display.
- Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Caveat: Regional custom, not established as a pan-Indian Purāṇic vrata.

### Jyeṣṭha: encyclopedia audit summary
Temple festivals, Tantric observances and community festivals must retain their source category. Pañcāṅga rules are to be sourced separately from a named calendar or dharma-nibandha; narrative chapters do not automatically prove a festival's date.

## Encyclopedia source policy (kept as given)
A, direct primary source. B, primary narrative with later festival linkage. C, ritual digest, manual or established regional tradition. D, attribution not verified; not to be published as scripture without a located Sanskrit passage and recension.

## Āṣāḍha Māsam (ఆషాఢం)

Status: sourced only from the encyclopedia document. Source grades are the encyclopedia's own: A is a direct textual anchor, B is a textual story with the calendar supplied by later tradition, C is a regional or living tradition. Where a verse is quoted, the encyclopedia itself flags several as needing verification against a printed edition. That flag is kept.

### 1. Jagannātha Ratha Yātrā / Guṇḍicā Yātrā
- Timing: Āṣāḍha Śukla Dvitīyā, at Purī.
- Significance: The chariot procession of Jagannātha, Balabhadra and Subhadrā to the Guṇḍicā pavilion. The Lord leaves the inner sanctum and becomes open to all people.
- Textual anchor (grade A): Skanda Purāṇa, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa, Puruṣottama-kṣetra-māhātmya 33. Verse 35 places the dawn worship on Āṣāḍha bright second. Verses 47–54 speak of the participation of all people.
- Verse (Skanda Purāṇa, cited chapter, v. 54 in translation numbering):
  - Devanāgarī: अत्र स्वयं जगन्नाथो रथस्थो व्रजते प्रति ।
  - IAST: atra svayaṃ jagannātho rathastho vrajate prati |
  - Sense: Here the Lord of the universe himself proceeds each year, seated upon the chariot.
- Caveat kept: The encyclopedia says the Sanskrit should be checked against a critical or printed edition before production use. Only the chapter and the translated verse number are verified.
- Caveat kept: The popular explanation of a visit to an aunt's house is later devotional narration, not the Skanda Purāṇa frame.
- Caveat kept: City ratha-yātrās elsewhere are devotional extensions, not replicas of the Purī rites.
- (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc370753.html)

### 2. Devāśayanī / Śayanī Ekādaśī
- Timing: Āṣāḍha Śukla Ekādaśī. Also called Hariśayanī, and Āṣāḍhī Ekādaśī in Maharashtra. It opens the Cāturmāsya season.
- Significance: The ritual reclining of Viṣṇu, lasting until the Kārtika awakening. Devotees fast according to capacity, worship Viṣṇu, hear his stories, keep vigil where customary, and make a Cāturmāsya resolve. Medical needs come first.
- Textual anchor (grade A): Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa 53. Yudhiṣṭhira asks about the Āṣāḍha bright-half Ekādaśī. The chapter names it Śayanī and relates it to Viṣṇu and Bali.
- Verse (Padma Purāṇa, Uttara 53.1, normalized from the chapter):
  - Devanāgarī: आषाढशुक्लपक्षे तु का नामैकादशी भवेत् ।
  - IAST: āṣāḍhaśuklapakṣe tu kā nāmaikādaśī bhavet |
  - Meaning: What is the name of the Ekādaśī in the bright half of Āṣāḍha?
- Caveat kept: The Purāṇic Śayanī account and the historical Vārī tradition support each other devotionally. They are not the same textual institution.
- Caveat kept: Viṣṇu's sleep is ritual theology and seasonal symbolism. It does not mean that preservation stops.
- (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365630.html)

### 3. Cāturmāsya Vrata
- Timing: From Śayanī Ekādaśī to Prabodhinī Ekādaśī. Some ascetic calendars count from Āṣāḍha Pūrṇimā.
- Significance: A rainy-season discipline. It may take the form of food restraint, study, worship, staying in one place, service, or an ethical vow. The encyclopedia advises one realistic saṅkalpa, kept steadily and concluded formally in Kārtika.
- Textual anchor (grade A/C): Padma Purāṇa, Uttara 53.27–37 gives the span and month-specific restraints. Skanda Purāṇa, Nāgara-khaṇḍa, Tīrtha-māhātmya 237 defines vrata as Viṣṇu-centred and stresses truth, mercy, nonviolence, sense-control and freedom from anger. Detailed application varies by sampradāya and later digest.
- Verse (Skanda Purāṇa chapter 237, v. 11):
  - Devanāgarī: विष्णुभक्तिसमायुक्तं व्रतं विष्णुपरायणम् ।
  - IAST: viṣṇubhaktisamāyuktaṃ vratam viṣṇuparāyaṇam |
  - Sense: A vrata is a disciplined observance directed toward Viṣṇu and inspired by devotion.
- Caveat kept: The encyclopedia says the wording is to be verified before liturgical reuse.
- Caveat kept: Cāturmāsya is not merely a list of foods to avoid.
- (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365630.html ; https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc502216.html)

### 4. Āṣāḍhī Ekādaśī and the Paṇḍharpur Vārī
- Timing: Āṣāḍha Śukla Ekādaśī, in Maharashtra.
- Significance: Pilgrim processions carry the pādukās of saints such as Jñāneśvar and Tukārām to Viṭṭhala at Paṇḍharpur.
- Source status (grade C): The Ekādaśī theology has a Purāṇic source (item 2). The organized routes and saint lineages are historical Vārkarī institutions. They must be sourced from saint literature, temple records and histories, not assigned to a Purāṇa.
- Verse (Tukārām-abhaṅga; the encyclopedia says to quote only from a verified public-domain edition):
  - Devanāgarī: सुंदर ते ध्यान उभे विटेवरी । कर कटावरी ठेवूनिया ॥
  - IAST: suṃdara te dhyāna ubhe viṭevarī | kara kaṭāvarī ṭhevūniyā ||
  - Meaning: Beautiful is the contemplation of the Lord standing upon the brick, hands placed at his waist.
- Caveat kept: The complete Vārī system is not described in Padma Purāṇa 53.
- Source: no separate URL is given by the encyclopedia for the Vārī. The Ekādaśī source is https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365630.html

### 5. Vyāsa Pūrṇimā / Guru Pūrṇimā
- Timing: Āṣāḍha Pūrṇimā.
- Significance: Honour to the teacher principle, especially to Vyāsa, arranger of Vedic knowledge. Observance is to thank one's teachers, lineage and parents, study a passage, and offer service.
- Source status (grade B/C): The texts attest Vyāsa's birth, teaching lineage and arrangement of the Veda. The equation of his birthday with Āṣāḍha Pūrṇimā and the modern Guru Pūrṇimā procedure belong to later tradition. Mahābhārata, Śānti Parvan 350 and Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.4 give the narrative frame.
- Verse (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.4.24):
  - Devanāgarī: त एव वेदा दुर्मेधैर्धार्यन्ते पुरुषैर्यथा । एवं चकार भगवान् व्यासः कृपणवत्सलः ॥
  - IAST: ta eva vedā durmedhair dhāryante puruṣair yathā | evaṃ cakāra bhagavān vyāsaḥ kṛpaṇavatsalaḥ ||
  - Meaning: Vyāsa compassionately arranged the Vedas so that people of limited capacity could retain them.
- Caveat kept: The verse gurur brahmā gurur viṣṇuḥ is devotional and important, but the encyclopedia says it should not be called a Vedic mantra. Its textual history needs a separate note.
- Caveat kept: Buddhist Āsāḷha Pūjā and Jain monastic teaching are related but must not be merged into one origin.
- (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-mahabharata-mohan/d/doc826308.html ; https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/4/24/)

### 6. Āṣāḍha Gupt Navarātri
- Timing: Nine nights from Āṣāḍha Śukla Pratipadā, in some Śākta and tantric lineages.
- Significance: A Goddess observance. The encyclopedia advises publishing only general worship, japa, recitation and ethical discipline. Esoteric mantras, nyāsa and Daśamahāvidyā procedures need initiation.
- Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Source status (grade C/D): Devī Bhāgavata 3.26.3–7 recommends Navarātri in Caitra and Āśvina only. It does not authenticate a universal Āṣāḍha Navarātri. A named lineage source is required.
- Caveat kept: The claim that Devī Bhāgavata 3.26 prescribes four Navarātris is false.
- (Source: https://anantasatsang.org/scripture/radha-bhakti/devi-bhagavata/third-skandha-chapter-026)

### 7. Bonālu (Telangana)
- Timing: Usually Āṣāḍha. Dates differ by temple and locality.
- Significance: Communities offer decorated pots of cooked food (bonam) to local forms of Mahākālī, Yellamma, Pochamma, Maisamma and other grāma-devatās. It includes procession, music and community vows.
- Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Source status (grade C): A living regional tradition, not reducible to one Purāṇa chapter. It is to be sourced from Telangana temple records, government cultural documentation and ethnography. Local memories link the modern form to protection from epidemic disease. These differ by temple and are to be attributed locally.
- Caveat kept: Bonālu is not simply another Navarātri. Local goddesses should not be merged into one Sanskritic identity. Possession and animal offering are to be described neutrally.
- Source: no URL given by the encyclopedia.

## Śrāvaṇa Māsam (శ్రావణం)

### 8. Śrāvaṇa Somavāra Vrata
- Timing: Mondays of Śrāvaṇa.
- Significance: Worship of Śiva by fasting or restraint, abhiṣeka, bilva offering, japa and temple visit. The encyclopedia advises a health-safe fast.
- Source status (grade C/D): Monday worship of Śiva and the sacredness of Śrāvaṇa are established. A single verified Purāṇic passage for the modern all-Mondays package was not found. Claims to the Skanda Purāṇa should not be printed without chapter and verse. The poison-drinking story (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.7) is securely Purāṇic, but its link to Śrāvaṇa Mondays is devotional interpretation.
- Verse (Śrī Rudram, Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.5.8):
  - Devanāgarī: नमः शिवाय च शिवतराय च ।
  - IAST: namaḥ śivāya ca śivatarāya ca |
  - Meaning: Salutations to the auspicious One and to the One who is more auspicious still.
- Caveat kept: Amānta and pūrṇimānta reckoning shift the month. Large quantities of milk are not spiritually superior to reverent, non-wasteful worship.
- (Source: https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/8/7/)

### 9. Maṅgala Gaurī Vrata
- Timing: Tuesdays of Śrāvaṇa. Kept by married women, mainly in Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana.
- Significance: Gaurī worship with lamps, offerings, vrata-kathā, gifts and shared food. The number of years, lamps and the udyāpana come from the family paddhati.
- Source status (grade C/D): Later digests and regional manuals are the proper sources. Nārada Purāṇa 112 lists several Tṛtīyā Gaurī vratas. It is not evidence for the Śrāvaṇa-Tuesday observance, and the two must not be merged.
- Verse (Devī Māhātmya 5.16):
  - Devanāgarī: या देवी सर्वभूतेषु शक्तिरूपेण संस्थिता । नमस्तस्यै नमस्तस्यै नमस्तस्यै नमो नमः ॥
  - IAST: yā devī sarvabhūteṣu śaktirūpeṇa saṃsthitā | namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ||
  - Meaning: Salutations again and again to the Goddess who abides in all beings as power.
- Caveat kept: The vrata should not be taught as a guarantee that a wife's fasting controls her husband's lifespan.
- Source: Nārada Purāṇa chapter 112 is named, but no URL is given by the encyclopedia.

### 10. Nāga Pañcamī
- Timing: Most commonly Śrāvaṇa Śukla Pañcamī. Āṣāḍha Kṛṣṇa Pañcamī (Manasā Pañcamī) is recorded in the east.
- Significance: Worship of nāgas by image, drawing or a lawful anthill. Support for snake rescue and habitat.
- Source status (grade C/D): Nāga worship is ancient, and the Mahābhārata keeps the Āstīka narrative (Ādi Parvan). A Bhaviṣya Purāṇa discussion is attributed, but its exact chapter and Sanskrit passage were not located in the audit.
- Verse (Bhagavad Gītā 10.29, naming the serpent powers):
  - Devanāgarī: अनन्तश्चास्मि नागानां वरुणो यादसामहम् ।
  - IAST: anantaś cāsmi nāgānāṃ varuṇo yādasām aham |
  - Meaning: Among nāgas, Kṛṣṇa identifies himself with Ananta; among water-beings, with Varuṇa.
- Caveat kept: The milk-offering story is not a Vedic injunction. Never handle, capture, feed milk to or crowd wild snakes.
- (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/nagapancami)

### 11. Varalakṣmī Vrata
- Timing: The Friday before Śrāvaṇa Pūrṇimā. Important in Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu.
- Significance: A decorated kalaśa is worshipped as Lakṣmī, with Śrī Sūkta, a nine-knotted thread, the Cārumatī kathā, prasāda and charity.
- Source status (grade C; Purāṇa attribution D): The concrete witness is Lolla Lakṣmīdhara's Sāmrājya-lakṣmī-pīṭhikā 90.5–16, as summarized in a scholarly study. The study notes that most digest writers do not mention the vrata.
- Verse (Śrī Sūkta, commonly numbered 1):
  - Devanāgarī: हिरण्यवर्णां हरिणीं सुवर्णरजतस्रजाम् । चन्द्रां हिरण्मयीं लक्ष्मीं जातवेदो म आवह ॥
  - IAST: hiraṇyavarṇāṃ hariṇīṃ suvarṇarajatasrajām | candrāṃ hiraṇmayīṃ lakṣmīṃ jātavedoma āvaha ||
  - Meaning: O Fire, bring to me Lakṣmī, golden in radiance, shining like the moon, adorned with gold and silver.
- Caveat kept: The claim that the Skanda Purāṇa narrates Cārumatī's vrata is not verified and must not be asserted. A reliable printed edition of the Sāmrājya-lakṣmī-pīṭhikā is still to be found.
- (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/essay/samrajya-lakshmi-pithika-study/d/doc1528187.html)

### 12. Śrāvaṇa Putradā / Pavitropāna Ekādaśī
- Timing: Śrāvaṇa Śukla Ekādaśī. Called Pavitrāropaṇa in some Vaiṣṇava traditions.
- Significance: Standard Ekādaśī observance: fast, Viṣṇu worship, japa, reading, charity, Dvādaśī pāraṇa. The encyclopedia widens the prayer for progeny to care for all children, and says a vrata does not replace medical care.
- Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.
- Source status (grade D, exact primary locus pending): The popular Bhaviṣya Purāṇa story of King Mahījit and Sage Lomāśa was not verified in a primary text.
- Caveat kept: Padma Purāṇa Uttara 41 (Putradā) concerns Pauṣa Śukla Ekādaśī and King Suketumat. Uttara 57 is Bhādrapada Padmā Ekādaśī. Neither may be cited for the Śrāvaṇa observance.
- (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365618.html ; https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365634.html)

### 13. Rakṣā Bandhan / Śrāvaṇī
- Timing: Śrāvaṇa Pūrṇimā.
- Significance: A protective thread is tied with a blessing, followed by gifts and reciprocal commitments. It includes siblings, chosen family, priests and patrons.
- Source status (grade C/D): Ritual protective cords are attested in Āgamic and ritual literature. Śrāvaṇa Pūrṇimā usage is established in later practice and lexicography. The stories of Indra and Śacī, Kṛṣṇa and Draupadī, Yama and Yamunā, and Lakṣmī and Bali are later explanations. None is to be called the original.
- Verse (traditional rakṣā mantra; exact Bhaviṣya-purāṇa recension not verified):
  - Devanāgarī: येन बद्धो बली राजा दानवेन्द्रो महाबलः । तेन त्वामपि बध्नामि रक्षे मा चल मा चल ॥
  - IAST: yena baddho balī rājā dānavendro mahābalaḥ | tena tvām api badhnāmi rakṣe mā cala mā cala ||
  - Meaning: With the protection by which mighty King Bali was bound, I bind you; O protection, remain firm.
- Caveat kept: Label it a traditional ritual mantra, not a verified Vedic mantra. It is not a Ṛgvedic sibling hymn.
- (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/rakshabandhana)

### 14. Veda Upākarma / Avani Avittam / Janai Pūrṇimā
- Timing: Yajurveda commonly Śrāvaṇa Pūrṇimā. Ṛgveda and Sāmaveda follow other tithi and nakṣatra rules, by śākhā.
- Significance: Yearly renewal and recommencement of Vedic study, with change of the yajñopavīta where prescribed. Bathing, expiation, ṛṣi remembrance and recitation.
- Source status (grade A/C): It rests on Gṛhya and Dharma-sūtra and śākhā-specific tradition, not on a Purāṇa. Procedure must be taken from one's own sūtra, śākhā and ācārya. The encyclopedia gives no single universal procedure.
- Verse (Ṛgveda 1.89.1):
  - Devanāgarī: आ नो भद्राः क्रतवो यन्तु विश्वतः ।
  - IAST: ā no bhadrāḥ kratavo yantu viśvataḥ |
  - Meaning: May noble purposes come to us from every direction.
- Caveat kept: Upākarma is not simply Rakṣā Bandhan for Brahmins. The śākhā-specific sūtra passages are still to be audited.
- Source: no URL given by the encyclopedia.

### 15. Nārālī Pūrṇimā
- Timing: Śrāvaṇa Pūrṇimā, among fishing communities of Maharashtra and neighbouring coasts.
- Significance: Coconuts are offered to the sea and Varuṇa as the monsoon eases and fishing resumes.
- Source status (grade C): A regional occupational tradition. No Mahāpurāṇa origin is to be invented. Varuṇa's Vedic antiquity does not prove the coconut offering is Vedic.
- Verse (Bhagavad Gītā 10.29, partial):
  - Devanāgarī: वरुणो यादसामहम् ।
  - IAST: varuṇo yādasām aham |
  - Meaning: Among water-beings, the divine identifies with Varuṇa.
- Caveat kept: It is not a regional name for Rakṣā Bandhan. The two share a tithi but have different ritual centres.
- Source: no URL given by the encyclopedia.

### 16. Hayagrīva Jayantī / Hayagrīva Worship
- Timing: Some calendars place it on Śrāvaṇa Pūrṇimā. Lineage calendars vary.
- Significance: Worship of Viṣṇu's horse-headed form as the source of knowledge. Offering books, beginning study, reciting the stotra, and supporting students.
- Source status (grade B/C): Hayagrīva worship is present in Purāṇic and Pāñcarātra literature. Garuḍa Purāṇa 34 (Dutt numbering) describes it but does not fix a Śrāvaṇa Pūrṇimā birthday. Śrīvaiṣṇava practice follows Vedānta Deśika.
- Verse (Vedānta Deśika, Hayagrīva Stotra 1):
  - Devanāgarī: ज्ञानानन्दमयं देवं निर्मलस्फटिकाकृतिम् । आधारं सर्वविद्यानां हयग्रीवमुपास्महे ॥
  - IAST: jñānānandamayaṃ devaṃ nirmalasphaṭikākṛtim | ādhāraṃ sarvavidyānāṃ hayagrīvam upāsmahe ||
  - Meaning: We contemplate Hayagrīva, embodiment of knowledge and bliss, pure like crystal, foundation of every branch of learning.
- Caveat kept: Do not say that Garuḍa Purāṇa 34 fixes the Jayantī date unless the calendrical verse is separately located.
- (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-garuda-purana-dutt/d/doc122509.html)

### 17. Śrī Kṛṣṇa Janmāṣṭamī / Gokulāṣṭamī
- Timing: Kṛṣṇa Aṣṭamī with Rohiṇī. Śrāvaṇa in many amānta calendars, Bhādrapada in pūrṇimānta calendars. The encyclopedia says to keep one canonical article with two month-display rules.
- Significance: Kṛṣṇa's birth. Fast by ability, worship, reading the birth chapter, singing names, and breaking the fast by the lineage's calculation.
- Source status (grade A for the birth narrative, C for festival rules): Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.3 and Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5.3 narrate the birth. Tithi precedence comes from later digests and sampradāya calendars.
- Verse (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.3.8):
  - Devanāgarī: तमद्भुतं बालकमम्बुजेक्षणं चतुर्भुजं शङ्खगदाद्युदायुधम् ।
  - IAST: tam adbhutaṃ bālakam ambujekṣaṇaṃ caturbhujaṃ śaṅkhagadādyudāyudham |
  - Meaning: Vasudeva saw the wondrous lotus-eyed child, first revealed with four arms and divine weapons.
- Caveat kept: Smārta and Vaiṣṇava calendars may differ by a day. A difference between two legitimate pañcāṅgas does not make one wrong.
- (Source: https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/3/)

### 18. Kajari Tīj and Hariyālī Tīj
- Timing: Hariyālī Tīj is Śrāvaṇa Śukla Tṛtīyā in North and Northwest India. Kajari Tīj is Kṛṣṇa Tṛtīyā of the following dark fortnight, named Śrāvaṇa or Bhādrapada by calendar.
- Significance: Regional monsoon festivals honouring Pārvatī, with swings, songs and women's gatherings.
- Source status (grade C/D): Pārvatī's tapas has Purāṇic narratives. The named Tīj festivals must be sourced from ethnography and regional calendars. Every Tīj kathā is not to be assigned to a Purāṇa.
- Verse (Kumārasambhava, offered by the encyclopedia as a public Śiva-Pārvatī salutation):
  - Devanāgarī: वागर्थाविव सम्पृक्तौ वागर्थप्रतिपत्तये । जगतः पितरौ वन्दे पार्वतीपरमेश्वरौ ॥
  - IAST: vāgarthāviva sampṛktau vāgarthapratipattaye | jagataḥ pitarau vande pārvatīparameśvarau ||
  - Meaning: Kālidāsa bows to Pārvatī and Śiva, united like word and meaning.
- Caveat kept: The encyclopedia cites this as Kumārasambhava 5.2 but says to use a verified edition before quotation. The verse shows their complementarity and is not a Tīj injunction. Hariyālī, Kajari and Hartālikā Tīj are not interchangeable. Hartālikā falls in Bhādrapada Śukla Tṛtīyā.
- Source: no URL given by the encyclopedia.

## Bhādrapada Māsam (భాద్రపదం)

Sourced only from the encyclopedia (Bhādrapada section). Dates are by the pūrṇimānta/amānta convention the encyclopedia states; exact annual dates depend on a trusted pañcāṅga, location and sampradāya.

### Observances the encyclopedia gives
- **Hartālikā Tṛtīyā / Gaurī Vrata.** Bhādrapada śukla tṛtīyā. Women worship Pārvatī and Śiva; Pārvatī's resolve to marry Śiva is the theme. Observed chiefly in North and Western India and Nepal.
- **Svarṇa Gaurī Vrata.** Usually Bhādrapada śukla tṛtīyā, the day before Gaṇeśa Caturthī; strong in Karnataka. Gaurī is welcomed as an auspicious mother; a thread of sixteen knots is used in some lineages.
- **Gaṇeśa Caturthī / Vināyaka Caturthī.** Bhādrapada śukla caturthī. The chief yearly worship of Gaṇeśa; the image is kept one, three, five, seven or ten to eleven days, then given respectful visarjana.
- **Ṛṣi Pañcamī.** Bhādrapada śukla pañcamī. The Seven Sages, with Arundhatī, are honoured with a purifying bath and a simple meal or fast.
- **Rādhāṣṭamī.** Bhādrapada śukla aṣṭamī. The appearance day of Śrī Rādhā, kept in Gauḍīya, Nimbārka and Vraja communities.
- **Jyeṣṭhā Gaurī / Mahalakṣmī Pūjā (Maharashtra).** During the Gaṇeśa festival, by Jyeṣṭhā nakṣatra: arrival, meal and departure of the Gaurī images.
- **Mahālakṣmī Vrata (sixteen days).** From Bhādrapada śukla aṣṭamī to kṛṣṇa aṣṭamī in some North and Central Indian traditions. Not universal.
- **Pārśva / Parivartinī / Padmā Ekādaśī.** Bhādrapada śukla ekādaśī. Viṣṇu fast and vigil; the calendar name varies.
- **Vāmana Jayantī.** Śukla dvādaśī, especially with Śravaṇa nakṣatra. It is dvādaśī-based, not another name for the Ekādaśī.
- **Ananta Caturdaśī / Ananta Vrata.** Bhādrapada śukla caturdaśī. Viṣṇu as Ananta is worshipped with a thread of fourteen knots. In Maharashtra it also closes the public Gaṇeśa festival, but the two are distinct.
- **Bhādrapada Pūrṇimā and the opening of Pitṛ Pakṣa.** The fortnight after the full moon is Pitṛ Pakṣa. In the pūrṇimānta reckoning it belongs to Āśvina; in amānta it is Bhādrapada kṛṣṇa pakṣa. It is one window described by two month systems.

### Significance, verses and story notes

**Hartālikā Tṛtīyā.** Pārvatī, helped by a friend, escapes an unwanted marriage, makes a liṅga in the forest and keeps her austerity until Śiva accepts her. Significance: she chooses and perseveres.
- Thematic verse (the encyclopedia does not claim it as the festival's source):
  तपसा दुष्करेणापि प्राप्यते परमं पदम् ।
  tapasā duṣkareṇāpi prāpyate paramaṃ padam
  "Even through demanding discipline, the highest aim can be reached."
- Verse source: locus not given in the encyclopedia. It says to omit this line from production until a verified locus is found.
- Scriptural claim: verse not given in the encyclopedia. It says the popular attribution to the Skanda or Bhaviṣya Purāṇa has no canto, chapter or verse, so Stuti must say "traditional Hartālikā vrata-kathā".

**Svarṇa Gaurī.** Gaurī is welcomed for household well-being before her son Gaṇeśa. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. It says to cite the living lineage or an identifiable temple manual, not an invented Purāṇa verse.

**Gaṇeśa Caturthī.** Origin: Pārvatī creates a guardian, he is beheaded, Śiva restores him with an elephant's head and gives him first place in worship. The encyclopedia names the Śiva Purāṇa, Rudra-saṃhitā, Kumāra-khaṇḍa, and notes that the Gaṇeśa and Mudgala Purāṇas give other tellings.
- Verse (Ṛgveda 2.23.1):
  गणानां त्वा गणपतिं हवामहे कविं कवीनामुपमश्रवस्तमम् ।
  gaṇānāṃ tvā gaṇapatiṃ havāmahe kaviṃ kavīnām upamaśravastamam
  "We invoke you, lord of hosts, seer among seers, renowned beyond compare."
- The encyclopedia notes that in its Vedic setting the hymn addresses Brahmaṇaspati/Bṛhaspati, and that later Gaṇeśa worship received it.
- Birth-account verses of the Śiva Purāṇa: verse not given in the encyclopedia.

**Ṛṣi Pañcamī.** Significance: knowledge has a lineage, and the day asks for self-correction.
- Verse (called a traditional liturgical verse, not Veda or Purāṇa):
  कश्यपोऽत्रिर्भरद्वाजो विश्वामित्रोऽथ गौतमः ।
  जमदग्निर्वसिष्ठश्च सप्तैते ऋषयः स्मृताः ॥
  kaśyapo 'trir bharadvājo viśvāmitro 'tha gautamaḥ / jamadagnir vasiṣṭhaś ca saptaite ṛṣayaḥ smṛtāḥ
  (The Devanagari is as given; the roman line is the same text set in IAST.)
- Its transmission varies, and lists of the seven sages vary by manvantara and text.
- The vrata story itself: verse not given in the encyclopedia. The Bhaviṣya Purāṇa attribution has no stable parvan, chapter or verse.

**Rādhāṣṭamī.** Rādhā appears in the home of Vṛṣabhānu and Kīrtidā; her closed eyes open on seeing Kṛṣṇa, a theological image.
- Verse offered, from Rūpa Gosvāmin (Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi), incomplete as given:
  राधामाधवयोः पादपद्मयोः प्रणमाम्यहम् ।
  rādhā-mādhavayoḥ pāda-padmayoḥ praṇamāmy aham
- The encyclopedia says to publish only after checking the exact critical wording.
- Purāṇic verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia. The Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa and Garga-saṃhitā are named as sources of the Rādhā tradition without verses.

**Pārśva Ekādaśī and Vāmana Jayantī.** Aditi's vow; Viṣṇu is born as Vāmana, asks Bali for three steps, and becomes Trivikrama. Bali keeps his word.
- Verse (Bhāgavata 8.18.5):
  श्रवणद्वादशीं प्राप्य मुहूर्तेऽभिजिति प्रभुः ।
  सर्वे नक्षत्रताराद्याश्चक्रुस्तज्जन्म दक्षिणम् ॥
  śravaṇa-dvādaśīṃ prāpya muhūrte 'bhijiti prabhuḥ / sarve nakṣatra-tārādyāś cakrus taj-janma dakṣiṇam
  Sense given: on Śravaṇa-dvādaśī, in the Abhijit period, the Lord appeared under an auspicious configuration.
- The encyclopedia says to consult the Sanskrit for the exact edition reading. The Ekādaśī kathā and the turning of Viṣṇu in cāturmāsya: verse not given in the encyclopedia.

**Mahālakṣmī Vrata and Jyeṣṭhā Gaurī.** Verse not given in the encyclopedia for either.

**Ananta Caturdaśī.** Story of Suśīlā and Kauṇḍinya: he destroys the thread, loses his stability, repents and renews the vow.
- Traditional mantra (to be verified against the household paddhati):
  अनन्तसंसारमहासमुद्रे मग्नं समभ्युद्धर वासुदेव ।
  अनन्तरूपे विनियोजयस्व ह्यनन्तरूपाय नमो नमस्ते ॥
  ananta-saṃsāra-mahā-samudre magnaṃ samabhyuddhara vāsudeva / ananta-rūpe viniyojayasva hy ananta-rūpāya namo namaste
  "O Vāsudeva, lift me from the vast ocean of endless worldly existence; unite me with the Infinite. Salutations to the One of infinite form."
- Scriptural verse for the vrata: verse not given in the encyclopedia. It points to the Nārada Purāṇa, pūrvabhāga, chapter 123, to be checked.

**Pitṛ Pakṣa opening.** Duties to ancestors rest on Vedic, Gṛhya, Dharmaśāstra, Mahābhārata and Purāṇic sources; day rules are set out in the nibandhas Nirṇayasindhu and Dharmasindhu.
- Verse (Bhagavad Gītā 1.42):
  सङ्करो नरकायैव कुलघ्नानां कुलस्य च ।
  पतन्ति पितरो ह्येषां लुप्तपिण्डोदकक्रियाः ॥ १.४२ ॥
  saṅkaro narakāyaiva kulaghnānāṃ kulasya ca / patanti pitaro hy eṣāṃ luptapiṇḍodakakriyāḥ
- The encyclopedia says this is Arjuna speaking in distress, not Kṛṣṇa's final teaching. Its value is as evidence that piṇḍa and water offerings mattered in the epic setting.

### The encyclopedia's own caveats and audit flags
- Hartālikā: source status B/C. Do not write "according to the Skanda Purāṇa" without exact edition, khaṇḍa, chapter and verse. It is not the same as every Gaurī Tṛtīyā, Gangaur or Telugu/Kannada Gaurī vrata.
- Svarṇa Gaurī: status C/B. It is not simply the Kannada form of Hartālikā; do not merge their kathās or procedures.
- Gaṇeśa Caturthī: status B. The origin has several Purāṇic tellings. The moon-sighting rules belong to later digests, not the birth chapter. No fixed immersion duration is compulsory. The Syamantaka story must be cited separately by version.
- Ṛṣi Pañcamī: status B/C. Do not print a Bhaviṣya Purāṇa citation until matched to an edition. The story must not teach that menstruation is sinful or that illness proves ritual fault.
- Rādhāṣṭamī: status B/C, sampradāya-specific. The Bhāgavata Purāṇa does not narrate a named Rādhā birth story; saying it does is inaccurate. It is not equally central in every Vaiṣṇava school.
- Jyeṣṭhā Gaurī: status C. Regional household tradition; not to be confused with the sixteen-day Mahālakṣmī Vrata or the Kolhapur temple calendar.
- Mahālakṣmī Vrata: status B/C. The Bhaviṣya Purāṇa attribution has no verified Sanskrit locus. It is not Varalakṣmī Vrata.
- Pārśva Ekādaśī and Vāmana Jayantī: status A for Vāmana's appearance, B for the Ekādaśī names and rites. Smārta and Vaiṣṇava fasting and parāṇa must not be merged. Onam is related but separate.
- Ananta Caturdaśī: status B. Do not cite "Vana Parva" for Kṛṣṇa teaching the vrata to Yudhiṣṭhira without chapter and edition. It is not Gaṇeśa's birthday. The fourteen-knot symbolism is lineage symbolism.
- Pitṛ Pakṣa: status A/B. Karṇa stories should not be cited to the Mahābhārata without a verified passage. There is no universal śrāddha menu. People without full ritual access may remember ancestors, offer water, feed living beings, or give in charity.
- Cross-month list of claims not to enter production unverified: the Skanda claim for Hartālikā; the Bhaviṣya chapter for Ṛṣi Pañcamī; Kṛṣṇa teaching Ananta Vrata in the Mahābhārata; "all Hindus keep Vāmana Jayantī on Ekādaśī"; "the Bhāgavata names Rādhā and describes Rādhāṣṭamī"; medical claims for fasting.
- Production advice: keep four separate fields (scriptural narrative source, calendar/vrata source, regional practice source, editorial interpretation).

## Āśvina Māsam (ఆశ్వయుజం)

Sourced only from the encyclopedia (Āśvina section). Month names differ by amānta and pūrṇimānta reckoning; the encyclopedia asks that dates be calculated locally.

### Observances the encyclopedia gives
- **Pitṛ Pakṣa, Mahālaya and Sarvapitṛ Amāvāsyā.** The fortnight after the Bhādrapada full moon, ending on the last amāvāsyā, which is the collective ancestral day, useful when the individual death-tithi is unknown or missed.
- **Śāradīya Navarātri.** Āśvina śukla pratipad, culminating on the eighth, ninth and tenth tithis. Devī is worshipped for nine nights.
- **Durgā Pūjā (Bengal and Eastern India).** Ṣaṣṭhī through daśamī, with bodhana, Sandhi Pūjā and immersion.
- **Bathukamma (Telangana).** A floral festival across the latter part of Bhādrapada and Āśvina, ending as Saddula Bathukamma near Durgāṣṭamī or Mahānavamī.
- **Sarasvatī Pūjā, Pustaka Pūjā and Āyudha Pūjā.** On Durgāṣṭamī or Mahānavamī in southern traditions; books and tools are set aside and resumed on Vijayadaśamī.
- **Durgāṣṭamī, Mahānavamī, Kumārī Pūjā and Sandhi Pūjā.** The climactic Navarātri days; Sandhi Pūjā is at the aṣṭamī–navamī junction.
- **Vijayadaśamī / Daśaharā.** Āśvina śukla daśamī, concluding the festival.
- **Pāśāṅkuśā Ekādaśī.** Āśvina śukla ekādaśī, a Viṣṇu fast.
- **Śarad / Kojāgarī Pūrṇimā.** The Āśvina full moon: Lakṣmī vigil, moon-viewing, remembrance of the rāsa, and Odisha's Kumāra Pūrṇimā.
- **Vālmīki Jayantī.** Commemorated on Āśvina pūrṇimā by many communities.

### Significance, verses and story notes

**Pitṛ Pakṣa and Mahālaya.** The fortnight turns family memory into gratitude. In Bengal, Mahālaya dawn also turns attention to Durgā's arrival.
- Offering formula (generic, not a substitute for a priest-guided śrāddha):
  ॐ पितृभ्यः स्वधायिभ्यः स्वधा नमः ।
  oṃ pitṛbhyaḥ svadhāyibhyaḥ svadhā namaḥ
- The encyclopedia says exact mantras are specific to śākhā and rite.
- Scriptural verse for the amāvāsyā rite: verse not given in the encyclopedia.

**Śāradīya Navarātri.** Janamejaya asks Vyāsa, who recommends worship of Caṇḍikā in the two seasonal transitions. The Devī Māhātmya frames its teaching through King Suratha and the merchant Samādhi, and narrates victories over Madhu–Kaiṭabha, Mahiṣāsura and Śumbha–Niśumbha.
- Verse (Devī Bhāgavata 3.26.3–7):
  शृणु राजन् प्रवक्ष्यामि नवरात्रव्रतं शुभम् ।
  शरत्काले विशेषेण कर्तव्यं विधिपूर्वकम् ॥
  वसन्ते च प्रकर्तव्यं तथैव प्रेमपूर्वकम् ॥
  śṛṇu rājan pravakṣyāmi navarātra-vrataṃ śubham / śarat-kāle viśeṣeṇa kartavyaṃ vidhi-pūrvakam // vasante ca prakartavyaṃ tathaiva prema-pūrvakam
  "Listen, O king: I shall explain the auspicious Navarātri vow. It is especially to be performed properly in autumn, and likewise with devotion in spring."
- The nine forms on nine days: verse not given in the encyclopedia; it says the mapping varies and the nine colours come from modern convention.

**Durgā Pūjā.** The devas' powers unite as the Goddess, who defeats Mahiṣa; later Śumbha, Niśumbha, Caṇḍa, Muṇḍa and Raktabīja. In Bengal she is also seen as returning to her parents' home.
- Verse (Devī Māhātmya 5.16):
  या देवी सर्वभूतेषु शक्तिरूपेण संस्थिता ।
  नमस्तस्यै नमस्तस्यै नमस्तस्यै नमो नमः ॥
  yā devī sarvabhūteṣu śakti-rūpeṇa saṃsthitā / namas tasyai namas tasyai namas tasyai namo namaḥ
  "To the Goddess abiding in all beings as power: salutations to her, again and again."
- Bodhana and the akāl-bodhana story of Rāma: verse not given in the encyclopedia; the encyclopedia says it is not in Vālmīki and comes from later Bengali tradition.

**Bathukamma.** The name is taken as an appeal to the Mother to live and flourish; seasonal flowers form the icon and songs carry village memory. Verse: none. The encyclopedia says no Purāṇic locus is verified and that a traditional song may be used only with rights and provenance cleared; newly written lyrics must not be labelled an ancient śloka.

**Sarasvatī, Pustaka and Āyudha Pūjā.** Knowledge and labour are honoured together.
- Verse (Ṛgveda 1.3.10):
  पावका नः सरस्वती वाजेभिर्वाजिनीवती ।
  यज्ञं वष्टु धियावसुः ॥
  pāvakā naḥ sarasvatī vājebhir vājinīvatī / yajñaṃ vaṣṭu dhiyāvasuḥ
  "May purifying Sarasvatī, rich in inspiration and nourishing power, favor our offering."
- Purāṇic verse prescribing the southern rules: verse not given in the encyclopedia.

**Durgāṣṭamī, Mahānavamī, Kumārī and Sandhi Pūjā.** Kālī/Cāmuṇḍā defeats Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa; Raktabīja is contained; the many forms are seen as one Devī.
- Verse (Devī Māhātmya 10.5, numbering can vary):
  एकैवाहं जगत्यत्र द्वितीया का ममापरा ।
  ekaivāhaṃ jagaty atra dvitīyā kā mamāparā
  "I alone am here in the universe; what second, other than me, is there?"
- Only the first pāda is given.

**Vijayadaśamī.** Several stories meet: Rāma's victory, the Goddess's victory, and the Pāṇḍavas retrieving weapons from the śamī tree (Mahābhārata, Virāṭa Parva).
- Traditional śamī prayer (source varies by paddhati):
  शमी शमयते पापं शमी शत्रुविनाशिनी ।
  अर्जुनस्य धनुर्धारी रामस्य प्रियदर्शिनी ॥
  śamī śamayate pāpaṃ śamī śatru-vināśinī / arjunasya dhanur-dhārī rāmasya priya-darśinī
- The encyclopedia says recensions read the second line differently and asks that it be verified with the regional manual.

**Pāśāṅkuśā Ekādaśī.** The story of the hunter or king Krodhana and worship of Padmanābha is in popular vrata collections, attributed to the Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa.
- Verse offered (Bhagavad Gītā 9.26), which supports simple devotion, not the date:
  पत्रं पुष्पं फलं तोयं यो मे भक्त्या प्रयच्छति ।
  तदहं भक्त्युपहृतमश्नामि प्रयतात्मनः ॥
  patraṃ puṣpaṃ phalaṃ toyaṃ yo me bhaktyā prayacchati / tad ahaṃ bhaktyupahṛtam aśnāmi prayatātmanaḥ
  "Whoever offers me a leaf, flower, fruit, or water with devotion—that devoted offering of the disciplined person I accept."
- Padma Purāṇa verse for the vrata: verse not given in the encyclopedia.

**Śarad / Kojāgarī Pūrṇimā.** Kṛṣṇa's flute calls the gopīs; pride arises and he disappears, deepening their longing. In Lakṣmī tradition households keep vigil, asking who is awake.
- Verse (Bhāgavata 10.29.1):
  भगवानपि ता रात्रीः शारदोत्फुल्लमल्लिकाः ।
  वीक्ष्य रन्तुं मनश्चक्रे योगमायामुपाश्रितः ॥
  bhagavān api tā rātrīḥ śāradotphulla-mallikāḥ / vīkṣya rantuṃ manaś cakre yogamāyām upāśritaḥ
  "Seeing those autumn nights bright with blossoming jasmine, the Lord resolved to sport, taking refuge in Yogamāyā."
- The encyclopedia says this opens the rāsa season and does not prescribe the milk-rice rite. Kojāgarī Lakṣmī verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.

**Vālmīki Jayantī.** Verse (Bāla Kāṇḍa 1.2.15), after the krauñca bird is killed:
  मा निषाद प्रतिष्ठां त्वमगमः शाश्वतीः समाः ।
  यत्क्रौञ्चमिथुनादेकमवधीः काममोहितम् ॥
  mā niṣāda pratiṣṭhāṃ tvam agamaḥ śāśvatīḥ samāḥ / yat krauñca-mithunād ekam avadhīḥ kāma-mohitam
  "O hunter, may you find no lasting honor, because you killed one of the krauñca pair absorbed in love."
- The tradition takes this as the birth of śloka metre. The date of Vālmīki's birth: verse not given in the encyclopedia.

### The encyclopedia's own caveats and audit flags
- Pitṛ Pakṣa and Mahālaya: status A/B for ancestral rites, C for some modern Mahālaya forms. The dawn radio recitation is a twentieth-century cultural work, is copyrighted, and may be discussed but not copied. "Durgā was created that morning" is devotional shorthand, not universal Purāṇic chronology.
- Navarātri: status A. The Devī Māhātmya does not itself encode the modern nine-colour or daily-goddess scheme. It is not exclusively North Indian and not only a women's vrata. Chapter numbering "81–93" is the encyclopedia's own note on common Mārkaṇḍeya numbering.
- Durgā Pūjā: status B/C. The akāl-bodhana story is not in Vālmīki. Sandhi and sacrifice practices differ by region. The daughter-returning-home frame is Bengali devotional interpretation, not the plot of the Devī Māhātmya.
- Bathukamma: status C, regional living heritage; several origin narratives, none verified as the sole origin. It is not simply "Telangana Navarātri".
- Sarasvatī and Āyudha Pūjā: status B/C. Bengal more often keeps Sarasvatī in Māgha. Machinery and weapons must be handled safely; it is not a licence for unsafe display.
- Durgāṣṭamī and Mahānavamī: status A/B. Modern Sandhi details are regional paddhati, not the Purāṇa. Animal sacrifice is neither practised nor required by all Śākta communities. Child safeguarding and consent are mandatory in Kumārī Pūjā.
- Vijayadaśamī: status B/C. The Devī Māhātmya does not state that Mahiṣa was killed on the modern date; Vālmīki does not establish the Dussehra calendar or a 108-blue-lotus Durgā Pūjā; the śamī association with the modern date is later. There is no single pan-Indian origin story and Rāvaṇa effigies are not universal.
- Pāśāṅkuśā Ekādaśī: status B. Padma Purāṇa chapter numbering differs markedly among editions; insert a chapter only after checking the Sanskrit. Phalaśruti is religious praise, not permission to bypass repentance and changed conduct. Vaiṣṇava and Smārta dates can differ.
- Kojāgarī: status A for rāsa season, B/C for Kojāgarī rites. Claims that moon rays chemically cure milk are neither scriptural nor medical advice; food left outdoors must be protected. Kojāgarī Lakṣmī Pūjā and Dīpāvalī Lakṣmī Pūjā are separate.
- Vālmīki Jayantī: date status C, role status A. The "Ratnākara the robber" life story is later tradition and must be labelled so.
- Cross-month list of claims not to enter production: "Rāma performed Durgā Pūjā in Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa"; "Vālmīki's own epic supplies his Āśvina-pūrṇimā birthday"; medical claims for moon-exposed food.

## Kārttika Māsam (కార్తీకం)

Sourced only from the encyclopedia (Kārttika section). The encyclopedia notes that many southern calendars use the amānta month, while many northern calendars begin Kārttika after Āśvina Pūrṇimā; hence Dīpāvalī is Āśvina Amāvāsyā in one system and Kārttika Amāvāsyā in another. It also gives four source labels: primary narrative, festival prescription, living tradition, and interpretation.

### Observances the encyclopedia gives
- **Kārttika-māsa vrata.** The whole month: early bath, a lamp at the shrine or near Tulasī at dusk, Tulasī or Viṣṇu worship, charity, and Monday worship and abhiṣeka in Śaiva homes (Kārttika Somavāra, strong in Andhra–Telangana and Karnataka).
- **Dhanteras / Dhanatrayodaśī, Dhanvantari Jayantī and Yama-dīpa.** Dark-fortnight trayodaśī before Dīpāvalī.
- **Naraka Caturdaśī / Abhyaṅga-snāna.** Dark caturdaśī; a pre-dawn oil bath, in much of South India the main Deepavali morning.
- **Dīpāvalī Amāvāsyā and Lakṣmī Pūjā.** The new-moon night; Kali Pūjā in Bengal is a separate Śākta matter.
- **Govardhana Pūjā / Annakūṭa.** Usually bright pratipad after Dīpāvalī.
- **Bali Pratipadā / Balipāḍyami.** Bright pratipad; the same tithi can carry both Govardhana and Bali layers.
- **Yama-dvitīyā / Bhāī Dūj, and Citragupta Pūjā.** Bright dvitīyā.
- **Nāgula Cavithi / Nāga Caturthī.** Kārttika bright caturthī in Telugu practice and parts of Karnataka.
- **Prabodhinī / Devotthāna Ekādaśī and Tulasī Vivāha.** Bright ekādaśī, closing Cāturmāsya; Tulasī Vivāha on ekādaśī, dvādaśī or another locally prescribed day.
- **Kārttika Pūrṇimā.** Tripurārī Pūrṇimā, Dev Dīpāvalī and sacred bathing.

### Significance, verses and story notes

**Kārttika-māsa vrata.** Ordinary repeated acts become a sustained discipline; the lamp trains attention. The encyclopedia calls the Skanda Purāṇa, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa, Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya a genuine festival-prescription source, with chapters on lamps, bathing, Tulasī, Ekādaśī and the close. Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.

**Dhanteras.** Dhanvantari appears from the churning of the Milk Ocean with the vessel of amṛta.
- Verse (Bhāgavata 8.8.34):
  स वै भगवतः साक्षाद् विष्णोरंशांशसम्भवः ।
  धन्वन्तरिरिति ख्यात आयुर्वेददृगिज्यभाक् ॥
  sa vai bhagavataḥ sākṣād viṣṇor aṁśāṁśa-sambhavaḥ / dhanvantarir iti khyāta āyur-veda-dṛg ijya-bhāk
  "He became known as Dhanvantari, connected with Viṣṇu and learned in Āyurveda."
- Yama-dīpa: the Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa 122.4ff prescribes a lamp for Yama outside the house on dark trayodaśī, to avert untimely death. Verse not given in the encyclopedia.

**Naraka Caturdaśī.** Kṛṣṇa, with Satyabhāmā, destroys Mura and kills Naraka, freeing the captive women; Bhūmi returns the stolen objects and asks protection for his son (Bhāgavata 10.59).
- Verse (Bhāgavata 10.59.25, opening):
  भूमिरुवाच — नमस्ते देवदेवेश शङ्खचक्रगदाधर ।
  bhūmir uvāca — namas te deva-deveśa śaṅkha-cakra-gadā-dhara
  Bhūmi begins by saluting the Lord bearing conch, discus and mace.
- Festival prescription: Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa 122 prescribes an early bath, remembering Gaṅgā in water and Lakṣmī in oil, and libations with the names of Yama. Verse not given in the encyclopedia.

**Dīpāvalī Amāvāsyā.** Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa 122 is explicitly a Dīpāvalī chapter linking Yama lamp, Caturdaśī bath, lamps, Amāvāsyā rites, awakening Lakṣmī, Govardhana, Bali and Yama-dvitīyā. The exact Sanskrit invocation: verse not given in the encyclopedia. It says to lift the Sanskrit only from the linked Sanskrit tab after a line-by-line check, and not to reverse-translate the English.

**Govardhana Pūjā.** Kṛṣṇa questions the sacrifice to Indra and directs worship of cows, brāhmaṇas and Govardhana (Bhāgavata 10.24); he raises the hill against Indra's rain (10.25).
- Verse (10.24.35):
  कृष्णस्त्वन्यतमं रूपं गोपविश्रम्भणं गतः ।
  शैलोऽस्मीति ब्रुवन् भूरि बलिमादद् बृहद्वपुः ॥
  kṛṣṇas tv anyatamaṁ rūpaṁ gopa-viśrambhaṇaṁ gataḥ / śailo 'smīti bruvan bhūri balim ādad bṛhad-vapuḥ
  Kṛṣṇa assumes a great form, declares "I am the mountain," and receives the offerings.
- Verse (10.25.19):
  इत्युक्त्वैकेन हस्तेन कृत्वा गोवर्धनाचलम् ।
  दधार लीलया विष्णुश्छत्राकमिव बालकः ॥
  ity uktvaikena hastena kṛtvā govardhanācalam / dadhāra līlayā viṣṇuś chatrākam iva bālakaḥ
  He lifts Govardhana with one hand as easily as a child holds a mushroom.
- Festival prescription: Padma Purāṇa Uttara 122.26ff, for Govardhana and cow worship on the first bright day. Verse not given in the encyclopedia.

**Bali Pratipadā.** The Vāmana–Bali cycle (Bhāgavata 8.18–23). Padma Purāṇa Uttara 122.43–70 prescribes drawing and worshipping King Bali, remembers his wife Vindhyāvalī, and calls the celebration Kaumudī. Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.

**Yama-dvitīyā.** Padma Purāṇa Uttara 122.89–103 says Yamunā fed and honoured her brother Yama on bright dvitīyā, and that a brother should eat from his sister's hand and give her gifts. Verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.

**Nāgula Cavithi.** Verse: none. The encyclopedia gives no primary-text locus and states that none has been verified; it supplies no "Nāgula Cavithi Purāṇa chapter". The general serpent narratives (Śeṣa, Vāsuki, Kadrū's sons, Janamejaya's sacrifice, Āstīka) are in the Mahābhārata, Ādi Parvan, Āstīka Parvan.

**Prabodhinī Ekādaśī and Tulasī Vivāha.** Skanda Purāṇa, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa, Kārttika-māsa-māhātmya chapter 31 describes the marriage rite of Tulasī and Viṣṇu, in that recension with bright navamī and a three-day rite, evening worship, Puruṣasūkta, the twelve-syllable mantra and homa.
- Mantra named in the chapter:
  ॐ नमो भगवते वासुदेवाय
  oṁ namo bhagavate vāsudevāya
- The Puruṣasūkta text and the Vṛndā backstory: verse not given in the encyclopedia.

**Kārttika Pūrṇimā.** Śiva burns the three cities with the Pāśupata arrow (Śiva Purāṇa, Rudra-saṃhitā, Yuddha-khaṇḍa 5.10); those who seek refuge are saved.
- Verse fragment as given (sense of Rudra-saṃhitā 5.10.27):
  विष्ण्वात्मकः स बाणोऽथ वह्न्यग्रः …
  viṣṇvātmakaḥ sa bāṇo 'tha vahnyagraḥ …
- The encyclopedia says to use the Sanskrit tab to confirm the full pāda before production. The rest of the verse is not given.

### The encyclopedia's own caveats and audit flags
- Kārttika does not begin on one universal civil date; month system, location and sunrise rules matter. Southern practice details are regional; one family's fasting rule is not universal śāstra. Kārttikai Dīpam in Tamil solar Kārttikai is related by light but is not the same lunar observance.
- Dhanteras: the Bhāgavata gives the appearance narrative but not the modern buying customs, which are later commercial or household tradition.
- Naraka Caturdaśī: Bhāgavata 10.59 does not explicitly date the battle to this caturdaśī. Firecrackers are not part of the Purāṇic core. The Satyabhāmā-as-slayer form (from the mother-boon) is a later regional Telugu retelling; in the chapter Kṛṣṇa's discus cuts off Naraka's head. Label it unless a precise source is supplied.
- Dīpāvalī: there is no single pan-Indian original story; Rāma's return is a later association and Vālmīki does not name a Dīpāvalī festival; Lakṣmī Pūjā is not equally dominant everywhere.
- Bali Pratipadā: Bhāgavata 8.18–23 is a source for Bali's story, not by itself for the Kārttika date.
- Yama-dvitīyā: the cited chapter's Dvitīyā section centres on Yama and Yamunā; it does not give a complete Citragupta Pūjā paddhati. The source's gendered household setting should be explained historically, not used to exclude families.
- Nāgula Cavithi: status living regional tradition; not the same as Nāga Pañcamī; popular vrata-kathās must not be called Purāṇic without edition, section and verse. Safety: do not pour milk into inhabited anthills, handle snakes or block burrows.
- Tulasī Vivāha: not fixed to one tithi; the encyclopedia says the chapter itself shows practice broader than "always Dvādaśī". Versions of the Jalandhara or Śaṅkhacūḍa backstory differ sharply and contain ethically difficult elements; do not fold them into one universal story.
- Kārttika Pūrṇimā: the Tripura-dāha chapter is authentic, but a separate calendrical authority is needed to prove every detail of its Kārttika Pūrṇimā observance. The association is traditional and widespread. Guru Nanak Gurpurab, Boita Bandana, Jain and Vaiṣṇava observances are separate modules needing their own sourcing.
- Editorial note: sources not named in the encyclopedia are not supplied here.

## Mārgaśīrṣa Māsam (मार्गशीर्ष)

Calendar note. In amānta calendars the month begins after Kārttika Amāvāsyā. Mokṣadā Ekādaśī, Gītā Jayantī and Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī must not be collapsed into one. (Source: encyclopedia, Mārgaśīrṣa calendar note)

### Viśiṣṭata of the month
Kṛṣṇa declares the month his own in the Bhagavad Gītā. This is the clear textual basis for its prestige.

मासानां मार्गशीर्षोऽहमृतूनां कुसुमाकरः ॥
māsānāṁ mārgaśīrṣo 'ham ṛtūnāṁ kusumākaraḥ
"Among months I am Mārgaśīrṣa; among seasons, the flower-bearing spring." (Bhagavad Gītā 10.35, second half)
(Source: https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/srimad?choose=1&field_chapter_value=10&field_nsutra_value=35)

### Observances

1. Thursday Lakṣmī worship. Every Thursday of the month. Regional: Maharashtra, Odisha (Māṇabāsā Gurubāra, with the Lakṣmī Purāṇa tradition) and parts of South India. The home is cleaned, a lamp is lit, Lakṣmī is worshipped and food is offered. The encyclopedia says these are not one pan-Indian vrata and must be labelled by region. The Odia Lakṣmī Purāṇa is a medieval Odia work, not one of the Sanskrit Mahāpurāṇas. Scriptural verse for the vrata: verse not given in the encyclopedia. (Source: Bhagavad Gītā 10.35 link above)

2. Kālabhairava Jayantī, Bhairavāṣṭamī. Dark Aṣṭamī (Kālāṣṭamī). Pūrṇimānta calendars call it Mārgaśīrṣa Kṛṣṇa Aṣṭamī. Amānta calendars may call the same day Kārttika Kṛṣṇa Aṣṭamī. Bhairava is Śiva's fierce guardianship. Caveat kept: the encyclopedia has not verified an exact text and verse for the festival. The Purāṇic accounts of Brahmā's fifth head differ and must not be harmonized. It advises obtaining a critical edition locus before publishing a Purāṇic birth story. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. (Source: encyclopedia, section 12; no URL given)

3. Vivāha Pañcamī. Śukla Pañcamī. The wedding of Sītā and Rāma, celebrated at Mithilā and Ayodhyā. Narrative: Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa 66 to 73. The epic does not itself verify the modern festival date. Some families avoid holding human weddings on this day. This is a regional folk belief, not a pan-Indian rule.

इयं सीता मम सुता सहधर्मचरी तव ।
प्रतीच्छ चैनां भद्रं ते पाणिं गृह्णीष्व पाणिना ॥
iyaṁ sītā mama sutā sahadharma-carī tava / pratīccha caināṁ bhadraṁ te pāṇiṁ gṛhṇīṣva pāṇinā
"This Sītā, my daughter, will walk with you in dharma; receive her and take her hand." (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla 73.26. Numbering can vary slightly by edition.)
(Source: https://sanskritdocuments.org/sites/valmikiramayan/)

4. Subrahmaṇya Ṣaṣṭhī, Skanda Ṣaṣṭhī. Śukla Ṣaṣṭhī. Observed in Karnataka and parts of Andhra and Telangana, notably at Kukke Subrahmaṇya. Tamil Skanda Ṣaṣṭi is a different festival. It is the six days after Dīpāvalī. Narrative: Skanda Purāṇa, Māheśvara-khaṇḍa, Kaumārika-khaṇḍa 31, on Kumāra and Tāraka. This supports the story, not the date. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc366017.html)

5. Mokṣadā Ekādaśī and Gītā Jayantī. Śukla Ekādaśī. Mokṣadā is the vrata name. Gītā Jayantī remembers the Gītā teaching. They coincide but have different source histories. The Mokṣadā story of King Vaikhānasa and his father is attributed to the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa. Caveat kept: the audit found only sectarian retellings and no stable chapter and verse. It must be published as "traditional Ekādaśī-māhātmya, commonly attributed to Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa". The Mahābhārata does not state that the Gītā was spoken on this date. That is received tradition.

सर्वधर्मान्परित्यज्य मामेकं शरणं व्रज ।
अहं त्वा सर्वपापेभ्यो मोक्षयिष्यामि मा शुचः ॥
sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja / ahaṁ tvā sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ
"Take refuge in Me alone; I shall release you from wrongdoing; do not grieve." (Bhagavad Gītā 18.66)
(Source: https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/srimad?choose=1&field_chapter_value=18&field_nsutra_value=66&scmad=1 ; Mokṣadā account, attributed and not critically verified: https://www.iskconofbergen.org/ekadashi-stories/mokshada-ekadashi)

6. Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī, Mukkōṭi Ekādaśī. Śukla Ekādaśī in the solar Dhanur-māsa (Tamil Mārgaḻi). Śrīvaiṣṇava temples open the Paramapada Vāsal (Śrīraṅgam Adhyayana Utsava, Tirumala Vaikuṇṭha Dvāra Darśanam). It is not always Mokṣadā. In some years it falls in lunar Pauṣa. Caveat kept: no Purāṇic chapter for the gate-opening rite was verified. The Mura-demon story must not be used as proof of the rite. "Mukkōṭi" must not be read as a literal count of thirty-three crore gods. Living-tradition sources should be temple publications, not commercial blogs. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. (Source: none given; temple publications recommended)

7. Dattātreya Jayantī. Mārgaśīrṣa Pūrṇimā. Important in Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh and Gujarat. The Bhāgavata gives the birth context (4.1.15 and 17 to 33). The Pūrṇimā date belongs to later calendrical tradition. Śrī Guru Caritra is a later Marathi work and must not be called a Purāṇa. Variant kept: the story that the Trimūrti were turned into infants after testing Anasūyā is widespread but is not in Bhāgavata 4.1.15 to 33, and needs its own source.

अत्रेः पत्न्यनसूया त्रीञ्जज्ञे सुयशसः सुतान् ।
दत्तं दुर्वाससं सोममात्मेशब्रह्मसम्भवान् ॥
atreḥ patny anasūyā trīñ jajñe suyaśasaḥ sutān / dattaṁ durvāsasaṁ somam ātmeśa-brahma-sambhavān
Anasūyā, Atri's wife, bore the renowned sons Datta, Durvāsas and Soma. (Bhāgavata 4.1.15)
(Source: https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/1/15/ ; https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/1/)

8. Annapūrṇā Jayantī. Mārgaśīrṣa Pūrṇimā in some North Indian calendars only. It is not a universal pairing with Dattātreya Jayantī. The day recalls Pārvatī as Annapūrṇā giving food. Caveat kept: no chapter and verse for the date was verified, and the encyclopedia says not to invent one. It flags the Skanda Purāṇa Kāśī-khaṇḍa passage for checking. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. (Source: encyclopedia, section 18; no URL given)

9. Dhanu Saṅkrānti, Dhanur-māsa and Tiruppāvai. A solar track, calculated astronomically, not by tithi. Pre-dawn worship. Tamil Śrīvaiṣṇavas recite the thirty verses of Āṇḍāḷ's Tiruppāvai through Mārgaḻi. Tiruppāvai is a Tamil devotional work, not a Purāṇa. Odisha's Dhanu Yātrā and Dhanu Muan are separate. Caveat kept: the whole solar month must not be called Mārgaśīrṣa, and Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī must not be promised to fall in lunar Mārgaśīrṣa. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. (Source: none given; a reliable Śrīvaiṣṇava edition recommended)

### Audit flags for Mārgaśīrṣa (encyclopedia, section III)
Needs further primary verification: the Kālabhairava Jayantī date source, the Mokṣadā māhātmya's claimed Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa locus, the Annapūrṇā Jayantī date, and the Vaikuṇṭha-dvāra authority. Story chapters may not give the modern festival date. Narrative source and calendar source are to be kept in separate fields.

## Pauṣa Māsam (पौष)

### Viśiṣṭata of the month
The encyclopedia gives no month-level glorification for Pauṣa. It gives observances only. Month-level verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.

### Observances

1. Saphalā Ekādaśī. Kṛṣṇa Ekādaśī. Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa 40. Prince Lumpaka, cold and weak in the forest, offers fruits under an aśvattha tree to Viṣṇu. He reforms. The chapter repeatedly calls the day saphalā and viṣṇu-vāsara. Verse: not given in the encyclopedia. It asks that a checked Sanskrit verse be lifted from the Sanskrit tab of the chapter. Caution kept: do not confuse it with Pauṣa Śukla Putradā. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365617.html)

2. Putradā Ekādaśī. Śukla Ekādaśī. Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa 41. King Suketumān and Queen Campakā, without a child, are taught the vrata by the Viśvedevas and later receive a son. Textual anchor given as a phrase only: putradeti samākhyātā, "it is known as Putradā". A full verse is not given in the encyclopedia. Caveats kept: the rite must not be used to blame those without children. There are two Putradā Ekādaśīs, Pauṣa Śukla and Śrāvaṇa Śukla. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365618.html)

3. Śākambharī Jayantī, Pūrṇimā. Pauṣa Pūrṇimā in Śākta tradition. Devī Bhāgavata 7.28 tells of Śatākṣī and Śākambharī, who fed the hungry with vegetables and defeated Durgama. Caveat kept: the chapter does not establish Pauṣa Pūrṇimā as her birthday. That date is from later calendrical and temple tradition. The exact calendrical source used by each temple is still to be confirmed.

इति तेषां वचः श्रुत्वा शाकान् स्वकरसंस्थितान् ।
स्वादूनि फलमूलानि भक्षणार्थं ददौ शिवा ॥ … शाकम्भरीति नामापि तद्दिनात् समभून्नृप ॥
iti teṣāṃ vacaḥ śrutvā śākān svakarasaṃsthitān / svādūni phalamūlāni bhakṣaṇārthaṃ dadau śivā … śākambharīti nāmāpi taddināt samabhūn nṛpa
Hearing their plea, the Goddess gave vegetables, sweet fruits and roots for food; from that day she became known as Śākambharī. (Devī Bhāgavata 7.28.45 to 47)
(Source: https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/देवीभागवतपुराणम्/स्कन्धः_०७/अध्यायः_२८)

4. Makara Saṅkrānti. A solar ingress, about 14 to 15 January, computed for the year and place. Basis: Dharma-sindhu, Pauṣa section (N), which gives the decision rule and recommends bath, gifts, śrāddha without piṇḍa, sesame rites, and Śiva and Sūrya worship. It is not a Purāṇic birth-story festival. Caveat kept: it must not be called a lunar Pauṣa festival without qualification. Astronomical Uttarāyaṇa at the December solstice is not the same instant. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. (Source: https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/dharmasindhu/bookview.php?chapnum=11)

5. Regional harvest cluster: Lohri, Pongal, Kanuma. Regional living tradition (R). Lohri is a Punjabi bonfire before Saṅkrānti. Pongal has Bhogi, Sūrya Pongal, Māṭṭu Pongal and Kāṇum Pongal. Kanuma and Mukkanuma honour cattle in Telugu regions. Dullā Bhaṭṭī belongs to Punjabi folklore, not to the Purāṇas. Each is to be a separate article and not a name for Makara Saṅkrānti. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. No source URL is given; tradition-owned sources are still to be obtained.

### Audit flags for Pauṣa
Śākambharī: story and date need separate citations. Makara Saṅkrānti is solar. Lohri, Pongal, Kanuma, Dol, Yaosang and Gaura Pūrṇimā need tradition-owned sources. The Kamakoti Dharma-sindhu page is a condensed translation, and the Sanskrit edition should be consulted.

## Māgha Māsam (माघ)

### Viśiṣṭata of the month
Māgha is a month-long discipline of pre-dawn bathing, restraint, worship and charity. Basis: Dharma-sindhu, Māgha section (N), which also cites Purāṇic material for Prayāga. The encyclopedia calls the digest a safer source than claims that one Purāṇa created every melā custom.

दुःखदारिद्र्यनाशाय विष्णोस्तोषणाय च ।
प्रातःस्नानं करोम्यद्य माघे पापविनाशनम् ॥
duḥkha-dāridrya-nāśāya viṣṇos toṣaṇāya ca / prātaḥ-snānaṃ karomy adya māghe pāpa-vināśanam
"In Māgha I undertake this morning bath for the ending of suffering and poverty, for the pleasure of Viṣṇu and for purification." (Dharma-sindhu digest)
(Source: https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/dharmasindhu/bookview.php?chapnum=12)

Safety note kept: no text should pressure the elderly, pregnant, very young or medically vulnerable into cold-water immersion.

### Observances

1. Māgha Snāna. Often begins on Pauṣa Pūrṇimā. Its nodes are Maunī Amāvasyā and Māgha Pūrṇimā. Sesame, food, clothing and aid are given. Caveat kept: the classical locus for every modern claim about Maunī Amāvasyā is not verified. It is presented as a regional and pilgrimage observance within Māgha snāna.

2. Ṣaṭtilā Ekādaśī. Kṛṣṇa Ekādaśī. Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa 42. A woman of severe vows refuses alms. Her heavenly house is empty of food until she learns Ṣaṭtilā and gives. Six uses of sesame: bathing, rubbing, fire offering, sesame water, giving, eating. Caution kept: this is not licence for waste. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365619.html)

3. Vasant Pañcamī, Sarasvatī Pūjā. Śukla Pañcamī. Devī Bhāgavata 9.4 places Sarasvatī worship and the beginning of learning on this day. Dharma-sindhu also names it Vasant Pañcamī and notes Rati and Manmatha worship. Anchor given as a phrase from the prose translation: māgha-māse site pakṣe pañcamyām, "on the fifth day of Māgha's bright fortnight". A full verse is not given in the encyclopedia. Caveats kept: do not call it only Sarasvatī's birthday. Yellow dress and foods are regional. In parts of South India the main vidyārambha is in Śāradā Navarātri. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/devi-bhagavata-purana/d/doc57317.html ; https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/dharmasindhu/bookview.php?chapnum=12)

4. Ratha Saptamī, Acalā Saptamī. Śukla Saptamī. Bhaviṣya Purāṇa, Brāhma-parvan 52. Sāmba observes it and is freed of afflictions. Dharma-sindhu gives the dawn tithi rule. Nārada-purāṇa summaries call it Acalā Vrata.

शाम्बोऽपि कृत्वा विधिवत्सप्तमीं रथसप्तमीम् ।
आधिभिर्व्याधिभिर्मुक्तो जगामाशु स्वमन्दिरम् ॥
śāmbo 'pi kṛtvā vidhivat saptamīṃ rathasaptamīm / ādhibhir vyādhibhir mukto jagāmāśu svamandiram
Sāmba duly observed Ratha Saptamī, was freed from afflictions and returned home. (Bhaviṣya Purāṇa 1.52.2)
Caveats kept: the seven arka leaves are a regional detail. Calling it Sūrya's birthday is a popular simplification.
(Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/bhavishya-purana-sanskrit/d/doc1257460.html ; https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/dharmasindhu/bookview.php?chapnum=12)

5. Bhīṣmāṣṭamī. Śukla Aṣṭamī. Dharma-sindhu, Māgha section (N), prescribes tarpaṇa to Bhīṣma with water and sesame. The Mahābhārata narrates his time on the bed of arrows. The epic and the annual rule are to be cited separately. Caveat kept: this rite is not the source of the Bhīṣma Ekādaśī customs. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. The Kamakoti page is a condensed translation, so the Sanskrit edition wording is still to be consulted. (Source: https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/dharmasindhu/bookview.php?chapnum=12)

6. Jayā Ekādaśī, called Bhaimī. Śukla Ekādaśī. Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa 43. Mālyavān and Puṣpavatī, cursed as piśācas, are released by an unplanned fast and vigil. Anchor, given only as chapter sense: māgha-śukle tu yā rājan ekādaśī jayā smṛtā. The encyclopedia says to use the checked Sanskrit tab for the exact verse and number. Caveat kept: Bhaimī is a later or regional name and must not be put into the chapter citation. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365620.html)

7. Bhīṣma Ekādaśī and Viṣṇu-sahasranāma. Śukla Ekādaśī, in Telugu and some South Indian communities. Regional tradition plus epic narrative. The hymn is in the Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana-parvan (often 13.135 in the critical edition, Ganguli section CXLIX). Caveat kept: the epic does not establish the annual Ekādaśī date. Do not assert that Bhīṣma gave the hymn on this date without a calendar text. Cross-numbering of the hymn and Bhīṣma's death chapters is still to be verified.

जगत्प्रभुं देवदेवमनन्तं पुरुषोत्तमम् ।
स्तुवन्नामसहस्रेण पुरुषः सततोत्थितः ॥
jagat-prabhuṃ deva-devam anantaṃ puruṣottamam / stuvan nāma-sahasreṇa puruṣaḥ satatotthitaḥ
A person should continually praise the endless Supreme Person, Lord of the world and God of gods, through the thousand names.
(Source: https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/महाभारतम्/अनुशासनपर्व)

8. Māgha Gupta Navarātri. Nine nights from Śukla Pratipadā, in some Śākta and Tantric lineages. Regional and Tantric tradition. Caveat kept: no single Purāṇic passage was verified for the name or a universal procedure. Secret mantras and initiatory procedures must not be published. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. No source URL is given.

9. Māgha Pūrṇimā. Completes the bathing discipline. Charity, and Viṣṇu or Satyanārāyaṇa worship in many homes. Basis: nibandha plus regional practice. Caveat kept: no single origin story. Buddhist associations belong in separate articles. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. (Source: encyclopedia, section 14)

### Audit flags for Māgha
Consult the Sanskrit Dharma-sindhu or Nirṇaya-sindhu for Bhīṣmāṣṭamī wording. Verify cross-numbering before displaying verse IDs for the Sahasranāma. Do not quote modern English translations at length.

## Phālguna Māsam (फाल्गुन)

### Viśiṣṭata of the month
The encyclopedia gives no month-level glorification for Phālguna. It notes that some pūrṇimānta calendars call the lunar month of Mahāśivarātri Māgha. This is a naming difference, not a different festival. Month-level verse: verse not given in the encyclopedia.

### Observances

1. Vijayā Ekādaśī. Kṛṣṇa Ekādaśī. Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa 44. Rāma, at the ocean before the crossing to Laṅkā, keeps the vrata on the advice of Bakadālbhya. Caveat kept: the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa does not narrate this episode. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365621.html)

2. Mahāśivarātri. Kṛṣṇa Caturdaśī (amānta). Śiva Purāṇa, Koṭirudra-saṃhitā 39 and 40. The hunter Gurudruha keeps the night awake in a bilva tree; water and leaves fall on a liṅga; the deer's truthfulness stirs his conscience. Dharma-sindhu gives niśītha and four-yāma practice. The day is chosen by Caturdaśī extending through niśītha. Caveat kept: liṅgodbhava, the marriage of Śiva and Pārvatī, the dance and the hunter are different lenses, and none is the single reason. Monthly Śivarātri is every Kṛṣṇa Caturdaśī.

अज्ञानेनापि यः कुर्याच्छिवरात्रिव्रतं नरः ।
सोऽपि याति परं स्थानं किं पुनर्भक्तिसंयुतः ॥
ajñānenāpi yaḥ kuryāc chivarātri-vrataṃ naraḥ / so 'pi yāti paraṃ sthānaṃ kiṃ punar bhakti-saṃyutaḥ
If even an unknowing observance leads upward, how much more one joined with devotion. (Śiva Purāṇa 4.40.97. The encyclopedia says to check the Sanskrit against the edition before production.)
(Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226541.html ; https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/dharmasindhu/bookview.php?chapnum=12)

3. Āmalakī Ekādaśī. Śukla Ekādaśī. Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa 45. In the kingdom of Vaidīśa a hunter listens through the night beside an āmalakī tree and is changed. Caveat kept: not the same as the Akṣaya Navamī āmalā customs. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365622.html)

4. Holikā Pūjā and Holikā Dahana. Phālguna Pūrṇimā evening, with attention to Bhadrā. Dharma-sindhu, Phālguna section (N), gives the rite and a Dhundhā-rākṣasī frame.

अस्माभिर्भयसन्त्रस्तैः कृता त्वं होलिके यतः ।
अतस्त्वां पूजयिष्यामि भूति-भूति-प्रदा भव ॥
asmābhir bhaya-santrastaiḥ kṛtā tvaṃ holike yataḥ / atas tvāṃ pūjayiṣyāmi bhūti-bhūti-pradā bhava
"Because we approach you while distressed by fear, O Holikā, we worship you; grant well-being and prosperity." (Dharma-sindhu digest)
Caveats kept: the Bhāgavata, skandha 7, has Prahlāda but does not name a sister Holikā or the fireproof shawl. The exact early source of that story is unresolved. Do not invent a Vedic Holikā mantra. Holikā Dahana and the colour play are separate. The Kamakoti page is a condensed translation.
(Source: https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/dharmasindhu/bookview.php?chapnum=13 ; https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/)

5. Holī, Dhūḷivandana, Dol and Vasantotsava. Colour play follows on Kṛṣṇa Pratipadā in pūrṇimānta reckoning. Bengal and Odisha keep Dol Pūrṇimā on the full moon. Dharma-sindhu says Vasantotsava begins on Kṛṣṇa Pratipadā. The Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa colour theology is strong in Braj and later Vaiṣṇava literature and must not be projected onto early sources. Lathmār Holī, Dol Jātrā, Yaosang, Dhūḷivandana and Rang Pañcamī have separate histories. Consent is required. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. (Source: https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/dharmasindhu/bookview.php?chapnum=13)

6. Phālguna Pūrṇimā, a multi-tradition date. Holikā, Dol, month completion and community vows share the date. There is no single origin story. Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas keep Gaura Pūrṇimā for Śrī Caitanya, based on Caitanya-bhāgavata and Caitanya-caritāmṛta, not an ancient Purāṇa. Maharashtra may stress Śimga and Rang Pañcamī. Yaosang is Manipuri. A shared date does not mean a shared origin. Verse not given in the encyclopedia.

7. Payovrata. Śukla Pratipadā through Dvādaśī, twelve days. Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.16. Kaśyapa teaches Aditi a milk-diet vow to Viṣṇu, and Viṣṇu later appears as Vāmana. Caveats kept: it is demanding and needs health care and guidance. It is not merely a fast for wishes. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. (Source: https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/8/16/)

### Audit flags for Phālguna
Do not cite Bhāgavata 7 for the named Holikā. Do not present Holī as uniform. Do not give fasts as medical advice. Add location-aware tithi rules only through a tested pañcāṅga engine.

## Recurring and fixed-count vratas

Editorial status from the encyclopedia. This is a source-audited research draft and not a universal manual. Fasting is not the essence of devotion. Children, pregnant or nursing people, older adults, diabetics and those on medication should use a safe form of restraint. Do not copy booklet prose.

1. Somavāra Vrata (Monday, Śiva). P for the general vrata; R/U for the counts of 11 and 16. Skanda Purāṇa, Prabhāsa-khaṇḍa 25 (Gośṛṅga and the Gandharva; Soma installs Someśvara). The chapter's own cycle ends with an udyāpana on the ninth Monday. It does not prove 11 or 16. The 16-Monday tale is a later booklet tradition, and "purāṇokta" on a title page proves nothing.
ॐ नमः शिवाय
oṃ namaḥ śivāya. "Reverence to Śiva."
(Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc626813.html)

2. Sapta Śanivāra (seven Saturdays). R/U. No Purāṇic source for the seven-week package was verified. Do not merge it with Śani Pradoṣa, Saturday Hanumān worship or temple oil practices. The Vikramāditya and Śani tales exist in several recensions. No gemstone or costly remedy is to be advised.
ॐ शनैश्चराय नमः
oṃ śanaiścarāya namaḥ. "Reverence to Śanaiścara."
Source URL: none given.

3. Pradoṣa Vrata. P. Skanda Purāṇa, Brahma-khaṇḍa, Brahmottara-khaṇḍa 7. Trayodaśī in either fortnight, worship just before sunset. Śāṇḍilya teaches the woman and the boys Śucivrata and Dharmagupta. The chapter allows worship according to means. The core is the tithi, not the weekday. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc423670.html)

4. Ekādaśī framework. P. Nārada Purāṇa, Pūrva-bhāga 1.23. Preparation on Daśamī, fast and worship on Ekādaśī, completion on Dvādaśī. The story of Bhadraśīla remembers King Dharmakīrti. Grain rules and dates depend on the sampradāya and pañcāṅga. Harsh and caste-exclusive passages must not be reproduced as timeless instruction.
पुण्डरीकाक्ष शरणं मे भवाच्युत
puṇḍarīkākṣa śaraṇaṃ me bhavācyuta. "Lotus-eyed Acyuta, be my refuge." (paraphrased meaning, as the encyclopedia states)
(Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/narada-purana-english/d/doc1501987.html)

5. Saṅkaṣṭī, Saṅkaṭahara Caturthī. N/R. Gaṇeśa, Kṛṣṇa Caturthī, after moonrise. Aṅgārakī when on Tuesday. The witness is a Sanskrit prayoga, which is a living paddhati and not early Purāṇic proof. The monthly kathās vary and are marked U.
एकदन्तं महाकायं तप्तकाञ्चनसन्निभम् ।
लम्बोदरं विशालाक्षं वन्देऽहं गणनायकम् ॥
ekadantaṃ mahākāyaṃ taptakāñcanasannibham / lambodaraṃ viśālākṣaṃ vande'haṃ gaṇanāyakam
"I bow to the one-tusked, great-bodied leader of the gaṇas, radiant like heated gold, broad-eyed and large-bellied."
(Source: https://hindunidhi.com/sankata-hara-chaturthi-puja-vidhanam-sanskrit/ , a secondary transmission to be checked against a printed prayoga)

6. Satyanārāyaṇa Vrata. P, recension-sensitive. Transmitted as part of the Skanda Purāṇa, Revā-khaṇḍa, with chapter numbers differing among editions. Bhaviṣya Purāṇa 3.2.24 names the vrata at verse 37. Performed on Pūrṇimā or another auspicious occasion.
सत्यनारायणव्रतमिह सर्वान् कामान् ददाति हि ।
satyanārāyaṇa-vratam iha sarvān kāmān dadāti hi
"The Satyanārāyaṇa vow is said here to grant desired aims." (Bhaviṣya Purāṇa 3.2.24.37)
Note: the Devanāgarī and IAST are joined in one line in the encyclopedia. The cast of the story varies by recension.
(Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/bhavishya-purana-sanskrit/d/doc1267945.html ; https://archive.org/details/NrEY_skanda-puranam-vol-5-uttarardham-avanti-khanda-reva-khanda-by-veda-vyas-guru-man)

7. Pūrṇimā and Amāvāsyā frameworks. Not single vratas. There is no universal Pūrṇimā story. Amāvāsyā is linked to ancestors, but is not automatically inauspicious. Simple remembrance must not be confused with Vedic śrāddha, which needs eligibility. Verse not given in the encyclopedia.

8. Varalakṣmī Vrata. N/R, attribution U. Friday before Śrāvaṇa Pūrṇimā. The story of Cārumatī of Kuṇḍina is a paddhati narrative attributed to the Bhaviṣyottara Purāṇa, to be labelled as such until the locus is verified. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. (Source: https://aupasana.com/prayoga/prayoga/chintamani/varalakshmi.html)

9. Vaibhava Lakṣmī Vrata. R/M/U. Fridays, counts of 7, 11 or 21. No early Purāṇic chapter was found. Booklets are often copyrighted, and an Internet Archive upload does not prove authorship. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. (Source, discovery only: https://archive.org/details/vaibhava-lakshmi-vratha-orginal)

10. Other weekday vratas. Tuesday (several deity streams), Thursday (Bṛhaspati, Viṣṇu, Dattātreya or Rāghavendra), Friday (Lakṣmī, Santoshi Mā, Durgā) and Wednesday (R/U) are all regional and are kept separate. Sunday Sūrya worship is P/N/R, and one must never look directly at the Sun. Verse not given in the encyclopedia.

11. Sai Thursday. M, modern devotional tradition tied to Shirdi Sai Baba (d. 1918). Not Vedic or Purāṇic. Nine-Thursday manuals vary and are often copyrighted. Verse not given in the encyclopedia.

12. Telugu nomulu. Regional oral heritage, not to be falsely upgraded to Purāṇa. Named: Aṭla Tadde (Āśvayuja Kṛṣṇa Tṛtīyā), Undrāḷḷa Taddi (Bhādrapada Kṛṣṇa Tṛtīyā), Maṅgaḷagaurī Nomu (Śrāvaṇa Tuesdays), Varalakṣmī, Kedāreśvara Nomu (Dīpāvali Amāvāsyā), Nāgula Caviti (Kārtika Śukla Caturthī), Polāla Amāvāsyā (Śrāvaṇa Amāvāsyā), Śrāvaṇa Friday Lakṣmī pūjā, and Kārtika Tulasī and Dāmodara observances. Under verification: Nandikeśvara Nomu, Sampat Śukravāra Nomu, Kanne Taddi, Ratha Saptamī household variants, lakṣa-vatti and koti-dīpa completions. Verse not given in the encyclopedia. Nāgula Caviti's kathā and the Kedāreśvara booklet story are flagged for verification.

13. Dīkṣā cycles. Temple or lineage practice. Ayyappa Maṇḍala, about 41 days. Telugu Hanumān dīkṣā, 41 days from Caitra Pūrṇimā to Vaiśākha Kṛṣṇa Daśamī, which does not prove Caitra Pūrṇimā is Hanumān's universal birth date. Bhavānī and Durgā dīkṣā. The app should inform and not initiate. Verse not given in the encyclopedia.

14. Pārāyaṇa cycles. The scripture may be P, but the 7, 9, 11 and 41 day divisions are N/R/M. Covered: Rāmāyaṇa, Bhāgavata saptāha, Devī Māhātmya (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa 81 to 93 in common numbering), Viṣṇu-sahasranāma, Lalitā-sahasranāma (Brahmāṇḍa tradition, Śrīvidyā layers may need initiation), Guru Caritra, Sai Satcharita, Sundarakāṇḍa, Hanumān Cālīsā. Seed-mantras and nyāsas are not to be published. Verse not given in the encyclopedia.

Production checklist and authority matrix. The encyclopedia lists a fifteen-point checklist for each article and a compact authority matrix. Its central rules are: give the source badge, give exact text and edition or "not yet verified", give one short verified Sanskrit verse only, separate textual prescription from household form, label interpretation, promise no worldly result and no unsafe fasting.
