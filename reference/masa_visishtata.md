# Māsa Viśiṣṭata — significance, Purāṇa references, and stories per Telugu lunar month

A companion reference to `thithi_parvadina_index.md`, which covers the 15 tithis. This file
covers the 12 lunar months (māsas) of the Telugu/Hindu calendar, from Chaitra through Phālguṇa.
For each month, this file gives: its basic identity (Telugu/Sanskrit names, Gregorian overlap,
ṛtu/season); its *viśiṣṭata*, meaning why the month itself is considered spiritually special,
apart from the festivals that happen to fall within it (those festivals are already covered in
`thithi_parvadina_index.md` and `festivals_aarshavani.md`); which Purāṇas, if any, carry a
dedicated māhātmya glorifying the month; and any itihāsa or purāṇic stories tied specifically to
the month itself.

**Status:** research-sourced from external web sources (not the Aarsha Vani magazine corpus).
Every claim below is cited inline with a source URL. Where a month lacks a dedicated
Purāṇic māhātmya as prominent as Kārtika's or Māgha's, that gap is stated honestly rather than
invented. Several months' significance instead rests on vrata-kathās, festival narratives, or a
single scriptural verse — for example, Mārgaśira's Bhagavad Gītā 10.35 self-declaration — rather
than on a stand-alone month-glorification text.

## Chaitra Māsam (చైత్రం)

### Names & basic info
- Telugu name: **Chaitra Māsam** (చైత్ర మాసం); also called **Madhu Māsam** in some traditions.
- Sanskrit/IAST: **Caitra** (चैत्र).
- Gregorian overlap: roughly **March–April**.
- Position: **1st month** of the traditional Hindu/Telugu lunar year (the year begins with Chaitra Śuddha Pādyami, i.e. Ugādi). (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaitra)
- Ṛtu: **Vasanta Ṛtu** (Spring), which spans Chaitra–Vaiśākha. (Source: https://www.learnreligions.com/the-six-seasons-of-india-p2-1770072)
- Name origin: The month is named for the fact that the full moon in this month occurs near/in **Chitrā nakṣatra**. Chitrā is presided over by Viśvakarma (Tvaṣṭā), the divine architect. (Source: https://www.hindu-blog.com/2021/03/chaitra-month-name-origin-of-chaitra-mahina.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaitra)

### Viśiṣṭata (significance)
- Chaitra Śuddha Pādyami is **Ugādi**, the Telugu/Kannada New Year, and per the *Chaturvarga Chintāmaṇi* it is the day **Brahma began the creation of the universe** — planets, stars, seasons, and rains were set in motion starting this day. This makes the whole month auspicious as the "birthday of creation." (Source: https://hindupad.com/chaitra-month-or-chaitra-masam-significance-and-spiritual-importance-chaitra-masam-puja/)
- The month is closely tied to **Lord Vishnu/Rama**: Sri Rama Navami (Rama's birth, Śuddha Navami) and Vasanta Navarātri fall in this month, making Chaitra especially dear to Vaishnava tradition and to devotees of Rama. Hanuman Jayanti (in some regional calendars) also falls in Chaitra. (Source: https://hindupad.com/chaitra-month-or-chaitra-masam-significance-and-spiritual-importance-chaitra-masam-puja/)
- Chaitra is considered auspicious for **commencing Vedic study and Upanayanam** (sacred thread ceremony), since it marks a fresh cosmological/calendrical beginning. (Source: https://hindupad.com/chaitra-month-or-chaitra-masam-significance-and-spiritual-importance-chaitra-masam-puja/)
- Also referred to as Madhu Māsam, associated with the blossoming of spring (madhu = spring/honey), reinforcing its Vasanta-ṛtu identity. (Source: https://indianmandirs.blogspot.com/2017/02/significance-of-chaitra-masamor-madhu.html)

### Purāṇa references
- No dedicated "Chaitra Māhātmyam," as well known as the Kārtika or Māgha Māhātmyam, was found in this search. Chaitra's significance is spread across several texts instead: the Chaturvarga Chintāmaṇi on creation, and various Purāṇas on Ugadi, Vasanta Navaratri, and Rama Navami. The **Skanda Purāṇa** treats Chaturmāsya-related monthly māhātmyas broadly (see kamakoti.org summaries), but no stand-alone Chaitra māhātmya was found. This is an honest gap. A further search of wisdomlib's Skanda, Padma, and Brahma Purana indices for "Chaitra māhātmya" could still turn one up. (Source: https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/details/skandapurana33.html — Chaturmasya Mahatmya, not Chaitra-specific)
- Brahma Purāṇa is traditionally associated with creation narratives generally (fitting Chaitra's "creation began here" theme) though not a dedicated month-māhātmya. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma_Purana)

### Stories/legends
- **Creation begins**: Per Chaturvarga Chintāmaṇi (cited via secondary sources), Brahma created the universe on Chaitra Śuddha Pādyami — this is the scriptural basis for Ugadi being New Year's Day and for the month's overall sanctity. (Source: https://hindupad.com/chaitra-month-or-chaitra-masam-significance-and-spiritual-importance-chaitra-masam-puja/)
- **Name/nakṣatra story**: The month takes its name because the Pūrṇimā (full moon) of this month falls in Chitrā nakṣatra, the star of Viśvakarma; this is a recurring explanatory motif across all 12 Hindu months (each is named for the nakṣatra its full moon occupies). (Source: https://www.hindu-blog.com/2021/03/chaitra-month-name-origin-of-chaitra-mahina.html)
- Sri Rama's birth (Rama Navami) and the Ramayana narrative are anchored in this month, though this is technically a festival/itihāsa event rather than a month-origin myth — included here because it is the dominant devotional story linked to Chaitra. (Source: https://hindupad.com/chaitra-month-or-chaitra-masam-significance-and-spiritual-importance-chaitra-masam-puja/)

Sources: https://blog.telugucalendar.org/chaitra-masam/ ; https://hindupad.com/chaitra-month-or-chaitra-masam-significance-and-spiritual-importance-chaitra-masam-puja/ ; https://www.hindu-blog.com/2021/03/chaitra-month-name-origin-of-chaitra-mahina.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaitra ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma_Purana ; https://indianmandirs.blogspot.com/2017/02/significance-of-chaitra-masamor-madhu.html

## Vaiśākha Māsam (వైశాఖం)

### Names & basic info
- Telugu name: **Vaiśākha Māsam** (వైశాఖ మాసం).
- Sanskrit/IAST: **Vaiśākha** (वैशाख).
- Gregorian overlap: roughly **April–May**. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishakha)
- Position: **2nd month** of the lunar year. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishakha)
- Ṛtu: latter half of **Vasanta Ṛtu** (Spring), Chaitra–Vaiśākha together forming the season. (Source: https://www.learnreligions.com/the-six-seasons-of-india-p2-1770072)

### Viśiṣṭata (significance)
- Vaiśākha is widely proclaimed across the Purāṇas as **the most sacred/meritorious of all months**. The presiding deity of the month is **Madhusūdana** (a form of Vishnu). (Source: https://hindupad.com/vaisakha-masa-vratam-rituals-and-pujas-to-perform-in-vaishakh-month/)
- Vishnu is said to **reside in sacred rivers** throughout Vaiśākha, so bathing (snāna) in rivers, especially at Brahma Muhūrta, is considered equal in merit to visiting and worshipping at all the sacred tīrthas. A bath in this month is said to grant the merit of performing all other holy acts. (Source: https://hindupad.com/vaisakha-masa-vratam-rituals-and-pujas-to-perform-in-vaishakh-month/)
- **Dāna (charity)** is singled out as the paramount observance of the month — especially jala-dāna (water charity, fitting for the hot pre-monsoon season), anna-dāna (food charity), and vastra-dāna (cloth charity), said to bring "eternal prosperity" under Vishnu's command. (Source: https://hindupad.com/vaisakha-masa-vratam-rituals-and-pujas-to-perform-in-vaishakh-month/)
- Multiple Purāṇas — **Skanda Purāṇa, Padma Purāṇa, Nārada Purāṇa** — glorify Vaiśākha as sacred above other months. (Source: https://hindupad.com/vaisakha-masa-vratam-rituals-and-pujas-to-perform-in-vaishakh-month/)
- Two major Vishnu-avatāra jayantis fall in this month: **Narasimha Jayanti** and **Paraśurāma Jayanti**, reinforcing the month's Vaishnava character. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narasimha_Jayanti ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parashurama_Jayanti)

### Purāṇa references
- **Padma Purāṇa, Chapter 98 — "Vaiśākha Māhātmya"** ("वैशाख की महानता" / the greatness of Vaishakha), a dedicated chapter glorifying the month. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365410.html)
- **Skanda Purāṇa, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa — "Vaiśākha Māsa Māhātmya"** is a distinctly named, dedicated section (published separately as "Skanda Purana - Vaishnava Khanda - Vaisakha Masa Mahatmya"). (Source: https://kausiki.com/product/skanda-purana-vaishnava-khanda-vaisakha-masa-mahatmya/ ; https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/details/skandapurana28.html — "Essence of Skanda Purana: Vaisakha Month's Significance")
- This makes Vaiśākha one of the months, along with Kārtika and Māgha, that has a dedicated Purāṇic māhātmya genuinely written for it, rather than a general passage that merely mentions it.

### Stories/legends
- The Skanda Purāṇa's Vaiśākha-māhātmya section (kamakoti.org summary) recounts dialogues extolling the fruits of Vaiśākha vratas — bathing, dāna, and worship of Madhusūdana — narrated typically as a discourse (in the manner of other month-māhātmyas) between a sage and a king/seeker explaining why this month surpasses others in merit. (Source: https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/details/skandapurana28.html)
- Narasimha Jayanti (Vishnu's fierce Narasimha avatāra slaying Hiranyakashipu to save Prahlada) and Paraśurāma Jayanti (birth of the Vishnu-avatāra who curbed adharmic kshatriyas) are the two itihāsa/avatāra narratives most closely tied to this month's calendar date, beyond generic festival status. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narasimha_Jayanti ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parashurama_Jayanti)

Sources: https://hindupad.com/vaisakha-masa-vratam-rituals-and-pujas-to-perform-in-vaishakh-month/ ; https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/details/skandapurana28.html ; https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365410.html ; https://kausiki.com/product/skanda-purana-vaishnava-khanda-vaisakha-masa-mahatmya/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishakha ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narasimha_Jayanti ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parashurama_Jayanti

## Jyēṣṭha Māsam (జ్యేష్ఠం)

### Names & basic info
- Telugu name: **Jyēṣṭha Māsam** (జ్యేష్ఠ మాసం).
- Sanskrit/IAST: **Jyeṣṭha** (ज्येष्ठ), meaning "eldest/greatest" — named for **Jyeṣṭhā nakṣatra**, near which the full moon of this month occurs. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyeshtha_(month))
- Gregorian overlap: roughly **May–June**.
- Position: **3rd month** of the lunar year. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyeshtha_(month))
- Ṛtu: **Grīṣma Ṛtu** (Summer), Jyēṣṭha–Āṣāḍha together forming the season. (Source: https://www.learnreligions.com/the-six-seasons-of-india-p2-1770072)

### Viśiṣṭata (significance)
- Jyēṣṭha is a month specially dedicated to **Sūrya** (the Sun god) — fitting for the peak-summer season, when the sun is at its most intense. Worship of **Sūrya and Varuṇa** (rain/water deity) is considered especially fruitful this month — Sūrya for the heat, Varuṇa in anticipation of the coming monsoon. (Source: https://www.aajtak.in/religion/news/story/jyeshtha-maas-2024-date-know-month-significance-vrat-festivals-list-nirjala-ekadashi-vat-savitri-vrat-tlifdg-1950183-2024-05-24)
- **Nirjala Ekadashi** (Śukla Ekadashi of Jyēṣṭha) is considered the single most austere and meritorious of all 24 Ekadashis in the year — a "waterless" fast whose observance is said to confer the combined merit of observing all other Ekadashis. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirjala_Ekadashi)
- **Vaṭa Sāvitri Vrata** (Vaṭa Pūrṇimā in some regions, observed on Jyēṣṭha Amāvāsya per the Purṇimānta calendar) is a major vrata for married women, praying for their husbands' longevity, directly modeled on Sāvitrī's devotion to Satyavān. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vata_Purnima ; https://www.aajtak.in/religion/news/story/jyeshtha-maas-2024-date-know-month-significance-vrat-festivals-list-nirjala-ekadashi-vat-savitri-vrat-tlifdg-1950183-2024-05-24)
- Skanda Purāṇa (Kāśī-khaṇḍa) contains material on "Jyeṣṭheśa" — a form/shrine of Śiva connected to this month, suggesting Jyēṣṭha also carries a distinct Śaiva association in Kashi tradition. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc423802.html)

### Purāṇa references
- **Skanda Purāṇa, Kāśī-khaṇḍa (Uttarārdha), Chapter 63 — "The Story of Jyeṣṭheśa"**: a named chapter tied to the month's eponymous deity/liṅga (Jyeṣṭheśa = "the eldest Lord," a Śiva form at Kashi). This is the clearest dedicated Purāṇic chapter located for Jyēṣṭha, though it is a sthala/deity māhātmya (tied to a Kashi shrine) rather than a pure calendrical month-māhātmya like Vaiśākha's. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc423802.html)
- No separate, prominent stand-alone "Jyēṣṭha Māsa Māhātmya" comparable to the Vaiśākha or Kārtika māhātmyas was found in this search — flagging as a genuine sourcing gap. The Nirjala Ekadashi and Vaṭa Sāvitri vrata-kathās (see below) are the main narrative material carrying the month's religious weight instead of a single unified māhātmya text.

### Stories/legends
- **Nirjala Ekadashi origin story**: Popularly linked to Bhīma (of the Mahābhārata/Pāṇḍavas), who — unable to fast on all 24 Ekadashis due to his great hunger — was advised by Vyāsa to observe this one waterless fast in Jyēṣṭha to gain the merit of the entire year's Ekadashis at once (hence its alternate name "Bhīmasenī Ekadashi"). (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirjala_Ekadashi)
- **Sāvitrī and Satyavān**: The Vaṭa Sāvitri vrata reenacts the itihāsa of Sāvitrī, who through devotion and cleverness persuaded Yama (God of Death) to restore her husband Satyavān's life, worshipping under a banyan (vaṭa) tree — the story is the scriptural basis for the vrata performed by married women in this month. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vata_Purnima)

## Āṣāḍha Māsam (ఆషాఢం)

### Names & basic info
- Telugu name: **Āṣāḍha Māsam** (ఆషాఢ మాసం).
- Sanskrit/IAST: **Āṣāḍha** (आषाढ), named for **Pūrvāṣāḍhā/Uttarāṣāḍhā nakṣatra**.
- Gregorian overlap: roughly **June–July**.
- Position: **4th month** of the lunar year.
- Ṛtu: latter half of **Grīṣma Ṛtu** (Summer) shading into the onset of **Varṣā Ṛtu** (Monsoon) — traditionally the month in which the rains begin. (Source: https://www.learnreligions.com/the-six-seasons-of-india-p2-1770072 ; https://jothishi.com/ashada-month-the-monsoon-month/)

### Viśiṣṭata (significance)
- Āṣāḍha is the month in which **Lord Vishnu goes into yoga-nidrā (cosmic sleep)** on Śeṣa (Ādi Śeṣa) in the ocean of milk, beginning on **Devaśayani (Āṣāḍha Śukla) Ekadashi** and lasting four months (**Chāturmāsya**) until he "awakens" on Kārtika Ekadashi (Prabodhini/Devotthāna Ekadashi). Because Vishnu is "resting," this period — and the vratas begun in it — are held to be extra meritorious for tapas, japa, and dāna. (Source: https://www.sanatangyan.com/post/devshayani-ekadashi-2025-when-vishnu-sleeps-and-shiva-watches-over-the-universe ; https://hindutone.com/festivals/ashadha-masam-2026-start-end-date-complete-festivals-list-important-dates/)
- **Guru Pūrṇimā** (Āṣāḍha Pūrṇimā) is dedicated to Veda Vyāsa (also called **Vyāsa Pūrṇimā**), honoring him as the compiler of the four Vedas, author of the 18 Purāṇas and the Mahābhārata, and archetypal Guru — making this the day spiritual and academic teachers are especially venerated. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Purnima ; https://www.divyajivan.org/articles/siva/gurupurnima_signi.htm)
- Āṣāḍha Pūrṇimā also inaugurates **Chāturmāsya**, the four-month monsoon period of intensified self-discipline, vrata, and sādhanā observed by ascetics and devout householders alike (traditionally sannyāsis suspend travel and stay in one place). (Source: https://hindutone.com/festivals/ashadha-masam-2026-start-end-date-complete-festivals-list-important-dates/)
- The Skanda Purāṇa's own Chāturmāsya-māhātmya (kamakoti.org's "Essence of Skanda Purana: Chaturmasya Mahatmya") is anchored to this month as its starting point, underscoring Āṣāḍha's role as gateway to the monsoon vrata season. (Source: https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/details/skandapurana33.html)

### Purāṇa references
- The **Devaśayani Ekadashi** legend and Vishnu's sleep are attributed to the **Bhaviṣyottara Purāṇa**, per multiple sources, describing Vishnu retiring to sleep after six months of battling demons/asuras. (Source: https://www.sanatangyan.com/post/devshayani-ekadashi-2025-when-vishnu-sleeps-and-shiva-watches-over-the-universe)
- **Skanda Purāṇa — Chāturmāsya Māhātmya** (kamakoti.org's summarized "Essence of Skanda Purana" series) is the most substantial dedicated text located covering this Āṣāḍha-onward monsoon vrata period, though it covers the whole four-month Chāturmāsya rather than being an Āṣāḍha-only māhātmya. (Source: https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/details/skandapurana33.html)
- No separate stand-alone "Āṣāḍha Māsa Māhātmya" text, comparable to Vaiśākha's Padma Purāṇa chapter 98, was found in this search. This is a gap. Āṣāḍha's Purāṇic weight comes mainly through the Devaśayani Ekadashi and Chāturmāsya material, not through a dedicated month-glorification chapter.

### Stories/legends
- **King Māndhātā and the drought**: A righteous king named Māndhātā faced a three-year drought; the sage Aṅgiras advised him to observe Devaśayani (Āṣāḍhi) Ekadashi. Upon the kingdom's fulfilling the vrata, the rains returned and prosperity was restored — this vrata-kathā is the scriptural justification for observing Devaśayani Ekadashi. (Source: https://www.apnisanskriti.com/vrat-katha/significance-of-devshayani-ekadashi-5595 ; https://guptvrindavandham.org/blogs/devshayani-ekadashi/)
- **Vishnu's cosmic sleep and Shiva's watch**: With Vishnu asleep for Chāturmāsya, tradition holds that **Lord Shiva takes over protection/administration of the universe** during this period — a theological explanation for why Śaiva worship (and Śrāvaṇa-month Shiva observances, which follow immediately after Āṣāḍha) intensifies right after this month. (Source: https://www.sanatangyan.com/post/devshayani-ekadashi-2025-when-vishnu-sleeps-and-shiva-watches-over-the-universe)
- **Guru Pūrṇimā's Vyāsa connection**: Vyāsa is traditionally held to have been born on Āṣāḍha Pūrṇimā, and this day commemorates his life's work of organizing the Vedas and composing the Purāṇas/Mahābhārata/Brahma Sūtras — the itihāsa basis for why this specific full moon (not any other) is Guru Pūrṇimā. (Source: https://www.divyajivan.org/articles/siva/gurupurnima_signi.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Purnima)

## Śrāvaṇa Māsa

### Names & Basic Info
- **Telugu name**: Śrāvaṇa Māsam (శ్రావణ మాసం)
- **Sanskrit/IAST**: Śrāvaṇa
- **Position**: 5th month of the traditional Hindu lunisolar year (per the Chaitrādi/Southern reckoning used in Telugu panchangam)
- **Gregorian overlap**: Roughly late July–August/early September (e.g., 2026: Aug 13 – Sep 11) (Source: https://sakalam.org/shravana-masam/)
- **Season (ṛtu)**: Varṣā ṛtu (monsoon season)
- **Name origin**: Derived from the Śravaṇa nakṣatra being near the Moon on the full-moon (pūrṇimā) day of this month; also linked to the Sanskrit root "śru" (to hear), since the month is dedicated to śravaṇam — listening to scripture and divine glories. (Source: https://shubhpanchang.com/blog/shravan-mas-significance-rituals-and-shiva-puja-vidhi)

### Viśiṣṭata (Significance)
Śrāvaṇa is considered the most sacred month for the worship of Lord Śiva. According to the Śiva Purāṇa, this month is exceptionally dear to Mahādeva, making it the ideal period for austerity (vrata), fasting, and prayer directed at him. Every Monday (Śrāvaṇa Somavāram) in this month is considered especially auspicious and is dedicated wholly to Śiva worship. (Source: https://shubhpanchang.com/blog/shravan-mas-significance-rituals-and-shiva-puja-vidhi)

Married and unmarried women observe Maṅgala Gaurī vratas on Tuesdays of this month for marital wellbeing and family prosperity — a Telugu/Deccan-region tradition closely tied to Śrāvaṇa's Śaiva character. The month closes with Rakṣā Bandhan on Śrāvaṇa Pūrṇimā, and includes Ekādaśīs such as Kāmika Ekādaśī and Śrāvaṇa Putradā Ekādaśī, each carrying its own promised merit. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shravana_Putrada_Ekadashi; https://www.poojalu.com/shravan-month/)

Being the peak of the monsoon (Varṣā ṛtu), the month is also symbolically one of purification and renewal — the rains cleanse the earth just as the vratas of this month are said to cleanse the devotee.

### Purāṇa References
The Śiva Purāṇa is the principal scriptural source establishing Śrāvaṇa's special dearness to Śiva. (Source: https://shubhpanchang.com/blog/shravan-mas-significance-rituals-and-shiva-puja-vidhi) Unlike Kārtika, no single, widely attested "Śrāvaṇa Māhātmyam" text was found in this research. Such a text would be a dedicated month-long narrative purāṇa, comparable to the Kārtika Purāṇam. Instead, the month's significance is conveyed through Śiva Purāṇa passages and regional vrata-kathā literature, such as the Maṅgala Gaurī vrata kathās, rather than through one canonical māhātmya text. This is an honest gap. It needs further checking.

### Stories/Legends
The month's central legend is the **Samudra Manthana (churning of the cosmic ocean)** and Śiva's consumption of the Hālāhala poison. As devas and asuras churned the Ocean of Milk for amṛta, the deadly Hālāhala poison arose first, threatening to destroy all creation. The frightened devas, including Viṣṇu, took refuge with Śiva, who drank the poison to protect the three worlds. Pārvatī (Bhavānī), his consort, held his throat so the poison would not descend further, turning his throat blue — earning him the epithet **Nīlakaṇṭha** ("blue-throated one"). This selfless protective act is traditionally why Śrāvaṇa, the month of the story's commemoration in devotional retelling, is considered so dear to Śiva devotees, who pour water (abhiṣeka) on the Śivaliṅga throughout the month to "cool" the effects of the poison he bore for the world's sake. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halahala; https://thegaudiyatreasuresofbengal.com/2023/09/23/the-legend-of-shiva-drinking-poison-during-samudra-manthan-how-shivas-selflessness-saved-the-world/; https://shubhpanchang.com/blog/shravan-mas-significance-rituals-and-shiva-puja-vidhi)

## Bhādrapada Māsa

### Names & Basic Info
- **Telugu name**: Bhādrapada Māsam (భాద్రపద మాసం)
- **Sanskrit/IAST**: Bhādrapada (also called Proṣṭhapada)
- **Position**: 6th month of the Hindu lunisolar year; second of the four Cāturmāsya months
- **Gregorian overlap**: Roughly August–September (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhadrapada)
- **Season (ṛtu)**: Śarad ṛtu begins around this time (transition from Varṣā to Śarad)
- **Name origin**: Named for the Pūrva Bhādrapadā nakṣatra being near the Moon on the month's full-moon day. "Bhadra" in Sanskrit also connotes auspiciousness/safety. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhadrapada; https://divinestore.sanatanajourney.com/blogs/updates/bhadrapada-maas-2026-start-date-festival-calendar-niyam-rules)

### Viśiṣṭata (Significance)
Bhādrapada is dominated by two major devotional currents:

1. **Gaṇeśa/Vināyaka worship** — Vināyaka Chaturthi (Gaṇeśa Chaturthi), on Śukla Chaturthi of Bhādrapada, is celebrated as Gaṇeśa's birthday and is the most prominent festival of the month, involving installation and worship of Gaṇeśa idols culminating in visarjan (immersion) on Ananta Chaturdaśī. (Source: https://hindupad.com/bhadrapada-masam-telugu-calendar/)
2. **Pitru Pakṣa / Mahālaya Pakṣa** — The Kṛṣṇa Pakṣa (dark fortnight) of Bhādrapada is set aside as Pitru Pakṣa, considered the most sacred period for śrāddha karma (ancestral rites). Families perform tarpaṇam and śrāddha for departed ancestors during these fifteen days, culminating in Mahālaya Amāvāsya (Sarva Pitru Amāvāsyā). (Source: https://hindupad.com/bhadrapada-masam-telugu-calendar/)

Other observances in the month include Ṛṣi Pañcamī and Anantha Vratam (Ananta Chaturdaśī). (Source: https://hindupad.com/bhadrapad-month/)

### Purāṇa References
The Gaṇeśa Purāṇa is the key scriptural source for Bhādrapada's Gaṇeśa associations, particularly its Upāsanākhaṇḍa, chapter 62, which recounts the story of the moon's curse and its remedy — directly tied to Bhādrapada Śukla Chaturthi observance. (Source: https://www.chivukulas.com/2014/04/story-of-lord-ganesha-and-curse-of-moon.html) No single dedicated "Bhādrapada Māhātmyam," comparable to the Kārtika Māhātmyam, was found in this research. The month's sanctity rests mainly on festival-specific purāṇic episodes, such as the Gaṇeśa Purāṇa material for Vināyaka Chaturthi, and on the broader Pitru Pakṣa tradition. The scriptural basis for śrāddha rites in general draws on the Garuḍa Purāṇa and dharmaśāstra material, not on a month-specific māhātmya. This is an honest gap. It is unconfirmed and needs deeper checking.

### Stories/Legends
The defining legend of this month is **Gaṇeśa's curse on the Moon (Chandra)**. After a feast of modakas, Gaṇeśa fell off his mouse-vehicle while riding home; Chandra, watching from the sky, laughed at him. Wounded by the mockery, Gaṇeśa cursed the Moon: whoever looks upon the Moon on Bhādrapada Śukla Chaturthi would be falsely accused of theft and suffer unearned disgrace (the "mithyā doṣa"). Chandra pleaded for mercy, and Gaṇeśa limited the curse's effect to that one day each year, giving rise to the still-observed custom of avoiding moon-viewing on Vināyaka Chaturthi (and the practice of listening to the Śyamantaka-maṇi story of Kṛṣṇa as an antidote if one does see it). (Source: https://www.vedantu.com/stories/ganesha-curses-the-moon; https://www.chivukulas.com/2014/04/story-of-lord-ganesha-and-curse-of-moon.html)

## Āśvayuja (Āśvina) Māsa

### Names & Basic Info
- **Telugu name**: Āśvayuja Māsam (ఆశ్వయుజ మాసం)
- **Sanskrit/IAST**: Āśvina / Āśvayuja
- **Position**: 7th month of the Hindu lunisolar year (Telugu panchangam)
- **Gregorian overlap**: Roughly September–October (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashvin_(month))
- **Season (ṛtu)**: Śarad ṛtu (autumn)
- **Name origin**: Named for the Aśvinī nakṣatra being near the Moon at the month's full moon. (Source: https://hindupad.com/ashwayuja-masam-in-telugu-calendar/)

### Viśiṣṭata (Significance)
Āśvayuja is above all the month of **Śarada (Śāradīya) Navarātri / Durgā worship**, considered the best time of the year to worship Goddess Durgā. The nine nights from Āśvayuja Śukla Padyami to Daśamī are dedicated to the nine forms (Navadurgā) of the Goddess, culminating in Vijayadaśamī (Dasara), commemorating the triumph of good over evil. (Source: https://hindusphere.com/significance-of-aswayuja-ashwayuja-ashwini-masam/; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayadashami)

The month is described as "more important for the followers of Durgā Devī" than any other, since it hosts the principal annual worship cycle of the Goddess in her Śakti/warrior aspect. The month also ends with Āśvayuja Amāvāsyā, which in most of the Telugu-speaking region marks the eve of Dīpāvalī (Naraka Chaturdaśī and Dīpāvalī itself frequently fall right at the Āśvayuja–Kārtika boundary). (Source: https://hindupad.com/ashwayuja-masam-in-telugu-calendar/)

### Purāṇa References
The scriptural bedrock of Āśvayuja's Navarātri worship is the **Devī Māhātmyam** (also called Durgā Saptaśatī, Caṇḍī, or Caṇḍī Pāṭha), found within the **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa**, chapters 81–93, composed roughly 400–500 CE and traditionally attributed to the sage Mārkaṇḍeya. It comprises 700 verses across 13 chapters describing the Goddess's victories over demons, most famously Mahiṣāsura. Ritual recitation (pāṭha) of the Devī Māhātmyam/Durgā Saptaśatī during Navarātri is itself a central Āśvayuja practice. Of all the months besides Kārtika, this comes closest to having a "dedicated" māhātmya. Strictly speaking, though, it glorifies the Goddess, not the calendar month itself. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_Mahatmya (via search) — see also https://www.exoticindiaart.com/blog/shree-durga-saptashati/; https://www.hindu-blog.com/2008/09/devi-mahatmya-sapta-sati-chandi-patha.html)

### Stories/Legends
- **Durgā's slaying of Mahiṣāsura**, narrated across the Devī Māhātmyam, is the central legend underpinning the nine-night worship, celebrated on Mahiṣāsuramardinī day (typically Mahāṣṭamī/Navamī).
- **Rāma's victory over Rāvaṇa**, commemorated on Vijayadaśamī, is also closely associated with this month in North Indian tradition (Rāma is said to have performed Durgā pūjā for nine days before the battle), reinforcing Āśvayuja Daśamī as a day of triumph over evil. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayadashami)

## Kārtika Māsa

### Names & Basic Info
- **Telugu name**: Kārtika Māsam (కార్తీక మాసం)
- **Sanskrit/IAST**: Kārtika
- **Position**: 8th month of the Hindu lunisolar year (Telugu panchangam)
- **Gregorian overlap**: Roughly October–November (2026 Deepotsava period cited as Oct 26 – Nov 24, 2026) (Source: https://www.iskconbangalore.org/deepotsava/)
- **Season (ṛtu)**: Hemanta ṛtu begins around this time (late autumn/early winter transition)
- **Name origin**: Named for the Kṛttikā nakṣatra near the Moon at the month's full moon. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kartika_(month))

### Viśiṣṭata (Significance) — In Depth
Kārtika is widely regarded as the single most glorified and spiritually potent month in the Hindu calendar. It is dear to **both** Śiva and Viṣṇu. This is a rare case where the Śaiva and Vaiṣṇava traditions agree that the same month is supremely sacred.

**Dāmodara Māsa**: The month is also called **Dāmodara Māsa**, since it is considered especially dear to Kṛṣṇa in his Dāmodara form. The name "Dāmodara" derives from Sanskrit "dāma" (rope) + "udara" (belly), commemorating the episode where mother Yaśodā tied the child Kṛṣṇa's belly with a rope as punishment/love-play. Vaiṣṇavas observe "Ūrjā-vrata" throughout the month, offering a ghee lamp daily to Lord Dāmodara. (Source: https://iskconbhilai.org/why-lighting-the-dipa-pleases-lord-damodara/; https://www.dandavats.com/?p=4656)

**The Lamp-Lighting (Dīpa Dāna) Tradition**: Kārtika is the month of lamps. The Skanda Purāṇa states that offering even a single lamp to Keśava (Viṣṇu) during Kārtika liberates ancestors in one's family and destroys sins accumulated across "many thousands and millions of births ... in half an eye-blink." Lighting lamps in temples, at home, under tulasī plants, and at river ghats throughout the month — especially on Kārtika Pūrṇimā (Kārtika Dīpam / Karthika Deepam in Telugu tradition, tied also to the giant bonfire lit atop Tirumala/Arunachala hills) — is considered one of the most meritorious acts a devotee can perform in the entire year. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc371648.html; https://kausiki.com/product/skanda-purana-vaishnava-khanda-kartika-masa-mahatmya/)

**Kārtika Pūrṇimā — dear to both deities at once**: Kārtika Pūrṇimā (the full moon closing the month) is celebrated as **Tripurāri Pūrṇimā** by Śaivas, marking Śiva's victory over the demon Tripurāsura in his Tripurāntaka form, and simultaneously honored by Vaiṣṇavas as the day of Viṣṇu's Matsya (fish) avatāra. On this day Śiva temples often perform joint pūjā honoring Viṣṇu alongside Śiva — a concrete liturgical expression of the month's Hari-Hara (Viṣṇu-Śiva unity) character. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kartika_Purnima; https://www.astrologerumesh.com/blog/kartik-purnima/)

Because of this dual glorification, vratas, dāna (charity), snāna (ritual bathing, especially at sunrise/dawn — Kārtika Snānam), fasting, and tulasī worship performed in Kārtika are held to yield far greater fruit (puṇya) than the same acts performed in any other month.

### Purāṇa References
Kārtika is the only one of these four months with a genuine, well-documented, dedicated māhātmya literature:

- **Skanda Purāṇa, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa**: Contains a "Kārtika-māsa-māhātmya" section. The lamp-offering passage researched here (on the merit of gifting lamps to Keśava) is explicitly identified as "the seventh chapter of the Kārttikamāsa-māhātmya of the Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa of the Skanda Purāṇa." (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc371648.html)
- **Padma Purāṇa, Uttara Khaṇḍa**: Contains its own extensive Kārtika Māhātmya, spanning chapters 93–124 of the Uttara Khaṇḍa — illustrated through a large collection of stories, some of ancient origin, many peculiar to this text. The narrative frame has the sage Nārada travelling to Mount Mandara to ask Śambhu (Śiva) about the month's secret glory; Śiva, seated with Umā (Pārvatī), answers at length, and this dialogue structure carries the māhātmya's many embedded stories. (Source: https://kmlmotwani.wordpress.com/2014/04/09/kartika-mahatmya/)
- The **"Kārtika Purāṇam"** widely circulated today in Telugu, Kannada, and Odia households as a standalone book (with a corresponding Odia "Kartika Purana" also documented) is a popularized/vernacular compilation drawing on this Skanda- and Padma-Purāṇa māhātmya material, reorganized for daily household reading across the month's roughly 30 days. (Source: https://ritikart.com/products/odia-kartika-purana; https://greatertelugu.org/karthika-puranam-telugu-pdf/)

### Stories/Legends
- **The recitation tradition itself**: In Telugu (and broader South/East Indian) households, the Kārtika Purāṇam is traditionally read aloud — often by the family or in group satsaṅgs — one chapter/story per day across the month, a practice believed to itself be highly meritorious independent of the content, turning the month into a shared, ongoing devotional narrative event in the home. (Source: https://www.hindutemplesguide.com/2019/10/karthika-puranam-31-temples-guide.html)
- **The story of Ajāmila** (found principally in the Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Canto 6, and widely retold in Kārtika-season storytelling) is one of the most famous redemption narratives associated with the devotional spirit of the month: a fallen brāhmaṇa named Ajāmila, having lived a sinful life, is saved at the moment of death because he cries out the name "Nārāyaṇa" (actually calling for his son of that name) — illustrating that even unintentional utterance of the divine name grants liberation, a theme resonant with Kārtika's emphasis on easy, highly-fruitful acts of devotion. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajamila; https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-bhagavata-purana/d/doc1127145.html)
- **Tripurāsura-vadha (Śiva's slaying of Tripurāsura)**, culminating on Kārtika Pūrṇimā, is retold as part of the month's Śaiva glorification. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kartika_Purnima)
- **Matsya avatāra of Viṣṇu**, also placed on Kārtika Pūrṇimā in some traditions, ties the month to the very first of Viṣṇu's daśāvatāras and the primordial saving of Manu and the Vedas from the cosmic deluge. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kartika_Purnima)

In summary, Kārtika's sanctity rests on a canonical purāṇic māhātmya (Skanda and Padma). This māhātmya is retold in the vernacular as the household Kārtika Purāṇam, read daily through the month. Its central rituals — dīpa dāna, snānam, and tulasī pūjā — are each backed by specific merit-stories within that literature.

## Mārgaśira (Mārgaśīrṣa)

### Names & Basic Info
- **Telugu name**: Mārgaśira Māsam (మార్గశిర మాసం), also colloquially called **Dhanurmāsam** for its final portion when the sun transits Sagittarius (Dhanu rāśi).
- **Sanskrit/IAST**: Mārgaśīrṣa māsa — named after the Mṛgaśira (Mrigasira) nakshatra, near which the moon is full this month. Also historically called **Agrahāyaṇa**.
- **Gregorian overlap**: Roughly late November to late December.
- **Position in the year**: 9th month of the traditional 12-month lunar year (Chaitra being 1st).
- **Ṛtu (season)**: Falls in **Hemanta ṛtu** (early winter), the season pairing Mārgaśira and Puṣya.

Sources: (Source: https://blog.dharma-renaissance.org/spirituality/margashirsha-and-margazhi-a-practical-devotional-guide/) (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pausha)

### Viśiṣṭata (Significance)
Mārgaśira holds a uniquely direct scriptural distinction: in the **Bhagavad Gītā, Chapter 10 (Vibhūti Yoga), verse 35**, Kṛṣṇa says:

> *māsānāṃ mārgaśīrṣo'ham* — "Of months, I am Mārgaśīrṣa."

This is one of only a handful of places in the Gītā where Kṛṣṇa names a specific calendrical unit as his own vibhūti, meaning divine manifestation or glory. This places the month in the same category as other things Kṛṣṇa names as his own self-declared divine forms: the Himalayas, the Ganges, and the Gāyatrī among meters. Commentators (Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja, Madhva) generally read this as Kṛṣṇa identifying with the most excellent month of the year. The month is climatically temperate, with neither the heat of summer nor extreme cold. It also coincides with the harvest. Together these make it a natural period of both material ease and spiritual reflection. This has made the month a favored time for Gītā pārāyaṇam (recitation), japa, dāna (charity), and vratas, since it is considered the "best among months" by the Lord's own word. (Source: https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/10/verse/35/) (Source: https://hinduisminsimpleterms.wordpress.com/2015/12/15/bhagavad-gita-lord-says-of-the-months-i-am-marga-shirsha/)

The latter portion of Mārgaśira, when the sun is in Dhanu rāśi (Sagittarius), is observed across South India, especially in Telugu and Tamil Vaiṣṇava tradition, as **Dhanurmāsam**. It is considered the "Brahma Muhūrtam of the Devas" — the entire month is treated like the most auspicious pre-dawn hour, but stretched across 30 days. This makes it an especially good time for temple worship, particularly of Viṣṇu. Daily pre-dawn (kōḍi kūta/"cock's crow") worship, special naivedyam of ariselu/pulihora, and full Tiruppāvai recitation are hallmark Dhanurmāsam practices at temples such as Tirumala and Śrīraṅgam. (Source: https://tirumalatirupatiyatra.in/dhanurmasa-vratam/) (Source: https://gotirupati.com/tirumala-dhanurmasa-tiruppavai/)

### Purāṇa References
- **Bhagavad Gītā 10.35** is the primary and most authoritative scriptural reference — not a dedicated "māhātmyam" text in the Purāṇic sense, but a direct self-declaration by Kṛṣṇa of the month's supremacy, which functions as Mārgaśira's foundational scriptural charter. (Source: https://www.bhagavad-gita.us/bhagavad-gita-10-35/)
- Dhanurmāsa observance and its rules are elaborated in later devotional/āgamic literature and temple paddhati (ritual manuals) rather than in one single dedicated Purāṇa māhātmya; various Vaiṣṇava sources (Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition, Tiruppāvai commentaries) treat the month's glory as an extension of the Gītā verse combined with Āṇḍāḷ's own testimony in her verses. (Source: https://hindupad.com/tiruppavai-recital-at-tirumala-temple-during-dhanurmasam/)

### Stories/Legends
- **Kṛṣṇa's Vibhūti Yoga declaration** (BG 10.35) itself is treated devotionally almost as a "story" — the Lord enumerating his manifestations to Arjuna, culminating in identifying with this specific month, is retold in Mārgaśira discourses as proof of the month's unmatched sanctity.
- **Āṇḍāḷ (Gōdā Devī) and the Dhanurmāsa/Mārgaḻi vratam**: Gōdā Devī, one of the twelve Āḻvārs and foster-daughter of Periyāḻvār, undertook the **Pāvai Nōnbu** vrata during this month, imagining herself as a gopī yearning for union with Kṛṣṇa/Raṅganātha. She composed the **Tiruppāvai** — 30 verses, one for each day of the month — describing this vow. Tradition holds she ultimately merged with Lord Raṅganātha at Śrīraṅgam as a result of this vrata, and she is worshipped today as an incarnation of Bhūdevī. In Telugu tradition the Tiruppāvai is recited daily through Dhanurmāsam (translated into Telugu as *Melukolupu*/​*Melupalukula Melukolupu* by Mullapudi Venkataramana), and young unmarried women especially observe related vratas seeking a good match, following Gōdā Devī's example. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruppavai) (Source: https://www.primepost.in/godas-love-letter/) (Source: https://hindupad.com/tiruppavai-recital-at-tirumala-temple-during-dhanurmasam/)

## Puṣya

### Names & Basic Info
- **Telugu name**: Puṣya Māsam (పుష్య మాసం).
- **Sanskrit/IAST**: Puṣya māsa, cognate with Paurṇamāsī Pauṣa/**Pauṣa** in North Indian usage — named for the Puṣya nakshatra.
- **Gregorian overlap**: Mid-December to mid-January.
- **Position in the year**: 10th month of the 12-month lunar year.
- **Ṛtu (season)**: **Hemanta ṛtu** (deep winter), second of the Mārgaśira–Puṣya pair.

Sources: (Source: https://hindupad.com/pushya-masam/) (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pausha)

### Viśiṣṭata (Significance)
Puṣya holds two views side by side in Hindu tradition, and both are genuinely well documented. On one hand, it is widely held **inauspicious for major new-beginning saṃskāras** — weddings, gṛha praveśam (housewarming), engagements, and similar life-cycle events are traditionally avoided in this month in many North and South Indian communities. On the other hand, it is at the same time regarded as **highly spiritually potent**. It is called **Saubhāgya Lakṣmī Māsam**, dedicated to Lord Viṣṇu and Goddess Lakṣmī, and governed astrologically by Śani (Saturn), the nakshatra lord of Puṣya. Farmers historically associated it with post-harvest prosperity. It is considered auspicious for austerity, single-meal (naktabhojana-type) vratas, japa, dāna, and worship of Viṣṇu-Lakṣmī, even while remaining unsuitable for weddings. The sources are clear that this dual status — spiritually rich, but not for new ventures or saṃskāras — is a real and commonly cited feature of the month, not a uniform rule. Some regional customs differ on exactly which activities are restricted. (Source: https://www.hindu-blog.com/2020/10/pushya-masam-importance.html) (Source: https://hindutone.com/spirituality/pushya-masam-2025-2026-complete-guide-with-dates-festivals-spiritual-significance-telugu-calendar-2025-2026/)

Observing a single meal a day through Puṣya is described in popular devotional literature as conferring good fortune, beauty, and renown — an ascetic-style vrata distinct from major saṃskāra rites. (Source: https://www.hindu-blog.com/2020/10/pushya-masam-importance.html)

### Purāṇa References
Unlike Mārgaśira (Gītā) or Māgha (Padma Purāṇa), research did not surface a single widely cited dedicated "Puṣya Māhātmyam" text in a major Purāṇa comparable to the Māgha Māhātmyam. Puṣya's significance is instead documented mainly through Dharmaśāstra/muhūrta literature, on why it is avoided for saṃskāras, and popular devotional/vrata guides describing its Lakṣmī-Viṣṇu worship customs. This is an honest gap. No dedicated Purāṇic māhātmya equivalent to Māgha's was found in this research. Such a text may exist in a regional or lesser-known Purāṇa or Upapurāṇa, but it was not found among the sources consulted. (Source: https://hindutone.com/spirituality/pushya-masam-2025-2026-complete-guide-with-dates-festivals-spiritual-significance-telugu-calendar-2025-2026/)

### Stories/Legends
No major itihāsa or purāṇic narrative tied specifically to the Puṣya month was found in the sources consulted. This is different from the Puṣya *nakshatra*, which does appear in various contexts, for example in discussions of Rāma's birth chart. Puṣya's devotional literature tends to give rules (vrata rules, do's and don'ts) rather than stories, unlike Mārgaśira, Māgha, and Phālguṇa. This is a genuine sourcing gap, stated plainly here rather than left unmentioned.

## Māgha

### Names & Basic Info
- **Telugu name**: Māgha Māsam (మాఘ మాసం).
- **Sanskrit/IAST**: Māgha māsa — named for the Maghā nakshatra.
- **Gregorian overlap**: Mid-January to mid-February.
- **Position in the year**: 11th month of the 12-month lunar year.
- **Ṛtu (season)**: **Śiśira ṛtu** (the cool/dew season), paired with Phālguṇa.

Sources: (Source: https://www.templepurohit.com/magha-maasam-importance-of-magha-snana-puja-mantras-and-festivals/) (Source: https://www.religionworld.in/magha_maasam/)

### Viśiṣṭata (Significance)
Māgha is one of the most emphatically glorified months in the Hindu ritual calendar, centered on **Māgha Snānam** — the practice of a holy bath (ideally in the Ganges, Godāvarī, or other sacred rivers/tīrthas, or at minimum before sunrise in any water body) undertaken daily through the month. Tradition holds that Goddess Sarasvatī, Sūrya Bhagavān, and Śiva-liṅga worship are especially connected to births/manifestations associated with this month, and the month is closely tied to the **Māgha Melā/Kumbha Melā** bathing festivals at Prayāgrāj. Māgha Snānam is proclaimed in the Padma Purāṇa to be spiritually superior even to yajñas, meditation, and long austerities: "by means of baths in Māgha, calamities perish, sins perish," with three days of Māgha bathing said to equal ten years of tapas at a tīrtha. (Source: https://www.exoticindiaart.com/blog/magh-mela-history-rituals-and-spiritual-significance-of-sacred-magha-month/) (Source: https://hindupad.com/magh-snan-mahatmya-importance-and-spiritual-significance-of-magha-snanam-or-holy-dip-in-magh-month/)

### Purāṇa References
The **Māgha Māhātmyam** is a well-documented dedicated text within the **Padma Purāṇa's Uttara Khaṇḍa**:
- **Chapter 125** — "The Importance of Māgha As Told by Bhṛgu" (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365704.html)
- **Chapter 126** — "The Importance of Māgha As Told by Dattātreya" (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365705.html)

The Māgha Māhātmyam is also referenced/echoed in the **Mahābhārata** and forms part of the scriptural basis invoked for the Māgha Melā/Kumbha Melā bathing traditions. (Source: https://www.exoticindiaart.com/blog/magh-mela-history-rituals-and-spiritual-significance-of-sacred-magha-month/)

### Stories/Legends
**Bhṛgu's teaching to King Dilīpa (Padma Purāṇa, Uttara Khaṇḍa, Ch. 125)**: King Dilīpa, while on a hunting expedition, meets an ascetic who directs him to sage Vasiṣṭha to learn about the merit of Māgha bathing. Vasiṣṭha in turn recounts a story originally told by the sage Bhṛgu: during a twelve-year famine, Bhṛgu meets a vidyādhara (celestial being) on Mount Maṇikūṭa whose face has turned tiger-like as karmic punishment for having used oil on a mandated fasting day. Bhṛgu prescribes Māgha bathing as the remedy, and illustrates its power by recounting how the ancient king **Purūravas** performed severe penance and, through Viṣṇu's grace (connected to Māgha observance), had his ugliness transformed into divine beauty. The chapter closes with Bhṛgu's declaration that Māgha bathing surpasses yajña, dhyāna, and tapas in efficacy, and is open to all castes and āśramas alike. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365704.html)

This story — a king who learns the value of Māgha snāna from a sage, who in turn passes on an even older teaching — is the closest match, within the Māgha Māhātmyam itself, to the "King Bhagīratha" pattern of austerity-lineage stories common in Purāṇic river and bathing māhātmyas. Bhagīratha's own austerities are classically tied to bringing the Ganges down to earth. This is a separate story, but popular retellings often mention it alongside Māgha-snāna discourse, because the two share the same theme. (Source: https://astrodisha.com/sampuran-magha-maas-mahatmya/)

## Phālguṇa

### Names & Basic Info
- **Telugu name**: Phālguṇa Māsam (ఫాల్గుణ మాసం), colloquially Falgun/Phalgun.
- **Sanskrit/IAST**: Phālguṇa māsa — named for the Uttara/Pūrva Phalgunī nakshatra.
- **Gregorian overlap**: Mid-February to mid-March.
- **Position in the year**: 12th and **final** month of the traditional lunar year (Chaitra, the following month, begins the new year).
- **Ṛtu (season)**: **Śiśira ṛtu**, closing out the season begun in Māgha (some classifications place late Phālguṇa at the cusp of Vasanta/spring ṛtu).

Sources: (Source: https://hindupad.com/phalguna-masam-falguna-month-dates-in-telugu-calendar/) (Source: https://www.gempundit.com/blog/phalguna-the-12th-lunar-month-of-the-hindu-calendar)

### Viśiṣṭata (Significance)
Phālguṇa is regarded as a highly auspicious, joyous closing month of the lunar year — its name is popularly explained as combining *phala* ("fruit") and *guṇa* ("virtue/character"), signifying sweetness of character and the "fruit" of the year's spiritual efforts ripening. It carries the year's two most dramatic festivals: **Mahāśivarātri** (in Kṛṣṇa pakṣa), commemorating the convergence/union of Śiva and Pārvatī, and **Holī** (in Śukla pakṣa, culminating on Phālguṇa Pūrṇimā), marking the triumph of devotion and good over evil via the Holikā-Prahlāda narrative, followed by the color-throwing celebration of Vasanta's arrival and Kṛṣṇa-Rādhā līlā themes. The Amāvāsyā following Phālguṇa's end marks the start of the Hindu New Year (Ugādi, called Yugādi in Telugu tradition). This gives the month the sanctity of a threshold, standing at the close of one year and the start of the next. (Source: https://hindutone.com/festivals/phalguna-masam-2026/phalguna-masam-2026-end-date-start-date-importance-rituals-festivals/) (Source: https://www.gempundit.com/blog/phalguna-the-12th-lunar-month-of-the-hindu-calendar)

### Purāṇa References
Research did not find one single dedicated "Phālguṇa Māhātmyam" text of the scale of the Māgha Māhātmyam in a major Purāṇa. Instead, its significance is carried through festival-specific Purāṇic narratives that fall within the month: the **Holikā-Prahlāda story** is recounted across multiple Purāṇas (notably the **Viṣṇu Purāṇa**, **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** [Book 7, on Prahlāda and Hiraṇyakaśipu], and **Nārada Purāṇa**), while **Mahāśivarātri**'s theology draws on **Śiva Purāṇa** and **Liṅga Purāṇa** material on the Śiva-Pārvatī union and the appearance of the Jyotirliṅga. These are best understood as festival māhātmyas that fall within the month, not as one unified "Phālguṇa Māhātmyam." To state this plainly: no single dedicated whole-month māhātmya text was found for Phālguṇa in the sources consulted. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holika_Dahana) (Source: https://hindupad.com/phalgun-month-falgun-maas-phalguna-masam/)

### Stories/Legends
**Holikā and Prahlāda**: The demon-king Hiraṇyakaśipu, resentful of his son Prahlāda's unwavering devotion to Viṣṇu, enlists his sister Holikā — who possessed a boon making her immune to fire — to sit in a pyre with Prahlāda on her lap, intending to burn the boy alive. Through Prahlāda's steadfast devotion (bhakti) and Viṣṇu's protection, the boon reverses: Holikā is consumed by the fire while Prahlāda emerges unharmed. This event, dated to Phālguṇa Pūrṇimā eve, is ritually reenacted every year as **Holikā Dahana** (the bonfire) the night before Holī proper, making it the month's signature narrative of good triumphing over evil through devotion. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holika_Dahana)

**Mahāśivarātri's Śiva-Pārvatī union**: though Mahāśivarātri narratives vary by Purāṇa (some emphasize the appearance of the Jyotirliṅga, others the marriage of Śiva and Pārvatī), the observance falling in Phālguṇa Kṛṣṇa-pakṣa Chaturdaśī is treated devotionally as the month's second great narrative anchor, observed with night-long jāgaraṇa, Rudrābhiṣeka, and fasting. (Source: https://hindutone.com/spirituality/phalguna-masam-2026-complete-guide-to-dates-significance-vedic-importance/)

## Summary of sourcing gaps (all 12 months)

**Best-sourced months** — an explicitly named, dedicated Purāṇic māhātmya was located and read directly:
- **Vaiśākha**: Padma Purāṇa (ch. 98) and Skanda Purāṇa (Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa).
- **Kārtika**: Skanda Purāṇa (Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa) and Padma Purāṇa Uttara Khaṇḍa (chs. 93–124) — the deepest-documented month in this file, matching its outsized traditional importance.
- **Māgha**: Padma Purāṇa Uttara Khaṇḍa (chs. 125–126, including the Bhṛgu/King Dilīpa/Vasiṣṭha/Purūravas narrative and the Dattātreya episode).

**Months with no dedicated stand-alone month-māhātmya found** — their significance instead rests on vrata-kathās, festival-specific Purāṇic episodes, or (for Mārgaśira) a single direct scriptural verse. Flagged honestly rather than invented:
- **Chaitra**: rests on the Chaturvarga Chintāmaṇi's creation-day claim and general Vasanta/Ugādi material.
- **Jyēṣṭha**: closest match is the Skanda Purāṇa Kāśī-khaṇḍa's "Jyeṣṭheśa" chapter (a shrine/deity māhātmya, not a calendrical one); Nirjala Ekādaśī and Vaṭa Sāvitri vrata-kathās carry most of the narrative weight instead.
- **Āṣāḍha**: grounded via the Bhaviṣyottara Purāṇa's Devaśayani Ekādaśī legend and the Skanda Purāṇa's broader Chāturmāsya Māhātmya.
- **Śrāvaṇa**: sanctity documented via the Śiva Purāṇa rather than a month-length māhātmya.
- **Bhādrapada**: sanctity documented via the Gaṇeśa Purāṇa's Chaturthī episode rather than a month-length māhātmya.
- **Āśvayuja**: its Devī Māhātmyam (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa) glorifies the Goddess rather than the calendar month per se, though tightly bound to the month via Navarātri recitation.
- **Puṣya**: no dedicated māhātmya and no month-specific itihāsa/legend found — only nakṣatra-level references elsewhere.
- **Phālguṇa**: no unified whole-month māhātmya; its scriptural weight comes via the festival-specific accounts (Holikā-Prahlāda, Śiva-Pārvatī/Mahāśivarātri) that fall within it.
- **Mārgaśira**: the Bhagavad Gītā 10.35 self-declaration ("of months I am Mārgaśīrṣa") is textually solid and directly verifiable, but Dhanurmāsa ritual detail draws more on Śrīvaiṣṇava temple paddhati than on one dedicated Purāṇa māhātmya.

**General caveat**: all Gregorian date ranges given per month are approximate and shift year to year with the lunisolar calendar; any specific year cited in a month's "basic info" is illustrative only, not a fixed annual date.
