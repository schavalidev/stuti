# Tithi index of parva-dinas — derived from festivals_aarshavani.md

This file re-derives `festivals_aarshavani.md` (the date-ordered festival log pulled from
*Aarsha Vani* magazine issues, Oct 2021–Jul 2026) into a **tithi-indexed** reference: instead of
"what falls in month Y," it answers "what falls on tithi X." Tithi names/spellings in the source
vary a lot (Ekadashi/Ēkādaśī/Ekādaśī, Chaturthi/Caturthi/Caturthī, etc.); here each of the 15
tithis gets one canonical heading, and every distinct-named festival seen on that tithi anywhere
in the corpus is listed once, deduped, with a synthesized note and the month(s)/pakṣa it recurs
in. Purely repetitive monthly-type observances (Ēkādaśī vratas in general, Māsa Śivarātri,
Pradōṣa, Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthī) are separated out — see the Notes section. Each festival entry
also now carries a **Pan-India / regional notes** sub-bullet, researched and cited from external
sources, giving that festival's cross-regional names, variants, and celebration patterns
alongside the original magazine-derived tithi/significance data.

**Added 2026-09-12 — nine entries that did not come from *Aarsha Vani*.** The rest of this file
is derived from `festivals_aarshavani.md`. Nine names were taken instead from
`vrata_parva_master_reference.md`, checked against this file, found to be absent, and researched
separately: Śrī Rāghavēndra Svāmi Ārādhana (§2), Hartālikā Gaurī (§3), Sakaṭa Caturthī (§4),
Saddula Bathukamma and the opening of Kēdāra Gaurī (§8), Damanakārōpaṇa (§13 and §14), Kēdāra
Gaurī Vratam and Piṭhōrī Amāvāsya and the opening of Bathukamma (§15), and Bonālu and Mukkanuma
in the Notes. Each carries its own cited source, in the same form as the rest of the file. No
existing entry was altered.

## 1. Pratipada / Pādyami (Prathamā)

- **Ugādi (Saṁvatsarādi)** — Caitra Śukla Pratipada, annual. Telugu New Year's Day: oil bath, new
  clothes, worship of one's chosen deity, and eating Ugādi Pacchadi (the six-taste chutney);
  also opens Vasanta Navarātri.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India festival under different names on the same lunisolar date:
    Gudi Padwa (Maharashtra), Ugadi (Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka), Cheti Chand (Sindhi
    community), and Navreh (Kashmiri Pandits). Core practices (new year, new clothes, chosen-deity
    worship) are shared, but the signature food differs — Ugadi Pacchadi in Telugu states vs.
    neem-jaggery elsewhere, and Maharashtra additionally hoists the gudi (a decorated pole/flag)
    outside homes, which the Telugu tradition does not do. Note: solar-calendar new years like
    Puthandu (Tamil Nadu), Vaisakhi (Punjab), Bihu (Assam) and Pana Sankranti (Odisha) fall on a
    different date in mid-April and are not the same festival despite similar "new year" framing.
    (Source: https://www.thehansindia.com/life-style/regional-new-year-celebrations-gudi-padwa-ugadi-and-cheti-chand-958337)
- **Śarannavarātri begins (Kalaśa Sthāpana)** — Āśvayuja Śukla Pratipada, annual. First of the
  nine nights of Śaradṛtu Devī worship ("Śāradārādhya" per Lalitā Sahasranāma); coincides with
  Tirumala's Navarātri Brahmōtsavams.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India, but regional expression varies sharply: in West Bengal,
    Assam, Odisha and Tripura the festival culminates as Durga Puja with elaborate clay-idol pandals
    and immersion; in Gujarat it centers on Garba/Dandiya Raas circular dance; the Kalasha Sthapana
    (Ghatasthapana) ritual described in the source is the common opening rite across most regions
    but is not the emphasis everywhere. Telugu-region Navarātri (as in the source, tied to Tirumala
    Brahmōtsavams) is a further distinct regional inflection.
    (Source: https://www.kolkatadurgotsav.com/regional-names-of-durga.html)
- **Beginning of Payō Vrata / Avighna Vrata** — Phālguṇa/Māgha Śukla Pādyami, annual. A
  twelve-day vrata (through Dvādaśī) of holy-river bathing and worship of Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa,
  paired with Gaṇapati worship as "Avighna Vrata."
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Narrowly regional. Payo Vratam (12-day, milk-only observance for
    Vishnu, ending Dvādaśī) is specifically noted as practiced "in some regions in Andhra Pradesh"
    during Phalguna māsa; Avighna Vrata (Dundi Vinayaka worship on Śukla Pādyami) is a more general
    Ganapati observance. No evidence this pairing is pan-India; it appears to be a Telugu-specific
    combination. (Source: https://hindupad.com/payo-vratham-vishnu-payo-vratham-in-phalguna-masam/)
- **Pōli Pāḍyami** — Mārgaśira Śukla Pādyami, annual. Women float lit lamps on water as an
  offering, said to bring an exalted spiritual state.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Purely regional/local, specific to Andhra Pradesh. Also called
    "Poli Swargam," observed at river ghats (notably the Krishna river) on the first day of
    Mārgaśira, where women bathe, then float lit lamps on banana-stalk rafts. No pan-India or
    other-region equivalent found; this is a Telugu folk-temple tradition rather than a widely
    observed festival. (Source: https://hindupad.com/poli-swargam/)
- **Balipāḍyami / Gōvardhana Pūja** — start of Kārtika māsa (day after Dīpāvali Amāvāsya),
  annual. Worship of Gōvardhana hill (recalling Kṛṣṇa lifting it) with a cow-dung replica; also
  commemorates Viṣṇu's Vāmana-Bali story.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India, but under many regional names for essentially two
    overlapping observances. Govardhan Puja (Krishna lifting Govardhan hill, cow-dung hill replica)
    is mainly a North Indian practice, especially around Mathura/Braj. Bali Pratipada/Bali Padyami
    (Vamana-Bali story) is the name and emphasis used in Karnataka, Maharashtra (Bali Padwa) and
    Andhra Pradesh (Bali Padyami); other regional names include Barlaj (Himachal), Raja Bali
    (Jammu), and it also marks the Gujarati/Marwari New Year (Bestu Varas) in western India — so
    the same tithi carries genuinely different ritual content (hill-worship vs. new-year vs.
    Bali-legend) depending on the region.
    (Source: https://www.krazzymag.com/significance-of-balipratipada-govardhan-puja-and-padwa/)

## 2. Dvitīyā (Vidiya)

- **Śrī Jagannātha Ratha Yātra** — Āṣāḍha Śukla Vidiyā, annual. Chariot procession of Jagannātha,
  Balabhadra, Subhadra and Sudarśana at Puri; darśan on the ratha is said to prevent rebirth.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India in reach but Odisha-specific in origin/primacy. Puri
    remains the definitive celebration; the next-oldest is at Mahesh (Hooghly district, West
    Bengal, 600+ years old), followed by large processions in Ahmedabad, Gujarat (third-largest
    after Puri and Kolkata) and ISKCON-led Rath Yatras in Mumbai, Chennai and Hyderabad. The
    chariot-procession form and deities (Jagannātha, Balabhadra, Subhadra) stay consistent
    wherever it is replicated, unlike festivals that change name/practice regionally.
    (Source: https://www.outlooktraveller.com/experiences/heritage/4-prominent-rath-yatras-in-india)
- **Prīti Dvitīya** — Āśvayuja Śukla Dvitīya, during Navarātri, annual. Minor Navarātri day (also
  coincides with Tirumala Brahmōtsavam flag-hoisting in some years); no independent note given.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* This is essentially the second day of Śarad Navarātri (worship of
    Devī Brahmacharini in the pan-India Navarātri sequence), so it inherits Navarātri's regional
    variation: extended devotional recitation in North India, Garba in Gujarat, Golu (doll) display
    in Tamil Nadu. As a standalone "Prīti Dvitīya" name/observance tied specifically to Tirumala
    Brahmōtsavam flag-hoisting, it is a Telugu/temple-specific label rather than a name used
    elsewhere. (Source: https://www.zalkari.com/blogs/zalkari-tales/2nd-day-of-navratri)
- **Yama Pūja / Bhaginihasta Bhōjana (Bhai Dooj)** — Kārtika Śukla Dvitīya, annual. Siblings'
  festival: eating food served by one's sister, and gifting her clothes, for mutual longevity;
  worship of Yama wards off hell's torments.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India festival with many regional names: Bhaiya Dooj (UP,
    Bihar), Bhau Beej/Bhav Bij (Maharashtra, Goa, Gujarat, Karnataka), Yama Dwitiya (South India,
    matching the source's naming), Bhai Phota (Bengal, held the day after Kali Puja), and Bhai
    Tika (Nepal, one of the most significant post-Dashain festivals, with a Maithili variant called
    Bhardutiya Bhai Tika). The core ritual — sister feeding/blessing brother for longevity — is
    shared everywhere; only the name and adjoining local customs differ.
    (Source: https://www.memeraki.com/blogs/posts/regional-flavors-of-bhai-dooj-a-festive-tribute-to-sibling-connections)
- **Śrī Rāghavēndra Svāmi Ārādhana** — Śrāvaṇa Kṛṣṇa Dvitīyā, annual. Commemorates the day in
  1671 on which Śrī Rāghavēndra Tīrtha entered the Bṛndāvana at Mantrālayam. Observed at
  Mantrālayam and at Rāghavēndra Bṛndāvanas everywhere, with ārādhana over three days of which
  this tithi is the middle and principal one.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Sampradāya-specific rather than regional. It is a Mādhva
    (Dvaita) observance, kept wherever that sampradāya has spread — the Telugu and Kannada
    districts most densely, and Mādhva communities in Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra and abroad. It is
    not part of the general smārta festival calendar, and a household that does not follow the
    Mādhva tradition will not keep it.
    (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raghavendra_Tirtha)

## 3. Tṛtīyā (Tadiya)

- **Aṭla Tadiya (Candrōdaya Umā Vratam)** — Āśvayuja Kṛṣṇa Tṛtīyā, annual. Young unmarried
  women's day-long fast and evening Gaurī vratam, with arghya to the moon on moonrise; sought
  for a good husband and a trouble-free married life.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Regional, not pan-India: sources explicitly describe Atla Tadde
    as a festival "mainly in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana." It is functionally described as "the
    Telugu equivalent of Karva Chauth" (North India's analogous married-women's fasting festival
    for husbands' welfare) — i.e., a parallel regional tradition rather than a shared name for the
    same festival. Distinctive local practices include swinging on an uyyala and eating atlu (rice
    pancakes) to break the fast.
    (Source: https://hindupad.com/atla-tadde-2011-atla-thadde-gowri-vratam-in-andhra-pradesh/)
- **Akṣaya Tṛtīya (with Trētā Yugādi, Paraśurāma Jayanti, Balarāma Jayanti)** — Vaiśākha Śukla
  Tṛtīya, annual. Any charity, japa, homa or worship on this day is believed to yield "akṣaya"
  (inexhaustible) merit; also Paraśurāma's incarnation day and the Candanōtsavam at Siṁhāchalam.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Genuinely pan-India, with regional names: Akha Teej in Gujarat
    and Rajasthan (where mass weddings are traditionally held without needing a separate muhurat)
    and Akti in Chhattisgarh. Practices differ by region: Maharashtra emphasizes the Haldi-Kumkum
    women's gathering and the start of monsoon sowing; Gujarat emphasizes Lakshmi Pujan, grain
    charity and gold-buying by trading families; Rajasthan emphasizes mass marriages. The
    Telugu-specific Candanōtsavam at Siṁhāchalam noted in the source is a local temple-specific
    addition not found elsewhere.
    (Source: https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/trends/story/akshaya-tritiya-2026-from-gold-rush-to-farming-rituals-how-different-states-in-india-celebrate-the-festival-differently-525727-2026-04-15)
- **Undralla Tadiya (Modaka Tṛtīya)** — Bhādrapada Tṛtīya, annual. Women fast till evening, worship
  Gowri Devi with sixteen upacāras, and offer rice-flour balls (undrallu) to other women.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Narrowly regional, Telugu-specific (Andhra Pradesh and Telangana
    only per sources); also called Brihatyuma Vratam. No pan-India equivalent name found; it is a
    localized women's/girls' vrata for Gauri-Shiva with distinctive undrallu (rice-flour ball)
    offerings and swing festivities, parallel in spirit to Aṭla Tadiya but a separate, smaller
    regional observance. (Source: https://hindusphere.com/undrala-undralla-taddi-vrat-katha/)
- **Hartālikā Gaurī Vratam (Svarṇa Gaurī Vratam, Tadiya Gauramma Vratam)** — Bhādrapada Śukla
  Tṛtīyā, annual. Women's day-long fast and worship of Gaurī, made with sand or turmeric,
  together with Śiva; sought for marital harmony and progeny. Falls on the day before Vināyaka
  Cavitī.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India on one tithi under several names. The Hindi-speaking
    states (Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Jharkhand, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh) and Nepal call it
    Hartālikā Tīj; Karnataka calls it Gaurī Habba or Svarṇa Gaurī Vratam; Andhra Pradesh and
    Telangana call it Tadiya Gauramma Vratam. The core — Pārvatī worship and a prayer for marital
    harmony — is shared. The South Indian form emphasises Gaurī worship and a turmeric-decorated
    kalaśa, and in Karnataka and the Telugu districts it is tied directly to the Gaṇeśa festival
    that follows the next day, which the North Indian form is not.
    (Source: https://www.drikpanchang.com/festivals/teej/info/hartalika-puja-vidhi.html)

## 4. Caturthī

- **Vināyaka Chaviti (Vinayaka/Ganesh Chaturthi)** — Bhādrapada Śukla Caturthī, annual, the
  year's principal Gaṇapati festival. Siddhi Vināyaka worshipped in a clay image with 21
  modakas/upacāras; the standard "arghya to Agastya" custom is also tied to this window.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India, India's most widely celebrated Ganesha festival, but
    with real regional variation in scale and form: Maharashtra (especially Mumbai/Pune) is famous
    for massive 10-day public pandals with community idol immersion (Mumbai alone immerses roughly
    150,000 statues annually); Tamil Nadu emphasizes home/temple worship at Pillayar shrines with
    kozhukattai sweets; Karnataka favors more intimate home worship over public pandals;
    Telangana/Andhra Pradesh (where it is called Vinayaka Chavithi, matching the source) is known
    for landmark large idols like Hyderabad's Khairatabad Ganesh.
    (Source: https://thefederal.com/category/news/ganesh-chaturthi-how-indian-states-celebrate-birth-of-lord-ganesha-203417)
- **Varada Gaṇēśa Caturthi (Tila/Kunda Caturthi)** — Māgha Śukla Caturthī, annual. Gaṇapati
  (including Dhundhi Gaṇapati at Kāśī) worshipped in the evening after a day's fast; Śiva also
  worshipped with jasmine flowers.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Primarily a Maharashtra/Konkan observance (also called Ganesha
    Jayanti, Tilkund Chaturthi, or Varad Chaturthi there), rather than a pan-India festival; it is
    considered the most meritorious of the year's Ganesha Chaturthis per the Mudgala Purana. It
    overlaps conceptually with the broader South Indian practice of observing each month's
    Sankashti Chaturthi, but the source's Dhundhi Gaṇapati/Kāśī framing is not corroborated as a
    distinct North Indian tradition in the sources found.
    (Source: https://www.freepressjournal.in/lifestyle/when-is-varada-chaturthi-2026-know-correct-date-muhurat-katha-significance-more)
- **Nāgula Chaviti** — Kārtika Śukla Caturthī, annual. Milk and rice-flour/sesame sweets offered
  at anthills to the serpent gods; no cutting vegetables or using blades this day.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Regional, not pan-India: explicitly a Telugu serpent-worship day
    (Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, with some observance in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu), distinct from
    Nāga Pañcami, which is the analogous serpent festival kept mainly in North and West India during
    Śrāvaṇa. So the source's Kārtika-timed Nāgula Chaviti and the pan-India Nāga Pañcami (see
    Section 5 below) are two separate festivals honoring the same deities on different tithis/
    months, not regional-name variants of one festival.
    (Source: https://grokipedia.com/page/Nagula_Chavithi)
- **Umā Caturthi (Varada Gaṇēśa Pūja)** — Jyēṣṭha Caturthī, annual. Worship of the Divine Mother
  as Umā, paired with Gaṇapati worshipped as "Pradyumna Gaṇapati."
- **Durva Gaṇapati Vratam (Nāgacaturthi)** — Śrāvaṇa Śukla Caturthī, annual. Gaṇapati worshipped
  with pañca-pallava (five sacred leaves); Nāga Dēvatā also honored the same day.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Wider than purely Telugu: sources describe it as "observed
    throughout India, especially in Maharashtra, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Karnataka, Goa and Andhra
    Pradesh," falling exactly a month before Ganesh Chaturthi and centered on offering 21 blades of
    durva grass rather than elaborate pandal worship — a simpler, more private vrata than the main
    Chaturthi. No distinct regional name found; it appears to travel under the same name across the
    states where it is kept.
    (Source: https://www.omspiritualshop.com/te/blogs/%E0%B0%A8%E0%B1%8D%E0%B0%AF%E0%B1%82%E0%B0%B8%E0%B1%8D/durva-ganapati-chaturthi-2026)
- **Tilacaturthi (Putra Gaṇapati Vratam, Śānta Caturthi)** — Phālguṇa Śukla Caturthī, annual.
  Vināyaka worshipped with a sesame-seed homam; day fast, night meal.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Regional under a different name in the North: referred to as
    "Tilo Chauth" or Tilkund Chaturthi in Uttar Pradesh, where Ganesha is invoked specifically on
    behalf of a family's son — matching the source's "Putra Gaṇapati" framing. This suggests the
    Phālguṇa-month Tila Caturthi observance is shared in name/intent between Telugu and North
    Indian (UP) traditions, though detailed ritual comparison was not found beyond this naming
    overlap. (Source: https://www.baba.org/tila-chaturthi-.html)
- **Vikaṭa Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthi** — Caitra Kṛṣṇa Caturthī, annual — the calendar year's first
  Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthi, believed to remove that year's obstacles at the outset.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India in structure: Sankashti/Sankashtahara Chaturthi
    (Krishna Paksha Chaturthi every month, each dedicated to one of Ganesha's eight forms) is
    observed across India, and Vikata Sankashti (the Chaitra occurrence, worshipping the "Vikata"
    form) is simply that month's instance of a nationwide monthly vrata rather than a distinct
    regional festival — consistent with the source's framing as "the year's first" Sankashtahara.
    (Source: https://www.thehansindia.com/life-style/vikata-sankashti-chaturthi-date-time-and-ritual-873412)
- **Āṇḍōḷikā Vratam (Saubhāgya Gaurī Vratam)** — Caitra Caturthī/Vidiya (varies by year), annual.
  Women's vrata for marital fortune: Umā-Maheśvara worshipped, Gaurī cradled, invited women
  gifted mango, turmeric and kumkum.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Regional: Saubhāgya/Soubhagya Gauri Vratam ("Gauri Tritiya") is
    observed by women in Andhra Pradesh and Maharashtra, and is described as a Telangana/Andhra
    Pradesh variation of Gangaur — the major Rajasthani/North Indian Gauri festival for marital
    fortune. So this is best understood as a regional cousin of Gangaur under a different name and
    shorter observance, not identical practice.
    (Source: https://www.hindu-blog.com/2009/03/soubhagya-gauri-vrata-gauri-puja-in.html)
- **Lakṣmī Pūja (Śrīvratam) / Nāga Damana Pūja** — sometimes framed as Caturthī/Pañcamī of
  Caitra, annual. Lakṣmī worship paired with serpent worship using Damana leaves, milk and ghee.
- **Sakaṭa Caturthī (Sakat Chauth, Tila-kuṭa Caturthī, Māghī Chauth)** — Māgha Kṛṣṇa Caturthī,
  annual. The Māgha occurrence of the monthly Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthī, kept in North India as a
  named festival in its own right: a mothers' fast for the wellbeing of children, broken at
  moonrise, with sesame and jaggery offered to Gaṇapati.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* One tithi, two levels of observance. In the South — Tamil Nadu,
    Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana — this day is kept simply as that month's
    Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthī, with no special name. In Uttar Pradesh, Bihar and parts of Rajasthan
    it is a distinct festival, Sakat Chauth, with the children's-welfare fast and tilkuṭ as
    prasāda; in other parts of Rajasthan, Haryana and Punjab the prayer is for the harvest;
    Maharashtra calls the day Lambodara Saṅkaṣṭī Caturthī. So the tithi is pan-India but the
    named festival is not.
    (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankashti_Chaturthi)

*(Monthly Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthī — see Notes below.)*

## 5. Pañcamī

- **Nāga Pañcami (Garuḍa Pañcami)** — recurs almost every month across the corpus (Kārtika,
  Mārgaśira, Śrāvaṇa, etc.), but is most emphasized in Kārtika. Worship of Garuḍa/serpent gods
  by offering milk at anthills or serpent hills; farmers observe it for a bumper harvest.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Genuinely pan-India, though the exact paksha/fortnight and local
    ritual form vary: most of India keeps it in Śrāvaṇa Śukla Pañcamī, but Karnataka, Rajasthan,
    Bihar and Gujarat mark it in the Krishna Paksha instead. Regional practices differ
    substantially: Kerala's snake groves host Sarpabali, Sarpam Thullal and monthly Ayilya Puja;
    Maharashtra features women drawing snake images at doorways; Gujarat frames snakes as crop
    protectors with agricultural-flavored rituals; UP/Bihar hold fairs; South India (Karnataka,
    Tamil Nadu) has married women fasting for family welfare. This is one of the more genuinely
    nationwide festivals in this list, unlike the more Telugu-specific Nāgula Chaviti above.
    (Source: https://www.exoticindiaart.com/article/naga-panchami-the-serpent-in-story-symbol-and-sacred-ritual/)
- **Ṛṣi Pañcami** — Bhādrapada Śukla Pañcamī, annual. Women worship the Saptarṣis (with their
  wives) for release from menstrual-cycle lapses, with charity of bananas, ghee, sugar, money.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Wider than Telugu-only: kept by women in Maharashtra, Gujarat,
    Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Nepal and South India, with Maharashtra having the most distinctive local
    custom — a simple, unspiced "Rishi Panchami Bhaji" (mixed-vegetable dish) eaten to mark ritual
    purity, and Maharashtra/Gujarat additionally including worship of wild herbs/vegetables. The
    underlying purpose (release from menstrual-cycle related ritual lapses, Saptarshi worship) is
    consistent across regions.
    (Source: https://curlytales.com/india/food/rishi-panchami-2025-significance-rituals-rishi-chi-bhaji-a-21-vegetable-recipe-made-on-this-day/)
- **Śrī Pañcami (Madana/Vasanta Pañcami)** — Māgha Śukla Pañcamī, annual. Celebrates Saraswatī's
  manifestation as Parā Śakti; worshipped with white sandalwood, white flowers and rice pudding.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India under the name Vasant/Basant Panchami with strong
    regional variation in emphasis: in West Bengal it is Saraswati Puja, a major student-centered
    pandal festival (yellow basanti sarees for girls, dhoti-kurta for boys, books and instruments
    worshipped alongside the idol); in Punjab and Haryana it is "Basant Panchami" and is primarily
    a kite-flying festival tied to the spring/mustard-flower season; Bihar, UP and Rajasthan hold
    cultural gatherings. The Telugu emphasis on white sandalwood/white flowers in the source is a
    regional ritual detail not mirrored in the Bengali or Punjabi observances.
    (Source: https://harishyam.com/blogs/news/how-different-regions-of-india-celebrate-vasant-panchami-bengali-north-indian-south-indian-customs)
- **Upāṅga Lalitā Gauri Vratam / Nāga Pūja** — Āśvayuja Śukla Pañcamī, during Navarātri, annual.
  Lalitādēvi worshipped as "Pañcamī/Pañcabhūtēśī"; paired serpent worship relieves snake-doṣa.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Regional emphasis rather than uniformly pan-India: Upang Lalita
    Vrat (5th day of Śarad Navarātri, worship of Devī Lalitā/Tripurasundari) is described as most
    popular specifically in Gujarat and parts of Maharashtra, with South Indian traditions instead
    identifying Lalitā more with Chandi. The source's "Pañcabhūtēśī" framing and paired
    serpent-doṣa worship reflect a Telugu-specific inflection of this Navarātri day rather than the
    Gujarati/Maharashtrian version of the same vrata.
    (Source: https://www.oneindia.com/lifestyle/upang-lalita-vrat-celebration-2025-011-7870371.html)
- **Skanda Pañcamī** — Āṣāḍha Pañcamī, annual (often paired with Skanda/Kumāra Ṣaṣṭhī the next
  day). No elaborated note survives in the source beyond the date itself.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Regional to South India: kept with fervor at Muruga/Subrahmanya
    temples across Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Kerala, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh, essentially
    functioning as the fasting lead-in to the following day's Skanda/Kumāra Ṣaṣṭhī (fasting begins
    on Pañcamī morning, ends on Ṣaṣṭhī). No pan-India or North Indian equivalent name or observance
    was found; Murugan/Skanda worship in this concentrated form is a South Indian phenomenon.
    (Source: https://www.thedivineindia.com/skanda-panchami-kumar-shashti-subrahmanya-puja-benefits/7779)

## 6. Ṣaṣṭhī

- **Subrahmaṇya/Skanda/Kumāra Ṣaṣṭhī** — recurs in several months (notably Mārgaśira and
  Āṣāḍha), celebrating Kumārasvāmi's birthday; worship is said to relieve snake- and
  Kuja-(Mars)-related afflictions.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Known across South India as Skanda Sashti or Kanda Shashti; the
    deity himself carries many names regionally (Kartikeya, Murugan, Guha, Shanmukha, Velayudhan).
    It is predominantly a South Indian festival — Tamil Nadu (biggest, as Soorasamharam at temples
    like Tiruchendur), Kerala, Karnataka (e.g., the Kukke Subramanya temple's own "Kukke
    Subrahmanya Sashti") and Andhra/Telangana — with only limited North Indian observance, since
    Kartikeya/Murugan worship is much less prominent there.
    (Source: https://hindupad.com and https://www.hindu-blog.com/2009/10/skanda-sashti-2009.html)
- **Agnipūja / Mahā Ṣaṣṭhi (Subrahmaṇyārādhana)** — Kārtika Śukla Ṣaṣṭhī, annual. Fire/lamp
  worship combined with worship of Subrahmaṇya as "Agnigarbha" for general wellbeing.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* This Kārtika/Mārgaśira Śukla Ṣaṣṭhī observance is part of the same
    broader Subrahmanya Sashti tradition celebrated "with immense fervour" in Kerala, Tamil Nadu,
    Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh, with devotees doing abhishekams, fasts and temple processions. No
    distinct North Indian name or parallel was found — it reads as a regional (South Indian)
    Śaiva/Kaumāra observance rather than a pan-India festival.
    (Source: https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/andhra-pradesh/subrahmanya-sashti-festival-celebrated-grandly-1845004)
- **Sarasvatī Pūja (of books)** — Āśvayuja Śukla Ṣaṣṭhī on Mūla nakṣatra, during Navarātri,
  annual. Students worship their books/Sarasvatī for educational success.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Book/Sarasvatī worship during Navarātri is widespread but with
    real regional variation in timing and pairing: in Tamil Nadu and Kerala it is done on the 9th day
    (Navami) together with Āyudha Pūjā, while in Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh it is observed on the
    10th day (Vijayadashami). Kerala additionally uses this period for Vidyārambham, initiating
    young children into writing — thousands are inducted at temples like Sarasvati Mandir,
    Kottayam. Not tied to one exclusive regional name; the underlying "worship your tools/books"
    idea (Āyudha Pūjā) is pan-South-Indian, while Bengal's Saraswati Puja proper is a separate,
    spring (Vasant Panchami) festival, not this Navarātri one.
    (Source: https://www.prokerala.com/festivals/saraswati-pooja.html and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayudha_Puja)
- **Varuṇa Ṣaṣṭhi (Ṣaṣṭhīdēvi Pūja)** — Māgha Śukla Ṣaṣṭhī, annual. Varuṇa invoked in a kalaśa
  with red flowers; Ṣaṣṭhīdēvi separately worshipped for the welfare of progeny.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Sources describe Varuna Sashti as observed mainly in Andhra
    Pradesh, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu with red-flower/water-kalasha worship of Varuna — consistent
    with the source text. No distinct North Indian name found; appears to be a regional South
    Indian rite rather than a pan-India festival.
    (Source: https://hindupad.com/varuna-sashtivaruna-shashti/)
- **Araṇya Ṣaṣṭhi (Vana Gauri Vratam, Śītalā Ṣaṣṭhi)** — Jyēṣṭha Ṣaṣṭhī, annual. Gauri and Śītalā
  worshipped with Kārtikēya under a Kadamba tree; protects pregnancy and grants good progeny.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Real regional divergence here. In Bengal this Jyēṣṭha Ṣaṣṭhī is
    celebrated as "Jāmāi Ṣaṣṭhī" — a completely different-flavored festival centered on honoring
    sons-in-law, where mothers-in-law feed and bless their daughters' husbands, rather than the
    Kadamba-tree Gauri/Kārtikēya worship for progeny described in the Telugu source. In Andhra it
    is called Araṇya Gauri Vratam / Vanagauri Vratam, matching the source. Odisha has a related but
    distinct festival, Sitalsasthi, which instead commemorates the wedding of Shiva and Parvati and
    is observed mainly by Utkal and Aranyaka Brahmins. So the same tithi hosts genuinely different
    festivals region to region rather than one festival under different names.
    (Source: https://hindutempletalk.org/2023/05/25/5147/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitalsasthi)
- **Sūrya Ṣaṣṭhī (Skanda Darśanam)** — Bhādrapada Śukla Ṣaṣṭhī, annual. Pre-sunrise bath and Sūrya
  worship; Subrahmaṇya also honored.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* No strong independent sourcing found distinguishing this
    Bhādrapada Ṣaṣṭhī observance from the broader Chhath-adjacent Surya-Sashti family of festivals
    (Chhath Puja itself falls on Kartika Sashti in Bihar/UP/Nepal and is a massive North
    Indian/Bihari festival with its own distinct rituals of standing in water and offering to the
    setting/rising sun). The specific Bhādrapada-month, Subrahmanya-linked combination as described
    in the source appears to be a regional Telugu/Andhra observance; it should not be conflated
    with Chhath, which is a separate, much larger North Indian/Bihari Sashti festival with its own
    name and rites. (Source: general knowledge cross-check, no single dedicated URL found for this
    exact combination — flagged as uncertain.)

## 7. Saptamī

- **Ratha Saptami (Surya Jayanti)** — Māgha Śukla Saptamī, annual, a major Sūrya festival marking
  Uttarāyaṇa's turning point; milk pudding offered on green-bean-tree leaves at brahma muhūrta.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Major pan-India Sun festival with genuinely different regional
    names and customs: in Andhra Pradesh/Telangana it is "Surya Jayanti" with pre-dawn river baths
    at Godavari/Krishna and big crowds at the Arasavalli Sun temple; Karnataka calls it "Surya
    Pongal" and cooks a special pongal dish; North India (UP, Bihar, Rajasthan) observes fasting
    and water offerings to the rising sun without a distinct local name. Common South Indian
    practice includes placing seven arka (calotropis) leaves on the head/shoulders/chest during
    the ritual bath. Major temple celebrations occur at Tirumala, Konark, and Arasavalli.
    (Source: https://www.treebo.com/blog/ratha-saptami/ and https://www.4to40.com/indian-festivals/ratha-saptami/)
- **Bhānu Saptami** — recurs in many months whenever Saptamī falls on a Sunday (an alabhya-yōga
  combination); treated each time as an especially fruitful day for charity/japa/pūjā, not a
  fixed once-a-year festival.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A recurring (not annual) Surya-worship occasion whenever Saptami
    falls on a Sunday, treated as an especially potent "mahayoga" day rather than a fixed festival
    — this matches the source's framing. Observed loosely across UP, Bihar, Rajasthan, Maharashtra
    and Tamil Nadu, with notable temple crowds at Konark and Suryanar Kovil (Tamil Nadu), but it
    does not carry a separate regional name — it is a recognized pan-India occasion for Sun worship
    rather than a localized festival.
    (Source: https://www.theindianpanorama.news/spirituality/bhanu-saptami-festival-of-glory-of-sun-god/)
- **Nandā/Mitra Saptami** — Mārgaśira Saptamī, annual. Sun worshipped with the nāma "Mitra,"
  said to be preeminent through the whole month.
- **Gaṅgōtpatti (Gaṅgā/Vidyāraṇya Jayanti)** — Vaiśākha Śukla Saptamī, annual. Commemorates
  Maharṣi Jahnu releasing the Gaṅgā; also Vidyāraṇya's birth anniversary.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* This Vaiśākha Śukla Saptamī is also known as Gaṅgā Saptamī /
    Jahnu Saptamī / Gaṅgāvataraṇa, commemorating the Ganga's descent — distinct from Gaṅgā Daśaharā
    (Jyēṣṭha Daśamī, see below), which is the much bigger pan-North-India Ganga festival. The
    Vidyāraṇya Jayanti component (birth anniversary of the Vijayanagara-era Sringeri pontiff
    Vidyaranya Swami) is a specifically South Indian/Sringeri Mutt observance with no North Indian
    equivalent. So this bullet combines a modest pan-India river festival with a narrowly regional
    guru-jayanti. (Source: https://hindupad.com/vidyaranya-jayanti-swami-vidyaranya-jayanthi/)
- **Vivasvat Saptami** — Āṣāḍha Saptamī, annual. Sūrya worship and recitation of Āditya Hṛdaya.
- **Āmuktābharaṇa Santāna Saptami Vratam** — Bhādrapada Saptamī, annual. Full-day fast and evening
  worship of Gowri-Parameśvara for progeny and children's welfare.
- **Varuṇa Saptami** — Jyēṣṭha Saptamī, annual. Varuṇa worshipped via a water-filled kalaśa for
  relief from summer heat and timely monsoon rains.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Consistent with the source, this Jyēṣṭha Saptami Varuna-worship-
    for-monsoon observance appears in South Indian (Andhra/Karnataka/Tamil Nadu) panchang
    traditions; no distinct North Indian counterpart or alternate name was found — appears to be a
    regional rain-invoking rite rather than a pan-India festival.
    (Source: https://hindupad.com/varuna-sashtivaruna-shashti/)

## 8. Aṣṭamī

- **Durgāṣṭamī (Mahāṣṭami)** — Āśvayuja Śukla Aṣṭamī, during Navarātri, annual, major. Worship of
  Durgā said to remove all troubles; central Navarātri day.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* This is one of the most regionally varied festivals in the
    calendar. In West Bengal, this is the emotional peak of Durga Puja, with pandal-hopping, dhunuchi dance,
    anjali flower offerings and elaborate Bengali feasting. In North India it is the day of Kanya
    Pujan (Kanjak), where young pre-puberty girls are invited home, worshipped as living forms of
    the Devi, fed, and given gifts — a ritual largely absent in the south. In Karnataka and Andhra
    it converges with Saraswati Puja and Ayudha Puja (tool/weapon worship), and in
    Maharashtra/Gujarat the night is the peak of Garba/Dandiya dancing rather than Devi darshan per
    se. So the same tithi produces markedly different lived festivals — Durga Puja (Bengal), Kanya
    Puja (North), Saraswati/Ayudha Puja (South), Garba (West) — under the shared Navaratri umbrella.
    (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Ashtami and https://blog.shloka.life/durga-ashtami-2025-rituals-significance-and-celebration-guide/)
- **Śrī Kṛṣṇāṣṭamī (Janmāṣṭamī)** — Śrāvaṇa Kṛṣṇa Aṣṭamī, annual, major. Full-day fast broken with
  evening worship marking Kṛṣṇa's manifestation with all sixteen kalās.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Huge regional variation in practice. Maharashtra is famous for
    Dahi Handi — govinda pathaks building human pyramids up to nine tiers high to break pots hung
    40 feet up, reenacting Krishna's butter theft. Tamil Nadu calls it Gokulashtami, decorating
    homes with kolam footprints leading to the altar; Andhra Pradesh holds its own Uriyadi
    pot-breaking events mirroring Dahi Handi. Mathura/Vrindavan and North India generally stage
    elaborate Rasa Leela dramatizations of Krishna's life, often performed by child actors. ISKCON
    temples worldwide (very prominent in North India and internationally) hold 24-hour akhand
    kirtan and a midnight abhishekam. It is genuinely pan-India but the ritual expression differs
    sharply by region.
    (Source: https://www.outlookindia.com/astrology/astro-picks/krishna-janmashtami-2025-celebrations-across-india-from-mathura-to-odisha and https://vedayatra.in/blog/krishna-jayanthi-across-india-diverse-celebrations-and-traditions/)
- **Bhīṣma Aṣṭami** — Māgha Śukla Aṣṭamī, annual. Marks the day Bhīṣma merged his soul with
  Kṛṣṇa; tarpaṇa performed for him.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Observed with tarpaṇa for Bhishma and one's own ancestors,
    particularly in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra, with large Ganga-ghat
    gatherings in North India; it is noted as a comparatively minor observance in Bengal and parts
    of the south. So it leans North Indian/pan-Gangetic rather than being a major South Indian
    festival, despite appearing in this Telugu source.
    (Source: https://www.sentinelassam.com/more-news/national-news/bhishma-ashtami-2025-significance-rituals-teachings-of-bhishma-pitamah)
- **Dūrvāṣṭami (Rādhāṣṭami)** — Bhādrapada Śukla Aṣṭamī, annual. Gaṇapati worshipped with dūrvā
  grass; Rādhādevī and Gowridevi honored with seven kinds of flowers/fruits.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Rādhāṣṭamī itself is a major North Indian/Braj festival,
    overwhelmingly centered on Barsana (Radha's girlhood home) and Vrindavan, where nine-day
    celebrations culminate in a palanquin procession of Radha's idol with singing and
    flower-showers — quite different in scale and character from the Telugu source's more modest
    Gaṇapati-with-dūrvā-grass plus Rādhā/Gowri flower-worship framing. The dūrvā-grass Gaṇapati
    worship component looks like a South Indian/Andhra overlay onto the same tithi, distinct from
    the Braj Radhashtami tradition.
    (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radhashtami and https://iskcontemple.org/en/blogs/how-radha-ashtami-celebrated-home-temples-and-barsana-vrindavan)
- **Kālabhairavāṣṭami** — Mārgaśira Kṛṣṇa Aṣṭamī, annual. Day of Kālabhairava's manifestation;
  worship is said to grant protection and fulfil desires.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Also called Bhairava Ashtami, Bhairava Jayanti or Kala Ashtami;
    observed monthly, with the Mārgaśīrṣa occurrence (Kāla Bhairava Jayanti) being most
    significant. Major celebrations happen at Kāl Bhairav temples in Ujjain and Varanasi (North
    India), differing in date convention from South India — the North Indian calendar and the
    Andhra/Karnataka/Maharashtra/Gujarat Panchangam sometimes place the "same" festival in
    different lunar fortnights. This is a genuinely pan-India festival, with Ujjain as its
    best-known pilgrimage center.
    (Source: https://hindupad.com/kalabhairavashtami-kalabhairav-ahstami/ and https://www.ujjaindarshan.com/ujjain-kalbhairav.html)
- **Bhavānī (Aśōka) Aṣṭami** — Caitra Śukla Aṣṭamī, annual. Commemorates Pārvatī's birth to Mēna
  and Himavān; Śiva-Pārvatī worshipped, Aśōka buds eaten as prasādam.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Independent sourcing places the major public
    celebrations of "Ashokashtami"/Bhavani Ashtami not in the south but in Odisha (grand week-long
    festivities and a car/chariot festival at the Lingaraj Temple, Bhubaneswar) and in Tripura
    (Unakoti, with a holy bath at Ashtamikund) — i.e., it is prominent in eastern India rather than
    being distinctively Telugu/Andhra. The Parvati-birth theme matches the source, but the
    celebrated regional character is eastern-Indian rather than pan-South-Indian.
    (Source: https://odishatv.in/news/festivals-events/significance-of-ashok-ashtami-in-hindu-tradition-and-why-it-is-celebrated-232751)
- **Gōṣṭhāṣṭami Vratam (Kārtavīryōtpatti)** — Kārtika Śukla Aṣṭamī, annual. Cow worship and
  circumambulation for long life/prosperity; also Kārtavīrya's (Dattātrēya devotee) birthday,
  invoked for recovering lost items.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* The cow-worship component (Gopashtami) is a major North Indian
    festival, celebrated with particular fervor in Mathura, Vrindavan and the Braj region, marking
    young Krishna's first day tending cows after the Govardhan episode; Maharashtra has an
    analogous cow/calf festival called Govatsa Dwadashi on a different tithi. The
    Kārtavīrya/Dattātreya-devotee birthday component tied to recovering lost items is a more
    localized South Indian/Andhra Dattatreya tradition, not something found paired with Gopashtami
    in North Indian sourcing — so again this bullet layers a pan-India cow festival with a regional
    Dattatreya legend.
    (Source: https://www.tirthayatra.org/gopashtami-todays-festival-of-cow-worship-and-krishnas-gopa-lila/)
- **Śrī Mahiṣāsura Mardini Pūja** — Āṣāḍha Aṣṭamī, annual. Reciting the Mahiṣāsura Mardini stotra
  is said to remove evil qualities and grant auspicious yōga.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* The Mahishasura Mardini Stotra (attributed to Adi Shankara) is
    recited pan-India, especially famous via All India Radio's Bengali broadcast on Mahalaya
    morning kicking off Durga Puja season — but that is a different occasion (Mahalaya, not Āṣāḍha
    Aṣṭamī). No independent sourcing was found for an "Āṣāḍha Aṣṭamī" festival specifically under
    this name; it appears to be a minor, possibly Andhra-specific observance built around reciting
    the stotra, not a separately recognized pan-India festival day.
    (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahishasura_Mardini_Stotra)
- **Trilōcanāṣṭami** — Jyēṣṭha Aṣṭamī, annual. Umā-Maheśvara worship for marital happiness (and,
  for the unmarried, a good spouse).
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Real regional variety on this Jyēṣṭha Śukla Aṣṭamī: in Bengal it
    is observed as Kamya Trilochan Puja; in Kashmir the same date hosts the Khir Bhavani Mela at
    the Khir Bhavani temple near Srinagar; various regions also mark Dhumavati Jayanti or Shukla
    Devi Puja on this date. The Umā-Maheśvara marital-happiness worship described in the source is
    one regional (South Indian) expression among several distinct festivals sharing this tithi.
    (Source: https://hindupad.com/trilochan-ashtami-2010-trilochana-ashtami-vrat/)
- **Saddula Bathukamma (Pedda Bathukamma)** — Āśvayuja Śukla Aṣṭamī, annual. The ninth and
  final day of Bathukamma, Telangana's floral festival of Gaurī, and the only one of the nine on
  which all the women of a household take part. Coincides with Durgāṣṭamī, two days before
  Vijaya Daśamī. The nine days open on Mahālaya Amāvāsya — see Section 15.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Narrowly regional and strongly identified with Telangana, where
    it has been a state festival since 2014; also kept in bordering districts of Andhra Pradesh.
    Each of the nine days carries its own name, taken from the naivēdyam offered that day. The
    flower stack itself, built in tiers and set afloat on a tank at the close, has no equivalent
    elsewhere in India, although the Navarātri timing and the worship of Gaurī are shared.
    (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathukamma)
- **Kēdāra Gaurī Vratam begins** — Āśvayuja Śukla Aṣṭamī. Opening of the twenty-one-day
  Kēdārēśvara vratam, which concludes on Dīpāvali Amāvāsya — see Section 15.

## 9. Navamī

- **Mahānavamī (Dasara)** — Āśvayuja Śukla Navamī, annual, major. Principal day of Navarātri
  Dasara pūjā, worship of Jagadamba.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Major pan-India day, historically significant as the state
    festival of the Vijayanagara Empire (hence "Mahanavami," celebrated grandly at Hampi). Today it
    converges with regional Navaratri/Durga Puja customs — in Bengal it is the penultimate day of
    Durga Puja before visarjan; in Karnataka's Mysuru Dasara, the royal Wadiyar family's Ayudha
    Puja involves worship of golden weapons; in many regions Navami is when vehicles, tools and
    machinery are ritually washed and worshipped (Ayudha Puja proper).
    (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayadashami)
- **Śrī Rāma Navamī** — Caitra Śukla Navamī, annual, major. Rāma's birth, celebrated with Sītā
  Rāma Kalyāṇaṁ (famously at Bhadrachalam) and sixteen-upacāra mandapa worship.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Major pan-India festival with sharply different regional flavors.
    In Ayodhya, huge crowds take a holy dip in the Sarayu and throng the new Ram Mandir, where a
    "Surya Tilak" — an engineered beam of sunlight — is made to fall on Ram Lalla's forehead at
    noon. Bhadrachalam (Telangana), called the "Ayodhya of the South," is the epicenter for Telugu
    observance, focused not on the birth alone but on the grand Sītā Rāma Kalyāṇaṁ (wedding), with
    the state government traditionally sponsoring the silk and pearl-rice offerings, matching the
    source text's emphasis. In Vontimitta (Andhra), the Kalyanam is uniquely performed at night per
    local legend. Tamil Nadu's celebration is comparatively understated — temple rituals and
    Carnatic-music concerts rather than large processions. Sitamarhi in Bihar, held to be Sita's
    birthplace, is a major North Indian pilgrimage site for the festival.
    (Source: https://www.outlookindia.com/brand-studio/ram-navami-2026-in-south-india-top-temples-kalyanam-traditions and https://nestasia.in/blogs/news/how-different-states-in-india-celebrate-ram-navami)
- **Akṣaya Navamī Vratam** — Kārtika Śukla Navamī, annual. River bathing, worship and charity on
  this day are held to give inexhaustible ("akṣaya") results.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Also widely called Amla/Aonla Navami or Kushmanda Navami,
    observed across North India, West Bengal and Odisha with community feasts held under an amla
    (Indian gooseberry) tree, believed to purify food and body. Notably, in West Bengal this same
    Kartika Shukla Navami is also the date of Jagaddhatri Puja, a major goddess festival (especially
    big in Chandannagar and Krishnanagar) — again illustrating how one tithi carries very different
    named festivals in different regions. The river-bathing/charity framing in the source matches
    general pan-India practice.
    (Source: https://www.myzodiaq.in/en/online-library/festivals-and-astrology/festival-muhurat-rituals-and-customs/akshaya-navami-2025-date-significance-auspicious-timing-and-rituals)
- **Avidhava Navami** — Bhādrapada Pitṛpakṣa Navamī, annual. Special śrāddham for women who died
  as "Sumaṅgali" (married, predeceasing their husband); more prevalent in North India.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* The source's own note that this is "more prevalent in North
    India" is confirmed: it is part of Pitru Paksha shraddha rites for women who died as Sumangali
    (predeceasing their husbands), performed by the eldest son. There is a timing difference across
    regions — in North India it falls in Ashwin Krishna Paksha, while Gujarat, Maharashtra,
    Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh observe the equivalent rite in Bhadrapada Krishna Paksha. It is a
    pan-India Pitru Paksha custom (also called Matru Navami/Avidhava Shraddha) rather than a purely
    regional one, though the underlying Pitru Paksha framework itself is much more elaborately
    marked in North India (Gaya, Varanasi, Prayag pilgrimage rites) than in the south.
    (Source: https://www.hindu-blog.com/2009/09/what-is-avidhava-navami-how-to-perform.html)

## 10. Daśamī

- **Vijaya Daśamī (Dasara)** — Āśvayuja Śukla Daśamī, annual, major. Jagadamba worshipped a
  second time; Āyudha Pūja (weapons), Śamī-tree worship at dusk, and the "Vijayā" muhūrta.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* This tithi produces many distinct regional festivals, more than
    most in this list. North India centers on Ravana Dahan — burning towering firecracker-stuffed effigies of Ravana,
    Kumbhakarna and Meghnad at dusk, often preceded by weeks of Ramlila theatrical performances.
    Mysuru Dasara (Karnataka) is a royal-heritage festival centered on Goddess Chamundeshwari,
    culminating in the Jamboo Savari elephant procession through an illuminated city. Kullu
    Dussehra (Himachal Pradesh) uniquely begins (rather than ends) on this day, with hundreds of
    local deities converging in procession behind Raghunathji. In Bengal, this same day is "Bijoya
    Dashami," the sorrowful/joyous conclusion of Durga Puja, with Durga idol immersion (visarjan)
    and married women's sindoor khela (vermilion-smearing ritual) before bidding the goddess
    farewell. The source's Āyudha Pūjā/Śamī-tree/Vijaya-muhūrta framing reflects the South Indian
    (Telugu/Kannada) practice of this same day.
    (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayadashami and https://www.editorji.com/lifestyle-news/from-vijayadashami-to-mysuru-dasara-5-unique-dussehra-celebrations-across-india-1728543926541)
- **Dharmarāja Daśami** — Caitra Śukla Daśamī, annual. Worship of Dharmarāja and his brothers for
  wisdom to act rightly.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Also called Yama Dharmaraja Dashami, a Chaitra Śukla Daśamī vrata
    focused on removing the fear of death, addressed to Yudhishthira in his role as Dharmaraja.
    Notably distinct from this is the Mithila-region (Bihar/Nepal) tradition where "Dharmaraj" is
    worshipped as a kuldevta (family deity), with its own major festival called Ghari Puja drawing
    large crowds from across Mithila and Nepal — a different, folk-religion strand of "Dharmaraja"
    worship unrelated to the Mahabharata figure. The source's version is a minor, fairly
    localized South Indian vrata, separate from the Mithila kuldevta tradition of the same name.
    (Source: https://hindupad.com/dharmaraja-dasami-yamadharmaraj-dashami/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmaraj_(Kuldevta))
- **Daśa Pāpahara Daśami Vratam (Gaṅgā Dashahara)** — Jyēṣṭha Śukla Daśamī, annual. Bathing in
  (or worshipping) the Gaṅgā is said to remove the effects of ten kinds of sin.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A major pan-India, especially North Indian, river festival. The
    name itself ("removal of ten sins") matches the source directly. Celebrated most grandly at
    Haridwar's Har Ki Pauri and at the ghats of Varanasi, where pilgrims take holy dips, perform
    Ganga aarti, and float leaf-boats laden with lamps and flowers downstream at dusk. Also
    significantly observed in Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar and West Bengal. There is not a
    differently-named South Indian equivalent with comparable scale — this is fundamentally a North
    Indian Ganga-basin festival that Telugu almanacs also record for its merit-based observance
    (bathing/charity) rather than as a major local temple festival.
    (Source: https://www.festivalsofindia.in/ganga/ and https://cultureandheritage.org/2024/03/ganga-dussehra-how-india-pays-homage-to-its-lifeline-river-ganga.html)

## 11. Ēkādaśī

Each named Ēkādaśī below is its own once-a-year vrata tied to a specific lunar month/pakṣa —
distinct in story and merit even though all fall on the same tithi. Note on naming: the source
magazine uses the **amanta** lunar-month convention standard in South India (a Kṛṣṇa-pakṣa is
named for the month about to begin), while most pan-India/North Indian Ekādaśī lists use the
**pūrṇimānta** convention (Kṛṣṇa pakṣa named for the month just ended) — so a Kṛṣṇa-pakṣa
Ēkādaśī's month-label below will often appear "one month later" than its pan-India label; this is
a calendar-convention artifact, not a different festival, and is called out per-entry below only
where it matters.

- **Aindra Ēkādaśī (Dharmaprabha Ēkādaśī)** — Bhādrapada Kṛṣṇa. Giving oil-seeds in charity;
  linked to Satya Hariścandra regaining his wife, son and kingdom.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* In the standard pūrṇimānta cycle, this slot (Āśvayuja/Āśvina
    Kṛṣṇa Ēkādaśī) is universally known as **Indirā Ēkādaśī** — dedicated to the departed and to
    King Indrasena per the Padma Purāṇa. "Aindra" is simply the adjectival Sanskrit form derived
    from Indra/Indirā, so this is the same vrata under the amanta month-label, not a different one;
    "Dharmaprabha" is a secondary/local title tied to Satya Hariścandra's story that is less
    commonly attached to Indirā Ēkādaśī outside Telugu sources.
    (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ekadashi ; https://www.iskcondelhi.com/full-year-ekadashi-list/)
- **Ramā Ēkādaśī** — Āśvayuja Kṛṣṇa. Vrata dedicated to Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* This matches the standard pan-India name exactly — **Rāma
    Ēkādaśī** (Kārtika Kṛṣṇa in pūrṇimānta terms, just before Dīpāvalī) is dedicated to
    Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa and is observed the same way and under the same name across North and South
    India, often as the fast that precedes Diwali. No naming discrepancy here beyond the expected
    amanta/pūrṇimānta month-shift. (Source: https://www.iskcondelhi.com/full-year-ekadashi-list/)
- **Pāśāṅkuśa Ēkādaśī (Parāṅkuśa/Ramyaikādaśī)** — Āśvayuja Śukla. Protects the observer "like by
  a goad"; if dedicated to the departed, uplifts them from hell.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Standard pan-India name is **Pāpāṅkuśā Ēkādaśī** (Āśvina Śukla),
    and "Pāśāṅkuśa/Parāṅkuśa" is a well-attested phonetic variant of the same name (aṅkuśa =
    elephant-goad, matching the source's own gloss "protects like a goad"). This is a Śukla-pakṣa
    entry so the month-label lines up directly with pan-India lists; naming is essentially
    identical, differing only by transliteration/regional pronunciation.
    (Source: https://www.iskcondelhi.com/full-year-ekadashi-list/ ; https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Uttpanna Ēkādaśī** — Kārtika Kṛṣṇa. Marks the day the presiding deity of Ēkādaśī itself
  manifested.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Utpannā Ēkādaśī**
    (Mārgaśīrṣa/Kārtika Kṛṣṇa depending on convention) exactly — it is universally described as the
    day the presiding devī of Ēkādaśī herself manifested from Viṣṇu to slay the demon Mura. No
    regional divergence in naming; only the expected month-label shift applies.
    (Source: https://www.iskcondelhi.com/full-year-ekadashi-list/)
- **Utthāna/Ananta Ēkādaśī (Prabodhini)** — Kārtika Śukla. Viṣṇu rises from yōganidrā; opens
  Bhīṣma Pañcaka Vratam.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* This is one of the biggest pan-India Ēkādaśīs. The standard name
    is **Prabodhinī / Devutthāna / Dev-Uṭhanī Ēkādaśī** (Kārtika Śukla) — the day Viṣṇu is said to
    awaken from yoga-nidrā, ending Cāturmāsya and opening the Hindu wedding season; it is
    inseparably linked to **Tulasī Vivāha** (the ceremonial wedding of Tulasī/Vṛndā to
    Śāligrāma/Viṣṇu) performed that evening across North and South India alike. The source's
    "Utthāna/Ananta" and "Prabodhini" are both attested alternate names for the same day; "Bhīṣma
    Pañcaka Vratam" beginning here is a South Indian/Smārta elaboration layered on the same
    pan-India tithi. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prabodhini_Ekadashi)
- **Mōkṣadā Ēkādaśī (Gītā Jayanti)** — Mārgaśira Śukla. Coincides with the Bhagavad Gītā's
  traditional day of recitation.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name and observance exactly —
    **Mokṣadā Ēkādaśī** (Mārgaśīrṣa Śukla) is celebrated nationwide as **Gītā Jayantī**, the day
    Kṛṣṇa is traditionally held to have recited the Bhagavad Gītā to Arjuna at Kurukṣetra; ISKCON
    and mainstream Vaiṣṇava calendars everywhere mark it the same way.
    (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mokshada_Ekadashi)
- **Saphala Ēkādaśī** — Mārgaśira Kṛṣṇa. Removes the effect of evils, bestows Nārāyaṇa's grace.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Saphalā Ēkādaśī** (Pauṣa/
    Mārgaśīrṣa Kṛṣṇa) exactly, associated with removing sin and bestowing Nārāyaṇa's grace/
    "fruitful" results in every regional tradition. Only the usual amanta/pūrṇimānta month-shift
    applies; no regional divergence in name. (Source: https://www.iskcondelhi.com/full-year-ekadashi-list/)
- **Mukkōṭi / Vaikuṇṭha Ēkādaśī (incl. Saphala/Putradā variants by tradition)** — Puṣya Śukla.
  Vaikuṇṭha's north gate said to open for darśan; especially auspicious at Srirangam.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* **Vaikuṇṭha Ēkādaśī is itself pan-India**, not merely a South
    Indian label — it is the single most widely recognized "special" Ēkādaśī name across Vaiṣṇava
    India, associated everywhere with the belief that Viṣṇu's northern gate (Vaikuṇṭha Dvāram/
    Vasal) opens for darśan. It is celebrated at massive scale at Tirumala (Tirupati)'s Vaikuṇṭha
    Dvāram darśan (roughly a 10-day festival window) and at Srirangam's 21-day Vaikuṇṭha Ēkādaśī
    festival with the opening of the Vaikuṇṭha Vāsal/Paramapada Vāsal, drawing lakhs of pilgrims;
    the day is Pauṣa Śukla Ēkādaśī (within Dhanurmāsa) per Telugu/Kannada/North Indian calendars.
    "Mukkōṭi" ("thirty-three crore [gods]") is the more distinctly Tamil/Telugu regional epithet
    layered on top of the pan-India Vaikuṇṭha name; in strict pan-India Vaiṣṇava nomenclature this
    slot is separately also called **Pauṣa Putradā Ēkādaśī**, which the source itself flags as a
    variant. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaikuntha_Ekadashi ;
    https://imvoyager.com/temples-to-visit-during-vaikunta-ekadasi/ ;
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pausha_Putrada_Ekadashi)
- **Vimala Ēkādaśī (Ṣaṭtila Ēkādaśī)** — Puṣya Kṛṣṇa. Sesame-water bath, sesame homa and charity.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Standard pan-India name is **Ṣaṭtilā Ēkādaśī** (Māgha Kṛṣṇa),
    well known nationwide for its sesame (til) observances — six different uses of sesame in
    bathing, homa, eating, and charity. "Vimala" appears to be a secondary/local title in the
    source; the sesame-centred practice described matches the pan-India Ṣaṭtilā tradition exactly.
    (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Bhīṣma/Bhīma Ēkādaśī** — Māgha Śukla. Opens the five-day Bhīṣma Pañcaka Vratam of tarpaṇa.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* **Flagged possible naming/identity conflation.** This entry
    conflates two genuinely distinct pan-India Ēkādaśīs that share a name-root: (1)
    **Bhīṣmāṣṭamī**-linked "Bhīṣma Pañcaka" observances actually begin at Prabodhinī (Kārtika
    Śukla, see above), not Māgha; and (2) the far more famous **Bhīmā/Nirjalā Ēkādaśī** (Jyeṣṭha
    Śukla, also called Pāṇḍava/Bhīmasenī Ēkādaśī) is the nationally best-known "Bhīma"-named
    Ēkādaśī, tied to the Mahābhārata story of Bhīma being advised by Vyāsa to keep one strict
    waterless fast to gain the merit of all 24. The source's Māgha Śukla "Bhīṣma/Bhīma Ēkādaśī"
    most likely reflects a regional Telugu grouping of Bhīṣma-tarpaṇa observances rather than the
    famous Nirjalā/Bhīma vrata itself — worth double-checking against the source magazine's
    original Telugu wording. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirjala_Ekadashi)
- **Vijaya Ēkādaśī** — Māgha/Phālguṇa Kṛṣṇa. Grants victory in obtaining the four puruṣārthas.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Vijayā Ēkādaśī**
    (Phālguṇa/Māgha Kṛṣṇa) exactly — known everywhere for the story of Rāma performing this vrata
    (on Nārada's advice) before crossing the ocean to Laṅkā, granting victory in undertakings.
    (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Amalaka Ēkādaśī (Dhātri/Amṛta Ēkādaśī)** — Phālguṇa Śukla. Worship of Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa under
  an Amalaka (gooseberry) tree.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Āmalakī Ēkādaśī** (Phālguṇa
    Śukla) exactly, observed nationwide with worship of Viṣṇu under an āmalakī (Indian
    gooseberry/amla) tree, which is held to have sprung from Brahmā's tears of joy. "Dhātri" (a
    name for the amla tree) and "Amṛta" are attested pan-India alternate epithets, not
    South-Indian-specific ones. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalaka_Ekadashi)
- **Pāpavimōcana Ēkādaśī (Vijayaikādaśī)** — Phālguṇa Kṛṣṇa. Removes sins of many births;
  footwear charity is prescribed.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Pāpamōcanī Ēkādaśī**
    (Caitra Kṛṣṇa in pūrṇimānta terms) exactly — universally described as removing sins of many
    births, with footwear/umbrella-type charity prescribed in various regional retellings. The
    bracketed "Vijayaikādaśī" alias in the source appears to be a mislabel/duplication with the
    separate Vijayā Ēkādaśī entry above rather than a standard alternate name for Pāpamōcanī.
    (Source: https://www.iskcondelhi.com/full-year-ekadashi-list/)
- **Kāmadā Ēkādaśī** — Caitra Śukla. Grants all wishes; paired with Kṛṣṇa's cradle worship.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Kāmadā Ēkādaśī** (Caitra
    Śukla) exactly, the first Ēkādaśī of the traditional Caitra-starting Hindu year in most
    pan-India lists, associated with fulfilling wishes and the story of Lalitā/Lalita released from
    a rākṣasa curse. No regional naming divergence. (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Varudhini Ēkādaśī** — Caitra Kṛṣṇa. Per the Bhaviṣya Purāṇa, released King Dundhumāra from a
  curse of Śiva.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Varūthinī Ēkādaśī**
    (Vaiśākha Kṛṣṇa) exactly, with the same Bhaviṣya Purāṇa story of King Dhundhumāra/Māndhātā's
    curse. Only transliteration differs ("Varudhini" vs "Varūthinī").
    (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Mōhinī Ēkādaśī** — Vaiśākha Śukla. Fasting, stotra recitation, japa and meditation.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Mohinī Ēkādaśī** (Vaiśākha
    Śukla) exactly, widely observed nationwide and associated in some North Indian tellings with
    Rāma's own observance of it during his exile to relieve his grief.
    (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Apara Ēkādaśī** — Vaiśākha/Jyēṣṭha Kṛṣṇa. Worship of Nārāyaṇa as Vāmana removes sins of many
  births; house repairs are avoided this day.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Aparā Ēkādaśī** (Jyeṣṭha
    Kṛṣṇa) exactly (also called Acalā/Bhadrakālī Ēkādaśī in some Bengali/Eastern traditions — a
    genuine regional alternate not mentioned in the source), held nationwide to remove great sins
    including brahma-hatyā; avoidance of house-building/repairs on this day is a widely shared, not
    South-India-specific, custom. (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Nirjala Ēkādaśī (also Kūrma Jayanti, "Trivikramaikādaśī")** — Jyēṣṭha Śukla. Fasting without
  even water — held to carry the merit of all the year's Ēkādaśīs combined.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* One of the most famous pan-India Ēkādaśīs of all — **Nirjalā
    Ēkādaśī** (Jyeṣṭha Śukla), also called **Bhīmasenī/Pāṇḍava Ēkādaśī**, is considered the most
    austere of the 24 and, per the Mahābhārata story of Bhīma being advised by Vyāsa, is held to
    carry the combined merit of every other Ēkādaśī in the year if kept without even water — this
    exactly matches the source's description. It draws huge crowds at Vaiṣṇava temples nationwide
    (Mathura-Vrindavan, Varanasi, Puri, Tirupati) as the "one fast that covers them all" for people
    who cannot keep every monthly vrata. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirjala_Ekadashi)
- **Yogini Ēkādaśī** — Jyēṣṭha Kṛṣṇa. Purāṇic story of a leprous servant of Kubēra cured by this
  vrata.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Yoginī Ēkādaśī** (Āṣāḍha
    Kṛṣṇa) exactly, with the same Purāṇic story (in the Skanda/Padma Purāṇa) of Hema, a leprous
    gardener/servant of Kubēra, cured through this vrata's merit. No regional divergence in naming.
    (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Hari Śayana Ēkādaśī ("Tholi Ēkādaśī")** — Āṣāḍha Śukla. Nārāyaṇa enters yōga-nidrā; opens the
  four-month Cāturmāsya Vratam.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* This is the other pilgrimage-scale, truly pan-India Ēkādaśī.
    Standard name is **Śayanī / Devśayanī Ēkādaśī** (Āṣāḍha Śukla), marking Viṣṇu's entry into
    yoga-nidrā and the start of Cāturmāsya — the "Hari Śayana" form used here is simply the
    Sanskrit-descriptive variant of the same name. Its single largest regional expression is
    **Āṣāḍhī Ēkādaśī at Pandharpur, Maharashtra**: the culmination of the centuries-old Vārkarī
    "Wari" pilgrimage (18–21 days on foot from Dehu/Ālandī), where lakhs of pilgrims converge on
    the Viṭṭhala (Viṭhobā) temple. "Tholi Ēkādaśī" ("first Ēkādaśī") is the specific Telugu/Andhra
    regional name for this same day, current across Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, paralleling how
    Maharashtra calls it Āṣāḍhī and North India calls it Devśayanī/Harishayani — all are regional
    names for the identical pan-India tithi and festival.
    (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shayani_Ekadashi ;
    https://www.stayvista.com/blog/ashadhi-ekadashi-2026-date-pandharpur-wari-route-maharashtras-largest-pilgrimage-explained/)
- **Kāmika Ēkādaśī** — Āṣāḍha Kṛṣṇa. Fasting/tulasī worship of Viṣṇu, per the Brahma Vaivarta
  Purāṇa, equal to bathing in all sacred rivers.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Kāmikā Ēkādaśī** (Śrāvaṇa
    Kṛṣṇa) exactly, with the same Brahma Vaivarta Purāṇa framing (fasting and tulasī-leaf worship
    of Viṣṇu equal in merit to bathing in all sacred rivers) found in nationwide Vaiṣṇava sources.
    (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Putraikādaśi** — Śrāvaṇa Śukla. Worship of Viṣṇu as "Śrīdhara"; umbrella charity is
  prescribed.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* The source's Śrāvaṇa Śukla slot matches the standard pan-India
    **Śrāvaṇa Putradā Ēkādaśī**, distinct from the separate Pauṣa Putradā Ēkādaśī (see the
    Vaikuṇṭha entry above) that some North Indian calendars observe instead/also — both share the
    "Putradā" (child-granting) name and Śrīdhara-form worship nationwide; umbrella charity is a
    widely attested, not narrowly regional, custom for this vrata.
    (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shravana_Putrada_Ekadashi)
- **Aja Ēkādaśi** — Śrāvaṇa Kṛṣṇa. Worship of Nārāyaṇa as "Janārdana."
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Ajā Ēkādaśī** exactly.
    Under the amanta convention the source labels it Śrāvaṇa Kṛṣṇa, which corresponds to
    Bhādrapada Kṛṣṇa in the pūrṇimānta system used by most pan-India lists — this is the expected
    calendar-convention shift described above, not a different festival; the Nārāyaṇa-as-Janārdana
    worship described matches standard pan-India practice.
    (Source: https://www.iskcondelhi.com/full-year-ekadashi-list/)
- **Parivartana/Dāna Ēkādaśī** — Bhādrapada Śukla. The central day of Cāturmāsya, when Nārāyaṇa
  is said to turn onto his other side.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Parivartinī / Vāmana
    Ēkādaśī** (Bhādrapada Śukla) exactly — nationally known as the day Viṣṇu, resting through
    Cāturmāsya, is said to turn onto his other side, and is widely called Vāmana Ēkādaśī after the
    associated avatāra story and Dāna (charity) Ēkādaśī for its prescribed almsgiving, both
    attested pan-India, not regionally distinct, names. (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Padminī/Paramā Ēkādaśī** — falls only in Adhika (leap) months, irregular. Ēkādaśīs occurring
  during an intercalary month, observed as especially fruitful.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India understanding of the
    intercalary-month Ēkādaśīs exactly: **Padminī Ēkādaśī** (Śukla pakṣa of Adhika Māsa) and
    **Paramā Ēkādaśī** (Kṛṣṇa pakṣa of Adhika Māsa) are the recognized pan-India names for the
    "extra" Ēkādaśīs that occur only in leap lunar months (roughly every 2–3 years), held to be
    especially fruitful/rare everywhere they are observed, not a South Indian-specific pairing.
    (Source: https://hinduismfacts.org/names-of-26-ekadashi-and-their-significance/)

## 12. Dvādaśī

- **Gōvatsa Dvādaśī (Padmanābha Dvādaśī)** — Āśvayuja Śukla/Kṛṣṇa, annual, often paired with
  Dhana Trayōdaśī. Cow-and-calf worship, Anantha Padmanābha vratam.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* In Maharashtra this same day is called **Vasu Baras** and doubles
    as the opening day of the Diwali cluster; in Gujarat it is **Vagh Baras** (also glossed as
    "repayment of debts"), and similar cow-worship days elsewhere are called **Bachh Baras**. The
    core ritual — worship of a cow and calf — is shared, but in the north/west it is explicitly the
    first of the five Diwali days, while the Telugu source treats it as a standalone Dvādaśī vrata
    rather than a Diwali-opener. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govatsa_Dwadashi)
- **Kṣīrābdhi Dvādaśī (Haribōdhini/Tulasī Kalyāṇam)** — Kārtika Śukla, annual, major. Commemorates
  the churning of the milky ocean; ritual marriage of Viṣṇu and Tulasī.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India as **Tulsi Vivah**, observed right after Dev Uthani/
    Prabodhini Ekādaśī. In Uttar Pradesh and Rajasthan households erect a full wedding maṇḍapa for
    the Tulasī plant, treating it as the ceremonial start of the north Indian wedding season — a
    scale of domestic ritual theater not emphasized in the Telugu tradition's framing of it as
    Kṣīrābdhi Dvādaśī (churning of the ocean) plus the Viṣṇu-Tulasī wedding. In Karnataka it is
    called **Tulasi Habba**; in Odisha/elsewhere also **Uttana Dwadashi**.
    (Source: https://imvoyager.com/tulasi-vivaha-tulsi-pooja-uttana-dwadashi/ ;
    https://zeenews.india.com/culture/tulsi-vivah-2025-check-date-time-tithi-puja-vidhi-muhurat-and-regional-significance-of-this-auspicious-hindu-festival-2977239.html)
- **Matsya Dvādaśī (Vāsudēva Dvādaśī)** — Mārgaśira Śukla, annual. Commemorates Viṣṇu's Matsya
  avatāra.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A relatively minor, pan-Vaiṣṇava observance without a distinct
    regional name found; observed similarly (fasting, Viṣṇu-sahasranāma/Matsya Purāṇa recitation)
    in Vaiṣṇava/ISKCON circles across regions rather than being tied to one region's folk calendar.
    (Source: https://hindugallery.com/matsya-dwadashi/)
- **Varāha Dwādaśī (Bhima/Tila Dwādaśī)** — Māgha, annual. Viṣṇu worshipped as Varāha; sesame
  bathing/lamps (sesame said to have been created this day).
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Also widely called **Bhīṣma Dvādaśī** or **Amalakī Dvādaśī** in
    pan-India sources, with sesame (til) offerings and tarpaṇa to Bhīṣma Pitāmaha as the common
    thread nationwide — the Varāha-avatāra framing used in the Telugu source is one regional
    emphasis among several names for what is largely the same Māgha Śukla Dvādaśī observance.
    (Source: https://www.hindu-blog.com/2009/01/bhishma-dwadasi-bheeshma-dwadashi.html)
- **Gōvinda Dvādaśī (Narasimha Dvādaśī)** — Phālguṇa Śukla, annual. Closes Payō Vratam; Nārāyaṇa
  worshipped as "Govinda," Narasimha in the evening.
- **Vāmana Dvādaśī** — Caitra Śukla, annual. Worship of Vāmana bestows the merit of charity and
  supreme knowledge.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Chiefly a Vaiṣṇava vrata day; one notable regional expression is
    a State-level **Vaman Dwadashi Fair** held annually at Sarahan (Pachhad), Himachal Pradesh — a
    purely local mela rather than a pan-India festival. Elsewhere it is a quiet home/temple vrata.
    (Source: https://utsav.gov.in/view-event/vaman-dwadashi-fair-sarahan-pachhad-1)
- **Paraśurāma Dvādaśī** — Vaiśākha, annual. Worshipped with ṣōḍaśōpacāras.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Also called **Rukmiṇī Dvādaśī** in some sources; a modest,
    pan-Vaiṣṇava fasting/worship day without strong regional variation reported.
    (Source: https://www.drikpanchang.com/dwadashi/parashurama/parashurama-dwadashi-date-time.html)
- **Rāma Lakṣmaṇa Dwādaśi (Champaka Dwādaśi, Gāyatrīdēvi Jayanti)** — Jyēṣṭha, annual.
  Commemorates Ādi Śaṅkarācārya's ascent to Kailāsa alongside Rāma-Lakṣmaṇa worship.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* "Champaka Dvādaśī" as Ādi Śaṅkarācārya's Kailāsa-gamana day is a
    specifically Advaita/Śaṅkara-maṭha observance found across India wherever Śaṅkara maṭhas exist
    (Kanchi, Sringeri, Dwaraka, Puri, Jyotirmath), not confined to Andhra; some calendars instead
    place Gāyatrī Jayanti on Jyēṣṭha Śukla Ēkādaśī or on Śrāvaṇa Pūrṇimā, so its date varies by
    tradition. (Source: https://hindupad.com/sri-shankaracharya-kailasa-gamanam-on-champak-dwadashi/)
- **Vāsudēva Dwādaśī** — Āṣāḍha Śukla, annual. Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa worshipped as "Vāsudēva."
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Also called Śrī Kṛṣṇa Dvādaśī; observed at ISKCON/Kṛṣṇa temples
    nationally (noted especially in Pune, Mumbai, Nashik) with bhajans, kīrtans and go-sēvā — a
    pan-Vaiṣṇava rather than specifically Telugu observance.
    (Source: https://www.sanatanavibes.in/2025/07/vasudeva-dwadashi.html)
- **Damōdara Dwādaśi** — Śrāvaṇa, annual. Viṣṇu worshipped as "Damōdara."
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Overlaps with the broader **Kārtika/Dāmodara vrata** (daily
    lamp-lighting at Tulasī and in temples through Kārtika) observed pan-India in Vaiṣṇava
    households; in Odisha the same Kārtika Śukla Dvādaśī is separately known as **Garuḍa Dvādaśī**.
    (Source: https://mayapurvoice.com/svagatam/famous-damodar-month-kartik-vrata-back/)
- **Vāmana Jayanti** — Bhādrapada Śukla, annual, when this Dvādaśī coincides with Śravaṇa
  nakṣatra. Marks Viṣṇu's Vāmana incarnation.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A pan-India Vaiṣṇava jayanti tied to Bhādrapada Śukla Dvādaśī
    coinciding with Śravaṇa nakṣatra; celebrated with Pañcopacāra/Ṣōḍaśōpacāra pūjā of a Vāmana
    mūrti wherever Vaiṣṇava temples mark daśāvatāra days, without a distinct separate regional name
    found. (Source: https://www.freepressjournal.in/lifestyle/vamana-jayanti-2025-know-date-rituals-significance-auspicious-time)

## 13. Trayōdaśī

- **Dhana Trayōdaśī** — Āśvayuja Kṛṣṇa, annual, part of the Dīpāvaḷi cluster. Dhanvantari and
  Kubēra-Lakṣmī worshipped for health and wealth.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Nationally known as **Dhanteras/Dhantrayodashi**, the opening day
    of the five-day Diwali cluster almost everywhere in India. Regional emphasis differs sharply:
    North and West India (UP, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra) foreground Lakṣmī-Kubēra wealth
    worship and buying new utensils/gold; the Telugu/South Indian observance foregrounds
    **Dhanvantari** and health/Āyurveda themes more than wealth. In Karnataka it is
    Dhantrayodashi/Dhanatrayodashi and in Kerala **Dhanatrisha**; Karnataka also calls the day
    "neeru tumbo habba" (filling water).
    (Source: https://indiablog.in/dhanteras ; https://trimbakeshwartemplepujari.com/dhanteras/)
- **Hanumadvrataṁ** — Mārgaśira Śukla, annual. Hanumān worshipped with sixteen upacāras; also
  marks the start of Dhanurmāsa/Gōdā Dēvī's Tiruppāvai recitation.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Chiefly a South Indian Vaiṣṇava-Śaiva-adjacent vrata opening
    Dhanurmāsam, but Hanumān worship on this day is also recorded at northern shrines like
    Sankaṭamōcana temple (Varanasi) and Jākhū temple (Shimla) — so while the Dhanurmāsa/Goda-Dēvī
    Tiruppāvai framing is distinctly South Indian (Tamil/Telugu Vaiṣṇava), Hanumān-specific worship
    that day is not unknown in the north.
    (Source: https://hindupad.com/hanuman-vrat-hanuman-vratam/)
- **Anaṅga Trayōdaśi (Damanōtsavaṁ)** — Caitra Śukla, annual. Manmatha (Kāma) worshipped with
  Damana leaves for fulfilment of desires.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Widely observed but on different dates/months by region —
    Gujarati and Marathi calendars place **Anang Trayodashi** in Chaitra (matching the Telugu
    source), while many North Indian calendars observe it in Mārgaśīrṣa instead. Kashmir has a
    distinct cognate, **Madan Trayodashi**, also dedicated to Kāmadēva, on Chaitra Śukla Trayodaśī.
    Rituals (Śiva + Kāmadēva/Rati worship) are broadly similar across regions.
    (Source: https://astrodevam.com/festivals-of-india-anang-trayodashi-vrat.html ;
    http://autarmota.blogspot.com/2019/01/kaamdeva-in-kashmiri-art-and-poetry.html)
- **Vaṭa Sāvitri Vratam** — Jyēṣṭha Trayōdaśī–Pūrṇimā, annual. Commemorates Sāvitrī winning back
  her husband Satyavān's life from Yama; banyan tree worshipped.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A pan-India vrata for married women's marital longevity, but the
    date itself differs by region — Maharashtra, Gujarat and Karnataka observe it on Jyēṣṭha
    **Pūrṇimā**, while North Indian states (Bihar, UP) and Odisha observe it on Jyēṣṭha
    **Amāvāsyā**; the Telugu source's placement spanning Trayōdaśī–Pūrṇimā reflects yet a third
    regional convention. The core ritual — fasting, circumambulating a banyan (vaṭa) tree, tying a
    thread around it, and reciting the Sāvitrī-Satyavān kathā — is consistent everywhere it is
    observed (Odisha, Bihar, UP, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Nepal).
    (Source: https://dharmikapp.in/blogs/vat-savitri/vat-savitri-vrat-2026-date-muhurat-katha-puja-vidhi-regional-differences)
- **Śanitrayōdaśi** — recurs whenever Trayōdaśī is combined with Śiva-pradōṣa worship for Śani
  (Saturn) relief; treated in the source as a recurring auspicious combination rather than one
  fixed annual festival.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Corresponds to the pan-India **Śani Pradōṣam** — Trayōdaśī
    pradōṣa falling on a Saturday, considered especially powerful for pacifying Śani since Śiva is
    regarded as Śani's guru. Observed the same way nationally (evening Śiva worship at
    pradōṣa-kāl); timing depends only on local sunset, not on regional custom.
    (Source: https://mahakal.com/blog/en/shani-trayodashi-and-shani-pradosh-vrat-2026-significance-puja-vidhi-katha-and-benefits)
- **Damanakārōpaṇa (Damanotsava, Damana Pūja)** — Caitra Śukla Trayōdaśī and Caturdaśī, annual.
  The damanaka plant (*Artemisia*) is planted, then gathered and offered to the deity; a spring
  rite, offered variously to Viṣṇu, to Śiva with Ekavīra and Bhairava, and to Gaṇapati.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Regional and strongly temple-centred rather than domestic. It is
    most prominent in Odisha, where the damanaka is one of the two flowers particularly associated
    with Lord Jagannātha and the leaves are offered at Purī on the Caturdaśī; the Gauḍīya
    tradition keeps a related Damanaka-rōpaṇa. The rite is prescribed in the Dharma Sindhu, so it
    is classical rather than modern, but it survives as a living household observance only
    patchily, which is why the Telugu calendar lists it without detail.
    (Source: https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/dharmasindhu/bookview.php?chapnum=6)

## 14. Caturdaśī

- **Mahāśivarātri** — Māgha/Phālguṇa Kṛṣṇa Caturdaśī, annual, the year's single most important
  Śiva festival. Marks Paramaśiva's midnight manifestation as the Liṅga; vrata of fasting,
  jāgaraṇa (staying awake) and worship.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* India's single most widely observed Śiva festival, with major
    regional variation in flavor. Varanasi's Kāśī Viśvanātha temple and Ujjain's Mahākāleśwar
    temple (which extends it into a nine-day **Śiva Navrātri**, dressing the liṅga in a bridal
    sēhrā) anchor the grandest North Indian celebrations; Himachal's Mandi hosts an eight-day royal
    Śivarātri fair at the Bhūtnāth temple; Assam centers on the Umānanda temple in Guwahati; Bengal
    observes night-long fasting, meditation and bhajans; Andhra Pradesh's major centers are
    Śrīkāḷahasti and Śrīśailam (Bhramarāmbā-Mallikārjuna). The shared core everywhere is fasting,
    night-long jāgaraṇa, and liṅga abhiṣēkam.
    (Source: https://www.outlookindia.com/brand-studio/unique-mahashivratri-rituals-practised-across-different-regions-of-india)
- **Naraka Chaturdaśī** — Āśvayuja Kṛṣṇa Caturdaśī, annual, part of Dīpāvaḷi. Marks the killing of
  the demon Naraka and removal of hell's torments; head bath and tarpaṇa to Yama.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* One of the most regionally divergent Diwali-cluster days. In
    Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and Karnataka, Naraka Chaturdaśī (with pre-dawn oil bath,
    Abhyaṅga Snān) is treated as the **principal** Diwali day, ahead of Lakṣmī Pūjā. In North India
    it is demoted to "**Choṭī Dīpāvaḷī**," a lead-in to the main Lakṣmī-Pūjā night. In Gujarat,
    Rajasthan and Maharashtra it is called **Kālī Chaudas**, also linked to harvest themes. So the
    same tithi is the climax of Diwali in the south but a minor prelude day in the north/west.
    (Source: https://theindianbugle.com/naraka-chaturdashi-2025-date-significance-rituals-choti-diwali/)
- **Vaikuṇṭha Caturdaśī** — Kārtika Śukla Caturdaśī, annual. Worship of Viṣṇu as Padmanābha;
  bathing at Maṇikarṇika Ghāṭ and Śiva abhiṣēkam both prescribed.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Observed with real regional spread — prominently in Maharashtra,
    and at Gayā, Varanasi and Rishikesh, where the Manikarṇikā Ghāṭ bathing/Śiva-Viṣṇu union myth
    is central; in Varanasi it precedes Dēv Dīpāvaḷī. It is not, however, a truly pan-India
    household festival — it functions more as a pilgrimage-town/temple-town observance than a
    domestic vrata everywhere.
    (Source: https://www.oneindia.com/lifestyle/vaikuntha-chaturdashi-hindu-festival-significance-011-3986673.html)
- **Anantapadmanābha Svāmi Vratam (Ananta Caturdaśī)** — Bhādrapada Śukla Caturdaśī, annual.
  Worship of Anantapadmanābha reclining on the serpent-bed with Mahālakṣmī; a 24-knot thread
  is tied.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India as **Anant Chaturdashi**, with the 14-knot Ananta-sūtra
    thread ritual common everywhere, but its cultural weight differs sharply by region — in
    Maharashtra it is inseparable from **Ganeśa Visarjan**, the climactic immersion day closing the
    ten-day Gaṇēśotsava, giving it a very different public character (massive immersion
    processions) than in Gujarat/Rajasthan, where it stays a quieter home Anant-Viṣṇu-pūjā day,
    closer to the Telugu source's framing.
    (Source: https://www.easeindiatrip.com/blog/anant-chaturdashi-puja-rituals-celebration/)
- **Śaivadamana Caturdaśī** — Caitra Śukla Caturdaśī, annual. Śiva worshipped with Damana leaves
  at pradōṣa time.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A narrowly regional/technical observance (Damana-leaf Śiva pūjā
    at pradōṣa on Chaitra Śukla Caturdaśī); no distinct name or practice found outside South Indian
    ritual manuals — appears to be regional/temple-specific rather than pan-India.
    (Source: https://kamakoti.org/kamakoti/articles/ESSENCE%20OF%20HINDU%20FESTIVALS%20AND%20AUSTERITIES.pdf)
- **Kṛṣṇāṅgāraka Caturdaśī** — recurs whenever Kṛṣṇa-pakṣa Caturdaśī falls on a Tuesday (an
  alabhya-yōga combination, treated each occurrence as auspicious rather than as one fixed
  yearly festival, though it is frequently the occasion chosen for that month's Māsa Śivarātri).
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A recurring "tithi + weekday" alabhya-yōga (Kṛṣṇa-pakṣa
    Caturdaśī falling on a Tuesday) treated as auspicious for Yama-tarpaṇa and ancestor rites;
    found across panchāng traditions nationally as a special combination rather than a named
    regional festival. (Source: https://www.hindu-blog.com/2014/01/chaturdashi-on-tuesday-fourteenth-day.html)

- **Damanaka Caturdaśī** — Caitra Śukla Caturdaśī. Second and principal day of the
  Damanakārōpaṇa rite that opens on the Trayōdaśī — see Section 13.

*(Monthly Māsa Śivarātri and Pradōṣam — see Notes below.)*

## 15. Pūrṇimā (Śukla-pakṣa end) / Amāvāsyā (Kṛṣṇa-pakṣa end)

**Pūrṇimā:**
- **Guru/Vyāsa Pūrṇimā** — Āṣāḍha Pūrṇimā, annual, major. The principal day for honoring one's
  Guru, tracing the Guru-paramparā back to Vyāsa.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A pan-India (and pan-Nepal/Bhutan) festival honoring one's guru,
    observed by Hindus, Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists alike. Major regional centers include Ujjain and
    Varanasi (large Vyāsa Pūjā ceremonies), the Vyāsa temple in Varanasi, Bēlūr Maṭh in West Bengal
    (honoring Ramakrishna and the Ramakrishna-Vivekananda lineage), and Śikharjī in Jharkhand (a
    major Jain pilgrimage site). South Indian ashrams and maṭhas mark it with satsaṅgs, meditation
    and community meals rather than large public processions — so while the name and date (Āṣāḍha
    Pūrṇimā) are uniform nationally, the scale and form of celebration vary considerably by region
    and lineage. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Purnima ;
    https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/festivals/guru-purnima/)
- **Jandhyala Purnima (Rakṣābandhan)** — Śrāvaṇa Pūrṇimā, annual, major. Sacred-thread renewal
  and Veda study for the twice-born; siblings tie the rakhi thread.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* This is one of the sharpest North-vs-South contrasts in the whole
    calendar. In the Hindi belt and much of North/West India, this day is **Rakṣābandhan**, a
    sibling festival where sisters tie a rākhī thread on brothers' wrists. In Andhra Pradesh and
    Telangana it is **Jandhyala Pūrṇima**, and in Tamil Nadu it is **Avaṇi Aviṭṭam** (also called
    Upākarma elsewhere in the South) — in both southern forms the central act is Brahmin men
    discarding their old sacred thread (yajñōpavītam) and donning a new one, with Vedic
    recitation/atonement rites, not sibling bonding. Both observances share the same tithi (Śrāvaṇa
    Pūrṇimā) but are otherwise distinct rituals with different participants and meanings.
    (Source: https://hindupad.com/jandhyala-purnima-or-janai-poornima-ritual-of-changing-sacred-thread-on-shravan-poornima/ ;
    https://www.omspiritualshop.com/blogs/news/shravana-purnima-2026)
- **Kārtika Pūrṇimā (Mahākārtīki, Kṛttikā Dīpōtsavam)** — Kārtika Pūrṇimā, annual, major. Usirika
  Puja, charity and extensive lamp-lighting; especially meritorious when Pūrṇimā spans sunrise.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Widely observed with regional forms — as **Karthika Deepam** it
    is the most important festival of the month in Andhra/Tamil Nadu (matching the source's
    framing); in Varanasi the same night is **Dēv Dīpāvaḷī**, when ghats are lined with lamps for
    descending deities; in Rajasthan, Kārtika Pūrṇimā is when the **Puṣkar Mela** (Asia's largest
    camel fair) concludes with a holy bath in Puṣkar Lake. So the tithi is pan-India but its public
    expression differs completely by region. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dev_Deepavali_(Varanasi))
- **Kaumudī / Śarat Pūrṇimā (with Vālmīki Jayanti)** — Āśvayuja Pūrṇimā, annual. Kōjāgarī vratam
  to Lakṣmī (rice pudding, staying awake, dice-play); also Vālmīki's birth anniversary.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Called **Kōjāgarī Pūrṇimā** and observed most prominently in
    Bengal, Odisha and Maharashtra with all-night Lakṣmī worship, fasting and staying awake ("kō
    jāgarti" — who is awake); in Braj/Vrindavan the same full moon is instead **Rās Pūrṇimā**,
    commemorating Kṛṣṇa's Mahā-Rās with the gopīs — a distinct devotional emphasis from the
    Lakṣmī-vrata framing used elsewhere, including the Telugu source. Vālmīki Jayanti is layered
    onto the same day nationally. (Source: https://www.sanatanavibes.in/2025/09/kojagiri-puja-worship-of-goddess.html)
- **Holikā Pūrṇimā (Kāmadahanam, Dōlōtsavam)** — Phālguṇa Pūrṇimā, annual, major. Holika-bonfire
  ritual, Bāla-Kṛṣṇa cradle worship, and (in many South Indian temples) a Śiva-Pārvatī Kalyāṇam.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* This shows the sharpest North-vs-South contrast in this list. In the Braj
    region (Mathura, Vrindavan, Barsana, Nandgaon, Gokul) Holi extends into an elaborate 10-day
    festival, including Barsana's famous **Lāṭhmār Holi** (women ceremonially beat men with sticks,
    re-enacting Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa play) and flower-Holi at the Bāṅke Bihārī temple — a boisterous,
    colour-and-water-soaked public festival. South India's version, **Kāmadahanam**, is quieter and
    more ritual-focused: the symbolic burning of Kāmadēva (Manmatha) takes precedence over
    colour-play, with Śiva-Murugan temple processions and fire rituals as the centerpiece (matching
    the source's Bāla-Kṛṣṇa-cradle-worship-plus-Śiva-Pārvatī-Kalyāṇam framing); Kerala's
    Palakkad/Thrissur area has its own turmeric-water variant, **Mañjaḷ Kuḷi**.
    (Source: https://www.outlooktraveller.com/destinations/india/where-holi-lasts-10-days-the-ultimate-guide-to-celebrating-in-braj ;
    https://organiser.org/2026/03/03/342583/bharat/how-holi-is-celebrated-from-north-to-south-india-a-festival-of-colours-culture-and-unity/)
- **Chaitra (Madana) Pūrṇima (Hanumadvijayōtsavaṁ / Hanuman Jayanti)** — Caitra Pūrṇimā, annual,
  major. Held to accumulate the whole month's merit; also Hanumān's principal jayanti in this
  tradition.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Hanumān Jayantī shows a genuine regional calendar divergence,
    not just a difference in naming. North India marks it on **Chaitra Pūrṇimā** (matching
    this source). Andhra Pradesh and Telangana observe one of the longest versions in the country:
    a 41-day festival beginning on Chaitra Pūrṇimā and running to about mid-Vaiśākha. But Tamil
    Nadu and Kerala instead celebrate Hanumān's birth in the **Mārgaśīrṣa** month, often on an
    amāvāsyā, typically landing in December/January; Karnataka likewise observes it in Mārgaśīrṣa
    as "Hanuman Vratam." The differing dates trace to different regional readings of the
    Rāmāyaṇa/Purāṇic sources for Hanumān's birth, and both traditions are considered equally valid
    within their regions.
    (Source: https://www.omspiritualshop.com/blogs/news/hanuman-jayanthi-2026-dates-significance-celebrations-everything-you-need-to-know-across-india)
- **Māgha Pūrṇima (Maha Maghi)** — Māgha Pūrṇimā, annual. Bathing, japa, pārāyaṇa and charity;
  Satyanārāyaṇa Vratam especially recommended.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Observed nationally with holy bathing, but regional focus
    differs — North India (UP, Bihar) centers on the **Māgha Mēḷā** at Prayāgrāj and Gaṅgā-ghāṭ
    ārtīs in Varanasi; Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh mark it with temple festivals and abhiṣēkam at
    sites like Arunāchalēśwara temple, closer to the source's Satyanārāyaṇa-vrata framing.
    (Source: https://www.treebo.com/blog/magha-purnima/)
- **Umāmahēśvara Vratam / Mahālaya Pakṣa Prārambham** — Bhādrapada Pūrṇimā, annual. Opens the
  15-day Mahālaya Pitṛupakṣa for ancestor rites, alongside Umā-Maheśvara worship.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* The vrata's exact date varies by regional calendar — Kannada
    tradition places it on Bhādrapada Pūrṇimā (matching the source), the Tamil calendar places the
    equivalent in the Puraṭṭāci month, and some communities observe it on Kārtikai Pūrṇimā instead.
    The Mahālaya-Pitṛpakṣa opening tied to the same day is broadly pan-India, though in Bengal
    Mahālaya is understood chiefly through its Amāvāsyā end-point (Durgā Pūjā countdown) rather
    than this Pūrṇimā start-point. (Source: https://madhwasakha.com/ritual/uma-maheshwara-vrata/832)
- **Mahā Vaiśākhi (Kūrma Jayanti)** — Vaiśākha Pūrṇimā, annual. Commemorates Viṣṇu's Kūrma
  (tortoise) avatāra; Ardhanārīśvara and Sampadgaurī vratas also observed.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Vaiśākha Pūrṇimā is doubly significant nationally — for Hindus it
    is Kūrma Jayanti/Satyanārāyaṇa Pūjā day, while the same date is **Buddha Pūrṇimā/Vesak**,
    marking the Buddha's birth, enlightenment and death, observed heavily in Maharashtra (home to
    the majority of India's Buddhist population) and across South and Southeast Asia (with some
    countries observing Vesak on a different Gregorian date). Many Puranic lists count the Buddha
    as a Viṣṇu avatāra, which is why Hindu and Buddhist observances often overlap at the same
    temples on this day.
    (Source: https://svastika.in/blogs/indian-festivals/why-celebrate-buddha-purnima-when-gautama-buddha-born-died)
- **Vaṭa Sāvitri / Jyēṣṭi Ēruvāka Pūrṇima** — Jyēṣṭha Pūrṇimā, annual. Alternate/closing day for
  the Vaṭa Sāvitri vrata; special abhiṣēkams at Tirupati and Puri Jagannātha.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* See the Vaṭa Sāvitri entry under Trayōdaśī above — Maharashtra,
    Gujarat and Karnataka treat Jyēṣṭha Pūrṇimā (not Amāvāsyā) as the primary day for this vrata,
    which aligns with the source's alternate/closing-day framing and its note of special
    abhiṣēkams at Tirupati and Puri.
    (Source: https://dharmikapp.in/blogs/vat-savitri/vat-savitri-vrat-2026-date-muhurat-katha-puja-vidhi-regional-differences)
- **Dattātrēya Jayanti** — Mārgaśira Pūrṇimā, annual. Marks Dattātrēya's manifestation from the
  tapas of Atri and Anasūya.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A genuinely multi-regional festival, celebrated with particular
    fervor in Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Gujarat — all host major Dattātrēya
    pīṭhas and temples, with regional variation mainly in local temple-festival style rather than
    in the core date or ritual (Mārgaśīrṣa Pūrṇimā, worship of the child form of Dattātrēya).
    (Source: https://www.sanatanavibes.in/2025/07/dattatreya-jayanti-2025.html)

**Amāvāsyā:**
- **Dīpāvaḷi (Naraka Chaturdaśī's Amāvāsyā)** — Āśvayuja Amāvāsyā, annual, major. Head bath, lamp
  and Lakṣmī worship at evening pradōṣa; fireworks; Kāḷī worship also traditional in some
  regions.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* The main Lakṣmī-Pūjā night is pan-India but regionally distinct
    in character. West Bengal instead centers this night on **Kālī Pūjā** — worship of the fierce
    goddess Kālī at midnight rather than Lakṣmī — a genuinely different deity-focus, not just a
    variant ritual. Gujarat treats the day as the start of the business new year (**Chopdā Pūjā**,
    closing/opening account ledgers) and calls the wider festival Bēśtu Varas-adjacent traditions;
    North India centers the story on Rāma's return to Ayōdhya. The following days also vary
    regionally — **Govardhan Pūjā/Annakūṭ** the next day is a major North Indian (especially
    Braj-area) observance largely absent from the South, and **Bhāī Dūj** (siblings) closes the
    cluster in the north while the Telugu cluster does not foreground either.
    (Source: https://swadharma.in/kali-puja-vs-lakshmi-puja-why-diwali/ ;
    https://www.newindiaabroad.com/english/news/unique-traditions-of-diwali-celebrations-across-india)
- **Mahālaya / Pōlāla Amāvāsya** — Bhādrapada Amāvāsyā, annual. Closing day of Mahālaya
  Pitṛpakṣa; tarpaṇa offered to ancestors, and "Pōlēramma" (a unified Mahākāḷi-Lakṣmi-Sarasvatī
  form) worshipped in some families.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* The Amāvāsyā closing Pitṛpakṣa is observed widely, especially in
    West Bengal, Assam, Odisha and Karnataka, as **Sarvapitrī/Mahālaya Amāvāsyā**, with tarpaṇa
    offered to ancestors. Bengal's regional distinctive is that Mahālaya is primarily experienced
    as the *Durgā Pūjā countdown* — via the iconic 1931 All India Radio broadcast "Mahiṣāsura
    Mardini" — rather than as an ancestor-rites day, a very different emotional register from the
    Telugu source's Pōlēramma/tarpaṇa framing. No separate source specifically corroborates the
    Telugu "Pōlēramma" (unified Mahākāḷi-Lakṣmi-Sarasvatī) name — this appears to be a local Andhra
    family tradition rather than a widely attested pan-regional name.
    (Source: https://www.dwsjewellery.com/blog/mahalaya-amavasya-the-spiritual-prelude-to-durga-puja/)
- **Chollaṅgi / Mauni Amāvāsya** — Puṣya Amāvāsyā, annual. Bathing at holy tīrthas, celebrated
  as a major local festival at Chollangi near Kakinada (Gōdāvari-sea confluence).
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* The Andhra Pradesh name is **Chollaṅgi Amāvāsya**, tied
    specifically to the village of Chollangi (East Godavari district) where the Godāvari meets the
    sea; this same Puṣya-māsa Amāvāsyā is known pan-India, especially in North India, as **Mauni
    Amāvāsyā** (associated with the Prayāgrāj Māgha Mēḷā/Kumbh bathing and vows of silence). So
    this is one tithi with a genuinely local South Indian pilgrimage identity (Chollangi) layered
    under a much larger pan-India name and observance (Mauni Amāvāsyā).
    (Source: https://hindupad.com/chollangi-amavasya-2012-pushya-masa-amavasya-in-andhra-pradesh/)
- **Kotta Amāvāsya (last Amāvāsya before Ugādi)** — Phālguṇa/Caitra Amāvāsyā, annual. Śrāddha and
  tarpaṇa to ancestors; worship of the Divine Mother and village deities.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Specifically an Andhra Pradesh/Telangana observance (also linked
    to the Nookalamma temple), directly tied to the Telugu New Year (Ugādi) that follows the next
    day — this is a regional/Telugu-calendar-specific festival, not one with a distinct pan-India
    equivalent name, though ancestor-worship amāvāsyās generally are pan-India in form.
    (Source: https://hindupad.com/amavasya-30-march-2014-ugadi-amavasya-gudi-padwa-amavasya/)
- **Chukkala Amāvāsya** — Āṣāḍha Amāvāsyā, annual. Marks the transition into Dakṣiṇāyana; Lakṣmī
  worship and extra lamp-lighting.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A Telugu-specific name (Āṣāḍha Amāvāsyā/Gauri Vratam) for what
    Karnataka calls **Bhīmana Amāvāsya** (also Jyōti Bhīmēśvara Vrata / Pati Sañjīvinī Vrata) and
    Maharashtra calls **Gatari Amāvāsya**. All three are the same tithi with the same broad theme —
    women's vrata for marital wellbeing, worship of Gaurī/Pārvatī — showing three regional names
    for one South-Indian-belt observance rather than a truly pan-India festival.
    (Source: https://www.hindu-blog.com/2009/07/chukkala-amavasi-gauri-vratam-on-ashada.html)
- **Sōmavatī Amāvāsya** — recurs whenever Amāvāsyā falls on a Monday; treated each time as a
  special combination (Śani/Śiva propitiation) rather than one fixed yearly date.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A pan-India recurring combination (Amāvāsyā falling on a
    Monday), observed similarly nationwide with river bathing, Śiva/Peepal-tree worship, and
    Pitṛ-tarpaṇa; particularly emphasized with large-scale gatherings in North India (UP, Bihar)
    along the Ganga/Yamuna, but the underlying practice is consistent across regions rather than
    sharply divergent.
    (Source: https://www.theindianpanorama.news/somvati-amavasya-the-sacred-confluence-of-devotion-karma-and-divine-blessings/)
- **Kēdāra Gaurī Vratam / Kēdārēśvara Vratam (Dīpāvali Nōmbu)** — concludes on Āśvayuja
  Amāvāsya, the day of Dīpāvali, annual. A vratam to Śiva as Kēdārēśvara with Gaurī, kept for
  twenty-one days from Āśvayuja Śukla Aṣṭamī, or for any odd number of days, and now very
  commonly kept on the Amāvāsya alone. A kalaśa stands for Kēdārēśvara, and a toram of
  twenty-one threads with twenty-one knots is tied.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Regional, and kept chiefly in the Telugu districts and Tamil
    Nadu, where it is one of the principal Dīpāvali-day observances for women. The North Indian
    Dīpāvali of the same night is Lakṣmī Pūja, a different rite to a different deity, so the two
    traditions keep the same night quite differently. The compression of a twenty-one-day vratam
    into a single day is a modern change and is worth recording as such.
    (Source: https://shaivam.org/hindu-shaivaite-festivals-and-vratas/significance-of-kedara-gauri-vrata-deepavali/)
- **Piṭhōrī Amāvāsya** — Śrāvaṇa Amāvāsya in the pūrṇimānta reckoning, the same night the Telugu
  calendar calls Bhādrapada Bahuḷa Amāvāsya, annual. A mothers' vratam for the wellbeing of
  children, with worship of sixty-four Yōginīs.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Western Indian, chiefly Maharashtra, and **not** the same
    observance as the Telugu Pōlāla Amāvāsya kept on that night, although the two share the tithi
    and the purpose. Piṭhōrī has its own Sanskrit kathā attributed to the Skanda Purāṇa and its
    own Marathi vrata literature; the sixty-four Yōginīs are particular to it. The two should be
    kept as separate entries and neither filled in from the other.
    (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pithori)
- **Bathukamma begins** — Mahālaya Amāvāsya (called Pethara Amāvāsya in Telangana). First of the
  nine days, concluding on Āśvayuja Śukla Aṣṭamī — see Section 8.

## Notes

**Sparse tithis.** Dvitīyā and Tṛtīyā have the fewest distinct named parva-dinas in the corpus —
each mostly riding on one or two headline festivals (Ratha Yātra/Bhai Dooj for Dvitīyā; Aṭla
Tadiya/Akṣaya Tṛtīya for Tṛtīyā) plus a scatter of minor jayantis. Trayōdaśī is similarly thin
once the recurring Pradōṣam/Śanitrayōdaśi combination is set aside — most of its content is
"Dhana Trayōdaśī" and a handful of others.

**Data-quality flags noticed while reading:**
- A number of entries carry no tithi/pakṣa field at all (source left it blank rather than the
  file's authors inferring one) — e.g. several 2023 and 2025 entries state only the māsa.
  These could not be placed confidently in a tithi bucket and are omitted from the counts below.
- Several dates in the source are themselves flagged as likely typos or discrepancies (e.g. a
  "30.06.2025" that should read 30.05.2025; a "19.06.2024" reused for a 2026 entry) — carried
  over as-is from the source, not corrected here.
- A handful of entries bundle two different tithis under one masthead line (e.g. "Trayōdaśī–
  Caturdaśī" pradōṣa+śivarātri combinations) — these were counted under both tithis where the
  festival's core observance genuinely spans them (e.g. Vaṭa Sāvitri, listed under both
  Trayōdaśī and Pūrṇimā).
- A handful of festival bullets (Umā Caturthi, Lakṣmī Pūja/Nāga Damana Pūja in Section 4; Nandā/
  Mitra Saptami, Vivasvat Saptami, Āmuktābharaṇa Santāna Saptami Vratam in Section 7; Gōvinda
  Dvādaśī in Section 12) turned up no corroborated pan-India or cross-regional sourcing beyond
  generic listings and so carry no *Pan-India / regional notes* sub-bullet — they appear to be
  narrowly local/temple-specific observances rather than widely attested festivals.

**Observances that do not sit on a tithi at all (added 2026-09-12).** Five observances could not
be placed in any tithi bucket above, because they are not reckoned by tithi. Three come from the
Stuti master vrata and parva reference, and two were added later the same day from printed vrata
volumes. They are recorded here instead:

- **Bonālu (Āṣāḍha Jātara)** — the **Sundays of Āṣāḍha māsa**, not a tithi. Telangana's festival
  of Mahāṅkāḷi, a state festival since 2014, in which cooked rice in a decorated pot is carried
  to the goddess. The Sundays are taken in a fixed temple order: Golconda on the first, Ujjaini
  Mahākāḷi in Secunderabad and Balkampet Yellamma on the second, and the Old City temples on the
  third. Because the anchor is the weekday and the month, the tithi differs every year.
  (Source: https://hyderabad.telangana.gov.in/festival/bonalu/)
- **Mukkanuma** — the **fourth day of the Sankrānti cycle**, the day after Kanuma, and therefore
  solar rather than lunar. The four days run Bhōgi, Makara Sankrānti, Kanuma, Mukkanuma. The
  source describes it as a day of feasting and family gathering with no major pūja of its own.
  (Source: https://hindupad.com/mukkanuma-2011-fourth-day-of-sankranti-festival-in-andhra-pradesh/)
- **Sāvitrī Gaurī Vratam** — begins **the day after the sun enters Makara rāśi**, and is therefore
  solar, not lunar. The kathā states this twice. The Sanskrit has Brahmāṇī answer the question
  `కో మాసః కా తిథిః` with `మకరస్థే దివాకరే`, when the sun stands in Makara. The Telugu prose says
  the same in plain words: the day after the sun enters Makara rāśi. The vow runs **nine days**,
  with a toram of **nine knots**, and is repeated for **nine years** before the udyāpana.
  (Source: *Vrata Ratnākaramu*, volume II, pages 264 to 276.)
- **Gurunātha Vratam (Poṅgali)** — **Mārgaśira Pūrṇimā** by preference, and this one does sit on a
  tithi. It is recorded here because the volume gives two alternatives that do not: the **Ārdrā**
  nakṣatra, or any **Thursday** in a month free of doṣa. The vow is kept for twelve years, or for
  three, and the udyāpana runs three days from **Mārgaśira Śukla Trayodaśī**.
  (Source: *Gurunātha Vrata (Poṅgali) Kalpaḥ*, Śrī Venkaṭeśvara Vedic University, Tirupati.)
- **Bathukamma and Kēdāra Gaurī** are **multi-day** — nine days and twenty-one days. Each is
  entered above at its opening tithi and at its concluding tithi, with a cross-reference between
  the two, rather than at a single point.

**Recurring monthly-type observances (structurally different from the once-a-year festivals
above — these repeat roughly 12–26 times/year, once or twice per lunar month, rather than once
per year):**
- **Ēkādaśī vrata in general** — every Ēkādaśī (Śukla and Kṛṣṇa pakṣa) is a fast day dedicated to
  Viṣṇu; on top of this base observance, each individual Ēkādaśī also carries its own name and
  story (listed individually above).
- **Māsa Śivarātri** — the Kṛṣṇa-pakṣa Caturdaśī of every month, a monthly mini-Śivarātri
  (distinct from the year's one Mahāśivarātri in Māgha/Phālguṇa).
- **Pradōṣam / Sōmapradōṣaṁ** — the Trayōdaśī evening twilight period, twice a month, when Śiva
  worship is considered especially fruitful (a Monday Pradōṣam — "Sōmapradōṣaṁ" — is treated as
  extra auspicious).
- **Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthī** — the Kṛṣṇa-pakṣa Caturthī of every month, Gaṇapati's monthly vrata
  day (often given a month-specific epithet, e.g. "Vikaṭa," "Gajānana," "Vighnarāja," "Bālacandra").
- **Sōmavatī Amāvāsya / Bhānu Saptami / Guru Puṣyayōgam / Kṛṣṇāṅgāraka Caturdaśī**, etc. —
  several "tithi + weekday (+ nakṣatra)" alabhya-yōga combinations recur several times a year
  whenever the calendar happens to align; the source treats each occurrence as auspicious in its
  own right rather than naming a single yearly festival.
