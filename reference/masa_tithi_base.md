# Māsa–Tithi Base — a month-ordered merged reference

This file merges `reference/masa_visishtata.md` (viśiṣṭata, Purāṇa references, and stories for each
of the 12 lunar months) and `reference/thithi_parvadina_index.md` (the 15-tithi-indexed festival
list, itself derived from `reference/festivals_aarshavani.md`, the raw *Aarsha Vani* magazine-issue
log, Oct 2021–Jul 2026) into one single, month-ordered pañcāṅgam reference. Compiled 2026-09-02.
Purely recurring monthly-type observances — the general Ēkādaśī vrata, Māsa Śivarātri,
Pradōṣam/Sōmapradōṣaṁ, and Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthī, each of which repeats 12–26 times a year rather
than once — are not re-described in all 12 months; they are covered once in the dedicated
"## Recurring Monthly-Type Observances" section immediately below, and each month's own
"Recurring monthly observances" subsection just cross-references it. A small number of festival
entries whose month could not be determined confidently from the two source files are listed in
"## Unplaced entries" at the very end, rather than being guessed into a month.

**A note on calendar conventions.** All month names in this file (and in `masa_visishtata.md` /
`thithi_parvadina_index.md`) follow **Amanta** reckoning — a month runs Śukla Pakṣa then Kṛṣṇa
Pakṣa, ending at Amāvāsyā — as used in Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Gujarat
and Tamil Nadu. North Indian states typically use **Pūrṇimānta** reckoning instead — Kṛṣṇa Pakṣa
then Śukla Pakṣa, ending at Pūrṇimā. The two systems name any month's Śukla Pakṣa half
identically, but every Kṛṣṇa Pakṣa tithi's month name shifts forward by exactly one month under
Pūrṇimānta relative to Amanta — e.g. this file's Śrāvaṇa Kṛṣṇa Aṣṭamī (Kṛṣṇāṣṭamī/Janmāṣṭamī) is
the same physical day as Bhādrapada Kṛṣṇa Aṣṭamī under Pūrṇimānta. Rather than dual-labeling every
affected entry, that offset is stated once here: for any Kṛṣṇa Pakṣa entry below, the Pūrṇimānta
month name is simply the next month after the Amanta name given. Śukla Pakṣa entries need no
adjustment.

## Recurring Monthly-Type Observances

(Per `thithi_parvadina_index.md`'s own "Notes" section — described once here instead of being
repeated under all 12 months.)

- **Ēkādaśī vrata (general)** — every Ēkādaśī, Śukla and Kṛṣṇa pakṣa alike (2/month, ~24–26/year),
  is a fast day dedicated to Viṣṇu, on top of whatever individually-named/storied Ēkādaśī also
  falls on that date. Each named Ēkādaśī is listed in its own month's Parva-dinas section below.
- **Māsa Śivarātri** — the Kṛṣṇa-pakṣa Caturdaśī of every month, a monthly mini-Śivarātri, distinct
  from the year's one Mahāśivarātri (Māgha/Phālguṇa Kṛṣṇa Caturdaśī), which — because of its
  outsized importance — is listed as its own named festival under Phālguṇa below rather than
  folded into this generic line.
- **Pradōṣam / Sōmapradōṣaṁ** — the Trayōdaśī evening twilight period, twice a month (Śukla and
  Kṛṣṇa pakṣa), considered especially fruitful for Śiva worship; a Monday occurrence is
  "Sōmapradōṣaṁ" (extra auspicious). A Saturday occurrence ("Śanitrayōdaśi"/Śani Pradōṣam") is not
  tied to a fixed month — see Unplaced entries below.
- **Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthī** — the Kṛṣṇa-pakṣa Caturthī of every month, Gaṇapati's monthly vrata
  day, often carrying a month-specific epithet for the presiding Gaṇapati form (e.g. "Vikaṭa,"
  "Gajānana," "Vighnarāja," "Bālacandra"). Where the source individually names and stories a given
  month's occurrence (as with Chaitra's Vikaṭa Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthi, the year's first), it is
  listed as a named festival in that month's Parva-dinas section rather than repeated here; other
  months' occurrences are generic in the sources consulted.

(Source: `reference/thithi_parvadina_index.md`, "## Notes" section.)

## Chaitra Māsam (చైత్రం)

### Names & basic info
- Telugu name: **Chaitra Māsam** (చైత్ర మాసం); also called **Madhu Māsam** in some traditions.
- Sanskrit/IAST: **Caitra** (चैत्र).
- Gregorian overlap: roughly **March–April**.
- Position: **1st month** of the traditional Hindu/Telugu lunar year (the year begins with Chaitra Śuddha Pādyami, i.e. Ugādi). (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaitra)
- Ṛtu: **Vasanta Ṛtu** (Spring), which spans Chaitra–Vaiśākha. (Source: https://www.learnreligions.com/the-six-seasons-of-india-p2-1770072)
- Name origin: The month is named for the fact that the full moon in this month occurs near/in **Chitrā nakṣatra**. Chitrā is presided over by Viśvakarma (Tvaṣṭā), the divine architect. (Source: https://www.hindu-blog.com/2021/03/chaitra-month-name-origin-of-chaitra-mahina.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaitra)

### Viśiṣṭata (significance)
- Chaitra Śuddha Pādyami is **Ugādi**, the Telugu/Kannada New Year, and per the *Chaturvarga Chintāmaṇi* it is the day **Brahma began the creation of the universe** — planets, stars, seasons, and rains were set in motion starting this day. This makes the whole month auspicious as the "birthday of creation." (Source: https://hindupad.com/chaitra-month-or-chaitra-masam-significance-and-spiritual-importance-chaitra-masam-puja/)
- The month is closely tied to **Lord Vishnu/Rama**: Sri Rama Navami (Rama's birth, Śuddha Navami) and Vasanta Navarātri fall in this month, making Chaitra especially dear to Vaishnava tradition and to devotees of Rama. Hanuman Jayanti (in some regional calendars) also falls in Chaitra. (Source: https://hindupad.com/chaitra-month-or-chaitra-masam-significance-and-spiritual-importance-chaitra-masam-puja/)
- Chaitra is considered auspicious for **commencing Vedic study and Upanayanam** (sacred thread ceremony), since it marks a fresh cosmological/calendrical beginning. (Source: https://hindupad.com/chaitra-month-or-chaitra-masam-significance-and-spiritual-importance-chaitra-masam-puja/)
- Also referred to as Madhu Māsam, associated with the blossoming of spring (madhu = spring/honey), reinforcing its Vasanta-ṛtu identity. (Source: https://indianmandirs.blogspot.com/2017/02/significance-of-chaitra-masamor-madhu.html)

### Purāṇa references
- No single dedicated "Chaitra Māhātmyam" exists to match Kārtika or Māgha Māhātmyam. Chaitra's significance is spread across several texts: the Chaturvarga Chintāmaṇi on creation, and various Purāṇas on Ugadi, Vasanta Navarātri and Rāma Navamī. The **Skanda Purāṇa** treats Chaturmāsya-adjacent monthly māhātmyas broadly (see kamakoti.org summaries), but no separate Chaitra māhātmya was found. This is a genuine gap in the sources, not an oversight. (Source: https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/details/skandapurana33.html — Chaturmasya Mahatmya, not Chaitra-specific)
- Brahma Purāṇa is traditionally associated with creation narratives generally (fitting Chaitra's "creation began here" theme) though not a dedicated month-māhātmya. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma_Purana)

### Stories/legends
- **Creation begins**: Per Chaturvarga Chintāmaṇi (cited via secondary sources), Brahma created the universe on Chaitra Śuddha Pādyami — this is the scriptural basis for Ugadi being New Year's Day and for the month's overall sanctity. (Source: https://hindupad.com/chaitra-month-or-chaitra-masam-significance-and-spiritual-importance-chaitra-masam-puja/)
- **Name/nakṣatra story**: The month takes its name because the Pūrṇimā (full moon) of this month falls in Chitrā nakṣatra, the star of Viśvakarma; this is a recurring explanatory motif across all 12 Hindu months. (Source: https://www.hindu-blog.com/2021/03/chaitra-month-name-origin-of-chaitra-mahina.html)
- Sri Rama's birth (Rama Navami) and the Ramayana narrative are anchored in this month, included here because it is the dominant devotional story linked to Chaitra. (Source: https://hindupad.com/chaitra-month-or-chaitra-masam-significance-and-spiritual-importance-chaitra-masam-puja/)

### Parva-dinas this month
- **Ugādi (Saṁvatsarādi)** — Caitra Śukla Pratipada, annual. Telugu New Year's Day: oil bath, new clothes, worship of one's chosen deity, and eating Ugādi Pacchadi (the six-taste chutney); also opens Vasanta Navarātri. (The creation-day scriptural basis for this being New Year's Day is covered under Stories above.)
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India festival under different names on the same lunisolar date: Gudi Padwa (Maharashtra), Ugadi (Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka), Cheti Chand (Sindhi community), and Navreh (Kashmiri Pandits). Core practices are shared, but the signature food differs — Ugadi Pacchadi in Telugu states vs. neem-jaggery elsewhere — and Maharashtra additionally hoists the gudi (a decorated pole/flag), which Telugu tradition does not. Solar-calendar new years like Puthandu, Vaisakhi, Bihu and Pana Sankranti fall on a different date and are not the same festival. (Source: https://www.thehansindia.com/life-style/regional-new-year-celebrations-gudi-padwa-ugadi-and-cheti-chand-958337)
- **Vikaṭa Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthi** — Caitra Kṛṣṇa Caturthī, annual — the calendar year's first Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthi, believed to remove that year's obstacles at the outset. (This is the month's own instance of the general monthly Saṅkaṣṭahara cycle — see Recurring Monthly-Type Observances above.)
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India in structure: Sankashti/Sankashtahara Chaturthi (Krishna Paksha Chaturthi every month, each dedicated to one of Ganesha's eight forms) is observed across India, and Vikata Sankashti (the Chaitra occurrence) is simply that month's instance of a nationwide monthly vrata rather than a distinct regional festival. (Source: https://www.thehansindia.com/life-style/vikata-sankashti-chaturthi-date-time-and-ritual-873412)
- **Āṇḍōḷikā Vratam (Saubhāgya Gaurī Vratam)** — Caitra Caturthī/Vidiya (varies by year), annual. Women's vrata for marital fortune: Umā-Maheśvara worshipped, Gaurī cradled, invited women gifted mango, turmeric and kumkum.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Regional: Saubhāgya/Soubhagya Gauri Vratam ("Gauri Tritiya") is observed by women in Andhra Pradesh and Maharashtra, described as a Telangana/Andhra Pradesh variation of Gangaur — the major Rajasthani/North Indian Gauri festival for marital fortune. (Source: https://www.hindu-blog.com/2009/03/soubhagya-gauri-vrata-gauri-puja-in.html)
- **Lakṣmī Pūja (Śrīvratam) / Nāga Damana Pūja** — sometimes framed as Caturthī/Pañcamī of Caitra, annual. Lakṣmī worship paired with serpent worship using Damana leaves, milk and ghee. (No independent Pan-India/regional-notes sub-bullet is given for this entry in the source — see `thithi_parvadina_index.md`'s Notes section on entries lacking cross-regional sourcing.)
- **Bhavānī (Aśōka) Aṣṭami** — Caitra Śukla Aṣṭamī, annual. Commemorates Pārvatī's birth to Mēna and Himavān; Śiva-Pārvatī worshipped, Aśōka buds eaten as prasādam.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Independent sourcing places the major public celebrations of "Ashokashtami"/Bhavani Ashtami not in the south but in Odisha (a grand week-long car-festival at the Lingaraj Temple, Bhubaneswar) and in Tripura (Unakoti) — prominent in eastern India rather than distinctively Telugu/Andhra. (Source: https://odishatv.in/news/festivals-events/significance-of-ashok-ashtami-in-hindu-tradition-and-why-it-is-celebrated-232751)
- **Śrī Rāma Navamī** — Caitra Śukla Navamī, annual, major. Rāma's birth (story above), celebrated with Sītā Rāma Kalyāṇaṁ (famously at Bhadrachalam) and sixteen-upacāra mandapa worship.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Major pan-India festival with sharply different regional flavors. In Ayodhya, huge crowds take a holy dip in the Sarayu and throng the new Ram Mandir, where a "Surya Tilak" is engineered to fall on Ram Lalla's forehead at noon. Bhadrachalam (Telangana), the "Ayodhya of the South," focuses on the Sītā Rāma Kalyāṇaṁ. In Vontimitta (Andhra) the Kalyanam is performed at night per local legend. Tamil Nadu's celebration is understated (temple rituals, Carnatic concerts); Sitamarhi (Bihar), held to be Sita's birthplace, is a major North Indian pilgrimage site. (Source: https://www.outlookindia.com/brand-studio/ram-navami-2026-in-south-india-top-temples-kalyanam-traditions ; https://nestasia.in/blogs/news/how-different-states-in-india-celebrate-ram-navami)
- **Dharmarāja Daśami** — Caitra Śukla Daśamī, annual. Worship of Dharmarāja and his brothers for wisdom to act rightly.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Also called Yama Dharmaraja Dashami, focused on removing the fear of death, addressed to Yudhishthira as Dharmaraja. Distinct from the Mithila-region (Bihar/Nepal) tradition where "Dharmaraj" is a kuldevta (family deity) with its own major festival, Ghari Puja — a different, folk-religion strand of "Dharmaraja" worship. (Source: https://hindupad.com/dharmaraja-dasami-yamadharmaraj-dashami/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmaraj_(Kuldevta))
- **Kāmadā Ēkādaśī** — Caitra Śukla, annual. Grants all wishes; paired with Kṛṣṇa's cradle worship.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Kāmadā Ēkādaśī** exactly, the first Ēkādaśī of the traditional Caitra-starting Hindu year in most pan-India lists, associated with fulfilling wishes and the story of Lalitā released from a rākṣasa curse. (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Varudhini Ēkādaśī** — Caitra Kṛṣṇa (per this source's amanta-convention labeling). Per the Bhaviṣya Purāṇa, released King Dundhumāra from a curse of Śiva.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Varūthinī Ēkādaśī** (labeled Vaiśākha Kṛṣṇa in most pūrṇimānta pan-India lists — the expected amanta/pūrṇimānta month-shift described in `thithi_parvadina_index.md`'s Ēkādaśī section intro, not a different festival) with the same Bhaviṣya Purāṇa story of King Dhundhumāra/Māndhātā's curse. (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Vāmana Dvādaśī** — Caitra Śukla, annual. Worship of Vāmana bestows the merit of charity and supreme knowledge.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Chiefly a Vaiṣṇava vrata day; one notable regional expression is a State-level Vaman Dwadashi Fair at Sarahan (Pachhad), Himachal Pradesh — a purely local mela. Elsewhere a quiet home/temple vrata. (Source: https://utsav.gov.in/view-event/vaman-dwadashi-fair-sarahan-pachhad-1)
- **Anaṅga Trayōdaśi (Damanōtsavaṁ)** — Caitra Śukla, annual. Manmatha (Kāma) worshipped with Damana leaves for fulfilment of desires.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Widely observed but on different dates/months by region — Gujarati and Marathi calendars place Anang Trayodashi in Chaitra (matching this source), while many North Indian calendars observe it in Mārgaśīrṣa instead. Kashmir has a distinct cognate, Madan Trayodashi, also on Chaitra Śukla Trayodaśī. (Source: https://astrodevam.com/festivals-of-india-anang-trayodashi-vrat.html ; http://autarmota.blogspot.com/2019/01/kaamdeva-in-kashmiri-art-and-poetry.html)
- **Śaivadamana Caturdaśī** — Caitra Śukla Caturdaśī, annual. Śiva worshipped with Damana leaves at pradōṣa time.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A narrowly regional/technical observance; no distinct name or practice found outside South Indian ritual manuals. (Source: https://kamakoti.org/kamakoti/articles/ESSENCE%20OF%20HINDU%20FESTIVALS%20AND%20AUSTERITIES.pdf)
- **Chaitra (Madana) Pūrṇima (Hanumadvijayōtsavaṁ / Hanuman Jayanti)** — Caitra Pūrṇimā, annual, major. Held to accumulate the whole month's merit; also Hanumān's principal jayanti in this tradition.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* This is a clear example of genuine regional calendar divergence. North India marks it on Chaitra Pūrṇimā (matching this source). Andhra Pradesh and Telangana observe a 41-day festival beginning on Chaitra Pūrṇimā and running to about mid-Vaiśākha. Tamil Nadu and Kerala instead celebrate Hanumān's birth in Mārgaśīrṣa (often on an amāvāsyā); Karnataka likewise observes it in Mārgaśīrṣa as "Hanuman Vratam." Both traditions are considered equally valid within their regions. (Source: https://www.omspiritualshop.com/blogs/news/hanuman-jayanthi-2026-dates-significance-celebrations-everything-you-need-to-know-across-india)
- *Cross-reference:* **Kotta Amāvāsya**, the last Amāvāsya of the year, immediately precedes this month's Ugādi — full entry under Phālguṇa below (its own month, Phālguṇa/Caitra Amāvāsyā, spans the boundary).

### Recurring monthly observances
This month's two Ēkādaśīs are individually named and storied above (Kāmadā, Varudhini); its
Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthī is itself the named "Vikaṭa Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthi" above, the year's first.
Māsa Śivarātri (Kṛṣṇa Caturdaśī) and both Pradōṣams (Śukla and Kṛṣṇa Trayōdaśī) fall this month in
their generic form — see Recurring Monthly-Type Observances above.

## Vaiśākha Māsam (వైశాఖం)

### Names & basic info
- Telugu name: **Vaiśākha Māsam** (వైశాఖ మాసం).
- Sanskrit/IAST: **Vaiśākha** (वैशाख).
- Gregorian overlap: roughly **April–May**. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishakha)
- Position: **2nd month** of the lunar year. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishakha)
- Ṛtu: latter half of **Vasanta Ṛtu** (Spring), Chaitra–Vaiśākha together forming the season. (Source: https://www.learnreligions.com/the-six-seasons-of-india-p2-1770072)

### Viśiṣṭata (significance)
- Vaiśākha is widely proclaimed across the Purāṇas as **the most sacred/meritorious of all months**. The presiding deity of the month is **Madhusūdana** (a form of Vishnu). (Source: https://hindupad.com/vaisakha-masa-vratam-rituals-and-pujas-to-perform-in-vaishakh-month/)
- Vishnu is said to **reside in sacred rivers** throughout Vaiśākha, so bathing (snāna), especially at Brahma Muhūrta, is considered equal in merit to visiting and worshipping at all the sacred tīrthas. (Source: https://hindupad.com/vaisakha-masa-vratam-rituals-and-pujas-to-perform-in-vaishakh-month/)
- **Dāna (charity)** is singled out as the paramount observance of the month — especially jala-dāna, anna-dāna, and vastra-dāna, said to bring "eternal prosperity" under Vishnu's command. (Source: https://hindupad.com/vaisakha-masa-vratam-rituals-and-pujas-to-perform-in-vaishakh-month/)
- Multiple Purāṇas — **Skanda Purāṇa, Padma Purāṇa, Nārada Purāṇa** — glorify Vaiśākha as sacred above other months. (Source: https://hindupad.com/vaisakha-masa-vratam-rituals-and-pujas-to-perform-in-vaishakh-month/)
- Two major Vishnu-avatāra jayantis fall in this month: **Narasimha Jayanti** and **Paraśurāma Jayanti**, reinforcing the month's Vaishnava character. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narasimha_Jayanti ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parashurama_Jayanti)

### Purāṇa references
- **Padma Purāṇa, Chapter 98 — "Vaiśākha Māhātmya"**, a dedicated chapter glorifying the month. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365410.html)
- **Skanda Purāṇa, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa — "Vaiśākha Māsa Māhātmya"** is a distinctly named, dedicated section. (Source: https://kausiki.com/product/skanda-purana-vaishnava-khanda-vaisakha-masa-mahatmya/ ; https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/details/skandapurana28.html)
- This makes Vaiśākha one of the months (like Kārtika and Māgha) with a genuinely well-attested, dedicated Purāṇic māhātmya — not generic filler.

### Stories/legends
- The Skanda Purāṇa's Vaiśākha-māhātmya section recounts dialogues extolling the fruits of Vaiśākha vratas — bathing, dāna, and worship of Madhusūdana — narrated as a discourse between a sage and a king/seeker. (Source: https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/details/skandapurana28.html)
- Narasimha Jayanti (Vishnu's fierce avatāra slaying Hiranyakashipu to save Prahlada) and Paraśurāma Jayanti (birth of the avatāra who curbed adharmic kshatriyas) are the two itihāsa/avatāra narratives most closely tied to this month's calendar date. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narasimha_Jayanti ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parashurama_Jayanti)

### Parva-dinas this month
- **Akṣaya Tṛtīya (with Trētā Yugādi, Paraśurāma Jayanti, Balarāma Jayanti)** — Vaiśākha Śukla Tṛtīya, annual. Any charity, japa, homa or worship on this day is believed to yield "akṣaya" (inexhaustible) merit; also Paraśurāma's incarnation day (story above) and the Candanōtsavam at Siṁhāchalam.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Genuinely pan-India, with regional names: Akha Teej in Gujarat and Rajasthan (mass weddings held without a separate muhurat) and Akti in Chhattisgarh. Maharashtra emphasizes Haldi-Kumkum and monsoon sowing; Gujarat emphasizes Lakshmi Pujan, grain charity and gold-buying; Rajasthan emphasizes mass marriages. The Siṁhāchalam Candanōtsavam is a local Telugu temple-specific addition. (Source: https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/trends/story/akshaya-tritiya-2026-from-gold-rush-to-farming-rituals-how-different-states-in-india-celebrate-the-festival-differently-525727-2026-04-15)
- **Gaṅgōtpatti (Gaṅgā/Vidyāraṇya Jayanti)** — Vaiśākha Śukla Saptamī, annual. Commemorates Maharṣi Jahnu releasing the Gaṅgā; also Vidyāraṇya's birth anniversary.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Also known as Gaṅgā Saptamī/Jahnu Saptamī/Gaṅgāvataraṇa, distinct from Gaṅgā Daśaharā (Jyēṣṭha Daśamī, see below), the much bigger pan-North-India Ganga festival. The Vidyāraṇya Jayanti component (birth anniversary of the Vijayanagara-era Sringeri pontiff) is a narrowly South Indian/Sringeri Mutt observance. (Source: https://hindupad.com/vidyaranya-jayanti-swami-vidyaranya-jayanthi/)
- **Mōhinī Ēkādaśī** — Vaiśākha Śukla, annual. Fasting, stotra recitation, japa and meditation.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Mohinī Ēkādaśī** exactly, widely observed nationwide and associated in some North Indian tellings with Rāma's own observance of it during his exile. (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Paraśurāma Dvādaśī** — Vaiśākha, annual. Worshipped with ṣōḍaśōpacāras.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Also called **Rukmiṇī Dvādaśī** in some sources; a modest, pan-Vaiṣṇava fasting/worship day without strong regional variation reported. (Source: https://www.drikpanchang.com/dwadashi/parashurama/parashurama-dwadashi-date-time.html)
- **Mahā Vaiśākhi (Kūrma Jayanti)** — Vaiśākha Pūrṇimā, annual. Commemorates Viṣṇu's Kūrma (tortoise) avatāra; Ardhanārīśvara and Sampadgaurī vratas also observed.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Doubly significant nationally — for Hindus, Kūrma Jayanti/Satyanārāyaṇa Pūjā day, while the same date is **Buddha Pūrṇimā/Vesak**, observed heavily in Maharashtra and across South/Southeast Asia. Many Puranic lists count the Buddha as a Viṣṇu avatāra, which is why Hindu and Buddhist observances often overlap at the same temples this day. (Source: https://svastika.in/blogs/indian-festivals/why-celebrate-buddha-purnima-when-gautama-buddha-born-died)
- *Cross-reference:* **Apara Ēkādaśī** (given in the source as Vaiśākha/Jyēṣṭha Kṛṣṇa) is listed in full under Jyēṣṭha below, where its pan-India name (Aparā Ēkādaśī, Jyeṣṭha Kṛṣṇa) is standard.

### Recurring monthly observances
Vaiśākha's two Ēkādaśīs are individually named above (Mōhinī Ēkādaśī; Varūthinī Ēkādaśī is listed
under Chaitra per this source's own amanta-convention tithi label — see the cross-reference note
there). Its Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthī, Māsa Śivarātri and both Pradōṣams occur in generic form this
month — see Recurring Monthly-Type Observances above.

## Jyēṣṭha Māsam (జ్యేష్ఠం)

### Names & basic info
- Telugu name: **Jyēṣṭha Māsam** (జ్యేష్ఠ మాసం).
- Sanskrit/IAST: **Jyeṣṭha** (ज्येष्ठ), meaning "eldest/greatest" — named for **Jyeṣṭhā nakṣatra**, near which the full moon of this month occurs. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyeshtha_(month))
- Gregorian overlap: roughly **May–June**.
- Position: **3rd month** of the lunar year. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyeshtha_(month))
- Ṛtu: **Grīṣma Ṛtu** (Summer), Jyēṣṭha–Āṣāḍha together forming the season. (Source: https://www.learnreligions.com/the-six-seasons-of-india-p2-1770072)

### Viśiṣṭata (significance)
- Jyēṣṭha is a month specially dedicated to **Sūrya**, fitting for peak summer. Worship of **Sūrya and Varuṇa** is considered especially fruitful this month — Sūrya for the heat, Varuṇa in anticipation of the coming monsoon. (Source: https://www.aajtak.in/religion/news/story/jyeshtha-maas-2024-date-know-month-significance-vrat-festivals-list-nirjala-ekadashi-vat-savitri-vrat-tlifdg-1950183-2024-05-24)
- **Nirjala Ekadashi** (Śukla Ekadashi of Jyēṣṭha) is considered the single most austere and meritorious of all 24 Ekadashis in the year, said to confer the combined merit of observing all others. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirjala_Ekadashi)
- **Vaṭa Sāvitri Vrata** (observed on Jyēṣṭha Amāvāsya per the Purṇimānta calendar in some regions) is a major vrata for married women, modeled on Sāvitrī's devotion to Satyavān. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vata_Purnima ; https://www.aajtak.in/religion/news/story/jyeshtha-maas-2024-date-know-month-significance-vrat-festivals-list-nirjala-ekadashi-vat-savitri-vrat-tlifdg-1950183-2024-05-24)
- Skanda Purāṇa (Kāśī-khaṇḍa) contains material on "Jyeṣṭheśa" — a Śiva form/shrine connected to this month, suggesting a distinct Śaiva association in Kashi tradition. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc423802.html)

### Purāṇa references
- **Skanda Purāṇa, Kāśī-khaṇḍa (Uttarārdha), Chapter 63 — "The Story of Jyeṣṭheśa"**: a named chapter tied to the month's eponymous deity/liṅga, though it is a sthala/deity māhātmya rather than a pure calendrical month-māhātmya. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc423802.html)
- No separate, stand-alone "Jyēṣṭha Māsa Māhātmya" comparable to Vaiśākha or Kārtika was found. This is a genuine gap in the sources. The Nirjala Ekadashi and Vaṭa Sāvitri vrata-kathās carry the month's narrative weight instead.

### Stories/legends
- **Nirjala Ekadashi origin story**: Popularly linked to Bhīma, who — unable to fast on all 24 Ekadashis due to his great hunger — was advised by Vyāsa to observe this one waterless fast to gain the merit of the entire year's Ekadashis at once (hence "Bhīmasenī Ekadashi"). (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirjala_Ekadashi)
- **Sāvitrī and Satyavān**: The Vaṭa Sāvitri vrata reenacts the itihāsa of Sāvitrī, who through devotion and cleverness persuaded Yama to restore her husband Satyavān's life, worshipping under a banyan (vaṭa) tree. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vata_Purnima)

### Parva-dinas this month
- **Umā Caturthi (Varada Gaṇēśa Pūja)** — Jyēṣṭha Caturthī, annual. Worship of the Divine Mother as Umā, paired with Gaṇapati worshipped as "Pradyumna Gaṇapati." (No independent Pan-India/regional-notes sub-bullet found in the source.)
- **Varuṇa Saptami** — Jyēṣṭha Saptamī, annual. Varuṇa worshipped via a water-filled kalaśa for relief from summer heat and timely monsoon rains.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* This Jyēṣṭha Saptami Varuna-worship-for-monsoon observance appears in South Indian (Andhra/Karnataka/Tamil Nadu) panchang traditions; no distinct North Indian counterpart or alternate name found. (Source: https://hindupad.com/varuna-sashtivaruna-shashti/)
- **Araṇya Ṣaṣṭhi (Vana Gauri Vratam, Śītalā Ṣaṣṭhi)** — Jyēṣṭha Ṣaṣṭhī, annual. Gauri and Śītalā worshipped with Kārtikēya under a Kadamba tree; protects pregnancy and grants good progeny.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Real regional divergence: in Bengal this same Jyēṣṭha Ṣaṣṭhī is "Jāmāi Ṣaṣṭhī," a completely different festival honoring sons-in-law. In Andhra it is Araṇya Gauri Vratam/Vanagauri Vratam, matching the source. Odisha's related but distinct Sitalsasthi instead commemorates Shiva-Parvati's wedding. Genuinely different festivals region to region on the same tithi. (Source: https://hindutempletalk.org/2023/05/25/5147/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitalsasthi)
- **Trilōcanāṣṭami** — Jyēṣṭha Aṣṭamī, annual. Umā-Maheśvara worship for marital happiness (and, for the unmarried, a good spouse).
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Real regional variety: in Bengal it is Kamya Trilochan Puja; in Kashmir the same date hosts the Khir Bhavani Mela near Srinagar; other regions mark Dhumavati Jayanti or Shukla Devi Puja. The Umā-Maheśvara worship described here is one regional (South Indian) expression among several distinct festivals sharing this tithi. (Source: https://hindupad.com/trilochan-ashtami-2010-trilochana-ashtami-vrat/)
- **Daśa Pāpahara Daśami Vratam (Gaṅgā Dashahara)** — Jyēṣṭha Śukla Daśamī, annual. Bathing in (or worshipping) the Gaṅgā is said to remove the effects of ten kinds of sin.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A major pan-India, especially North Indian, river festival, celebrated most grandly at Haridwar's Har Ki Pauri and Varanasi's ghats, with holy dips, Ganga aarti, and floating leaf-boats of lamps at dusk. Also observed in Uttarakhand, UP, Bihar and West Bengal — fundamentally a North Indian Ganga-basin festival that Telugu almanacs also record. (Source: https://www.festivalsofindia.in/ganga/ ; https://cultureandheritage.org/2024/03/ganga-dussehra-how-india-pays-homage-to-its-lifeline-river-ganga.html)
- **Apara Ēkādaśī** — given in the source as Vaiśākha/Jyēṣṭha Kṛṣṇa; placed here as its standard pan-India slot. Worship of Nārāyaṇa as Vāmana removes sins of many births; house repairs are avoided this day.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Aparā Ēkādaśī** (Jyeṣṭha Kṛṣṇa) exactly (also called Acalā/Bhadrakālī Ēkādaśī in some Bengali/Eastern traditions), held nationwide to remove great sins including brahma-hatyā; avoidance of house-building/repairs is a widely shared custom, not South-India-specific. (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Nirjala Ēkādaśī (also Kūrma Jayanti, "Trivikramaikādaśī")** — Jyēṣṭha Śukla. Fasting without even water — held to carry the merit of all the year's Ēkādaśīs combined (story above).
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* One of the most famous pan-India Ēkādaśīs of all — also called Bhīmasenī/Pāṇḍava Ēkādaśī — draws huge crowds at Vaiṣṇava temples nationwide (Mathura-Vrindavan, Varanasi, Puri, Tirupati) as the "one fast that covers them all" for those who cannot keep every monthly vrata. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirjala_Ekadashi)
- **Yogini Ēkādaśī** — Jyēṣṭha Kṛṣṇa. Purāṇic story of a leprous servant of Kubēra cured by this vrata.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Yoginī Ēkādaśī** (Āṣāḍha Kṛṣṇa in most pūrṇimānta lists) exactly, with the same Purāṇic story (Skanda/Padma Purāṇa) of Hema, a leprous gardener/servant of Kubēra, cured through this vrata. (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Rāma Lakṣmaṇa Dwādaśi (Champaka Dwādaśi, Gāyatrīdēvi Jayanti)** — Jyēṣṭha, annual. Commemorates Ādi Śaṅkarācārya's ascent to Kailāsa alongside Rāma-Lakṣmaṇa worship.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* "Champaka Dvādaśī" as Ādi Śaṅkarācārya's Kailāsa-gamana day is a specifically Advaita/Śaṅkara-maṭha observance found wherever Śaṅkara maṭhas exist (Kanchi, Sringeri, Dwaraka, Puri, Jyotirmath), not confined to Andhra; some calendars instead place Gāyatrī Jayanti on Jyēṣṭha Śukla Ēkādaśī or Śrāvaṇa Pūrṇimā. (Source: https://hindupad.com/sri-shankaracharya-kailasa-gamanam-on-champak-dwadashi/)
- **Vaṭa Sāvitri Vratam (Trayōdaśī–Pūrṇimā)** — Jyēṣṭha Trayōdaśī through Pūrṇimā, annual. Commemorates Sāvitrī winning back her husband Satyavān's life from Yama (story above); banyan tree worshipped, with Jyēṣṭha Pūrṇimā also observed in some regions as its closing/alternate day ("Jyēṣṭi Ēruvāka Pūrṇima"), including special abhiṣēkams at Tirupati and Puri Jagannātha.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A pan-India vrata for married women's marital longevity, but the exact date differs by region — Maharashtra, Gujarat and Karnataka observe it on Jyēṣṭha Pūrṇimā; North Indian states (Bihar, UP) and Odisha observe it on Jyēṣṭha Amāvāsyā; this source's placement spanning Trayōdaśī–Pūrṇimā reflects yet a third regional convention. The core ritual — fasting, circumambulating a banyan tree, tying a thread around it, reciting the Sāvitrī-Satyavān kathā — is consistent everywhere it is observed. (Source: https://dharmikapp.in/blogs/vat-savitri/vat-savitri-vrat-2026-date-muhurat-katha-puja-vidhi-regional-differences)
- *Cross-reference:* **Subrahmaṇya/Skanda/Kumāra Ṣaṣṭhī** general observance also recurs in Mārgaśira and Āṣāḍha — full entry under Āṣāḍha below.

### Recurring monthly observances
Three of this month's Ēkādaśīs appear above (Apara, Nirjala, Yogini — the source's tithi index
gives Jyēṣṭha unusually many named Ēkādaśī occurrences owing to amanta/pūrṇimānta overlap at the
month's boundary); Māsa Śivarātri, both Pradōṣams and the Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthī fall this month in
generic form — see Recurring Monthly-Type Observances above.

## Āṣāḍha Māsam (ఆషాఢం)

### Names & basic info
- Telugu name: **Āṣāḍha Māsam** (ఆషాఢ మాసం).
- Sanskrit/IAST: **Āṣāḍha** (आषाढ), named for **Pūrvāṣāḍhā/Uttarāṣāḍhā nakṣatra**.
- Gregorian overlap: roughly **June–July**.
- Position: **4th month** of the lunar year.
- Ṛtu: latter half of **Grīṣma Ṛtu** (Summer) shading into the onset of **Varṣā Ṛtu** (Monsoon). (Source: https://www.learnreligions.com/the-six-seasons-of-india-p2-1770072 ; https://jothishi.com/ashada-month-the-monsoon-month/)

### Viśiṣṭata (significance)
- Āṣāḍha is the month in which **Lord Vishnu goes into yoga-nidrā (cosmic sleep)** on Śeṣa in the ocean of milk, beginning on **Devaśayani (Āṣāḍha Śukla) Ekadashi** and lasting four months (**Chāturmāsya**) until he "awakens" on Kārtika Ekadashi. Because Vishnu is "resting," this period is held to be extra meritorious for tapas, japa, and dāna. (Source: https://www.sanatangyan.com/post/devshayani-ekadashi-2025-when-vishnu-sleeps-and-shiva-watches-over-the-universe ; https://hindutone.com/festivals/ashadha-masam-2026-start-end-date-complete-festivals-list-important-dates/)
- **Guru Pūrṇimā** (Āṣāḍha Pūrṇimā) is dedicated to Veda Vyāsa (also **Vyāsa Pūrṇimā**), honoring him as compiler of the four Vedas, author of the 18 Purāṇas and the Mahābhārata, and archetypal Guru. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Purnima ; https://www.divyajivan.org/articles/siva/gurupurnima_signi.htm)
- Āṣāḍha Pūrṇimā also inaugurates **Chāturmāsya**, the four-month monsoon period of intensified self-discipline observed by ascetics and devout householders. (Source: https://hindutone.com/festivals/ashadha-masam-2026-start-end-date-complete-festivals-list-important-dates/)
- The Skanda Purāṇa's own Chāturmāsya-māhātmya is anchored to this month as its starting point. (Source: https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/details/skandapurana33.html)

### Purāṇa references
- The **Devaśayani Ekadashi** legend and Vishnu's sleep are attributed to the **Bhaviṣyottara Purāṇa**, describing Vishnu retiring to sleep after six months of battling asuras. (Source: https://www.sanatangyan.com/post/devshayani-ekadashi-2025-when-vishnu-sleeps-and-shiva-watches-over-the-universe)
- **Skanda Purāṇa — Chāturmāsya Māhātmya** is the most substantial dedicated text located covering this Āṣāḍha-onward monsoon vrata period, though it covers the whole four-month Chāturmāsya rather than being Āṣāḍha-only. (Source: https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/details/skandapurana33.html)
- No separate, stand-alone "Āṣāḍha Māsa Māhātmya" text was found. This is a genuine gap in the sources. Āṣāḍha's Purāṇic weight comes mainly through the Devaśayani-Ekadashi/Chāturmāsya material.

### Stories/legends
- **King Māndhātā and the drought**: A righteous king faced a three-year drought; the sage Aṅgiras advised him to observe Devaśayani (Āṣāḍhi) Ekadashi. Upon the kingdom's fulfilling the vrata, the rains returned and prosperity was restored — the scriptural justification for this Ekadashi. (Source: https://www.apnisanskriti.com/vrat-katha/significance-of-devshayani-ekadashi-5595 ; https://guptvrindavandham.org/blogs/devshayani-ekadashi/)
- **Vishnu's cosmic sleep and Shiva's watch**: With Vishnu asleep for Chāturmāsya, tradition holds Lord Shiva takes over protection of the universe during this period. (Source: https://www.sanatangyan.com/post/devshayani-ekadashi-2025-when-vishnu-sleeps-and-shiva-watches-over-the-universe)
- **Guru Pūrṇimā's Vyāsa connection**: Vyāsa is traditionally held to have been born on Āṣāḍha Pūrṇimā, commemorating his life's work organizing the Vedas and composing the Purāṇas/Mahābhārata/Brahma Sūtras. (Source: https://www.divyajivan.org/articles/siva/gurupurnima_signi.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Purnima)

### Parva-dinas this month
- **Śrī Jagannātha Ratha Yātra** — Āṣāḍha Śukla Vidiyā, annual. Chariot procession of Jagannātha, Balabhadra, Subhadra and Sudarśana at Puri; darśan on the ratha is said to prevent rebirth.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India in reach but Odisha-specific in origin/primacy. Puri remains definitive; the next-oldest is at Mahesh (Hooghly, West Bengal, 600+ years old), followed by Ahmedabad (third-largest after Puri and Kolkata) and ISKCON-led Rath Yatras in Mumbai, Chennai and Hyderabad. (Source: https://www.outlooktraveller.com/experiences/heritage/4-prominent-rath-yatras-in-india)
- **Skanda Pañcamī** — Āṣāḍha Pañcamī, annual (often paired with Skanda/Kumāra Ṣaṣṭhī the next day).
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Regional to South India: kept at Muruga/Subrahmanya temples across Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Kerala, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh, functioning as the fasting lead-in to the following day's Skanda/Kumāra Ṣaṣṭhī. No pan-India or North Indian equivalent found. (Source: https://www.thedivineindia.com/skanda-panchami-kumar-shashti-subrahmanya-puja-benefits/7779)
- **Subrahmaṇya/Skanda/Kumāra Ṣaṣṭhī** — recurs in several months, notably Āṣāḍha and Mārgaśira, celebrating Kumārasvāmi's birthday; worship relieves snake- and Kuja-(Mars)-related afflictions.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Known across South India as Skanda Sashti or Kanda Shashti; the deity carries many regional names (Kartikeya, Murugan, Guha, Shanmukha, Velayudhan). Predominantly South Indian — Tamil Nadu (biggest, as Soorasamharam at Tiruchendur), Kerala, Karnataka (Kukke Subramanya's own Sashti) and Andhra/Telangana — with only limited North Indian observance. (Source: https://hindupad.com and https://www.hindu-blog.com/2009/10/skanda-sashti-2009.html)
- **Vivasvat Saptami** — Āṣāḍha Saptamī, annual. Sūrya worship and recitation of Āditya Hṛdaya. (No independent Pan-India/regional-notes sub-bullet found in the source.)
- **Śrī Mahiṣāsura Mardini Pūja** — Āṣāḍha Aṣṭamī, annual. Reciting the Mahiṣāsura Mardini stotra is said to remove evil qualities and grant auspicious yōga.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* The Mahishasura Mardini Stotra (attributed to Adi Shankara) is recited pan-India, most famously via All India Radio's Bengali broadcast on Mahalaya morning — a different occasion (Mahalaya, not Āṣāḍha Aṣṭamī). No independent sourcing was found for an "Āṣāḍha Aṣṭamī" festival specifically under this name; likely a minor, possibly Andhra-specific observance. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahishasura_Mardini_Stotra)
- **Vāsudēva Dwādaśī** — Āṣāḍha Śukla, annual. Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa worshipped as "Vāsudēva."
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Also called Śrī Kṛṣṇa Dvādaśī; observed at ISKCON/Kṛṣṇa temples nationally (noted especially in Pune, Mumbai, Nashik) with bhajans, kīrtans and go-sēvā — pan-Vaiṣṇava rather than specifically Telugu. (Source: https://www.sanatanavibes.in/2025/07/vasudeva-dwadashi.html)
- **Hari Śayana Ēkādaśī ("Tholi Ēkādaśī")** — Āṣāḍha Śukla. Nārāyaṇa enters yōga-nidrā; opens the four-month Cāturmāsya Vratam (story above).
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* The other pilgrimage-scale, pan-India Ēkādaśī. Standard name Śayanī/Devśayanī Ēkādaśī; its single largest regional expression is Āṣāḍhī Ēkādaśī at Pandharpur, Maharashtra — culmination of the centuries-old Vārkarī "Wari" pilgrimage (18–21 days on foot from Dehu/Ālandī) to the Viṭṭhala temple. "Tholi Ēkādaśī" is the Telugu/Andhra regional name for the same day, paralleling Maharashtra's Āṣāḍhī and North India's Devśayanī/Harishayani. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shayani_Ekadashi ; https://www.stayvista.com/blog/ashadhi-ekadashi-2026-date-pandharpur-wari-route-maharashtras-largest-pilgrimage-explained/)
- **Kāmika Ēkādaśī** — Āṣāḍha Kṛṣṇa. Fasting/tulasī worship of Viṣṇu, per the Brahma Vaivarta Purāṇa, equal to bathing in all sacred rivers.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Kāmikā Ēkādaśī** (Śrāvaṇa Kṛṣṇa in most pūrṇimānta lists) exactly, with the same Brahma Vaivarta Purāṇa framing found in nationwide Vaiṣṇava sources. (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Guru/Vyāsa Pūrṇimā** — Āṣāḍha Pūrṇimā, annual, major. The principal day for honoring one's Guru, tracing the Guru-paramparā back to Vyāsa (story above).
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A pan-India (and pan-Nepal/Bhutan) festival honoring one's guru, observed by Hindus, Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists alike. Major centers include Ujjain and Varanasi (large Vyāsa Pūjā), Bēlūr Maṭh in West Bengal (Ramakrishna-Vivekananda lineage), and Śikharjī in Jharkhand (a major Jain pilgrimage site); South Indian ashrams mark it with satsaṅgs and community meals rather than large public processions. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Purnima ; https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/festivals/guru-purnima/)
- **Chukkala Amāvāsya** — Āṣāḍha Amāvāsyā, annual. Marks the transition into Dakṣiṇāyana; Lakṣmī worship and extra lamp-lighting.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A Telugu-specific name for what Karnataka calls Bhīmana Amāvāsya (also Jyōti Bhīmēśvara Vrata/Pati Sañjīvinī Vrata) and Maharashtra calls Gatari Amāvāsya — three regional names for one South-Indian-belt observance (women's vrata for marital wellbeing, Gaurī/Pārvatī worship) rather than a truly pan-India festival. (Source: https://www.hindu-blog.com/2009/07/chukkala-amavasi-gauri-vratam-on-ashada.html)

### Recurring monthly observances
Both this month's Ēkādaśīs are individually named above (Hari Śayana/Tholi Ēkādaśī, Kāmika
Ēkādaśī); Māsa Śivarātri, both Pradōṣams and the Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthī fall this month in generic
form — see Recurring Monthly-Type Observances above.

## Śrāvaṇa Māsa

### Names & Basic Info
- **Telugu name**: Śrāvaṇa Māsam (శ్రావణ మాసం)
- **Sanskrit/IAST**: Śrāvaṇa
- **Position**: 5th month of the traditional Hindu lunisolar year (per the Chaitrādi/Southern reckoning used in Telugu panchangam)
- **Gregorian overlap**: Roughly late July–August/early September (e.g., 2026: Aug 13 – Sep 11) (Source: https://sakalam.org/shravana-masam/)
- **Season (ṛtu)**: Varṣā ṛtu (monsoon season)
- **Name origin**: Derived from the Śravaṇa nakṣatra being near the Moon on the full-moon day of this month; also linked to the Sanskrit root "śru" (to hear), since the month is dedicated to śravaṇam — listening to scripture and divine glories. (Source: https://shubhpanchang.com/blog/shravan-mas-significance-rituals-and-shiva-puja-vidhi)

### Viśiṣṭata (Significance)
Śrāvaṇa is considered the most sacred month for the worship of Lord Śiva. According to the Śiva Purāṇa, this month is exceptionally dear to Mahādeva, making it the ideal period for austerity, fasting, and prayer directed at him. Every Monday (Śrāvaṇa Somavāram) is considered especially auspicious. (Source: https://shubhpanchang.com/blog/shravan-mas-significance-rituals-and-shiva-puja-vidhi)

Married and unmarried women observe Maṅgala Gaurī vratas on Tuesdays for marital wellbeing and family prosperity — a Telugu/Deccan-region tradition closely tied to Śrāvaṇa's Śaiva character. The month closes with Rakṣā Bandhan on Śrāvaṇa Pūrṇimā, and includes Ekādaśīs such as Kāmika Ekādaśī and Śrāvaṇa Putradā Ekādaśī. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shravana_Putrada_Ekadashi ; https://www.poojalu.com/shravan-month/)

Being the peak of the monsoon, the month is also symbolically one of purification and renewal — the rains cleanse the earth just as the vratas of this month are said to cleanse the devotee.

### Purāṇa References
The Śiva Purāṇa is the principal scriptural source establishing Śrāvaṇa's special dearness to Śiva. (Source: https://shubhpanchang.com/blog/shravan-mas-significance-rituals-and-shiva-puja-vidhi) Unlike Kārtika, no single, widely-attested "Śrāvaṇa Māhātmyam" was found in this research. Its significance is conveyed through Śiva Purāṇa passages and regional vrata-kathā literature, not through one canonical māhātmya text. This is a genuine gap, and further source-checking may close it.

### Stories/Legends
The month's central legend is the **Samudra Manthana** and Śiva's consumption of the Hālāhala poison. As devas and asuras churned the Ocean of Milk for amṛta, the deadly Hālāhala poison arose first, threatening all creation. The frightened devas took refuge with Śiva, who drank the poison to protect the three worlds; Pārvatī held his throat so it would not descend further, turning his throat blue — earning him the epithet **Nīlakaṇṭha**. This is traditionally why Śrāvaṇa, the month of the story's commemoration in devotional retelling, is considered so dear to Śiva devotees, who pour water (abhiṣeka) on the Śivaliṅga throughout the month to "cool" the effects of the poison he bore. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halahala ; https://thegaudiyatreasuresofbengal.com/2023/09/23/the-legend-of-shiva-drinking-poison-during-samudra-manthan-how-shivas-selflessness-saved-the-world/ ; https://shubhpanchang.com/blog/shravan-mas-significance-rituals-and-shiva-puja-vidhi)

### Parva-dinas this month
- **Durva Gaṇapati Vratam (Nāgacaturthi)** — Śrāvaṇa Śukla Caturthī, annual. Gaṇapati worshipped with pañca-pallava (five sacred leaves); Nāga Dēvatā also honored the same day.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Wider than purely Telugu: observed throughout India, especially in Maharashtra, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Karnataka, Goa and Andhra Pradesh, falling exactly a month before Ganesh Chaturthi and centered on offering 21 blades of durva grass — a simpler, more private vrata than the main Chaturthi. (Source: https://www.omspiritualshop.com/te/blogs/%E0%B0%A8%E0%B1%8D%E0%B0%AF%E0%B1%82%E0%B0%B8%E0%B1%8D/durva-ganapati-chaturthi-2026)
  - *Cross-reference:* **Nāga Pañcami** recurs across many months and is most commonly celebrated pan-India in Śrāvaṇa Śukla Pañcamī (though it is most emphasized in this source's Kārtika data) — full entry under Kārtika below.
- **Śrī Kṛṣṇāṣṭamī (Janmāṣṭamī)** — Śrāvaṇa Kṛṣṇa Aṣṭamī, annual, major. Full-day fast broken with evening worship marking Kṛṣṇa's manifestation with all sixteen kalās.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Huge regional variation. Maharashtra is famous for Dahi Handi — human pyramids up to nine tiers breaking pots hung 40 feet up. Tamil Nadu (Gokulashtami) decorates homes with kolam; Andhra Pradesh holds Uriyadi pot-breaking events. Mathura/Vrindavan and North India stage elaborate Rasa Leela dramatizations. ISKCON temples worldwide hold 24-hour akhand kirtan and midnight abhishekam. (Source: https://www.outlookindia.com/astrology/astro-picks/krishna-janmashtami-2025-celebrations-across-india-from-mathura-to-odisha ; https://vedayatra.in/blog/krishna-jayanthi-across-india-diverse-celebrations-and-traditions/)
- **Putraikādaśi** — Śrāvaṇa Śukla. Worship of Viṣṇu as "Śrīdhara"; umbrella charity is prescribed.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India **Śrāvaṇa Putradā Ēkādaśī**, distinct from the separate Pauṣa Putradā Ēkādaśī (see the Vaikuṇṭha entry under Puṣya below) some North Indian calendars observe instead/also; both share the "Putradā" (child-granting) name nationwide. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shravana_Putrada_Ekadashi)
- **Aja Ēkādaśi** — Śrāvaṇa Kṛṣṇa (per this source's amanta labeling, corresponding to Bhādrapada Kṛṣṇa in pūrṇimānta lists). Worship of Nārāyaṇa as "Janārdana."
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Ajā Ēkādaśī** exactly — the calendar-convention shift is expected, not a different festival. (Source: https://www.iskcondelhi.com/full-year-ekadashi-list/)
- **Damōdara Dwādaśi** — Śrāvaṇa, annual. Viṣṇu worshipped as "Damōdara."
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Overlaps with the broader Kārtika/Dāmodara vrata (daily lamp-lighting at Tulasī through Kārtika) observed pan-India in Vaiṣṇava households; in Odisha the same Kārtika Śukla Dvādaśī is separately known as Garuḍa Dvādaśī. (Source: https://mayapurvoice.com/svagatam/famous-damodar-month-kartik-vrata-back/)
- **Jandhyala Purnima (Rakṣābandhan)** — Śrāvaṇa Pūrṇimā, annual, major. Sacred-thread renewal and Veda study for the twice-born; siblings tie the rakhi thread.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* One of the clearest North-vs-South contrasts in the whole calendar. In the Hindi belt and much of North/West India, this day is **Rakṣābandhan**. In Andhra Pradesh and Telangana it is **Jandhyala Pūrṇima**, and in Tamil Nadu **Avaṇi Aviṭṭam** (also Upākarma elsewhere in the South) — in both southern forms the central act is Brahmin men discarding their old sacred thread and donning a new one, not sibling bonding. Both share the same tithi but are otherwise distinct rituals. (Source: https://hindupad.com/jandhyala-purnima-or-janai-poornima-ritual-of-changing-sacred-thread-on-shravan-poornima/ ; https://www.omspiritualshop.com/blogs/news/shravana-purnima-2026)

### Recurring monthly observances
This month's two Ēkādaśīs are individually named above (Putraikādaśi/Śrāvaṇa Putradā; Aja
Ēkādaśi); Māsa Śivarātri, both Pradōṣams and the Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthī fall this month in generic
form — see Recurring Monthly-Type Observances above. (Every Monday of Śrāvaṇa additionally carries
its own Śiva-Somavāram significance and Tuesdays their Maṅgala Gaurī vrata, per Viśiṣṭata above —
these are weekday-linked, not tithi-linked, observances.)

## Bhādrapada Māsa

### Names & Basic Info
- **Telugu name**: Bhādrapada Māsam (భాద్రపద మాసం)
- **Sanskrit/IAST**: Bhādrapada (also called Proṣṭhapada)
- **Position**: 6th month of the Hindu lunisolar year; second of the four Cāturmāsya months
- **Gregorian overlap**: Roughly August–September (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhadrapada)
- **Season (ṛtu)**: Śarad ṛtu begins around this time (transition from Varṣā to Śarad)
- **Name origin**: Named for the Pūrva Bhādrapadā nakṣatra being near the Moon on the month's full-moon day. "Bhadra" also connotes auspiciousness/safety. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhadrapada ; https://divinestore.sanatanajourney.com/blogs/updates/bhadrapada-maas-2026-start-date-festival-calendar-niyam-rules)

### Viśiṣṭata (Significance)
Bhādrapada is dominated by two major devotional currents:

1. **Gaṇeśa/Vināyaka worship** — Vināyaka Chaturthi (Gaṇeśa Chaturthi), on Śukla Chaturthi, is celebrated as Gaṇeśa's birthday and is the most prominent festival of the month, involving installation and worship of Gaṇeśa idols culminating in visarjan on Ananta Chaturdaśī. (Source: https://hindupad.com/bhadrapada-masam-telugu-calendar/)
2. **Pitru Pakṣa / Mahālaya Pakṣa** — The Kṛṣṇa Pakṣa of Bhādrapada is set aside as Pitru Pakṣa, the most sacred period for śrāddha karma. Families perform tarpaṇam and śrāddha for departed ancestors, culminating in Mahālaya Amāvāsya (Sarva Pitru Amāvāsyā). (Source: https://hindupad.com/bhadrapada-masam-telugu-calendar/)

Other observances include Ṛṣi Pañcamī and Anantha Vratam (Ananta Chaturdaśī). (Source: https://hindupad.com/bhadrapad-month/)

### Purāṇa References
The Gaṇeśa Purāṇa is the key scriptural source for Bhādrapada's Gaṇeśa associations, particularly its Upāsanākhaṇḍa, chapter 62, on the moon's curse and its remedy — directly tied to Bhādrapada Śukla Chaturthi observance. (Source: https://www.chivukulas.com/2014/04/story-of-lord-ganesha-and-curse-of-moon.html) No single dedicated "Bhādrapada Māhātmyam" was found. The month's sanctity rests mainly on festival-specific purāṇic episodes and the broader Pitru Pakṣa tradition (Garuḍa Purāṇa/dharmaśāstra material generally). This is a genuine gap, and it has not been confirmed by deeper source-checking.

### Stories/Legends
The defining legend is **Gaṇeśa's curse on the Moon (Chandra)**. After a feast of modakas, Gaṇeśa fell off his mouse-vehicle riding home; Chandra, watching from the sky, laughed at him. Wounded, Gaṇeśa cursed the Moon: whoever looks upon it on Bhādrapada Śukla Chaturthi would be falsely accused of theft ("mithyā doṣa"). Chandra pleaded for mercy, and Gaṇeśa limited the curse's effect to that one day each year, giving rise to the still-observed custom of avoiding moon-viewing on Vināyaka Chaturthi (and listening to the Śyamantaka-maṇi story of Kṛṣṇa as an antidote if one does see it). (Source: https://www.vedantu.com/stories/ganesha-curses-the-moon ; https://www.chivukulas.com/2014/04/story-of-lord-ganesha-and-curse-of-moon.html)

### Parva-dinas this month
- **Undralla Tadiya (Modaka Tṛtīya)** — Bhādrapada Tṛtīya, annual. Women fast till evening, worship Gowri Devi with sixteen upacāras, and offer rice-flour balls (undrallu) to other women.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Narrowly regional, Telugu-specific (Andhra Pradesh and Telangana only); also called Brihatyuma Vratam. No pan-India equivalent name found; a localized women's/girls' vrata for Gauri-Shiva, parallel in spirit to Aṭla Tadiya (see Āśvayuja below) but a separate, smaller observance. (Source: https://hindusphere.com/undrala-undralla-taddi-vrat-katha/)
- **Vināyaka Chaviti (Vinayaka/Ganesh Chaturthi)** — Bhādrapada Śukla Caturthī, annual, the year's principal Gaṇapati festival. Siddhi Vināyaka worshipped in a clay image with 21 modakas/upacāras; the "arghya to Agastya" custom also tied to this window. (Gaṇeśa's moon-curse legend, the reason moon-viewing is avoided this day, is told under Stories above.)
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India, India's most widely celebrated Ganesha festival, with real regional variation in scale: Maharashtra (especially Mumbai/Pune) is famous for massive 10-day public pandals with community immersion (Mumbai alone immerses roughly 150,000 statues annually); Tamil Nadu emphasizes home/temple worship with kozhukattai sweets; Karnataka favors intimate home worship; Telangana/Andhra Pradesh is known for landmark large idols like Hyderabad's Khairatabad Ganesh. (Source: https://thefederal.com/category/news/ganesh-chaturthi-how-indian-states-celebrate-birth-of-lord-ganesha-203417)
- **Ṛṣi Pañcami** — Bhādrapada Śukla Pañcamī, annual. Women worship the Saptarṣis (with their wives) for release from menstrual-cycle lapses, with charity of bananas, ghee, sugar, money.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Wider than Telugu-only: kept in Maharashtra, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Nepal and South India, with Maharashtra's distinctive unspiced "Rishi Panchami Bhaji" eaten to mark ritual purity, and Maharashtra/Gujarat also worshipping wild herbs/vegetables. The underlying purpose is consistent across regions. (Source: https://curlytales.com/india/food/rishi-panchami-2025-significance-rituals-rishi-chi-bhaji-a-21-vegetable-recipe-made-on-this-day/)
- **Sūrya Ṣaṣṭhī (Skanda Darśanam)** — Bhādrapada Śukla Ṣaṣṭhī, annual. Pre-sunrise bath and Sūrya worship; Subrahmaṇya also honored.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* No strong independent sourcing distinguishing this observance from the broader Chhath-adjacent Surya-Sashti family (Chhath Puja itself falls on Kartika Sashti in Bihar/UP/Nepal — a separate, much larger North Indian/Bihari festival). The Bhādrapada, Subrahmanya-linked combination here appears to be a regional Telugu/Andhra observance and should not be conflated with Chhath. This point is uncertain. (Source: general knowledge cross-check, no single dedicated URL found.)
- **Āmuktābharaṇa Santāna Saptami Vratam** — Bhādrapada Saptamī, annual. Full-day fast and evening worship of Gowri-Parameśvara for progeny and children's welfare. (No independent Pan-India/regional-notes sub-bullet found in the source.)
- **Dūrvāṣṭami (Rādhāṣṭami)** — Bhādrapada Śukla Aṣṭamī, annual. Gaṇapati worshipped with dūrvā grass; Rādhādevī and Gowridevi honored with seven kinds of flowers/fruits.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Rādhāṣṭamī itself is a major North Indian/Braj festival, centered on Barsana and Vrindavan, with nine-day celebrations culminating in a palanquin procession — quite different in scale from this source's modest dūrvā-grass Gaṇapati plus Rādhā/Gowri flower-worship framing, which looks like a South Indian/Andhra overlay onto the same tithi. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radhashtami ; https://iskcontemple.org/en/blogs/how-radha-ashtami-celebrated-home-temples-and-barsana-vrindavan)
- **Avidhava Navami** — Bhādrapada Pitṛpakṣa Navamī, annual. Special śrāddham for women who died as "Sumaṅgali" (predeceasing their husband).
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* This source's own note that it is "more prevalent in North India" is confirmed: part of Pitru Paksha shraddha rites for Sumangali women, performed by the eldest son. Timing differs across regions — North India in Ashwin Krishna Paksha, while Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh observe the equivalent rite in Bhadrapada Krishna Paksha. A pan-India Pitru Paksha custom (also Matru Navami/Avidhava Shraddha), though the underlying Pitru Paksha framework is much more elaborately marked in North India (Gaya, Varanasi, Prayag). (Source: https://www.hindu-blog.com/2009/09/what-is-avidhava-navami-how-to-perform.html)
- **Aindra Ēkādaśī (Dharmaprabha Ēkādaśī)** — Bhādrapada Kṛṣṇa. Giving oil-seeds in charity; linked to Satya Hariścandra regaining his wife, son and kingdom.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* In the standard pūrṇimānta cycle, this slot (Āśvayuja/Āśvina Kṛṣṇa Ēkādaśī) is universally known as **Indirā Ēkādaśī** — dedicated to the departed and King Indrasena per the Padma Purāṇa. "Aindra" is simply the adjectival Sanskrit form of Indra/Indirā, the same vrata under the amanta month-label; "Dharmaprabha" is a secondary/local title tied to Satya Hariścandra's story less commonly attached to Indirā Ēkādaśī outside Telugu sources. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ekadashi ; https://www.iskcondelhi.com/full-year-ekadashi-list/)
- **Parivartana/Dāna Ēkādaśī** — Bhādrapada Śukla. The central day of Cāturmāsya, when Nārāyaṇa is said to turn onto his other side.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Parivartinī/Vāmana Ēkādaśī** exactly — nationally known as the day Viṣṇu, resting through Cāturmāsya, turns onto his other side; widely called Vāmana Ēkādaśī after the avatāra story and Dāna Ēkādaśī for its prescribed almsgiving, both pan-India, not regionally distinct, names. (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Vāmana Jayanti** — Bhādrapada Śukla Dvādaśī, annual, when it coincides with Śravaṇa nakṣatra. Marks Viṣṇu's Vāmana incarnation.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A pan-India Vaiṣṇava jayanti tied to Bhādrapada Śukla Dvādaśī coinciding with Śravaṇa nakṣatra; celebrated with Pañcopacāra/Ṣōḍaśōpacāra pūjā of a Vāmana mūrti wherever Vaiṣṇava temples mark daśāvatāra days, without a distinct separate regional name found. (Source: https://www.freepressjournal.in/lifestyle/vamana-jayanti-2025-know-date-rituals-significance-auspicious-time)
- **Anantapadmanābha Svāmi Vratam (Ananta Caturdaśī)** — Bhādrapada Śukla Caturdaśī, annual. Worship of Anantapadmanābha reclining on the serpent-bed with Mahālakṣmī; a 14-knot thread is tied. (Also the day this month's installed Vināyaka idols, above, are traditionally immersed.)
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India as **Anant Chaturdashi**, with the knotted Ananta-sūtra thread ritual common everywhere, but its cultural weight differs sharply — in Maharashtra it is inseparable from Ganeśa Visarjan, the climactic immersion closing the ten-day Gaṇēśotsava (massive public processions), while in Gujarat/Rajasthan it stays a quieter home Anant-Viṣṇu-pūjā day, closer to this source's framing. (Source: https://www.easeindiatrip.com/blog/anant-chaturdashi-puja-rituals-celebration/)
- **Umāmahēśvara Vratam / Mahālaya Pakṣa Prārambham** — Bhādrapada Pūrṇimā, annual. Opens the 15-day Mahālaya Pitṛupakṣa for ancestor rites, alongside Umā-Maheśvara worship.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* The vrata's exact date varies by regional calendar — Kannada tradition places it on Bhādrapada Pūrṇimā (matching this source), Tamil calendar places the equivalent in Puraṭṭāci, and some communities observe it on Kārtikai Pūrṇimā instead. The Mahālaya-Pitṛpakṣa opening is broadly pan-India, though in Bengal Mahālaya is understood chiefly through its Amāvāsyā end-point (Durgā Pūjā countdown) rather than this Pūrṇimā start-point. (Source: https://madhwasakha.com/ritual/uma-maheshwara-vrata/832)
- **Mahālaya / Pōlāla Amāvāsya** — Bhādrapada Amāvāsyā, annual. Closing day of Mahālaya Pitṛpakṣa; tarpaṇa offered to ancestors, and "Pōlēramma" (a unified Mahākāḷi-Lakṣmi-Sarasvatī form) worshipped in some families.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* The Amāvāsyā closing Pitṛpakṣa is observed widely, especially in West Bengal, Assam, Odisha and Karnataka, as Sarvapitrī/Mahālaya Amāvāsyā, with tarpaṇa offered to ancestors. Bengal's distinctive is that Mahālaya is primarily experienced as the Durgā Pūjā countdown (the iconic 1931 All India Radio "Mahiṣāsura Mardini" broadcast) rather than an ancestor-rites day. No separate source corroborates the Telugu "Pōlēramma" name specifically — appears to be a local Andhra family tradition. (Source: https://www.dwsjewellery.com/blog/mahalaya-amavasya-the-spiritual-prelude-to-durga-puja/)

### Recurring monthly observances
This month's two Ēkādaśīs are individually named above (Aindra/Dharmaprabha Ēkādaśī; Parivartana/
Dāna Ēkādaśī); Māsa Śivarātri and both Pradōṣams fall this month in generic form — see Recurring
Monthly-Type Observances above. Note that this month's Śukla Caturdaśī (Ananta Caturdaśī, above)
is a Śukla-pakṣa observance, not the Kṛṣṇa-pakṣa Māsa Śivarātri date.

## Āśvayuja (Āśvina) Māsa

### Names & Basic Info
- **Telugu name**: Āśvayuja Māsam (ఆశ్వయుజ మాసం)
- **Sanskrit/IAST**: Āśvina / Āśvayuja
- **Position**: 7th month of the Hindu lunisolar year (Telugu panchangam)
- **Gregorian overlap**: Roughly September–October (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashvin_(month))
- **Season (ṛtu)**: Śarad ṛtu (autumn)
- **Name origin**: Named for the Aśvinī nakṣatra being near the Moon at the month's full moon. (Source: https://hindupad.com/ashwayuja-masam-in-telugu-calendar/)

### Viśiṣṭata (Significance)
Āśvayuja is above all the month of **Śarada (Śāradīya) Navarātri / Durgā worship**, considered the best time of the year to worship Goddess Durgā. The nine nights from Āśvayuja Śukla Padyami to Daśamī are dedicated to the nine forms (Navadurgā) of the Goddess, culminating in Vijayadaśamī (Dasara). (Source: https://hindusphere.com/significance-of-aswayuja-ashwayuja-ashwini-masam/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayadashami)

The month is described as "more important for the followers of Durgā Devī" than any other. It also ends with Āśvayuja Amāvāsyā, which in most of the Telugu-speaking region marks the eve of Dīpāvali. (Source: https://hindupad.com/ashwayuja-masam-in-telugu-calendar/)

### Purāṇa References
The scriptural bedrock of Āśvayuja's Navarātri worship is the **Devī Māhātmyam** (also Durgā Saptaśatī, Caṇḍī, or Caṇḍī Pāṭha), found within the **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa**, chapters 81–93, composed roughly 400–500 CE. It comprises 700 verses across 13 chapters describing the Goddess's victories over demons, most famously Mahiṣāsura. Ritual recitation during Navarātri is itself a central Āśvayuja practice. This is the closest any month besides Kārtika comes to having its own dedicated māhātmya. Strictly speaking, though, the text glorifies the Goddess, not the calendar month. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_Mahatmya ; https://www.exoticindiaart.com/blog/shree-durga-saptashati/ ; https://www.hindu-blog.com/2008/09/devi-mahatmya-sapta-sati-chandi-patha.html)

### Stories/Legends
- **Durgā's slaying of Mahiṣāsura**, narrated across the Devī Māhātmyam, is the central legend underpinning the nine-night worship, celebrated on Mahiṣāsuramardinī day (typically Mahāṣṭamī/Navamī).
- **Rāma's victory over Rāvaṇa**, commemorated on Vijayadaśamī, is closely associated with this month in North Indian tradition (Rāma is said to have performed Durgā pūjā for nine days before the battle). (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayadashami)

### Parva-dinas this month
- **Śarannavarātri begins (Kalaśa Sthāpana)** — Āśvayuja Śukla Pratipada, annual. First of the nine nights of Śaradṛtu Devī worship; coincides with Tirumala's Navarātri Brahmōtsavams.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India, but regional expression varies sharply: in West Bengal, Assam, Odisha and Tripura it culminates as Durga Puja with clay-idol pandals and immersion; in Gujarat it centers on Garba/Dandiya Raas; the Kalasha Sthapana (Ghatasthapana) opening rite is common across most regions but not the emphasis everywhere. (Source: https://www.kolkatadurgotsav.com/regional-names-of-durga.html)
- **Prīti Dvitīya** — Āśvayuja Śukla Dvitīya, during Navarātri, annual. Minor Navarātri day (also coincides with Tirumala Brahmōtsavam flag-hoisting in some years).
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Essentially the second day of Śarad Navarātri (worship of Devī Brahmacharini), so it inherits Navarātri's regional variation: extended recitation in North India, Garba in Gujarat, Golu (doll) display in Tamil Nadu. As "Prīti Dvitīya" tied specifically to Tirumala Brahmōtsavam flag-hoisting, it is a Telugu/temple-specific label. (Source: https://www.zalkari.com/blogs/zalkari-tales/2nd-day-of-navratri)
- **Aṭla Tadiya (Candrōdaya Umā Vratam)** — Āśvayuja Kṛṣṇa Tṛtīya, annual. Young unmarried women's day-long fast and evening Gaurī vratam, with arghya to the moon on moonrise; sought for a good husband.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Regional, not pan-India: mainly Andhra Pradesh and Telangana; described as "the Telugu equivalent of Karva Chauth." Distinctive local practices include swinging on an uyyala and eating atlu (rice pancakes) to break the fast. (Source: https://hindupad.com/atla-tadde-2011-atla-thadde-gowri-vratam-in-andhra-pradesh/)
- **Upāṅga Lalitā Gauri Vratam / Nāga Pūja** — Āśvayuja Śukla Pañcamī, during Navarātri, annual. Lalitādēvi worshipped as "Pañcamī/Pañcabhūtēśī"; paired serpent worship relieves snake-doṣa.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Regional emphasis rather than uniformly pan-India: Upang Lalita Vrat (5th day of Śarad Navarātri) is most popular in Gujarat and parts of Maharashtra, with South Indian traditions instead identifying Lalitā more with Chandi. This source's "Pañcabhūtēśī"/serpent-doṣa framing reflects a Telugu-specific inflection. (Source: https://www.oneindia.com/lifestyle/upang-lalita-vrat-celebration-2025-011-7870371.html)
- **Sarasvatī Pūja (of books)** — Āśvayuja Śukla Ṣaṣṭhī on Mūla nakṣatra, during Navarātri, annual. Students worship their books/Sarasvatī for educational success.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Widespread but with real regional variation in timing: Tamil Nadu and Kerala do it on the 9th day (Navami) with Āyudha Pūjā, while Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh observe it on the 10th day (Vijayadashami). Kerala additionally uses this period for Vidyārambham, initiating young children into writing. Bengal's Saraswati Puja proper is a separate, spring (Vasant Panchami) festival. (Source: https://www.prokerala.com/festivals/saraswati-pooja.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayudha_Puja)
- **Durgāṣṭamī (Mahāṣṭami)** — Āśvayuja Śukla Aṣṭamī, during Navarātri, annual, major. Worship of Durgā said to remove all troubles; central Navarātri day.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* One of the most substantively regional festivals in the calendar. In West Bengal, the emotional peak of Durga Puja (pandal-hopping, dhunuchi dance, anjali offerings). In North India, Kanya Pujan (Kanjak) — young girls worshipped as living forms of the Devi — largely absent in the south. In Karnataka/Andhra it converges with Saraswati Puja and Ayudha Puja; in Maharashtra/Gujarat the night is peak Garba/Dandiya. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Ashtami ; https://blog.shloka.life/durga-ashtami-2025-rituals-significance-and-celebration-guide/)
- **Mahānavamī (Dasara)** — Āśvayuja Śukla Navamī, annual, major. Principal day of Navarātri Dasara pūjā, worship of Jagadamba.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Major pan-India day, historically the state festival of the Vijayanagara Empire (celebrated grandly at Hampi). Today it converges with regional customs — in Bengal, the penultimate day of Durga Puja before visarjan; in Mysuru Dasara, the Wadiyar royal family's Ayudha Puja involves worship of golden weapons; in many regions vehicles, tools and machinery are ritually washed (Ayudha Puja proper). (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayadashami)
- **Vijaya Daśamī (Dasara)** — Āśvayuja Śukla Daśamī, annual, major. Jagadamba worshipped a second time; Āyudha Pūja, Śamī-tree worship at dusk, and the "Vijayā" muhūrta.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* The classic case of one tithi, many distinct regional festivals. North India centers on Ravana Dahan (burning towering effigies) after weeks of Ramlila. Mysuru Dasara (Karnataka) centers on Goddess Chamundeshwari and the Jamboo Savari elephant procession. Kullu Dussehra (Himachal) uniquely begins on this day. In Bengal, "Bijoya Dashami" is the sorrowful/joyous conclusion of Durga Puja with idol immersion and sindoor khela. The source's Āyudha Pūjā/Śamī-tree/Vijaya-muhūrta framing reflects South Indian (Telugu/Kannada) practice. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayadashami ; https://www.editorji.com/lifestyle-news/from-vijayadashami-to-mysuru-dasara-5-unique-dussehra-celebrations-across-india-1728543926541)
- **Ramā Ēkādaśī** — Āśvayuja Kṛṣṇa. Vrata dedicated to Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name exactly — **Rāma Ēkādaśī** (Kārtika Kṛṣṇa in pūrṇimānta terms, just before Dīpāvalī) is dedicated to Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa, observed the same way under the same name across North and South India, often as the fast preceding Diwali. (Source: https://www.iskcondelhi.com/full-year-ekadashi-list/)
- **Pāśāṅkuśa Ēkādaśī (Parāṅkuśa/Ramyaikādaśī)** — Āśvayuja Śukla. Protects the observer "like by a goad"; if dedicated to the departed, uplifts them from hell.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Standard pan-India name is **Pāpāṅkuśā Ēkādaśī**, and "Pāśāṅkuśa/Parāṅkuśa" is a well-attested phonetic variant (aṅkuśa = elephant-goad, matching the source's own gloss). This is a Śukla-pakṣa entry so the month-label lines up directly with pan-India lists. (Source: https://www.iskcondelhi.com/full-year-ekadashi-list/ ; https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Gōvatsa Dvādaśī (Padmanābha Dvādaśī)** — Āśvayuja Śukla/Kṛṣṇa, annual, often paired with Dhana Trayōdaśī. Cow-and-calf worship, Anantha Padmanābha vratam.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* In Maharashtra this day is **Vasu Baras**, opening day of the Diwali cluster; in Gujarat, **Vagh Baras** ("repayment of debts"); similar days elsewhere are called Bachh Baras. The core ritual (cow-and-calf worship) is shared, but in the north/west it is explicitly the first of the five Diwali days, while this source treats it as a standalone Dvādaśī vrata. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govatsa_Dwadashi)
- **Dhana Trayōdaśī** — Āśvayuja Kṛṣṇa, annual, part of the Dīpāvaḷi cluster. Dhanvantari and Kubēra-Lakṣmī worshipped for health and wealth.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Nationally known as **Dhanteras/Dhantrayodashi**, the opening day of the five-day Diwali cluster almost everywhere. Regional emphasis differs: North and West India foreground Lakṣmī-Kubēra wealth worship and buying new utensils/gold; the Telugu/South Indian observance foregrounds **Dhanvantari** and health/Āyurveda themes more than wealth. Karnataka: Dhantrayodashi/Dhanatrayodashi; Kerala: Dhanatrisha; Karnataka also calls it "neeru tumbo habba." (Source: https://indiablog.in/dhanteras ; https://trimbakeshwartemplepujari.com/dhanteras/)
- **Naraka Chaturdaśī** — Āśvayuja Kṛṣṇa Caturdaśī, annual, part of Dīpāvaḷi. Marks the killing of the demon Naraka and removal of hell's torments; head bath and tarpaṇa to Yama. (This is also this month's Kṛṣṇa-pakṣa Caturdaśī date — its own Māsa Śivarātri instance coincides with this bigger, individually-named festival; see Recurring monthly observances below.)
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* One of the most regionally divergent Diwali-cluster days. In Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and Karnataka, Naraka Chaturdaśī (pre-dawn Abhyaṅga Snān) is the **principal** Diwali day, ahead of Lakṣmī Pūjā. In North India it is demoted to "Choṭī Dīpāvaḷī," a lead-in to the main Lakṣmī-Pūjā night. In Gujarat, Rajasthan and Maharashtra it is "Kālī Chaudas," also linked to harvest themes. (Source: https://theindianbugle.com/naraka-chaturdashi-2025-date-significance-rituals-choti-diwali/)
- **Kaumudī / Śarat Pūrṇimā (with Vālmīki Jayanti)** — Āśvayuja Pūrṇimā, annual. Kōjāgarī vratam to Lakṣmī (rice pudding, staying awake, dice-play); also Vālmīki's birth anniversary.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Called **Kōjāgarī Pūrṇimā**, observed most prominently in Bengal, Odisha and Maharashtra with all-night Lakṣmī worship; in Braj/Vrindavan the same full moon is instead **Rās Pūrṇimā**, commemorating Kṛṣṇa's Mahā-Rās with the gopīs — a distinct devotional emphasis from the Lakṣmī-vrata framing used here. Vālmīki Jayanti is layered onto the same day nationally. (Source: https://www.sanatanavibes.in/2025/09/kojagiri-puja-worship-of-goddess.html)
- **Dīpāvaḷi (Naraka Chaturdaśī's Amāvāsyā)** — Āśvayuja Amāvāsyā, annual, major. Head bath, lamp and Lakṣmī worship at evening pradōṣa; fireworks; Kāḷī worship also traditional in some regions.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* The main Lakṣmī-Pūjā night is pan-India but regionally distinct. West Bengal centers this night on **Kālī Pūjā** — a genuinely different deity-focus, not just a variant ritual. Gujarat treats the day as the start of the business new year (**Chopdā Pūjā**); North India centers the story on Rāma's return to Ayōdhya. The following days also vary regionally — Govardhan Pūjā/Annakūṭ the next day is a major North Indian observance largely absent from the South, and Bhāī Dūj closes the cluster in the north while the Telugu cluster does not foreground either. (Source: https://swadharma.in/kali-puja-vs-lakshmi-puja-why-diwali/ ; https://www.newindiaabroad.com/english/news/unique-traditions-of-diwali-celebrations-across-india)

### Recurring monthly observances
This month's two Ēkādaśīs are individually named above (Ramā Ēkādaśī; Pāśāṅkuśa Ēkādaśī); its
Kṛṣṇa-pakṣa Caturdaśī (this month's Māsa Śivarātri date) coincides with the bigger, individually
named Naraka Chaturdaśī above; both Pradōṣams and the Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthī fall this month in
generic form — see Recurring Monthly-Type Observances above.

## Kārtika Māsa

### Names & Basic Info
- **Telugu name**: Kārtika Māsam (కార్తీక మాసం)
- **Sanskrit/IAST**: Kārtika
- **Position**: 8th month of the Hindu lunisolar year (Telugu panchangam)
- **Gregorian overlap**: Roughly October–November (2026 Deepotsava period cited as Oct 26 – Nov 24, 2026) (Source: https://www.iskconbangalore.org/deepotsava/)
- **Season (ṛtu)**: Hemanta ṛtu begins around this time
- **Name origin**: Named for the Kṛttikā nakṣatra near the Moon at the month's full moon. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kartika_(month))

### Viśiṣṭata (Significance) — In Depth
Kārtika is widely regarded as the single most glorified and spiritually potent month in the Hindu calendar, uniquely dear to **both** Śiva and Viṣṇu.

**Dāmodara Māsa**: The month is also called **Dāmodara Māsa**, especially dear to Kṛṣṇa in his Dāmodara form (from "dāma," rope, + "udara," belly, commemorating Yaśodā tying the child Kṛṣṇa's belly with a rope). Vaiṣṇavas observe "Ūrjā-vrata" throughout the month, offering a ghee lamp daily to Lord Dāmodara. (Source: https://iskconbhilai.org/why-lighting-the-dipa-pleases-lord-damodara/ ; https://www.dandavats.com/?p=4656)

**The Lamp-Lighting (Dīpa Dāna) Tradition**: Kārtika is the month of lamps. The Skanda Purāṇa states that offering even a single lamp to Keśava during Kārtika liberates ancestors and destroys sins accumulated across "many thousands and millions of births ... in half an eye-blink." Lighting lamps throughout the month — especially on Kārtika Pūrṇimā (Kārtika Dīpam, tied also to the bonfire lit atop Tirumala/Arunachala hills) — is considered one of the most meritorious acts a devotee can perform all year. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc371648.html ; https://kausiki.com/product/skanda-purana-vaishnava-khanda-kartika-masa-mahatmya/)

**Kārtika Pūrṇimā — dear to both deities at once**: celebrated as **Tripurāri Pūrṇimā** by Śaivas (Śiva's victory over Tripurāsura) and simultaneously honored by Vaiṣṇavas as the day of Viṣṇu's Matsya avatāra. Śiva temples often perform joint pūjā honoring Viṣṇu alongside Śiva — a concrete liturgical expression of the month's Hari-Hara character. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kartika_Purnima ; https://www.astrologerumesh.com/blog/kartik-purnima/)

Because of this dual glorification, vratas, dāna, snāna (Kārtika Snānam), fasting, and tulasī worship performed in Kārtika are held to yield far greater fruit than the same acts performed in any other month.

### Purāṇa References
Kārtika is the only one of these months with a genuine, well-documented, dedicated māhātmya literature:
- **Skanda Purāṇa, Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa**: contains a "Kārtika-māsa-māhātmya" section; the lamp-offering passage is explicitly "the seventh chapter of the Kārttikamāsa-māhātmya." (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc371648.html)
- **Padma Purāṇa, Uttara Khaṇḍa**: an extensive Kārtika Māhātmya spanning chapters 93–124, framed as Nārada travelling to Mount Mandara to ask Śambhu (Śiva) about the month's secret glory. (Source: https://kmlmotwani.wordpress.com/2014/04/09/kartika-mahatmya/)
- The **"Kārtika Purāṇam"** widely circulated in Telugu, Kannada, and Odia households is a popularized compilation drawing on this Skanda/Padma material, reorganized for daily household reading across the month. (Source: https://ritikart.com/products/odia-kartika-purana ; https://greatertelugu.org/karthika-puranam-telugu-pdf/)

### Stories/Legends
- **The recitation tradition itself**: In Telugu (and broader South/East Indian) households, the Kārtika Purāṇam is read aloud, one chapter/story per day across the month, believed itself highly meritorious. (Source: https://www.hindutemplesguide.com/2019/10/karthika-puranam-31-temples-guide.html)
- **The story of Ajāmila** (Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Canto 6): a fallen brāhmaṇa saved at the moment of death because he cries out "Nārāyaṇa" (calling for his son of that name) — illustrating that even unintentional utterance of the divine name grants liberation. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajamila ; https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-bhagavata-purana/d/doc1127145.html)
- **Tripurāsura-vadha** (Śiva's slaying of Tripurāsura), culminating on Kārtika Pūrṇimā. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kartika_Purnima)
- **Matsya avatāra** of Viṣṇu, also placed on Kārtika Pūrṇimā in some traditions, ties the month to the first of Viṣṇu's daśāvatāras and the primordial saving of Manu and the Vedas. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kartika_Purnima)

### Parva-dinas this month
- **Balipāḍyami / Gōvardhana Pūja** — start of Kārtika māsa (day after Dīpāvali Amāvāsya), annual. Worship of Gōvardhana hill with a cow-dung replica; also commemorates Viṣṇu's Vāmana-Bali story.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India under many regional names for two overlapping observances. Govardhan Puja (Krishna lifting the hill) is mainly North Indian, especially around Mathura/Braj. Bali Pratipada/Padyami (Vamana-Bali story) is the name used in Karnataka, Maharashtra (Bali Padwa) and Andhra Pradesh; other names include Barlaj (Himachal), Raja Bali (Jammu), and it marks the Gujarati/Marwari New Year (Bestu Varas) in western India. (Source: https://www.krazzymag.com/significance-of-balipratipada-govardhan-puja-and-padwa/)
- **Yama Pūja / Bhaginihasta Bhōjana (Bhai Dooj)** — Kārtika Śukla Dvitīya, annual. Siblings' festival: eating food served by one's sister, gifting her clothes; worship of Yama wards off hell's torments.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India with many regional names: Bhaiya Dooj (UP, Bihar), Bhau Beej (Maharashtra, Goa, Gujarat, Karnataka), Yama Dwitiya (South India), Bhai Phota (Bengal, day after Kali Puja), Bhai Tika (Nepal, with a Maithili Bhardutiya variant). The core ritual — sister feeding/blessing brother for longevity — is shared everywhere. (Source: https://www.memeraki.com/blogs/posts/regional-flavors-of-bhai-dooj-a-festive-tribute-to-sibling-connections)
- **Nāgula Chaviti** — Kārtika Śukla Caturthī, annual. Milk and rice-flour/sesame sweets offered at anthills to the serpent gods; no cutting vegetables or using blades this day.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Regional, not pan-India: a Telugu serpent-worship day (Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, with some observance in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu), distinct from Nāga Pañcami (see below), the analogous festival kept mainly in North/West India during Śrāvaṇa — two separate festivals honoring the same deities on different tithis/months. (Source: https://grokipedia.com/page/Nagula_Chavithi)
- **Nāga Pañcami (Garuḍa Pañcami)** — recurs almost every month across the corpus (Kārtika, Mārgaśira, Śrāvaṇa, etc.), most emphasized in Kārtika per this source. Worship of Garuḍa/serpent gods by offering milk at anthills; farmers observe it for a bumper harvest.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Genuinely pan-India, though the exact paksha and local ritual form vary: most of India keeps it in Śrāvaṇa Śukla Pañcamī, but Karnataka, Rajasthan, Bihar and Gujarat mark it in Krishna Paksha instead. Kerala's snake groves host Sarpabali and Sarpam Thullal; Maharashtra features women drawing snake images at doorways; Gujarat frames snakes as crop protectors; UP/Bihar hold fairs; Karnataka/Tamil Nadu have married women fasting for family welfare. One of the more genuinely nationwide festivals in this list, unlike the Telugu-specific Nāgula Chaviti above. (Source: https://www.exoticindiaart.com/article/naga-panchami-the-serpent-in-story-symbol-and-sacred-ritual/)
- **Agnipūja / Mahā Ṣaṣṭhi (Subrahmaṇyārādhana)** — Kārtika Śukla Ṣaṣṭhī, annual. Fire/lamp worship combined with worship of Subrahmaṇya as "Agnigarbha."
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Part of the same broader Subrahmanya Sashti tradition celebrated "with immense fervour" in Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh (abhishekams, fasts, processions). No distinct North Indian name or parallel found — a regional South Indian Śaiva/Kaumāra observance. (Source: https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/andhra-pradesh/subrahmanya-sashti-festival-celebrated-grandly-1845004)
- **Gōṣṭhāṣṭami Vratam (Kārtavīryōtpatti)** — Kārtika Śukla Aṣṭamī, annual. Cow worship and circumambulation for long life/prosperity; also Kārtavīrya's (Dattātrēya devotee) birthday, invoked for recovering lost items.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* The cow-worship component (Gopashtami) is a major North Indian festival, celebrated in Mathura, Vrindavan and Braj, marking young Krishna's first day tending cows after the Govardhan episode; Maharashtra has an analogous Govatsa Dwadashi on a different tithi. The Kārtavīrya/Dattātreya-devotee component is a more localized South Indian/Andhra tradition layered onto the same tithi. (Source: https://www.tirthayatra.org/gopashtami-todays-festival-of-cow-worship-and-krishnas-gopa-lila/)
- **Akṣaya Navamī Vratam** — Kārtika Śukla Navamī, annual. River bathing, worship and charity are held to give inexhaustible ("akṣaya") results.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Also widely called Amla/Aonla Navami or Kushmanda Navami, observed across North India, West Bengal and Odisha with community feasts under an amla tree, believed to purify food and body. In West Bengal this same day is also Jagaddhatri Puja, a major goddess festival (especially in Chandannagar and Krishnanagar). (Source: https://www.myzodiaq.in/en/online-library/festivals-and-astrology/festival-muhurat-rituals-and-customs/akshaya-navami-2025-date-significance-auspicious-timing-and-rituals)
- **Uttpanna Ēkādaśī** — Kārtika Kṛṣṇa. Marks the day the presiding deity of Ēkādaśī itself manifested.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Utpannā Ēkādaśī** (Mārgaśīrṣa/Kārtika Kṛṣṇa depending on convention) exactly — universally the day the presiding devī of Ēkādaśī manifested from Viṣṇu to slay the demon Mura. (Source: https://www.iskcondelhi.com/full-year-ekadashi-list/)
- **Utthāna/Ananta Ēkādaśī (Prabodhini)** — Kārtika Śukla. Viṣṇu rises from yōganidrā; opens Bhīṣma Pañcaka Vratam.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* One of the biggest pan-India Ēkādaśīs. Standard name **Prabodhinī/Devutthāna/Dev-Uṭhanī Ēkādaśī** — the day Viṣṇu awakens, ending Cāturmāsya and opening the wedding season; inseparably linked to **Tulasī Vivāha** performed that evening across North and South India alike. "Bhīṣma Pañcaka Vratam" beginning here is a South Indian/Smārta elaboration on the same pan-India tithi. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prabodhini_Ekadashi)
- **Kṣīrābdhi Dvādaśī (Haribōdhini/Tulasī Kalyāṇam)** — Kārtika Śukla, annual, major. Commemorates the churning of the milky ocean; ritual marriage of Viṣṇu and Tulasī.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India as **Tulsi Vivah**, right after Dev Uthani/Prabodhini Ekādaśī. In UP and Rajasthan households erect a full wedding maṇḍapa for the Tulasī plant, treating it as the ceremonial start of the north Indian wedding season — a scale of domestic ritual theater not emphasized in the Telugu tradition's framing of it as Kṣīrābdhi Dvādaśī plus the Viṣṇu-Tulasī wedding. In Karnataka, Tulasi Habba; in Odisha, also Uttana Dwadashi. (Source: https://imvoyager.com/tulasi-vivaha-tulsi-pooja-uttana-dwadashi/ ; https://zeenews.india.com/culture/tulsi-vivah-2025-check-date-time-tithi-puja-vidhi-muhurat-and-regional-significance-of-this-auspicious-hindu-festival-2977239.html)
- **Vaikuṇṭha Caturdaśī** — Kārtika Śukla Caturdaśī, annual. Worship of Viṣṇu as Padmanābha; bathing at Maṇikarṇika Ghāṭ and Śiva abhiṣēkam both prescribed.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Real regional spread — prominently in Maharashtra, and at Gayā, Varanasi and Rishikesh, where the Manikarṇikā Ghāṭ bathing/Śiva-Viṣṇu union myth is central; in Varanasi it precedes Dēv Dīpāvaḷī. Functions more as a pilgrimage-town/temple-town observance than a domestic vrata everywhere. (Source: https://www.oneindia.com/lifestyle/vaikuntha-chaturdashi-hindu-festival-significance-011-3986673.html)
- **Kārtika Pūrṇimā (Mahākārtīki, Kṛttikā Dīpōtsavam)** — Kārtika Pūrṇimā, annual, major. Usirika Puja, charity and extensive lamp-lighting (story above — Tripurāri Pūrṇimā/Matsya-avatāra narrative told under Stories); especially meritorious when Pūrṇimā spans sunrise.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* As **Karthika Deepam** it is the most important festival of the month in Andhra/Tamil Nadu; in Varanasi the same night is **Dēv Dīpāvaḷī**, when ghats are lined with lamps; in Rajasthan, Kārtika Pūrṇimā is when the **Puṣkar Mela** (Asia's largest camel fair) concludes with a holy bath in Puṣkar Lake. The tithi is pan-India but its public expression differs completely by region. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dev_Deepavali_(Varanasi))

### Recurring monthly observances
This month's two Ēkādaśīs are individually named above (Uttpanna Ēkādaśī; Utthāna/Ananta/
Prabodhini Ēkādaśī, the bigger of the two); its Śukla Caturdaśī (Vaikuṇṭha Caturdaśī, above) is a
Śukla-pakṣa observance, not the Kṛṣṇa-pakṣa Māsa Śivarātri date, which falls this month in generic
form along with both Pradōṣams and the Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthī — see Recurring Monthly-Type
Observances above.

## Mārgaśira (Mārgaśīrṣa)

### Names & Basic Info
- **Telugu name**: Mārgaśira Māsam (మార్గశిర మాసం), also colloquially called **Dhanurmāsam** for its final portion when the sun transits Sagittarius (Dhanu rāśi).
- **Sanskrit/IAST**: Mārgaśīrṣa māsa — named after the Mṛgaśira (Mrigasira) nakshatra. Also historically called **Agrahāyaṇa**.
- **Gregorian overlap**: Roughly late November to late December.
- **Position in the year**: 9th month of the traditional 12-month lunar year.
- **Ṛtu (season)**: **Hemanta ṛtu** (early winter), pairing Mārgaśira and Puṣya.

(Source: https://blog.dharma-renaissance.org/spirituality/margashirsha-and-margazhi-a-practical-devotional-guide/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pausha)

### Viśiṣṭata (Significance)
Mārgaśira holds a uniquely direct scriptural distinction: in the **Bhagavad Gītā, Chapter 10 (Vibhūti Yoga), verse 35**, Kṛṣṇa says *māsānāṃ mārgaśīrṣo'ham* — "Of months, I am Mārgaśīrṣa." This is one of only a handful of places in the Gītā where Kṛṣṇa names a specific calendrical unit as his own vibhūti, in the same category as the Himalayas, the Ganges, and the Gāyatrī among meters. Commentators (Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja, Madhva) read this as Kṛṣṇa identifying with the most excellent month of the year — climatically temperate and coinciding with the harvest. This has made the month a favored time for Gītā pārāyaṇam, japa, dāna, and vratas. (Source: https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/10/verse/35/ ; https://hinduisminsimpleterms.wordpress.com/2015/12/15/bhagavad-gita-lord-says-of-the-months-i-am-marga-shirsha/)

The latter portion of Mārgaśira, when the sun is in Dhanu rāśi, is observed across South India — especially Telugu and Tamil Vaiṣṇava tradition — as **Dhanurmāsam**, considered the "Brahma Muhūrtam of the Devas" stretched across 30 days. Daily pre-dawn worship, special naivedyam of ariselu/pulihora, and full Tiruppāvai recitation are hallmark practices at temples such as Tirumala and Śrīraṅgam. (Source: https://tirumalatirupatiyatra.in/dhanurmasa-vratam/ ; https://gotirupati.com/tirumala-dhanurmasa-tiruppavai/)

### Purāṇa References
- **Bhagavad Gītā 10.35** is the primary and most authoritative scriptural reference — not a Purāṇic māhātmyam, but a direct self-declaration by Kṛṣṇa functioning as Mārgaśira's foundational scriptural charter. (Source: https://www.bhagavad-gita.us/bhagavad-gita-10-35/)
- Dhanurmāsa observance is elaborated in later devotional/āgamic literature and temple paddhati rather than in one dedicated Purāṇa māhātmya; Śrīvaiṣṇava sources treat the month's glory as an extension of the Gītā verse combined with Āṇḍāḷ's own testimony. (Source: https://hindupad.com/tiruppavai-recital-at-tirumala-temple-during-dhanurmasam/)

### Stories/Legends
- **Kṛṣṇa's Vibhūti Yoga declaration** (BG 10.35) itself is treated devotionally almost as a "story" — the Lord enumerating his manifestations to Arjuna, culminating in this month, retold in Mārgaśira discourses as proof of the month's unmatched sanctity.
- **Āṇḍāḷ (Gōdā Devī) and the Dhanurmāsa/Mārgaḻi vratam**: Gōdā Devī, one of the twelve Āḻvārs, undertook the **Pāvai Nōnbu** vrata this month, imagining herself as a gopī yearning for Kṛṣṇa/Raṅganātha. She composed the **Tiruppāvai** — 30 verses, one per day — and tradition holds she ultimately merged with Lord Raṅganātha at Śrīraṅgam as a result, worshipped today as an incarnation of Bhūdevī. In Telugu tradition the Tiruppāvai is recited daily through Dhanurmāsam (translated as *Melukolupu*/*Melupalukula Melukolupu* by Mullapudi Venkataramana), and young unmarried women observe related vratas seeking a good match. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruppavai ; https://www.primepost.in/godas-love-letter/ ; https://hindupad.com/tiruppavai-recital-at-tirumala-temple-during-dhanurmasam/)

### Parva-dinas this month
- **Pōli Pāḍyami** — Mārgaśira Śukla Pādyami, annual. Women float lit lamps on water as an offering, said to bring an exalted spiritual state.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Purely regional/local, specific to Andhra Pradesh. Also "Poli Swargam," observed at river ghats (notably the Krishna river) where women bathe, then float lit lamps on banana-stalk rafts. No pan-India or other-region equivalent found — a Telugu folk-temple tradition. (Source: https://hindupad.com/poli-swargam/)
- **Subrahmaṇya/Skanda/Kumāra Ṣaṣṭhī** — recurs in several months, notably Mārgaśira and Āṣāḍha (full entry, with pan-India regional notes, under Āṣāḍha above).
- **Nandā/Mitra Saptami** — Mārgaśira Saptamī, annual. Sun worshipped with the nāma "Mitra," said to be preeminent through the whole month. (No independent Pan-India/regional-notes sub-bullet found in the source.)
- **Kālabhairavāṣṭami** — Mārgaśira Kṛṣṇa Aṣṭamī, annual. Day of Kālabhairava's manifestation; worship is said to grant protection and fulfil desires.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Also called Bhairava Ashtami, Bhairava Jayanti or Kala Ashtami; observed monthly, with the Mārgaśīrṣa occurrence (Kāla Bhairava Jayanti) most significant. Major celebrations at Kāl Bhairav temples in Ujjain and Varanasi, differing in date convention from South India's panchangam. A genuinely pan-India festival, with Ujjain as its best-known pilgrimage center. (Source: https://hindupad.com/kalabhairavashtami-kalabhairav-ahstami/ ; https://www.ujjaindarshan.com/ujjain-kalbhairav.html)
- **Mōkṣadā Ēkādaśī (Gītā Jayanti)** — Mārgaśira Śukla. Coincides with the Bhagavad Gītā's traditional day of recitation.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name and observance exactly — **Mokṣadā Ēkādaśī** is celebrated nationwide as **Gītā Jayantī**, the day Kṛṣṇa is traditionally held to have recited the Gītā to Arjuna at Kurukṣetra; ISKCON and mainstream Vaiṣṇava calendars everywhere mark it the same way. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mokshada_Ekadashi)
- **Saphala Ēkādaśī** — Mārgaśira Kṛṣṇa. Removes the effect of evils, bestows Nārāyaṇa's grace.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Saphalā Ēkādaśī** exactly, associated with removing sin and bestowing Nārāyaṇa's grace in every regional tradition. (Source: https://www.iskcondelhi.com/full-year-ekadashi-list/)
- **Matsya Dvādaśī (Vāsudēva Dvādaśī)** — Mārgaśira Śukla, annual. Commemorates Viṣṇu's Matsya avatāra.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A relatively minor, pan-Vaiṣṇava observance without a distinct regional name found; observed similarly (fasting, Viṣṇu-sahasranāma/Matsya Purāṇa recitation) in Vaiṣṇava/ISKCON circles across regions. (Source: https://hindugallery.com/matsya-dwadashi/)
- **Hanumadvrataṁ** — Mārgaśira Śukla Trayōdaśī, annual. Hanumān worshipped with sixteen upacāras; also marks the start of Dhanurmāsa/Gōdā Dēvī's Tiruppāvai recitation.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Chiefly a South Indian Vaiṣṇava-Śaiva-adjacent vrata opening Dhanurmāsam, but Hanumān worship this day is also recorded at northern shrines like Sankaṭamōcana temple (Varanasi) and Jākhū temple (Shimla) — the Dhanurmāsa/Goda-Dēvī framing is distinctly South Indian, but Hanumān worship that day is not unknown in the north. (Source: https://hindupad.com/hanuman-vrat-hanuman-vratam/)
- **Dattātrēya Jayanti** — Mārgaśira Pūrṇimā, annual. Marks Dattātrēya's manifestation from the tapas of Atri and Anasūya.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* A genuinely multi-regional festival, celebrated with particular fervor in Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Gujarat — all host major Dattātrēya pīṭhas and temples, with regional variation mainly in local temple-festival style rather than in the core date or ritual. (Source: https://www.sanatanavibes.in/2025/07/dattatreya-jayanti-2025.html)

## Puṣya

### Names & Basic Info
- **Telugu name**: Puṣya Māsam (పుష్య మాసం).
- **Sanskrit/IAST**: Puṣya māsa, cognate with **Pauṣa** in North Indian usage — named for the Puṣya nakshatra.
- **Gregorian overlap**: Mid-December to mid-January.
- **Position in the year**: 10th month of the 12-month lunar year.
- **Ṛtu (season)**: **Hemanta ṛtu** (deep winter), second of the Mārgaśira–Puṣya pair.

(Source: https://hindupad.com/pushya-masam/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pausha)

### Viśiṣṭata (Significance)
Puṣya carries two opposite qualities at once, and the sources state both plainly. On one hand, it is widely held **inauspicious for major new-beginning saṃskāras** — weddings, gṛha praveśam, engagements — and is traditionally avoided for these in many North and South Indian communities. On the other hand, it is also **highly spiritually potent**. It is called **Saubhāgya Lakṣmī Māsam**, is dedicated to Viṣṇu and Lakṣmī, and is governed astrologically by Śani, the nakshatra lord of Puṣya. Farmers historically associated it with post-harvest prosperity. It is considered auspicious for austerity, single-meal vratas, japa, dāna, and Viṣṇu-Lakṣmī worship, even while unsuitable for weddings. Multiple sources report this dual status as a real and commonly noted feature of the month. (Source: https://www.hindu-blog.com/2020/10/pushya-masam-importance.html ; https://hindutone.com/spirituality/pushya-masam-2025-2026-complete-guide-with-dates-festivals-spiritual-significance-telugu-calendar-2025-2026/)

Observing a single meal a day through Puṣya is described in popular devotional literature as conferring good fortune, beauty, and renown. (Source: https://www.hindu-blog.com/2020/10/pushya-masam-importance.html)

### Purāṇa References
Unlike Mārgaśira (Gītā) or Māgha (Padma Purāṇa), no single, widely-cited, dedicated "Puṣya Māhātmyam" comparable to the Māgha Māhātmyam was found. Puṣya's significance is documented mainly through Dharmaśāstra/muhūrta literature (on why it is avoided for saṃskāras) and popular devotional/vrata guides. This is a genuine gap: no dedicated Purāṇic māhātmya equivalent to Māgha's was found. (Source: https://hindutone.com/spirituality/pushya-masam-2025-2026-complete-guide-with-dates-festivals-spiritual-significance-telugu-calendar-2025-2026/)

### Stories/Legends
No major itihāsa/purāṇic narrative specifically anchored to Puṣya month (as opposed to the Puṣya nakshatra) was found. Puṣya's devotional literature tends to be prescriptive (vrata rules, do's and don'ts) rather than narrative or legend-based, unlike Mārgaśira, Māgha, and Phālguṇa. This is a genuine gap in the sources, stated here directly rather than glossed over.

### Parva-dinas this month
- **Mukkōṭi / Vaikuṇṭha Ēkādaśī (incl. Saphala/Putradā variants by tradition)** — Puṣya Śukla. Vaikuṇṭha's north gate said to open for darśan; especially auspicious at Srirangam.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* **Vaikuṇṭha Ēkādaśī is pan-India, not just a South Indian name.** It is the most widely recognized special Ēkādaśī across Vaiṣṇava India. Everywhere, it is linked to the belief that Viṣṇu's northern gate opens for darśan on this day. It is celebrated at massive scale at Tirumala's Vaikuṇṭha Dvāram darśan (about a 10-day window) and at Srirangam's 21-day festival, drawing lakhs of pilgrims. "Mukkōṭi" is the Tamil/Telugu regional epithet layered on the pan-India name; in strict pan-India nomenclature this slot is also separately called **Pauṣa Putradā Ēkādaśī**, which the source itself flags as a variant. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaikuntha_Ekadashi ; https://imvoyager.com/temples-to-visit-during-vaikunta-ekadasi/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pausha_Putrada_Ekadashi)
- **Vimala Ēkādaśī (Ṣaṭtila Ēkādaśī)** — Puṣya Kṛṣṇa. Sesame-water bath, sesame homa and charity.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Standard pan-India name is **Ṣaṭtilā Ēkādaśī** (Māgha Kṛṣṇa in most lists), well known nationwide for its sesame (til) observances — six different uses of sesame in bathing, homa, eating, and charity. "Vimala" appears to be a secondary/local title in the source; the sesame-centred practice matches the pan-India tradition exactly. (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Chollaṅgi / Mauni Amāvāsya** — Puṣya Amāvāsyā, annual. Bathing at holy tīrthas, celebrated as a major local festival at Chollangi near Kakinada (Gōdāvari-sea confluence).
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* The Andhra Pradesh name is **Chollaṅgi Amāvāsya**, tied specifically to the village of Chollangi (East Godavari district) where the Godāvari meets the sea; the same Puṣya-māsa Amāvāsyā is known pan-India, especially in North India, as **Mauni Amāvāsyā** (Prayāgrāj Māgha Mēḷā/Kumbh bathing and vows of silence) — one tithi with a genuinely local South Indian pilgrimage identity layered under a much larger pan-India name. (Source: https://hindupad.com/chollangi-amavasya-2012-pushya-masa-amavasya-in-andhra-pradesh/)

This month's two Ēkādaśīs are individually named above (Mukkōṭi/Vaikuṇṭha Ēkādaśī; Vimala/Ṣaṭtila
Ēkādaśī); Māsa Śivarātri, both Pradōṣams and the Saṅkaṣṭahara Caturthī fall this month in generic
form — see Recurring Monthly-Type Observances above.

## Māgha

### Names & Basic Info
- **Telugu name**: Māgha Māsam (మాఘ మాసం).
- **Sanskrit/IAST**: Māgha māsa — named for the Maghā nakshatra.
- **Gregorian overlap**: Mid-January to mid-February.
- **Position in the year**: 11th month of the 12-month lunar year.
- **Ṛtu (season)**: **Śiśira ṛtu** (the cool/dew season), paired with Phālguṇa.

(Source: https://www.templepurohit.com/magha-maasam-importance-of-magha-snana-puja-mantras-and-festivals/ ; https://www.religionworld.in/magha_maasam/)

### Viśiṣṭata (Significance)
Māgha is one of the most emphatically glorified months in the Hindu ritual calendar, centered on **Māgha Snānam** — a holy bath (ideally in the Ganges, Godāvarī, or other sacred rivers, or at minimum before sunrise in any water body) undertaken daily through the month. Tradition holds Goddess Sarasvatī, Sūrya Bhagavān, and Śiva-liṅga worship are especially connected to births/manifestations associated with this month, and the month is closely tied to the **Māgha Melā/Kumbha Melā** bathing festivals at Prayāgrāj. Māgha Snānam is proclaimed in the Padma Purāṇa to be spiritually superior even to yajñas, meditation, and long austerities: "by means of baths in Māgha, calamities perish, sins perish," with three days of Māgha bathing said to equal ten years of tapas at a tīrtha. (Source: https://www.exoticindiaart.com/blog/magh-mela-history-rituals-and-spiritual-significance-of-sacred-magha-month/ ; https://hindupad.com/magh-snan-mahatmya-importance-and-spiritual-significance-of-magha-snanam-or-holy-dip-in-magh-month/)

### Purāṇa References
The **Māgha Māhātmyam** is a well-documented dedicated text within the **Padma Purāṇa's Uttara Khaṇḍa**:
- **Chapter 125** — "The Importance of Māgha As Told by Bhṛgu" (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365704.html)
- **Chapter 126** — "The Importance of Māgha As Told by Dattātreya" (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365705.html)

Also referenced/echoed in the **Mahābhārata**, forming part of the scriptural basis invoked for the Māgha Melā/Kumbha Melā bathing traditions. (Source: https://www.exoticindiaart.com/blog/magh-mela-history-rituals-and-spiritual-significance-of-sacred-magha-month/)

### Stories/Legends
**Bhṛgu's teaching to King Dilīpa (Padma Purāṇa, Uttara Khaṇḍa, Ch. 125)**: King Dilīpa, while on a hunting expedition, meets an ascetic who directs him to sage Vasiṣṭha to learn the merit of Māgha bathing. Vasiṣṭha recounts a story originally told by the sage Bhṛgu: during a twelve-year famine, Bhṛgu meets a vidyādhara on Mount Maṇikūṭa whose face has turned tiger-like as karmic punishment for using oil on a mandated fasting day. Bhṛgu prescribes Māgha bathing as remedy, illustrating its power via the ancient king **Purūravas**, whose ugliness was transformed into divine beauty through Viṣṇu's grace connected to Māgha observance. The chapter closes with Bhṛgu's declaration that Māgha bathing surpasses yajña, dhyāna, and tapas, open to all castes and āśramas alike. (Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365704.html)

This narrative is the closest documented equivalent, within the Māgha Māhātmyam itself, to the "King Bhagīratha" austerity-lineage story pattern common in Purāṇic river/bathing māhātmyas (Bhagīratha's austerities are classically tied to bringing the Ganges to earth, a separate but thematically resonant narrative often invoked alongside Māgha-snāna discourse). (Source: https://astrodisha.com/sampuran-magha-maas-mahatmya/)

### Parva-dinas this month
- **Varada Gaṇēśa Caturthi (Tila/Kunda Caturthi)** — Māgha Śukla Caturthī, annual. Gaṇapati (including Dhundhi Gaṇapati at Kāśī) worshipped in the evening after a day's fast; Śiva also worshipped with jasmine flowers.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Primarily a Maharashtra/Konkan observance (also Ganesha Jayanti, Tilkund Chaturthi, or Varad Chaturthi there), rather than pan-India; considered the most meritorious of the year's Ganesha Chaturthis per the Mudgala Purana. Overlaps conceptually with the broader South Indian practice of observing each month's Sankashti Chaturthi, but the Dhundhi Gaṇapati/Kāśī framing here is not corroborated as a distinct North Indian tradition. (Source: https://www.freepressjournal.in/lifestyle/when-is-varada-chaturthi-2026-know-correct-date-muhurat-katha-significance-more)
  - *Cross-reference:* This source also gives an alternate placement of "Beginning of Payō Vrata/Avighna Vrata" as Māgha/Phālguṇa Śukla Pādyami — full entry under Phālguṇa below, its more commonly cited month.
- **Śrī Pañcami (Madana/Vasanta Pañcami)** — Māgha Śukla Pañcamī, annual. Celebrates Saraswatī's manifestation as Parā Śakti; worshipped with white sandalwood, white flowers and rice pudding.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Pan-India under the name Vasant/Basant Panchami with strong regional variation: in West Bengal it is Saraswati Puja, a major student-centered pandal festival (yellow basanti sarees, dhoti-kurta, books and instruments worshipped); in Punjab/Haryana it is primarily a kite-flying festival tied to the spring/mustard-flower season; Bihar, UP and Rajasthan hold cultural gatherings. The Telugu emphasis on white sandalwood/flowers is a regional detail not mirrored elsewhere. (Source: https://harishyam.com/blogs/news/how-different-regions-of-india-celebrate-vasant-panchami-bengali-north-indian-south-indian-customs)
- **Varuṇa Ṣaṣṭhi (Ṣaṣṭhīdēvi Pūja)** — Māgha Śukla Ṣaṣṭhī, annual. Varuṇa invoked in a kalaśa with red flowers; Ṣaṣṭhīdēvi separately worshipped for the welfare of progeny.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Observed mainly in Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu with red-flower/water-kalasha worship of Varuna. No distinct North Indian name found; a regional South Indian rite rather than pan-India. (Source: https://hindupad.com/varuna-sashtivaruna-shashti/)
- **Ratha Saptami (Surya Jayanti)** — Māgha Śukla Saptamī, annual, a major Sūrya festival marking Uttarāyaṇa's turning point; milk pudding offered on green-bean-tree leaves at brahma muhūrta.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Major pan-India Sun festival with genuinely different regional names: Andhra Pradesh/Telangana call it "Surya Jayanti" with pre-dawn river baths at Godavari/Krishna and crowds at Arasavalli Sun temple; Karnataka calls it "Surya Pongal"; North India (UP, Bihar, Rajasthan) observes fasting and water offerings without a distinct local name. Common South Indian practice includes placing seven arka leaves on the head/shoulders/chest. Major celebrations at Tirumala, Konark, and Arasavalli. (Source: https://www.treebo.com/blog/ratha-saptami/ ; https://www.4to40.com/indian-festivals/ratha-saptami/)
- **Bhīṣma Aṣṭami** — Māgha Śukla Aṣṭamī, annual. Marks the day Bhīṣma merged his soul with Kṛṣṇa; tarpaṇa performed for him.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Observed with tarpaṇa for Bhishma and one's own ancestors, particularly in UP, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra, with large Ganga-ghat gatherings in North India; noted as comparatively minor in Bengal and parts of the south — leans North Indian/pan-Gangetic despite appearing in this Telugu source. (Source: https://www.sentinelassam.com/more-news/national-news/bhishma-ashtami-2025-significance-rituals-teachings-of-bhishma-pitamah)
- **Bhīṣma/Bhīma Ēkādaśī** — Māgha Śukla. Opens the five-day Bhīṣma Pañcaka Vratam of tarpaṇa.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* **This entry may conflate two different Ēkādaśīs that share part of a name** (the source itself notes this). Bhīṣmāṣṭamī-linked "Bhīṣma Pañcaka" observances actually begin at Prabodhinī (Kārtika Śukla, see above), not at Māgha. Separately, the far more famous **Bhīmā/Nirjalā Ēkādaśī** (Jyeṣṭha Śukla, see under Jyēṣṭha above) is the nationally best-known Ēkādaśī with "Bhīma" in its name. This source's Māgha Śukla entry most likely records a regional Telugu grouping of Bhīṣma-tarpaṇa observances, not the well-known Nirjalā/Bhīma vrata. - **Varāha Dwādaśī (Bhima/Tila Dwādaśī)** — Māgha, annual. Viṣṇu worshipped as Varāha; sesame bathing/lamps (sesame said to have been created this day).
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Also widely called **Bhīṣma Dvādaśī** or **Amalakī Dvādaśī**, with sesame (til) offerings and tarpaṇa to Bhīṣma Pitāmaha as the common thread nationwide — the Varāha-avatāra framing here is one regional emphasis among several names for largely the same Māgha Śukla Dvādaśī observance.   - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Observed nationally with holy bathing, but regional focus differs — North India (UP, Bihar) centers on the Māgha Mēḷā at Prayāgrāj and Gaṅgā-ghāṭ ārtīs in Varanasi; Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh mark it with temple festivals and abhiṣēkam at sites like Arunāchalēśwara temple, closer to this source's Satyanārāyaṇa-vrata framing. (Source: https://www.treebo.com/blog/magha-purnima/)

## Phālguṇa

### Names & Basic Info
- **Telugu name**: Phālguṇa Māsam (ఫాల్గుణ మాసం), colloquially Falgun/Phalgun.
- **Sanskrit/IAST**: Phālguṇa māsa — named for the Uttara/Pūrva Phalgunī nakshatra.
- **Gregorian overlap**: Mid-February to mid-March.
- **Position in the year**: 12th and **final** month of the traditional lunar year (Chaitra, the following month, begins the new year).
- **Ṛtu (season)**: **Śiśira ṛtu**, closing out the season begun in Māgha (some classifications place late Phālguṇa at the cusp of Vasanta/spring ṛtu).

(Source: https://hindupad.com/phalguna-masam-falguna-month-dates-in-telugu-calendar/ ; https://www.gempundit.com/blog/phalguna-the-12th-lunar-month-of-the-hindu-calendar)

### Viśiṣṭata (Significance)
Phālguṇa is regarded as a highly auspicious, joyous closing month of the lunar year — its name popularly explained as combining *phala* ("fruit") and *guṇa* ("virtue/character"), signifying the "fruit" of the year's spiritual efforts ripening. It carries the year's two most dramatic festivals: **Mahāśivarātri** (Kṛṣṇa pakṣa), commemorating the convergence of Śiva and Pārvatī, and **Holī** (Śukla pakṣa, culminating on Phālguṇa Pūrṇimā), marking the triumph of devotion over evil via the Holikā-Prahlāda narrative, followed by color-throwing and Kṛṣṇa-Rādhā līlā themes. The Amāvāsyā following Phālguṇa's end marks the start of the Hindu New Year (Ugādi in Telugu tradition), giving the month a "threshold" sanctity as the year's culmination. (Source: https://hindutone.com/festivals/phalguna-masam-2026/phalguna-masam-2026-end-date-start-date-importance-rituals-festivals/ ; https://www.gempundit.com/blog/phalguna-the-12th-lunar-month-of-the-hindu-calendar)

### Purāṇa References
Research did not find one single dedicated "Phālguṇa Māhātmyam" of the scale of the Māgha Māhātmyam. Instead, its significance is carried through festival-specific Purāṇic narratives falling within the month: the **Holikā-Prahlāda story** is recounted across multiple Purāṇas (Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Bhāgavata Purāṇa Book 7, Nārada Purāṇa), while **Mahāśivarātri**'s theology draws on **Śiva Purāṇa** and **Liṅga Purāṇa** material on the Śiva-Pārvatī union and the appearance of the Jyotirliṅga. Best understood as festival-māhātmyas embedded within the month rather than a unified "Phālguṇa Māhātmyam" — no single dedicated whole-month text was located. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holika_Dahana ; https://hindupad.com/phalgun-month-falgun-maas-phalguna-masam/)

### Stories/Legends
**Holikā and Prahlāda**: The demon-king Hiraṇyakaśipu, resentful of his son Prahlāda's devotion to Viṣṇu, enlists his sister Holikā — immune to fire — to sit in a pyre with Prahlāda on her lap. Through Prahlāda's steadfast devotion and Viṣṇu's protection, the boon reverses: Holikā is consumed while Prahlāda emerges unharmed. Dated to Phālguṇa Pūrṇimā eve, this is ritually reenacted every year as **Holikā Dahana** the night before Holī proper. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holika_Dahana)

**Mahāśivarātri's Śiva-Pārvatī union**: narratives vary by Purāṇa (some emphasize the appearance of the Jyotirliṅga, others the marriage of Śiva and Pārvatī), observed with night-long jāgaraṇa, Rudrābhiṣeka, and fasting on Phālguṇa Kṛṣṇa-pakṣa Chaturdaśī. (Source: https://hindutone.com/spirituality/phalguna-masam-2026-complete-guide-to-dates-significance-vedic-importance/)

### Parva-dinas this month
- **Beginning of Payō Vrata / Avighna Vrata** — Phālguṇa/Māgha Śukla Pādyami, annual. A twelve-day vrata (through Dvādaśī) of holy-river bathing and worship of Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa, paired with Gaṇapati worship as "Avighna Vrata." Closes on Gōvinda Dvādaśī, below.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Narrowly regional. Payo Vratam (12-day, milk-only observance for Vishnu) is specifically noted as practiced "in some regions in Andhra Pradesh" during Phalguna māsa; Avighna Vrata (Dundi Vinayaka worship on Śukla Pādyami) is a more general Ganapati observance. No evidence this pairing is pan-India — appears Telugu-specific. (Source: https://hindupad.com/payo-vratham-vishnu-payo-vratham-in-phalguna-masam/)
- **Tilacaturthi (Putra Gaṇapati Vratam, Śānta Caturthi)** — Phālguṇa Śukla Caturthī, annual. Vināyaka worshipped with a sesame-seed homam; day fast, night meal.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Regional under a different name in the North: "Tilo Chauth" or Tilkund Chaturthi in Uttar Pradesh, where Ganesha is invoked on behalf of a family's son — matching this source's "Putra Gaṇapati" framing. Suggests the observance is shared in name/intent between Telugu and North Indian (UP) traditions. (Source: https://www.baba.org/tila-chaturthi-.html)
- **Vijaya Ēkādaśī** — given in the source as Māgha/Phālguṇa Kṛṣṇa; placed here as its more commonly cited pan-India slot. Grants victory in obtaining the four puruṣārthas.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Vijayā Ēkādaśī** exactly — known everywhere for the story of Rāma performing this vrata (on Nārada's advice) before crossing the ocean to Laṅkā, granting victory in undertakings. (Source: https://vedicfeed.com/types-of-ekadashi/)
- **Amalaka Ēkādaśī (Dhātri/Amṛta Ēkādaśī)** — Phālguṇa Śukla. Worship of Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa under an Amalaka (gooseberry) tree.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Āmalakī Ēkādaśī** exactly, observed nationwide with worship of Viṣṇu under an āmalakī tree, held to have sprung from Brahmā's tears of joy. "Dhātri" and "Amṛta" are attested pan-India alternate epithets, not South-Indian-specific. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalaka_Ekadashi)
- **Pāpavimōcana Ēkādaśī (Vijayaikādaśī)** — Phālguṇa Kṛṣṇa (per this source's amanta labeling). Removes sins of many births; footwear charity is prescribed.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Matches the standard pan-India name **Pāpamōcanī Ēkādaśī** (Caitra Kṛṣṇa in pūrṇimānta terms) exactly — universally described as removing sins of many births, with footwear/umbrella-type charity in various regional retellings. The bracketed "Vijayaikādaśī" alias in the source appears to be a mislabel/duplication with the separate Vijayā Ēkādaśī entry above rather than a standard alternate name for Pāpamōcanī. (Source: https://www.iskcondelhi.com/full-year-ekadashi-list/)
- **Gōvinda Dvādaśī (Narasimha Dvādaśī)** — Phālguṇa Śukla, annual. Closes Payō Vratam, above; Nārāyaṇa worshipped as "Govinda," Narasimha in the evening. (No independent Pan-India/regional-notes sub-bullet found in the source.)
- **Mahāśivarātri** — Māgha/Phālguṇa Kṛṣṇa Caturdaśī, annual, the year's single most important Śiva festival, placed in Phālguṇa per `masa_visishtata.md` above. Marks Paramaśiva's midnight manifestation as the Liṅga (story above); vrata of fasting, jāgaraṇa and worship. This month's own Māsa Śivarātri instance IS Mahāśivarātri itself.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* India's single most widely observed Śiva festival, with major regional variation. Varanasi's Kāśī Viśvanātha temple and Ujjain's Mahākāleśwar temple (which extends it into a nine-day Śiva Navrātri, dressing the liṅga in a bridal sēhrā) anchor the grandest North Indian celebrations; Himachal's Mandi hosts an eight-day royal Śivarātri fair at the Bhūtnāth temple; Assam centers on the Umānanda temple in Guwahati; Bengal observes night-long fasting and bhajans; Andhra Pradesh's major centers are Śrīkāḷahasti and Śrīśailam. The shared core everywhere is fasting, night-long jāgaraṇa, and liṅga abhiṣēkam. (Source: https://www.outlookindia.com/brand-studio/unique-mahashivratri-rituals-practised-across-different-regions-of-india)
- **Holikā Pūrṇimā (Kāmadahanam, Dōlōtsavam)** — Phālguṇa Pūrṇimā, annual, major. Holika-bonfire ritual (story above), Bāla-Kṛṣṇa cradle worship, and (in many South Indian temples) a Śiva-Pārvatī Kalyāṇam.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* The most dramatic North-vs-South contrast of the set. In Braj (Mathura, Vrindavan, Barsana, Nandgaon, Gokul) Holi extends into an elaborate 10-day festival, including Barsana's famous Lāṭhmār Holi and flower-Holi at the Bāṅke Bihārī temple — boisterous, colour-and-water-soaked. South India's Kāmadahanam is quieter and more ritual-focused: the symbolic burning of Kāmadēva takes precedence over colour-play, with Śiva-Murugan temple processions and fire rituals as centerpiece (matching this source's Bāla-Kṛṣṇa-cradle-plus-Śiva-Pārvatī-Kalyāṇam framing); Kerala's Palakkad/Thrissur area has its own turmeric-water variant, Mañjaḷ Kuḷi. (Source: https://www.outlooktraveller.com/destinations/india/where-holi-lasts-10-days-the-ultimate-guide-to-celebrating-in-braj ; https://organiser.org/2026/03/03/342583/bharat/how-holi-is-celebrated-from-north-to-south-india-a-festival-of-colours-culture-and-unity/)
- **Kotta Amāvāsya (last Amāvāsya before Ugādi)** — Phālguṇa/Caitra Amāvāsyā, annual, the amāvāsyā closing Phālguṇa immediately before Chaitra's Ugādi. Śrāddha and tarpaṇa to ancestors; worship of the Divine Mother and village deities.
  - *Pan-India / regional notes:* Specifically an Andhra Pradesh/Telangana observance (also linked to the Nookalamma temple), directly tied to the Telugu New Year that follows the next day — a regional/Telugu-calendar-specific festival, though ancestor-worship amāvāsyās generally are pan-India in form. (Source: https://hindupad.com/amavasya-30-march-2014-ugadi-amavasya-gudi-padwa-amavasya/)
