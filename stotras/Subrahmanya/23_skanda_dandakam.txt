Title: Śrī Skanda Daṇḍakam
Devanāgarī: श्री स्कन्द दण्डकम्
Telugu: శ్రీ స్కంద దండకం

Author: traditional; no poet-signature, ṛṣi, or chandas viniyoga is given in either source consulted. The colophon names only the work itself ("iti śrī skanda daṇḍakam"), and the closing verse addresses the deity as "śrīharigītapattanapate" (lord of the town of Harigīta), identifying the composition's home shrine as the Śrī Subrahmaṇya Svāmi temple at Harippad, Kerala (an old name for Harippad reflected in "Harigītapuram/Harigītapattana"), rather than naming a human author.

Source / recension: primary source is stotranidhi.com's "Śrī Skanda Daṇḍakam" page, checked in three of its parallel-script editions (Devanāgarī, Telugu, and English/IAST), all three agreeing word for word. Cross-checked independently against sanskritdocuments.org's doc_subrahmanya catalog entry "skandadaNDakam" (file skandadaNDakam.itx; transliterated by Sivakumar Thyagarajan, proofread by Sivakumar Thyagarajan and PSA Easwaran) — its running text matches stotranidhi's word for word. Verse count: no internally numbered verses in either source. The hymn is one unbroken daṇḍaka compound — a single long chain of vocative epithets, traditionally counted as one unit — running from "ayi jaya jaya..." to "...māṁ pāhi pāhi prasīda prasīda ॥", followed by one closing verse in a distinct, shorter end-rhymed meter ("kāruṇyāmbunidhe... javāt ॥"), and a one-line prose colophon ("iti śrī skanda daṇḍakam"). Counted here as 2 verse-units plus a colophon line.

Sections:
దండకం — Daṇḍakam (the single unbroken vocative compound addressing Skanda through an unbroken chain of epithets — his six lotus faces kissed by Pārvatī, blood-reddened arrows, twelve lotus eyes, a smile that outshines jasmine, the śakti-spear in his lotus hand, his embrace of Vallī, his peacock mount and garments, and the Harippad shrine he adorns — closing with a direct plea for protection and grace)
ముగింపు శ్లోకం — Concluding Verse (one closing benedictory stanza in a different, shorter meter, sealing the daṇḍaka with a direct petition for protection and prosperity)
సమాప్తి వాక్యం — Colophon (the closing sentence naming the work)

Blurb: This is a daṇḍaka in the strict sense. It is not a set of separate ślokas, each ending on its own. It is one continuous chain of compound epithets in the vocative, heaped one upon another. They describe Skanda's form: his six lotus faces, kissed by Pārvatī; his arrows dark as blood; his twelve lotus eyes; his smile that puts the jasmine blossom to shame; the śakti spear in his lotus hand; his embrace of Vallī; his peacock mount; and his feet, whose brightness surpasses even the abode of Lakṣmī. The poem ties this image to one particular shrine, the Subrahmaṇya temple at Harigītapuram, which is Harippad in Kerala. It then breaks into a short closing verse and a colophon of one line.

en: Hail, victory, victory! You fill every quarter of space with an unbroken array of splendor that outshines the pride of freshly blossomed lotuses, and your own radiance is deep and joy-drenched; your six lotus faces are kissed, in a welling rush of tenderness, by the mouth of Pārvatī, consort of the five-faced Śiva, and your arrows run red with torrents of enemy blood; your twelve lotus eyes, loveliest among their own throng, are the very seat of the deepest love for your own lord, and you are the protector of the virtuous; your softly gleaming, gentle smile pours out ever-greater bliss upon the good multitude that clings to worshipping your feet, a radiance that has stripped the jasmine-spray of its pride in its own beauty, and the whole host of immortals sings your praise; you are the one refuge against the extreme calamity that beset the world, your broad chest sending up a trail of blood-dark, smoke-hued splendor as the śakti-spear gleams in your lotus hand, and you are a wish-granting tree sprung up on earth for your devotees; embracing the vine-soft body of Vallī has made your chest so glorious that it outshines the pomp of a twilight cloud clasping an unfading flash of lightning, and you are clothed in garments that blaze like molten gold; you shine gloriously astride your splendid peacock, the massed radiance of whose plume gathers up the unbroken luster of Indra's bow, and the glory of your very feet has stolen away the pride of Lakṣmī's own abode; you adorn the renowned shrine of Harigītāpuram — Harippad — O Kārtikeya, kinsman of the afflicted, ocean brimming with compassion: salutations to you, lord of all; protect me, protect me; be gracious, be gracious.

Recension note: Base text is stotranidhi.com's edition of Śrī Skanda Daṇḍakam (checked in its Devanāgarī, Telugu, and English/IAST parallel editions, all agreeing word for word), independently cross-checked against sanskritdocuments.org's doc_subrahmanya catalog file skandadaNDakam.itx (transliterated by Sivakumar Thyagarajan, proofread by Sivakumar Thyagarajan and PSA Easwaran), whose running text is identical. The two sources differ only in presentation, not wording: sanskritdocuments' itx opens with a scribal invocation "oṁ śrīgaṇeśāya namaḥ" before "ayi jaya jaya..." and marks several extra internal "॥" pauses inside what stotranidhi's edition prints as one unbroken comma-separated compound; this file follows stotranidhi's presentation (no separate Gaṇeśa-invocation section, the daṇḍaka body kept as a single verse-none unit) since it is the cleaner and more widely circulated Telugu-tradition printing, and the divergence is noted here rather than silently adopted or silently dropped. Because a daṇḍaka's compounds run continuously rather than breaking at pāda boundaries, the deva/iast text of the main daṇḍaka block is given as one long unit with line breaks inserted only at the source's own comma pauses, for legibility — these line breaks are not pāda marks and no daṇḍa punctuation was added beyond what the source itself carries; the single terminal ॥ is the only verse-end mark in that block, matching the source. In the closing verse, a hyphenated line-wrap in the source ("santāpadānodyata-" / "-sphāyaddarpa...") was joined into one unbroken word per the corpus rule that a soft wrap is not a pāda break; the resulting three-line, two-daṇḍa layout (। after the first line, ॥ after the third, none after the second) reproduces the source's own punctuation exactly rather than adding a symmetrical break that isn't there. This item was confirmed distinct from two similarly-named catalog entries at sanskritdocuments.org — "śrī subrahmaṇyadaṇḍakam" (subradaNDaka.itx, opening "jaya vajrisutākānta...") and "ṣaḍānanadaṇḍakam" (Rāmānuja Deśika's composition) — both fetched directly and found to open with different words and carry unrelated content. The text was not found on vignanam.org under any script-variant slug tried, nor via a site search, so cross-checking here rests on stotranidhi.com and sanskritdocuments.org (two independent transcriptions agreeing exactly) rather than a third, vignanam-based confirmation; this reduced third-source cross-check is flagged per the corpus brief. The daṇḍaka's compounds are extremely dense even by kāvya standards, and no published word-by-word (padaccheda/tātparya) commentary could be located for this specific text on any source consulted; the en/tel/hi renderings above reflect a careful compound-by-compound reading of the grammar rather than a verified traditional gloss, and are offered with that caveat rather than false certainty. No numbering irregularities arise since the source carries no verse numbers at all.
