Title: Śrī Ṣaṇmukha Daṇḍakam
Devanāgarī: श्री षण्मुख दण्डकम्
Telugu: శ్రీ షణ్ముఖ దండకం

Author: not named. The colophon gives only the title ("iti śrī ṣaṇmukha daṇḍakam"). One internal detail is suggestive: the opening petition says the poet has "begun to make a hymn to you couched in the language of the gods" (devabhāṣāgatāṁ) and asks Skanda to let the undertaking succeed — the remark of someone composing in Sanskrit as an acquired liturgical language rather than writing in it by default, which together with the Telugu-tradition transmission points to a South Indian devotional author rather than a court poet.

Source / recension: stotranidhi.com's "Śrī Ṣaṇmukha Daṇḍakam," taken in its Devanāgarī, Telugu and English (IAST) editions, which agree word for word throughout. No counterpart could be located on sanskritdocuments.org: its doc_subrahmanya catalog was searched directly and contains no "ṣaṇmukhadaṇḍakam." The two daṇḍakas in that catalog whose titles could be confused with this one were both fetched and checked and are different works — "ṣaḍānanadaṇḍakam" (file ShaDAnanadaNDakam.itx, by Rāmānuja Deśika, which opens "antevasanmānasarājahaṁsaṁ ṣaḍdarśanīnīradhipūrṇacandram") and "subrahmaṇyadaṇḍakam" (file subradaNDaka.itx). Flagged accordingly as a single-site sourcing, though the three independent script editions from that site agree exactly.

Verse count: no numbered verses. The work consists of an opening prose petition, a single unbroken daṇḍaka — one long chain of accusative epithets all governed by the verb "bhaje tvāṁ," I worship you — and a one-line colophon. Counted here as 2 units plus a colophon.

Sections:
ప్రార్థన — Prārthanā (the opening petition, in which the poet asks leave to compose and asks that the attempt succeed)
దండకం — Daṇḍakam (the body: one continuous chain of epithets running from "sadānandarūpaṁ" through a litany of ten "bhaje" clauses to a closing plea for purification, sealed by the threefold "bhaje'haṁ bhaje'haṁ bhaje'ham")
సమాప్తి వాక్యం — Colophon (the closing sentence naming the work)

Blurb: A daṇḍaka that opens with an unusual piece of self-awareness — the poet announces that he is about to attempt a hymn in Sanskrit and asks Skanda to make the attempt come off — and then delivers exactly that: one enormous unbroken sentence of epithets that swings from Vedāntic abstraction (undivided, supportless, mere radiance, beginningless and endless) to concrete iconography (the hand resting on the hip, the faint smile, the slightly opened lotus eyes) and closes by naming the speaker as ignorant, sunk in the mire, and habitually sinful, asking only to be made clean.

en: O son of Śrī Pārvatī, protect me, O lord of Vallī. I am given over to the service of your lotus feet, and I have set out to make a hymn of praise to you couched in the language of the gods — do you bring my resolve to success and to fruit.
tel: ఓ శ్రీపార్వతీపుత్రా, నన్ను రక్షించు, ఓ వల్లీశా. నేను నీ పాదపంకజాల సేవలో నిమగ్నుడనై ఉన్నాను; దేవభాషలో (సంస్కృతంలో) నీకు స్తుతిని రచించడానికి పూనుకున్నాను — నా సంకల్పాన్ని సిద్ధింపజేసి కృతార్థం చేయుము.
hi: हे श्री पार्वती-पुत्र, मेरी रक्षा करो, हे वल्लीश। मैं तुम्हारे चरण-कमलों की सेवा में रत हूँ, और देवभाषा (संस्कृत) में तुम्हारी स्तुति रचने का आरंभ किया है — मेरे संकल्प को सिद्ध और कृतार्थ करो।
