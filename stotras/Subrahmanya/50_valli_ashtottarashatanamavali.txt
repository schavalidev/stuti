Title: Śrī Vallī Aṣṭottaraśatanāmāvalī
Devanāgarī: श्री वल्ली-अष्टोत्तरशतनामावलिः
Telugu: శ్రీ వల్లీ అష్టోత్తరశతనామావళిః

Author: Traditional; the source names no Purāṇa or composer. Vallī is the consort of Subrahmaṇya, the daughter of the Moon (Śaśisutā), who was raised among forest hunters.

Source / recension: Primary text: **a Telugu-script nāmāvalī collection, Smārtha e-books (smarthamitra.in), "108–1008"** (scanned PDF, page images only, PDF metadata author "kprss", 2016), printed pp. 185–187 (PDF pp. 189–191). **Not a Gītā Press edition; Gītā Press's *Śatanāma Stotra Saṅgraha* (code 1850) has no Vallī list.** Transcribed from the Telugu page images into Devanāgarī. **No witness of this exact list was found.** sanskritdocuments.org has two Vallī nāmāvalīs. `vallyaShTottarashatanAmAvalI2.itx` is a **related list, not the same recension**: it opens `mahāvallyai śyāmatanave sarvābharaṇabhūṣitāyai pītāmbaradharāyai` like this one and shares many names (`pralambatāṭaṅkyai`, `tuṅgastanyai`, `sukañcukāyai`, `guñjāmālyadharāyai`, `tribhaṅginyai`, `pravāladharāyai`, `manonmanyai`, `cāmuṇḍāyai`, `skandabhāryāyai`, `parameṣṭhinyai`, `sadyojātāyai`, `varalakṣmyai`, `sarasvatyai`, `kumāryai`, `bhadrakālyai`, `durgāyai`, `sākṣiṇyai`, `purāṇyai`, `sarvatomukhyai`, `parāśaktyai`, `yoginyai`, `bindusvarūpiṇyai`, `pāpanāśinyai`, `padmavāsinyai`, `guṇatrayāyai`, `śaraṇāgatarakṣaṇāyai`), but its order and roughly half its names differ. `subra108valli.itx` is **a different list altogether** (a `va-/da-/na-…` acrostic opening `vallyai vandyāyai vanavāsāyai`). The collation is therefore **partial**: it confirms the shared names and cannot adjudicate the rest. That is said here plainly rather than implying a full second witness.

Recension note: **The Smārtha nāmāvalī is transcribed as printed; with no same-recension witness, nothing is emended.** **Relation to the Devasenā list.** The Smārtha Devasenā nāmāvalī that follows it in the same volume (`Subrahmanya/51_devasena_ashtottarashatanamavali.txt`) shares a large block of names with this one, often in the same order (`अणिमायै महादेव्यै … सर्वाभरणभूषितायै शुभरूपायै … प्रत्युषायै महेश्वर्यै`, `चन्द्रवर्णायै कलाधरायै पूर्णचन्द्रायै`, `इन्द्राण्यै इन्द्ररूपिण्यै इन्द्रशक्त्यै परायण्यै`, `कावेर्यै तुङ्गभद्रायै`, `मूलाधिपायै पराशक्त्यै पुष्करायै सर्वतोमुख्यै`, and the closing `गुणत्रयायै दयारूपिण्यै … शरणागतरक्षण… अशेषहृदयायै देव्यै`). The two consorts are praised as near-identical forms of the one Śakti; the overlap is recorded, not "corrected". **Readings kept as printed, sense uncertain:** no. 18 `व्यलम्बकरायै` (compare the related list's `savye lambakarāyai`, "whose left hand hangs down", the classic iconographic detail; the Smārtha print may have lost `स` of `सव्य-`); no. 35 `निर्णयायै`. **Duplicates:** `महावल्ल्यै` (nos. 1 and 55) and `इन्द्राण्यै` (nos. 63 and 79). **Count:** the print's tally marks and the list agree at 108. **Prefix.** The source prints `ఓం` (ॐ) before the first name of each column; this corpus prints `श्री` for every nāmāvalī without exception (user, 2026-09-08). **Cross-reference:** no other Vallī text is in the corpus; `Subrahmanya/49_kartikeya_ashtottarashatanama_stotram.txt` is Skanda's own list.

Blurb: Skanda's forest bride, dark-bodied and yellow-clad, coral-lipped, standing in the triple bend with a lotus in her hand and her other hand hanging loose. The list keeps turning her into every goddess at once — Brāhmī, Vaiṣṇavī, Vārāhī, Cāmuṇḍā, Bhadrakālī, Durgā, Sarasvatī, Varalakṣmī — and then, unexpectedly, into the rivers of the south: the Kāverī, the Tuṅgabhadrā, the Kṛṣṇaveṇī, the Bhīmā.
