Title: Śrī Subrahmaṇya Daṇḍakam
Devanāgarī: श्री सुब्रह्मण्य दण्डकम्
Telugu: శ్రీ సుబ్రహ్మణ్య దండకం

Author: not named in either source. The colophon in the sanskritdocuments recension reads simply "śrī subrahmaṇya daṇḍakaṁ sampūrṇam" and stotranidhi's "iti śrī subrahmaṇya daṇḍakam"; no poet-signature, ṛṣi or chandas viniyoga is supplied by either.

Source / recension: primary source is stotranidhi.com's "Śrī Subrahmaṇya Daṇḍakam," taken in its Devanāgarī, Telugu and English (IAST) editions, which agree word for word. Cross-checked independently against sanskritdocuments.org's "śrīsubrahmaṇyadaṇḍakam" (file subradaNDaka.itx, encoded and proofread by Antaratma), which transmits the same opening verse, the same daṇḍaka compound in the same order, and the same closing salutation. The sanskritdocuments transcription is the weaker witness — it carries several plain scribal errors that stotranidhi does not, itemized in the closing recension note. This item was explicitly checked against, and confirmed distinct from, the Ṣaṇmukha Daṇḍakam and the Skanda Daṇḍakam already in this section, and against Rāmānuja Deśika's Ṣaḍānana Daṇḍakam, all of which open with entirely different words.

Verse count: no numbered verses. The work consists of one opening anuṣṭubh verse of four "jaya" invocations, a single unbroken daṇḍaka compound, a two-clause closing salutation, and a colophon. Counted here as 3 units plus a colophon.

Sections:
జయ శ్లోకం — Jaya Śloka (the opening quatrain, four "victory to you" invocations)
దండకం — Daṇḍakam (the single unbroken compound, running from "jaya bhujabala..." to "...māṁ rakṣa," which begins in the third person as narrative epithet and ends in direct address with a plea for protection)
నమస్కారం — Namaskāra (the closing twofold salutation)
సమాప్తి వాక్యం — Colophon (the closing sentence naming the work)

Blurb: This is a short daṇḍaka. Its whole body is a single compound word. Its movement is a slow turn from war to marriage. It opens with Tāraka struck down, and the darkness of the gods' fear burned away. It passes to the birth in the bed of reeds, the milk of the Kṛttikās, and the fondling hands of Pārvatī. It goes on to Brahmā praising him from within his prison, and to Krauñca split by the point of the spear. It then arrives at the marriage, when the hand of Devasenā was taken, and at the winning of Vallī. In its last four words it leaves all this grandeur aside for a single request in the first person: I am utterly wretched; look upon me and protect me.

deva:
जय वज्रिसुताकान्त जय शङ्करनन्दन ।
जय मारशताकार जय वल्लीमनोहर ॥
iast:
jaya vajrisutākānta jaya śaṅkaranandana |
jaya māraśatākāra jaya vallīmanohara ||
en: Victory to you, beloved of the daughter of the wielder of the thunderbolt (Devasenā, Indra's daughter); victory to you, son of Śaṅkara; victory to you whose form is a hundred Māras; victory to you, charmer of Vallī's heart.
tel: జయము నీకు, వజ్రధరుని కుమార్తెకు (ఇంద్రుని పుత్రిక దేవసేనకు) ప్రియుడా; జయము నీకు, శంకరనందనా; జయము నీకు, నూరుగురు మన్మథుల ఆకారము కలవాడా; జయము నీకు, వల్లీ మనోహరా.
hi: तुम्हारी जय हो, वज्रधारी की पुत्री (इंद्र की कन्या देवसेना) के प्रिय; तुम्हारी जय हो, शंकर-नंदन; तुम्हारी जय हो, सौ कामदेवों के समान रूप वाले; तुम्हारी जय हो, वल्ली के मनोहर।

en: Victory! O sun to the darkness that was the terror of the gods — a darkness ended when Tāraka, who had conquered many universes by the strength of his arms and waged his terrifying war, was cut down. O six-faced one, whose own form arose from the effulgence born of the forehead-eye of Gaurī's lord and rose up among the cluster of lotuses on the river of the gods. O you of the radiance of a crore of suns; O goal of the brāhmaṇas; O you born in the reed-thicket; O six-faced one who came by his six faces through drinking the milk of the Kṛttikās; O you contented by the fondling of the lotus hands of the mountain's daughter; O you attended by the foremost heroes born of Kālī; O you by whose many childlike games the oceans, the mountains and the forest-tracts were left scattered and shaken. O you who delight in Devasenā, O lord of the gods. O you praised by the best of the gods, who showed them your own divine form; O you to be worshipped by the whole assembly of the immortals; O you of astonishing greatness, praised by the lotus-born Brahmā from within the prison he had been consigned to; O you by whose spear-point the lord of mountains was cleft; O you exalted by the divine consecration that the immortals, gladdened by the destruction of the demons, arranged for you; O you who gave joy to the lord of Śrī. O you who, like the god of the flower-arrows himself, came to great gladness in taking the hand of Devasenā, daughter of the king of the gods; O you of the special sports that captivated Vallī; O you mounted upon the lord of peacocks, whose blazing tail-fan is spoken of as Indra's bow — I am utterly wretched: protect me by the fall of your compassionate glance.

Recension note: Text taken from stotranidhi.com's Devanāgarī, Telugu and English (IAST) editions, which agree word for word, and cross-checked against sanskritdocuments.org's subradaNDaka.itx (encoded and proofread by Antaratma). Both witnesses carry the same opening quatrain, the same daṇḍaka compound in the same sequence, and the same closing salutation, so the text is securely attested; but every divergence found is a scribal error in the sanskritdocuments file, and stotranidhi's reading has been followed throughout. Those errors are: "tejasamurbhūta" for "tejaḥ samudbhūta"; "patmaṣaṇḍothita" and "patmādrijātā" for "padmaṣaṇḍothita" and "padmādrijātā"; "devatānāṁ vate" for "devatānāṁ pate" (lord of the gods); "divyāsvarūpa" for "divyasvarūpa"; and "stutyāścaryāmāhātmya" for "stutāścaryamāhātmya." Two further differences are orthographic rather than textual: the sanskritdocuments transcription writes the Dravidian ḷ in "kāḷī" and "saṁlāḷanā" where stotranidhi has plain l, and it runs the closing salutation on continuously where stotranidhi sets it off as its own line — the latter layout is followed here, which is why the salutation appears as its own block. Two compounds in the daṇḍaka body resist clean analysis in both witnesses alike and have been rendered by sense rather than emended: "vidyāṇḍa" in the opening chain, taken here as the many cosmic eggs or universes Tāraka had subdued, and "indrakodaṇḍabhāsvatkalāpocya," taken as the peacock's blazing tail-fan being spoken of as Indra's bow, i.e. the rainbow. IAST normalized to this corpus's house style: plain e/o in place of stotranidhi's ē/ō, uniform ṁ anusvāra, precomposed ṛ. The work carries no verse numbers in either source; each unit is accordingly carried here as a "verse none" block.
