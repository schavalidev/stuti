Recension note: **The Smārtha nāmāvalī is followed; the sanskritdocuments witness (SD) is recorded, not adopted.** **THE LIST HAS 107 NAMES, NOT 108.** The print's tally marks (10 … 100) agree with the count, and nothing is missing from the page. Against SD, the Smārtha print **lacks** `prāṇāya` (SD has `prāṇāya prāṇāyāmaparāyaṇāya`; the Smārtha has only no. 95 `प्राणायामपरायणाय`), `śyāmakandharāya` and `mahate`. It **has instead** no. 60 `वटवे` (SD `vasumate`) and no. 104 `वंशवृद्धिकराय`. SD's last name `vallīdevasenāsameta śrīsubrahmaṇyasvāmine` corresponds to the Smārtha no. 107 `श्रीसुब्रह्मण्याय`. Other differences (Smārtha → SD): no. 4 `फालनेत्रसुताय` → `bhālanetrasutāya`; no. 7 `कृत्तिकानांसूनवे` → `kṛttikāsūnave`; no. 13 `तारकासुरसंहारिणे` → `-saṁhartre`; no. 26 `क्रौञ्चधारणाय` ("bearing Krauñca") → `krauñcadāraṇāya` ("splitting Krauñca", the myth's sense; the Telugu print has ధ, and it is kept as printed, the gloss marked); no. 40 `आहूताय` → `āhutāya`; no. 80 `महासारस्वतावृताय` → `mahāsārasvatavratāya`; no. 101 `प्रीताय` → `guhaprītāya`. **Duplicates:** `शक्तिधराय` (nos. 11 and 24), `शङ्करात्मजाय` (nos. 30 and 70) and `गुहाय` (nos. 2 and 100), all shared with SD. **Bīja.** The print's heading note says `ఓం శ్రీం హ్రాం క్లీం సౌః శరవణభవ` "may also be added before each name"; it is recorded here, not applied. **Prefix.** The source prints `ఓం` (ॐ) before the first name of each column; this corpus prints `श्री` for every nāmāvalī without exception (user, 2026-09-08). **Cross-reference:** `Subrahmanya/49_kartikeya_ashtottarashatanama_stotram.txt` (a different Kārtikeya list), `Subrahmanya/50_valli_ashtottarashatanamavali.txt` and `51_devasena_ashtottarashatanamavali.txt` (his consorts, from the same volume).

Blurb: The war-god as the South knows him: six-faced and twelve-armed, riding a peacock, spear in hand, born of fire, of the Gaṅgā, of the reeds and of the six Kṛttikās, general of the gods and slayer of Tāraka. The list lingers on colour — one, two, three, four and five hues — and on ecstasy (exultant, rapturous, ecstatic), and it does not forget the hunter's daughter he married.
