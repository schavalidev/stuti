Title: Śrī Devasenā Aṣṭottaraśatanāmāvalī
Devanāgarī: श्री देवसेना-अष्टोत्तरशतनामावलिः
Telugu: శ్రీ దేవసేనా అష్టోత్తరశతనామావళిః

Author: Traditional; the source names no Purāṇa or composer. Devasenā is the consort of Subrahmaṇya given to him by Indra, her father in the usual account.

Source / recension: Primary text: **a Telugu-script nāmāvalī collection, Smārtha e-books (smarthamitra.in), "108–1008"** (scanned PDF, page images only, PDF metadata author "kprss", 2016), printed pp. 188–190 (PDF pp. 192–194). **Not a Gītā Press edition; Gītā Press's *Śatanāma Stotra Saṅgraha* (code 1850) has no Devasenā list.** Transcribed from the Telugu page images into Devanāgarī. **No witness of this exact list was found.** sanskritdocuments.org has two Devasenā nāmāvalīs. `devasenAShTottarashatanAmAvalI.itx` (proofread by Sunder Hattangadi and Ramesh Kutticad) is a **related list, not the same recension**: it shares a number of names (`pītāmbarāyai`, `utpaladhāriṇyai`, `jvālānetrāyai`, `maheśvaryai`, `brahmāṇyai`, `vaiṣṇavyai`, `acintyaśaktyai`, `akṣarāyai`, `candravarṇāyai`, `kalādharāyai`, `lokādhyakṣāyai`, `surādhyakṣāyai`, `dharmādhyakṣāyai`, `skandabhāryāyai`, `satprabhāvāyai`, `tuṅgabhadrāyai`, `vedagarbhāyai`, `parāśaktyai`, `tripurasundaryai`, `manonmanyai`, `sureśvaryai`, `dayārūpiṇyai`, `śaraṇāgatarakṣaṇāyai`, `devasenāyai`), but its order and most of its names differ. `devasena108.itx` is **a different list altogether** (a `da-/pa-/ma-…` acrostic opening `devasenāyai devalokajananyai divyasundaryai`). The collation is therefore **partial**: it confirms the shared names and cannot adjudicate the rest.

Recension note: **The Smārtha nāmāvalī is transcribed as printed; with no same-recension witness, nothing is emended.** **Relation to the Vallī list.** The Smārtha Vallī nāmāvalī that precedes it in the same volume (`Subrahmanya/50_valli_ashtottarashatanamavali.txt`) shares a large block of names with this one, often in the same order; see that file's Recension note. **Readings kept as printed, sense uncertain:** no. 29 `मयन्धरायै`; no. 70 `मूलधिपायै` (the Vallī list prints `मूलाधिपायै`; the long `ā` has probably dropped here); no. 77 `विशालक्ष्यै` (presumably for `विशालाक्ष्यै`); no. 99 `अभियातिगायै`. **Duplicates:** `देवसेनायै` appears **four times** (nos. 2, 74, 106 and 108), and `सुन्दर्यै` (nos. 40 and 87) and `सुराध्यक्षायै` (nos. 38 and 95) twice each. **Count:** the print's tally marks and the list agree at 108. The print also sets `ॐ` before no. 94 `विश्वतोमुख्यै` and no. 105 `सर्वेश्वर्यै`, mid-column. **Prefix.** The source prints `ఓం` (ॐ) before the first name of each column; this corpus prints `श्री` for every nāmāvalī without exception (user, 2026-09-08). **Cross-reference:** no other Devasenā text is in the corpus.

Blurb: Indra's daughter, the army of the gods made a bride for Skanda: yellow-clad, holding a water-lily, with eyes of flame. Like her sister-consort's list, this one keeps dissolving her into the great goddesses — Mahālakṣmī, Vārāhī, Sarasvatī, Umā, Pārvatī, Tripurasundarī, Cāmuṇḍā — and into the Veda itself, its essence and its womb, and it will not stop calling her by her own name, four times over.
