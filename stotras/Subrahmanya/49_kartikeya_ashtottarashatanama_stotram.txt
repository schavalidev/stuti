Title: Śrī Kārtikeya Aṣṭottaraśatanāma Stotram
Devanāgarī: श्रीकार्तिकेय-अष्टोत्तरशतनामस्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ కార్తికేయ అష్టోత్తరశతనామ స్తోత్రం

Author: Anonymous; Skanda Mahāpurāṇa, Māheśvara-khaṇḍa, Kumārikā-khaṇḍa. The hymn is placed in the mouth of Viśvāmitra, who composes it on taking refuge in Kumāra; from v. 17 Kārtikeya himself speaks the phalaśruti.

Source / recension: Primary text: **Gītā Press, Gorakhpur — Śatanāma Stotra Saṅgraha**, code 1850, pp. 19–23 (stotra pp. 19–21, nāmāvalī pp. 22–23), transcribed from the page images of the printed edition rather than from OCR. Entry 3 of the volume's forty. The colophon of both stotra and nāmāvalī gives the attribution in full: `इति श्रीस्कन्दमहापुराणे माहेश्वरखण्डान्तर्गते कुमारिकाखण्डे`. Stotra and nāmāvalī are paired in one file as the publisher prints them. Cross-checked internally: the 108 names of the nāmāvalī correspond in order to the name-sequence running from v. 3b to v. 16a, and that internal check is the collation this file rests on — the volume typesets the two lists separately, so agreement between them is real evidence. **No second edition was collated**, and no web witness to this text has yet been sought; the readings rest on Gītā Press alone.

Recension note: **Prefix.** Gītā Press prints `ॐ` before each name; this corpus prints `श्री` for every nāmāvalī without exception (user, 2026-09-08), as the universally admissible form. The divergence from the printed text is deliberate and recorded here rather than in the rows. **Case.** Every name is in the caturthī (dative), the form `नमः` governs, and the volume prints the `नमः` (namana) form throughout; the accusatives that `पूजयामि` and `तर्पयामि` would require are not attested here and have not been written into the file. Twenty-nine of the 108 re-inflect irregularly. **Numbering — two irregularities in the print, both retained.** (i) v. 1 is a single hemistich, `विश्वामित्रस्तु भगवान् कुमारं शरणं गतः`, numbered `॥ १ ॥` on its own; the volume counts a half-verse as a full verse here. (ii) The text closes with a further hemistich, `स्तवस्यास्य प्रभावेण दिव्यभावः पुमान्भवेत्`, which carries **no number at all** and stands immediately before the colophon. The printed numbering therefore runs 1–20 while the text contains twenty-one metrical units; this file gives the closing hemistich its own block (22) so that nothing printed is dropped, and the `Verse count` of 22 counts the dhyāna, the twenty numbered verses and that hemistich. **Three names repeat.** `महातेजस्` stands at nos. 48 and 57 (from vv. 9 and 10) and `सनातन` at nos. 37 and 108 (from vv. 8 and 16), so the list holds 106 distinct names in 108 slots, as the volume prints it. **`शत्रुघ्ने` (24) and `शत्रुघ्नाय` (98)** are not a third repetition but two different stems that fall together in English: no. 24 renders `शत्रुहा` of v. 7, an an-stem (`शत्रुहन्`) whose dative is `शत्रुघ्ने`, while no. 98 renders `शत्रुघ्नः` of v. 15, a regular a-stem. Both are retained exactly as printed. **`भुवे` (29), `भुवाय` (30)** likewise divide `भूर्भुवो` of v. 7 into two names on two different stems — `भू` (dative `भुवे`) and `भुव` (dative `भुवाय`) — which is what allows the sequence to reach 108. **Related texts in the corpus:** the Subrahmaṇya folder holds forty-eight other hymns to this deity; this is the first aṣṭottaraśatanāma among them, and the first to carry a nāmāvalī.

Blurb: Viśvāmitra takes refuge in Kumāra and makes him a hymn of a hundred and eight names, and the god answers by granting that whoever recites it will get what his heart desires. The names move from the Vedic Skanda — fire-born, son of the Kṛttikās, commander of the celestial army — through the paradoxes a war-god attracts (the eternal and the transient, the restraint and the leader of those who restrain) to the homely emblems of his cult, the cock and the spear.
