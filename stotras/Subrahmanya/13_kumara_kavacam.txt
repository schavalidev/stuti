కవచం — Kavacam (the entire text: one continuous prose litany of epithets addressed to Kumāra — first a long run of dative "salutation to..." epithets covering his births, weapons and metaphysical attributes, then a pivot into direct vocative address, a run of urgent imperative petitions, and a closing repeated namaste)

Blurb: This kavaca differs from the other two in this folder, which are items 7 and 8. It is not an armour made of numbered verses that guard the body part by part. It is a single unbroken prose litany, called gadya, addressed to Kumāra. It runs as one long string of epithets in the dative case, naming his birth, his weapons, and his nature as eternal, unborn, and beyond speech and mind. It then turns to direct address, with the words 'jaya jaya rudrakumāra'. After this comes a series of urgent doubled commands: remove, remove; gladden, gladden; deliver, deliver; revive, revive. It closes with a plain fourfold namaste.

en: Om, salutation to the Blessed Lord — to him who severs the bonds of worldly existence (bhavabandha), the refuge of true devotees, born in the reed-thicket (Śaravaṇa), endowed with Śambhu's (Śiva's) own majesty, the leader of yogis, the giver of enjoyment, surrounded by the great army of the gods, adorned with a multitude of great jewels, the cause of the destruction of wicked daityas, the one who split apart the forbidding Mount Krauñca, whose hands are adorned with the śakti-spear, trident, mace, sword, shield, noose, goad, club, javelin, lance, and the boon-granting and fear-dispelling gestures, whose lotus feet bear the vow of protecting those who take refuge in them, the sole deliverer of all the worlds, the secret held within all the Vedas (nigama), whose banner bears a cock, in whose belly rests the whole sphere of the cosmic egg, worshipped by Ākhaṇḍala (Indra), the sovereign of the heart and the moon rising over its inmost ocean, whose desires are wholly fulfilled, desireless, without compare, beyond the pairs of opposites, eternal, true, pure, awakened, liberated, unmanifest, unobstructable, indivisible, unconquerable, unsunderable, void of beginning and end, unborn, immeasurable, beyond the reach of speech and mind, supremely tranquil, utterly whole, higher than the highest, whose very form is the praṇava (the syllable Om), the destroyer of the afflictions of those who bow to him, the delighter of those who take refuge in him. Victory, victory to you, Rudra's son! O one of great strength and valor, O root of the bliss of the thirty-three crores of gods, O Skanda, O one of matchless bliss — remove, remove my debts, illnesses, and the afflictions caused by enemies; gladden, gladden me who am worn out with sorrow; deliver, deliver me from the fear of hell; revive, revive me, who am afflicted by the trouble of transmigratory existence (saṁsāra). You are the granter of boons, you are compassionate, you are capable; having granted great enjoyment and liberation to me who has taken refuge in you, protect me with a hundred years of life. O friend of the humble, O ocean of compassion, Kārtikeya, O Lord, be gracious, be gracious; become well-pleased, become the granter of boons; O Lord Subrahmaṇya — Om, salutations, salutations, salutations, salutation to you! Thus ends the Kumāra-kavaca.
