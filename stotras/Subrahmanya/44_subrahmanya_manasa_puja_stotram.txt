Title: Śrī Subrahmaṇya Mānasa Pūjā Stotram
Devanāgarī: श्री सुब्रह्मण्य मानसपूजा स्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ సుబ్రహ్మణ్య మానసపూజా స్తోత్రం

Author: not named. The colophon gives only the title ("iti śrī subrahmaṇya mānasa pūjā"). No ṛṣi, chandas or viniyoga is supplied — as is usual for a mānasa pūjā, which is a private devotional exercise rather than a mantra requiring ritual authorization.

Source / recension: stotranidhi.com's "Śrī Subrahmaṇya Mānasa Pūjā Stotram," taken in its Devanāgarī, Telugu and English (IAST) editions, which agree word for word across all 43 verses; the Telugu edition carries a per-verse prose meaning-gloss, used here to resolve the corrupt reading at v. 4 and to confirm the identification of the offerings through the long naivedya sequence. No counterpart exists on sanskritdocuments.org: its doc_subrahmanya catalog was enumerated, and the two mānasa-pūjā texts it does hold were both checked and are different works — "śaravaṇabhavamānasapūjāstotram," which opens in śikhariṇī with "gukāro hyākhyāti prabalamanivāryaṁ kila tamo," and "ṣaṇmukhamānasapūjā 2" attributed to Āṇḍavan Piccai Ammāḷ. Flagged accordingly as single-site sourcing, mitigated by the exact agreement of three independent script editions plus the Telugu gloss.

Verse count: 43, plus a one-line prose colophon

Sections:
ధ్యానమ్ — Dhyāna (vv. 1–7): the setting on Meru and the Skanda hill, the golden pavilion and lion-throne, and the form of the god from hair to feet
ఉపచారాః — Upacāras (vv. 8–41): the sixteen-fold service performed in the mind — invocation, seat, water for the feet, arghya, sipping-water, the great bath, ash and garment, sacred thread, ornaments, sandal, tilaka and akṣata, flowers, incense, lamp, the long food-offering, hand-washing, betel, the waving of camphor light, the royal services, circumambulation, salutation, and the asking of pardon
నమస్కారః — Namaskāra (v. 42): the four-fold closing salutation
ఫలశ్రుతి — Phalaśruti (v. 43): the fruit of performing this worship even once
సమాప్తి వాక్యం — Colophon (the closing sentence naming the work)

Blurb: This is a mānasa pūjā, performed wholly in the imagination, and it is unusually detailed. Eleven verses together, numbers 24 to 34, are given to the food offering alone. They name the papads fried in ghee, the pieces of pumpkin in their hot pan, the cakes of black gram and jaggery and wheat, the laḍḍus, the pāyasa, the oranges, the ginger and the pepper, the curd as white as the autumn moon, and at last the clear water to drink. A whole South Indian feast is thus prepared in the mind and set out in vessels of gold. After this the worshipper washes the god's hands, offers betel, waves the camphor flame, and asks pardon for a thousand faults.

--- verse 1 | section: Dhyāna ---
deva:
श्रीमन्मेरुधराधराधिप महासौभाग्यसंशोभिते
मन्दारद्रुमवाटिकापरिवृते श्रीस्कन्दशैलेमले
सौधे हाटकनिर्मिते मणिमये सन्मण्टपाभ्यन्तरे
ब्रह्मानन्दघनं गुहाख्यमनघं सिंहासनं चिन्तये ॥
iast:
śrīmanmerudharādharādhipa mahāsaubhāgyasaṁśobhite
mandāradrumavāṭikāparivṛte śrīskandaśailemale
saudhe hāṭakanirmite maṇimaye sanmaṇṭapābhyantare
brahmānandaghanaṁ guhākhyamanaghaṁ siṁhāsanaṁ cintaye ||
en: On the glorious Meru, chief among mountains, resplendent with great good fortune; on the spotless Skanda hill, ringed about with a garden of mandāra trees; within a fine pavilion inside a mansion built of gold and set with gems — there I contemplate the flawless lion-throne of him who is called Guha, a solid mass of the bliss of Brahman.
tel: శ్రీమంతమైన మేరువు అనే పర్వతరాజుపై, మహాసౌభాగ్యంతో శోభిల్లునది; మందార వృక్షాల వాటికతో చుట్టబడినది; నిర్మలమైన శ్రీస్కంద శైలంపై; బంగారంతో నిర్మించిన, మణిమయమైన సౌధంలోని చక్కని మంటపం లోపల — బ్రహ్మానందఘనమైన, గుహుడనే పేరు కలవాని అనఘమైన సింహాసనాన్ని నేను చింతిస్తున్నాను.
hi: श्रीमान् मेरु नामक पर्वतराज पर, महासौभाग्य से सुशोभित; मंदार वृक्षों की वाटिका से घिरे; निर्मल श्रीस्कंद शैल पर; स्वर्ण से निर्मित, मणिमय सौध के भीतर सुंदर मंडप में — ब्रह्मानंदघन, गुह नामक उस अनघ के सिंहासन का मैं चिंतन करता हूँ।

--- verse 4 | section: Dhyāna ---
deva:
गण्डस्थलचलच्छोत्र कुण्डलं चारुकन्धरम् ।
करासक्तकनःदण्डं रत्नहाराञ्चितोरसम् ॥
iast:
gaṇḍasthalacalacchotra kuṇḍalaṁ cārukandharam |
karāsaktakanaḥdaṇḍaṁ ratnahārāñcitorasam ||
en: With earrings swaying at his ears against his cheeks; with a lovely neck; with a golden staff held fast in his hand; his chest graced with necklaces of gems. The reading "kanaḥdaṇḍaṁ" is corrupt as transmitted; the Telugu edition's gloss reads it as kanaka-daṇḍa, a golden staff held in the hand, and that sense is followed here.
tel: చెక్కిళ్ళపై కదిలే శ్రోత్ర కుండలాలు కలవాడు; అందమైన కంఠము కలవాడు; చేతిలో బంగారు దండము ధరించినవాడు; రత్నహారాలతో అలంకృతమైన వక్షస్థలం కలవాడు. "కనఃదండం" అనే పాఠం సంక్రమణలో దోషపూరితమైనది; తెలుగు సంపుటి దీనిని కనక-దండ (చేతిలో బంగారు దండము) గా చదువుతుంది, ఆ అర్థమే ఇక్కడ అనుసరించబడింది.
hi: कपोलों पर हिलते कर्ण-कुंडलों वाले; सुंदर कंठ वाले; हाथ में स्वर्ण-दंड धारण किए; रत्न-हारों से अंचित वक्षस्थल वाले। "कनःदंडं" पाठ प्राप्त रूप में दूषित है; तेलुगु संस्करण की व्याख्या इसे कनक-दंड (हाथ में स्वर्ण-दंड) पढ़ती है, वही अर्थ यहाँ लिया गया है।

Recension note: Sourced from stotranidhi.com's Devanāgarī, Telugu and English (IAST) editions, which agree word for word across all 43 verses, with the Telugu edition's per-verse prose gloss used as an internal sense-check. No counterpart exists on sanskritdocuments.org: its doc_subrahmanya catalog was enumerated and the two mānasa-pūjā texts it holds were both fetched and confirmed to be different compositions — "śaravaṇabhavamānasapūjāstotram," which opens in śikhariṇī with "gukāro hyākhyāti prabalamanivāryaṁ kila tamo," and "ṣaṇmukhamānasapūjā 2" ascribed to Āṇḍavan Piccai Ammāḷ. This item is therefore flagged as single-site sourcing. Two places are corrupt as transmitted, identically across all three script editions, and have been left as received rather than emended, with the intended sense taken from the Telugu gloss and flagged in the translation fields: v. 4's "karāsaktakanaḥdaṇḍaṁ," which the gloss reads as karāsakta-kanaka-daṇḍaṁ, a golden staff held in the hand; and v. 38's "datternīrājayāmi tvāṁ karpūraprabhayānaya," where the syntax does not resolve but the act described — the waving of the camphor flame — is not in doubt. A third oddity is textual rather than corrupt: the compound ending "-añcitorasam" closes both v. 3 and v. 4, which reads as a repetition; v. 3 has accordingly been rendered without forcing the sense "chest" a second time. The long naivedya sequence (vv. 24–34) names many regional dishes whose identification is not always certain — khacarī, eḍḍumaka, maṇḍaka, polikā, śaṣkulī, kūrcikā, maṇḍikā, phāṇita — and these have been rendered by the closest available description, with the Sanskrit name retained where no confident English equivalent exists. IAST normalized to this corpus's house style: plain e/o in place of stotranidhi's ē/ō, uniform ṁ anusvāra, precomposed ṛ. Verse numbers have been removed from the deva and iast fields per corpus convention and are carried by the block headers instead.
