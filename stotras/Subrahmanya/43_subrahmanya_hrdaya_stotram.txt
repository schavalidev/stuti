Title: Śrī Subrahmaṇya Hṛdaya Stotram
Devanāgarī: श्री सुब्रह्मण्य हृदय स्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ సుబ్రహ్మణ్య హృదయ స్తోత్రం

Author: no human author. The text is Purāṇic: stotranidhi's colophon places it in the Skanda Purāṇa ("iti śrīskāndapurāṇe subrahmaṇyahṛdayastotram"), and the hymn is cast as Brahmā's reply to the sages in Satyaloka, who ask him to disclose the "hṛdaya" — the heart, the innermost secret — of Skanda. The viniyoga in the stotranidhi recension names Bhagavān Agastya as the ṛṣi of the mantra; the sanskritdocuments recension instead names Parabrahman (see the recension note, as this is one of several substantive divergences between the two).

Source / recension: primary source is stotranidhi.com's "Śrī Subrahmaṇya Hṛdaya Stotram," taken in its Devanāgarī, Telugu and English (IAST) editions, which agree word for word across the ritual prelude and all 37 verses. Cross-checked independently against sanskritdocuments.org's "śrīsubrahmaṇyahṛdayastotram" (file subrahridayastotra.itx, transliterated and proofread by Antaratma). The two are recognizably the same work — same frame narrative, same sequence of visualization, same closing rules of transmission — but they are genuinely different recensions rather than two copies of one text: the sanskritdocuments version runs to 38 verses against stotranidhi's 37, carries two half-verses of description that stotranidhi lacks, lacks stotranidhi's closing couplet, and differs materially in the viniyoga and nyāsa. The divergences are itemized in the closing recension note. stotranidhi's text has been followed throughout as the markedly cleaner witness.

Verse count: 37 numbered verses, preceded by a four-part ritual prelude (viniyoga, karanyāsa, hṛdayādi nyāsa, dhyāna) and followed by a colophon — 42 blocks in all

Sections:
వినియోగః — Viniyoga (the ritual specification of ṛṣi, meter, deity, seed, power and pin)
న్యాసః — Nyāsa (two units: the assignment of six names to the fingers, and the assignment of the same six to heart, head, tuft, armor, eyes and weapon, closed by the binding of the directions)
ధ్యానమ్ — Dhyānam (the single śārdūlavikrīḍita visualization verse, naming all twelve attributes of the twelve arms, with the rubric for the five-fold worship)
పీఠిక — Pīṭhikā (vv. 1–7): the frame — the sages in Satyaloka put their question to Brahmā, and he consents to disclose the secret
హృదయమ్ — Hṛdayam (vv. 8–30): the visualization proper, working outward-in from the golden pavilion to the jeweled throne to the eight-petalled lotus, and then over Skanda's body from crown to feet
ఫలశ్రుతి — Phalaśruti (vv. 31–37): the method of recitation, the fruits, and the rules governing who may and may not be given the text
సమాప్తి వాక్యం — Colophon (the closing sentence naming the work and its Purāṇic home)

Blurb: This is a hṛdaya in the technical sense. It is not a hymn of praise but a guarded inner teaching. It is presented as Brahmā granting the request of the sages, while warning in the same breath that it must never be given to one who has not been tested. Its long middle section is a carefully ordered visualisation. It first builds a golden pavilion of a thousand jewelled pillars. Within it, it sets a lion throne. Upon the throne it opens an eight-petalled lotus, with the Vedas and Āgamas hanging from it like bees. Only then does it place the god there. After this it moves down his body, ornament by ornament, from the six crowns and the crescent moon to the jewelled anklets upon his feet.

Recension note: Text taken from stotranidhi.com's Devanāgarī, Telugu and English (IAST) editions, which agree word for word across the ritual prelude and all 37 verses. Cross-checked against sanskritdocuments.org's subrahridayastotra.itx (transliterated and proofread by Antaratma). The two are the same work in two genuinely distinct recensions, not two copies of one text, and the differences are worth recording. In the ritual prelude: stotranidhi names Agastya as ṛṣi, anuṣṭubh as chandas, svāhā as śakti and śrīṁ as kīlaka, where sanskritdocuments names Parabrahman as ṛṣi, devī gāyatrī as chandas, prīṁ as śakti and Śrī Subrahmaṇyeśvara as kīlaka; stotranidhi's fourth nyāsa name is "ṣaṭkoṇasaṁsthitāya" (abiding in the hexagram) where sanskritdocuments has "ṣaḍtriṁśatkoṇāya" (of thirty-six angles); stotranidhi's hṛdayādi nyāsa has six limbs including "netratrayāya vauṣaṭ," which sanskritdocuments omits, redistributing the remainder; and where stotranidhi abbreviates the pañcapūjā to the rubric "lamityādi pañcapūjāṁ kuryāt," sanskritdocuments writes it out in full (laṁ pṛthivyātmane gandhaṁ kalpayāmi, and so on through saṁ sarvātmane). In the body: sanskritdocuments runs to 38 verses against stotranidhi's 37, carrying two descriptive units stotranidhi lacks — one on the face bright as a thousand full moons with a broad forehead and tossing curls, and one on Skanda as the support of the whole world with a golden-hued belly — while lacking stotranidhi's closing couplet promising Skanda's grace to the daily reciter, and dozens of half-lines differ in wording (for instance stotranidhi's v. 11 "maṇividrumavedībhirmahatībhirudañcitam" against sanskritdocuments' "māṇikyavidrumaiścaiva mahāśrībhiruñcitam"). Independently of these genuine variants, the sanskritdocuments transcription is heavily error-strewn — "sarvāścaryāmayaṁ," "viśākhaṁ" for viśālaṁ, "śucau devaśe" for śucau deśe, "prāṅmukhau" for prāṅmukho, "devānāmapi durlabhā" for durlabham — and stotranidhi's markedly cleaner text has been followed at every point. One typographic artifact was normalized: at v. 9 both sources print a stray single daṇḍa before the verse number, the sentence in fact running on into v. 10; a single closing daṇḍa is given here. IAST normalized to this corpus's house style: plain e/o in place of stotranidhi's ē/ō, uniform ṁ anusvāra, precomposed ṛ. Verse numbers have been removed from the deva and iast fields per corpus convention; the prelude units and the colophon, unnumbered in both sources, are carried as "verse none" blocks, with the source's own internal rubrics (pīṭhikā, atha hṛdayam, dhyānam) retained inside the text as transmitted material.
