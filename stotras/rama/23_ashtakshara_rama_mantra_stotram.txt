Blurb: The whole hymn is a machine for repeating one line. 'Śrī-rā-maḥ śa-ra-ṇaṁ ma-ma', meaning 'Śrī Rāma is my refuge', is exactly eight syllables. That eight-syllable mantra is made the closing pāda of thirty-four of the thirty-five verses. Everything else in the poem is the changing first three pādas that lead into it. What changes is unusually well ordered. Verses 5 to 16 lay out Rāma in the fivefold Śrīvaiṣṇava scheme, and in something close to its canonical sequence. Verse 9 gives the transcendent Rāma seated in the highest heaven among the eternally freed. Verse 8 gives the source of the four vyūha forms, beginning with Vāsudeva, and of the twenty-four. Verse 14 gives the descents as the fish and the rest. Verse 12 gives the inner self of all beings. Verses 15 and 16 give the arcā, the consecrated image, through which, by being seen and being touched, he lifts up the wretched. Only then, at verse 17, does the Rāmāyaṇa begin. Kauśalyā is the oyster-shell and Rāma the pearl that becomes the ornament at Jānakī's throat. From there it runs in order to the throne. The last verse breaks the pattern twice over. It drops the refrain, and it slips into Rāma's own voice, quoting back the promise of Bhagavad Gītā 18.66, about the one who abandons all duties and comes to him alone for refuge.

Recension note: The two stotranidhi editions (IAST and Devanāgarī) carry the identical 35-verse text with no variant readings; neither prints verse numbers, so the numbering here is this file's own. The title's aṣṭākṣara is the refrain itself: śrī-rā-maḥ-śa-ra-ṇaṁ-ma-ma is exactly eight syllables, and it closes vv. 1–34; v. 35 is the only verse that does not carry it, and also the only one spoken in Rāma's own voice, where "mama stotram" means the hymn addressed to him. Its first line is a close paraphrase of Bhagavad Gītā 18.66, though the printed form sarvadharmebhyoḥ is grammatically irregular (a genitive/ablative dual ending where the sense requires an ablative plural or the Gītā's own accusative construction); it is reproduced as the source has it in both editions, and translated for sense. Verses 5–16 set out the standard Śrīvaiṣṇava fivefold scheme — para (v. 9), vyūha (v. 8), vibhava (v. 14), antaryāmin (v. 12), arcā (vv. 15–16) — which together with the Bṛhad Brahma Saṁhitā colophon places this text in a Vaiṣṇava āgamic milieu, distinct from the Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa and Ānanda Rāmāyaṇa sources of most other files in this folder. Verses 28–29 attribute Hanumān's Sundarakāṇḍa deeds — the ocean crossing, the burning of Laṅkā — to Rāma as their author; this is the source's own construction and is preserved in the translations, with a bracketed clarification at v. 29.
