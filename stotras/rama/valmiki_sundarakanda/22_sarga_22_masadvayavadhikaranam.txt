Blurb: Rāvaṇa's patience ends. He observes that the more a man speaks sweetly to women the more he is slighted, says that desire alone is holding back his anger like a charioteer holding runaway horses, and then sets the term that governs the rest of the kāṇḍa: two months, and after that she comes to his couch or is cut up in his kitchen for his morning meal. The women of his household, themselves captives, comfort Sītā with their lips and eyes without speaking. She answers that no one near him is set upon his good, that his tongue should have fallen to pieces for speaking so, and that she does not burn him to ashes only because Rāma has not said to and because she guards her austerity. Rāvaṇa then commands the rākṣasa women, named by their deformities, to break her by any means, favourable or contrary, and by the raising of the rod. What ends the scene is neither of them. A rākṣasa woman named Dhānyamālinī embraces him and tells him a body is only tormented by desiring one who does not desire it, and he laughs and goes indoors.
