Blurb: This is Hanumān’s long speech in the court. He gives the whole story in order: Daśaratha, his eldest son sent to the forest, Lakṣmaṇa and Sītā with him, Sītā lost, the meeting with Sugrīva at Ṛśyamūka, the two promises exchanged, the killing of Vālin whom Rāvaṇa himself knew, and the vānaras sent out in every direction. He names himself as the son of the wind god, says he crossed the ocean, and says he has seen Sītā. Then he counsels. He tells Rāvaṇa that wise men do not cling to deeds that are against dharma. He says Sītā cannot be digested, like food mixed with poison. He meets Rāvaṇa’s confidence in the boon directly: the boon named gods, asuras and rākṣasas, and Sugrīva is none of these, and Rāma is a man. He says that dharma gathered by austerity is spent, and that the fruit of adharma will follow as surely. He tells him that Sītā is the night of destruction fastened about his own neck. He ends by saying that in all the worlds, among gods, gandharvas, vidyādharas, nāgas and siddhas, there is none who could stand against Rāma in battle. Rāvaṇa hears him out, and is filled with rage.
