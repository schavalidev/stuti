Blurb: Hanumān salutes Sītā once more before leaving. She speaks to him plainly. She says that he alone is able to do this work, but she asks him to act so that Rāma himself comes with his army and takes her back by his own valour, for that is what befits him. Hanumān assures her that Rāma will come quickly, surrounded by the foremost of the vānaras and the bears. Then he sets out. The greater part of the sarga is a description of Mount Ariṣṭa, and it is built on a single figure: the mountain is spoken of throughout as though it were alive. It seems to be waking under the rays of the sun, reciting with the deep sound of its streams, singing with the clear sound of its waterfalls, standing with arms raised in the form of its tall devadāru trees, trembling as the autumn clouds shake upon it, hissing in anger with the great serpents on it, and striding out on feet that are like the lower edges of clouds. Hanumān climbs it and grows large. When he springs, the mountain is pressed down under him. Its peaks shake, its trees are torn up, the serpents are crushed, the vidyādhara women fly up in alarm, and the mountain itself, ten yojanas wide, sinks until it is level with the earth.
