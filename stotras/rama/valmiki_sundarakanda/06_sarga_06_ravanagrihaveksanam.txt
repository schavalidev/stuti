Blurb: Hanumān has searched the mansions and not found Sītā, and he begins again. This sarga is a walk through the quarter of the rākṣasa lords. He comes first to Rāvaṇa's own dwelling, walled in a rampart the colour of the sun and guarded as a forest is guarded by lions, loud with drums and conches, deep and sounding like the sea. He thinks of it as the ornament of Laṅkā. Then he goes without fear from house to house, and the poet names the owners one after another for eleven verses: Prahasta, Mahāpārśva, Kumbhakarṇa, Vibhīṣaṇa, Mahodara, Virūpākṣa, Vidyujjihva, Indrajit, Jambumālin, Dhūmrākṣa, Śukanāsa, Śoṇitākṣa and many more. The list is the point. It measures the strength of the city by counting the men who hold it. He sees the guards asleep with spears and clubs in their hands, war elephants trained to break the elephants of the foe, and a thousand palanquins of gold. At the end he enters the great house itself, past its bowers and picture-halls and its house of love, ringing with anklets and girdles and the beat of drums.
