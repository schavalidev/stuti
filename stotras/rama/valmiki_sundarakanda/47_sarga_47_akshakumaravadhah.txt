Blurb: This sarga is written in a long metre from beginning to end. Rāvaṇa looks at his young son Akṣa, and that look alone sends the prince out to battle. His chariot is described at length: bright as the young sun, covered with a net of gold, yoked to eight horses swift as thought, carrying quivers, swords, lances and iron clubs. When the two meet, the earth cries out, the sun does not burn, the wind does not blow and the ocean is stirred. Akṣa sends three arrows into Hanumān’s head, and Hanumān, bleeding, shines like the sun garlanded with its own rays. The fight moves into the sky. What gives this sarga its weight is that Hanumān does not wish to kill the boy. He says openly that the prince fights like no child, that he is honoured by the nāgas, the yakṣas and the sages, and that his mind does not turn to killing him. He decides upon it only because a growing fire may not be left alone. He kills the eight horses with blows of his palms, breaks the chariot, seizes Akṣa by the feet as he springs into the sky, whirls him a thousand times and dashes him upon the earth. The gods and the sages look on in wonder, and Hanumān returns to the archway.
