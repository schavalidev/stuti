Blurb: Fourteen verses of reasoning. Sītā has looked up and seen a vānara in the tree, tawny as lightning and modest in his bearing, and the sight terrifies her so that she faints twice over. Her first thought is that this is a dream, and she says a prayer for Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa and her father, because seeing a monkey in a dream is held inauspicious. Then she corrects herself: it cannot be a dream, since grief allows her no sleep at all. Her second thought is that it may be her own longing taking shape, since she thinks of Rāma and speaks of Rāma until she sees and hears only him. Then she corrects that too, with the sharpest line in the sarga: a wish of the heart has no form, and this one stands before me plainly and speaks. Having no explanation left, she bows to Bṛhaspati and Indra and Brahmā and Agni, and asks only that what the forest-dweller has said may be true and not otherwise.

tel: ఇది మనోరథమేమోయని తలచెదను; అయినను బుద్ధితో దానిని విచారించెదను. కారణమేమి? మనోరథమునకు రూపము లేదు; ఇతడు స్పష్టమైన రూపముతో నాతో మాటలాడుచున్నాడు.
