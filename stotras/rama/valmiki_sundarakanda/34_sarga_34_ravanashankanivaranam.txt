Blurb: Hanumān delivers the message, and it almost fails. He gives Rāma's greeting and Lakṣmaṇa's salutation, and Sītā's joy is such that she quotes a proverb: joy comes to a living man even after a hundred years. But as he steps closer she suspects him again, and now with reason — it was Rāvaṇa in a wandering ascetic's disguise who took her at Janasthāna, so a changed shape is exactly what she has learnt to fear. She lets go the branch, sits on the ground, and refuses to look at him. Then she reasons her way through it a second time, asking whether this is a dream, or a working of the wind, or madness, or a mirage — and rejecting each, since she knows both herself and him. Because rākṣasas take any form at will, she falls silent rather than answer. Hanumān, understanding her silence, changes his approach: he stops speaking of himself and praises Rāma at length, then names himself and his errand, and asks her plainly to give up the doubt.
