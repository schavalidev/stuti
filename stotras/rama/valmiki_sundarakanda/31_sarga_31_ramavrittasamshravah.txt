Blurb: Hanumān speaks at last, and what he says is the whole story from the beginning, told aloud within her hearing as though to no one. He begins with King Daśaratha, upright and of great fame, and his eldest son Rāma, guardian of his own conduct and of dharma. Then the exile at his father's word, the killing of the rākṣasas in the forest, the slaughter at Janasthāna and the deaths of Khara and Dūṣaṇa, and Rāvaṇa's revenge: the golden deer, the deception, the carrying off. Then the friendship with Sugrīva, the death of Vālin, the vānaras sent out in thousands to every quarter, and his own leap of a hundred yojanas on Sampāti's word. He ends by saying that the form and the colour and the beauty he was told of are hers, and that she has been found. Then he falls silent. Sītā, filled with wonder, raises her face from under her hair and looks up into the śiṁśupā, and sees him there, like the sun risen in the east.
