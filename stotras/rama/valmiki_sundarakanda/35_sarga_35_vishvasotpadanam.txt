Blurb: The longest sarga of the kāṇḍa, and the one that finally convinces her. Sītā asks how he came to know Rāma, and for the marks of Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, so that grief may not overtake her again. Hanumān answers with a long formal description: Rāma's qualities first, then a technical catalogue of bodily marks counted in threes and fours and tens, of the kind used in the science of auspicious signs. Then the whole history, which she has not heard: the meeting at Ṛśyamūka, Hanumān carrying the two brothers on his back, the alliance, and the moment the vānaras brought Rāma the ornaments she had dropped, which set him weeping until Hanumān had to raise him up. Then the search, the despair on Vindhya, the vānaras sitting down to die, and Sampāti arriving like an answer, whose word about his brother's death told them where she was. He ends by naming his own father and birth, says the virtues were told for her trust alone, and asks what she wishes him to do. She weeps for joy, and her face shines like the moon released from Rāhu.
