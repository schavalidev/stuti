Blurb: This is the longest sarga of the Sundarakāṇḍa, and it is Hanumān’s own account of everything he has done. Jāmbavān asks him to tell it from the beginning, and he tells it in the first person. He gives the leap from Mahendra, Mount Maināka rising out of the sea to offer him rest, Surasā the mother of the serpents opening her mouth to test him, and Siṁhikā who seizes the shadow of what passes over her. He tells of entering Laṅkā by night, of the guardian goddess of the city whom he struck down, of the search through the houses, and of his despair when he could not find Sītā. He tells of finding her at last under the śiṁśapā tree, wasted by fasting, guarded by the rākṣasīs, and of Rāvaṇa coming to threaten her. He gives their whole conversation, her refusal to believe him at first, the ring by which she knew him, the crest jewel she gave him, and the token of the crow on Citrakūṭa. Then he gives the breaking of the grove, the killing of the kiṅkaras and of Akṣa, the binding by the weapon of Brahmā, his being brought before Rāvaṇa, the burning of his tail and the burning of the city. He ends by saying that what now remains is Rāma’s own work, and that the crossing must be made.

Recension note: This file follows the recension printed in the base text, in which the sarga runs to 167 verse units. The independent Southern witness divides the same text into 165 units. That is a difference of division and not of text. Six readings are printed with the other reading in round brackets after them, and in every case both independent witnesses agree against the base text. At verse 6 the base reads प्रणम्य and both witnesses read नमस्यन्, and both words mean "saluting". At verse 41 the base reads निकृतं and both read विकृतं, and both mean the offence done. At verse 79 the base reads प्रसाद्य and both read उत्थाप्य. At verse 91 the base reads विक्रान्तो and both read विश्रान्तो. At verse 104 the base reads तया and both read तदा, and the base itself already marks उत्तरं as the other reading in that line. At verse 144 the base reads मत्र्पभावमविज्ञाय, which is not a Sanskrit word; the Gītā Press edition, which is the authority here, reads मत्प्रभावमविज्ञाय, "not knowing my power", and that reading is given in brackets. The Southern witness carries the same transposed letters as the base, so two witnesses agreeing are not thereby right. At verse 117 the two independent witnesses read मया विध्वंसितो रुषा, "destroyed by me in anger", where the base reads स च विध्वंसितो मया. That is a difference of word order with one word added, so it is recorded here and not printed.
