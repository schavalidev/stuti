Blurb: The rescue of the kāṇḍa comes from inside Rāvaṇa's own household. An aged rākṣasa woman named Trijaṭā stops the others with a dream, and the dream is the whole sarga. She has seen Rāma in white, borne on an ivory palanquin drawn by a thousand swans, and again on a four-tusked white elephant with Lakṣmaṇa; Sītā in white upon a white mountain ringed by the sea, joined to Rāma as light to the sun, reaching from his lap to wipe the sun and moon with her hand. Against that she has seen Rāvaṇa on the ground smeared with oil, in red, drunk, then shaven and in black, dragged southward by a black woman, sunk in filth, riding an ass. Kumbhakarṇa and Indrajit go south with him. Vibhīṣaṇa alone stands in white under a white parasol. Laṅkā lies fallen in the sea with her gateways broken, burnt by a swift vānara who is Rāma's messenger — which is a dream of what Hanumān, listening in the tree above, has not yet done. Trijaṭā tells the others to beg Sītā's pardon, and reads the good omens in Sītā's own body: her eye throbbing, her left side thrilling, her arm trembling of itself.
