Blurb: Rāvaṇa accepts that an envoy is not to be killed, and orders a punishment instead. The tail is a dear ornament to a vānara, so let the tail be burnt, and let him be led through the city in that state for all his people to see. The rākṣasas wrap his tail in old cotton cloth, pour oil upon it and set it alight. Hanumān lets them. He knows he could break the bonds and kill them all, but he bears it for Rāma’s sake, and he sees a use in it: he could not see Laṅkā well at night, and now he will be carried through the whole of it in daylight. So he goes quietly, and examines the palaces, the enclosures, the streets, the crossings and the squares while the rākṣasas proclaim him a spy. The rākṣasīs carry the news to Sītā, and she turns to the fire and prays. If she has served her husband, if she has been faithful to one alone, if any good fortune remains to her, let the fire be cool to Hanumān. The fire blazes up before her as though answering, and the wind blows cool as over snow. Hanumān feels the flame resting on his tail like a fall of snow, and knows the reason: the friendship of the fire with his father the wind, the power of Rāma, and Sītā’s freedom from sin. He then makes himself small, throws off his bonds, springs up, takes an iron bar from the archway and kills the rākṣasas who held him.
