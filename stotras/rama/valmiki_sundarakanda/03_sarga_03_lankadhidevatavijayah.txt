Blurb: Hanumān climbs the rampart at night and looks down into the city. Much of the sarga is what he sees: gates of gold, altars of vaiḍūrya, floors set with diamond and pearl, stairways and courtyards, cranes and peacocks, and everywhere the sound of instruments and of ornaments. He judges that no force could storm the place, and names the few vānaras who could even reach it, himself among them. Then the city stops him. Laṅkā is not only a city but a guardian goddess, and she rises in her own form, dreadful of face, and demands to know who he is. He answers plainly that he has come to see the city. She strikes him with the flat of her hand. He strikes back with his left fist, and because she is a woman he does not use his full strength. She falls. Seeing her fallen, he shows her mercy. She then tells him what Brahmā once foretold: on the day a vānara overcomes her by force, ruin has come upon the rākṣasas. That day, she says, is today. She lets him in and tells him to search the city as he pleases.
