Blurb: Hanumān, free of his bonds, asks himself what is still left to do that would hurt the rākṣasas most. The grove is broken, the foremost rākṣasas are killed. The fortress remains, and a fortress once destroyed makes the rest of the work light. The fire is already burning on his tail, so he uses it. The poet then gives a long list of the houses he burns, named one after another: Prahasta, Mahāpārśva, Indrajit, Raśmiketu, Sūryaśatru, Dhvajagrīva, Vidyujjihva, Śoṇitākṣa, Kumbhakarṇa and many more. He spares one house only, that of Vibhīṣaṇa. The wind joins the fire and carries it through the mansions, and the gold and gems of the buildings melt and run down. Women fall from the burning houses with their hair loose. The gods, the siddhas and the great sages look on and ask one another whether this is Indra, or Yama, or the wrath of Brahmā, or the splendour of Viṣṇu that has taken the form of a vānara. The sarga ends with Hanumān standing on the upper slope of Trikūṭa, and the hosts of the gods praising him.

Recension note: This file follows the recension printed in the base text, in which the sarga runs to 52 verse units. The independent Southern witness divides the same text into 48 units, because it groups several of the long-metre verses differently. That is a difference of division and not of text. One reading is printed with the other reading in round brackets after it. At verse 29 the base text reads हरियूथपः, which does not construe in the sentence; both the Gītā Press edition, which is the authority here, and the Southern witness read च यथा तथा, and that reading is given in brackets. In the second half of the same verse the base text reads नाग्नेर्नापि विशस्तानां, "the rākṣasas slain by fire", where both the Gītā Press edition and the Southern witness read न हनूमद्विशस्तानां, "the rākṣasas slain by Hanumān". The two name the same agent. The fire that burns Laṅkā in this sarga is the fire Hanumān himself set, so one reading names the instrument and the other names the person who used it. The readings therefore do not disagree about what happened, and the other reading is given in brackets. The whole phrase is bracketed because the word division moves and a shorter bracket would leave half a word standing on its own.
