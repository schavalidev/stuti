Blurb: Hanumān repeats Sītā’s words to Rāma. The greater part of the sarga is the token she gave, which only Rāma could know. At Citrakūṭa she rose first while he slept. A crow came and pecked her again and again. Rāma then slept in her lap, and the crow returned and tore her between the breasts. Rāma woke, saw the blood, and asked who had done it. He saw the crow standing before her. That crow was the son of Indra. Rāma took a single blade of darbha grass from his bed, charged it with the weapon of Brahmā and cast it. The crow fled through all the worlds. His father abandoned him, and the gods and the sages would not receive him, and he came back to Rāma, who spared his life out of compassion and took only his right eye. Sītā’s question follows from that token. She asks why he who used that weapon for a crow does not use it now against the rākṣasas, and why those two brothers, whom the gods cannot assail, do not come for her. Hanumān repeats his own answer, that Rāma is overcome with grief and that Lakṣmaṇa burns with him. She then gave him the jewel from her garment, and told him to say that she will not live beyond a month.
