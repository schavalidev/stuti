Blurb: Hanumān has searched the whole household and not found her, and this sarga is what that does to him. He runs through the possibilities one by one, each worse than the last: that Rāvaṇa killed her for guarding her virtue, that she died of fear at the sight of his misshapen women, that he himself cannot now face Sugrīva, whose punishment is sharp. Then he thinks of the vānaras waiting, and of what he will say when they ask him what he did. The turn comes in a single line that the tradition has kept apart from the rest: freedom from despair is the root of prosperity, freedom from despair is the highest happiness, and it is what carries every purpose forward. On that he begins again, and searches the underground chambers and the shrine-houses and the small rooms within rooms, leaping up and down and throwing open doors, until there is not four fingers' width of that household he has not entered. He sees rākṣasa women, vidyādhara women, nāga maidens carried off by force. The poet repeats the same line four times with a different kind of woman each time, and each time ends: but not the daughter of Janaka.
