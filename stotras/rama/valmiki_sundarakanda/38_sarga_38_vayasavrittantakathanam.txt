Blurb: Hanumān asks for a token, and Sītā gives him a memory instead of an object — a thing no one else could know. At Citrakūṭa she had rested in Rāma's lap, and a crow came and pecked her until the blood ran. Rāma, waking, took a single blade of darbha grass from his seat and charged it with the brahmā weapon, and it followed the crow through all three worlds. Neither the gods nor the sages nor its own father Indra would shelter it, and at last it came back and fell before Rāma as a suppliant. Rāma could not make the weapon fruitless, so he took only its right eye and let it live. Then comes the point of the whole story, and it is a reproach: you spent the brahmā weapon on a crow, so how do you endure the one who carried me away? She asks after Rāma, speaks at length of Lakṣmaṇa, who serves his brother as a father and treats her as a mother, sets one month as the limit of her life, and at last takes the crest-jewel from her garment and gives it to him.
