Blurb: The night ends with the sound of brahma-rākṣasas chanting the Veda, and Rāvaṇa wakes to auspicious music with his garland and garment slipped awry. His first thought is Sītā, and the poet says plainly that he could not hide that desire within himself. He walks into the aśoka grove, and a hundred women follow him carrying golden lamps and fans and water-pitchers and a parasol bright as the full moon. They are described as the night has left them: necklaces awry, unguents rubbed off, hair loosened, faces damp, reeling with what is left of their drink, following him out of honour and out of love. He himself walks unsteadily, his mind fixed on Sītā, and the poet calls him dull. Hanumān, hidden in the leaves above, hears the girdles and anklets before he sees anything, and then watches this procession come to the door. He recognises Rāvaṇa, and though of fierce radiance himself, he is shaken by the other's, and draws further into the cover of the leaves.

en: Thus ends the eighteenth sarga, in the Sundarakāṇḍa of the holy Rāmāyaṇa of Vālmīki, the first poem, revealed by the sage.
tel: ఈ విధముగా ఋషిప్రోక్తమైన, ఆదికావ్యమైన శ్రీమద్వాల్మీకి రామాయణమునందలి సుందరకాండలో అష్టాదశ సర్గ ముగిసినది.
hi: इस प्रकार ऋषिप्रणीत आदिकाव्य श्रीमद्वाल्मीकीय रामायण के सुन्दरकाण्ड में अष्टादश सर्ग समाप्त हुआ।
