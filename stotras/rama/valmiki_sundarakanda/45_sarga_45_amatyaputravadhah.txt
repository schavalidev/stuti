Blurb: Seven sons of Rāvaṇa's chief minister go out with their own forces, and the sarga gives them their due as warriors before it kills them. They loose arrows at Hanumān from every side. He rises into the sky and, the poet says, plays with them there, shining among those archers as the lord of the winds shines among clouds that bear rainbows. Then he kills all seven with his hands and feet and chest and thighs, and the ground is left strewn with broken chariots, fallen horses and trumpeting elephants. One verse interrupts the march to record that the mothers of the dead guards were overcome with grief, and it is translated as it stands, without smoothing the break.
