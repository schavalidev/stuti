Blurb: This short sarga is built on one object. Hanumān passes through the halls of Rāvaṇa's dwelling, the armouries and the halls of conches and of bows, and the moon-chambers set on the mansions as on hills. Then he comes to the finest house of all, and within it to the aerial car named Puṣpaka. The poet describes it as a made world. Its earth is made full of mountain ranges, the mountains made full of trees, the trees full of blossom, the blossom full of filament and petal. Birds are wrought in it of vaiḍūrya and of silver and coral, serpents of many precious stones, horses of fair limb, elephants holding lotus petals in their trunks, and the goddess Lakṣmī herself standing among the lotuses with a lotus in her hand. Hanumān is filled with wonder. The last two verses turn it over. For all this seeing, he has still not found the daughter of Janaka, and the sarga closes on a mind grown very sorrowful.
