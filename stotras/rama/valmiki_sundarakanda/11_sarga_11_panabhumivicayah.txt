Blurb: The sarga opens by undoing the last one. Hanumān puts the thought away: a woman parted from Rāma would not sleep, nor eat, nor adorn herself, nor drink, and would not wait upon another man though he were lord of the gods. So this is not Sītā. He goes on to the drinking-floor, and the poet gives a long inventory of it — meats of deer and boar set out in portions, peacocks and partridges half eaten, wines of sugar and honey and flowers and fruit, vessels of gold and crystal and silver, some drained, some half full, some untouched, and jars overturned among scattered garlands. Then the women asleep among it, one wearing another's garment, their breath barely stirring the cloth on their bodies. Here the sarga turns inward. Hanumān asks himself whether looking at another man's sleeping wives has broken his dharma, and answers himself: the mind is what moves the senses, his own mind is steady and no disorder has arisen in it, and a woman cannot be searched for among does. He has looked where she had to be looked for. Sītā is still not there, and he leaves the drinking-floor to search elsewhere.

en: Thus ends the eleventh sarga, in the Sundarakāṇḍa of the holy Rāmāyaṇa of Vālmīki, the first poem, revealed by the sage.
tel: ఈ విధముగా ఋషిప్రోక్తమైన, ఆదికావ్యమైన శ్రీమద్వాల్మీకి రామాయణమునందలి సుందరకాండలో ఏకాదశ సర్గ ముగిసినది.
hi: इस प्रकार ऋषिप्रणीत आदिकाव्य श्रीमद्वाल्मीकीय रामायण के सुन्दरकाण्ड में एकादश सर्ग समाप्त हुआ।
