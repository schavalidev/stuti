Blurb: The Sundarakāṇḍa opens with a single leap. Hanumān stands on Mount Mahendra, resolved to find where Rāvaṇa has taken Sītā. He bows to the sun, to Indra, to the wind and to all beings, and he grows until the mountain is crushed beneath his feet. The sages and vidyādharas rise into the sky to watch. He tells the vānaras what he will do, and springs. Three things meet him over the sea. Maināka, the golden mountain, rises from the water to offer him rest, because the ocean wishes to repay the house of Ikṣvāku; Hanumān touches the peak with his hand and goes on, because he has given his word not to halt. Surasā, the mother of the serpents, opens her mouth across his path at the bidding of the gods, who wish to measure him; he grows to a hundred yojanas and then becomes the size of a thumb, passes through her mouth and out again, and so keeps her boon true without losing his way. Siṁhikā seizes his shadow and drags him down; he enters her mouth, tears her heart with his nails and rises. He comes down at last on the far shore, draws his great body back into its own shape, and looks upon Laṅkā, which stands on the mountain like the city of the gods.
