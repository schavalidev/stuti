Blurb: The sarga opens with a long comparison. The sky through which Hanumān returns is likened to a lake: the moon is its white swan, the stars its lotuses, the constellations Punarvasu and Mars its fish and its crocodile, Airāvata its island, and the clouds its waves. He swims across it, seeming to swallow the sky and to draw the clouds after him, appearing and disappearing among them, roaring as he comes. On the northern shore the vānaras who have waited for him hear the sound of his roar and the rush of his thighs. Jāmbavān understands at once what it means. He says that if the work had failed, the roar would not have that sound in it. The vānaras leap from treetop to treetop and wave branches like garments in their joy. Hanumān alights on the peak of Mahendra, and they surround him. He bows to Jāmbavān and to prince Aṅgada, and reports in three words that Sītā has been seen. Then he tells them where she is and in what state, worn by fasting, with matted hair, wearing a single braid, longing for the sight of Rāma. The vānaras shout, roar, raise their tails and leap down from the peaks to touch him. Aṅgada tells him that no one is his equal, and that he alone has given them back their lives.
