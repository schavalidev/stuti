Blurb: With Rāvaṇa gone, his guards begin. The sarga is a short sequence of set speeches, one rākṣasa woman after another, and each takes a different line. Ekajaṭā argues from lineage, tracing Rāvaṇa back through Viśravas to Pulastya, the fourth of the six Prajāpatis and a mind-born son of Brahmā, as though descent settled the matter. Harijaṭā, her eyes rolling like a cat's, argues from conquest: he defeated the three-and-thirty gods and their king. Praghasā argues from favour, saying he will leave his dearest wife and his whole household of a thousand women for her. Vikaṭā repeats the argument from conquest and calls her base for refusing. Durmukhī argues from fear, saying the sun does not burn and the wind does not blow for fear of him, and the clouds give water when he wishes. Then the persuasion drops its pretence. Take the word, she says, or you will be no more.
