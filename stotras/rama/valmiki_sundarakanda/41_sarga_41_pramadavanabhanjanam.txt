Blurb: Hanumān has leave to go, and does not go. He reasons that a messenger who only carries words has not taken the measure of the enemy, and that provoking Rāvaṇa will show what force Laṅkā can raise. So he wrecks the grove. The poet, who spent two whole sargas building that garden up, now takes it apart in the same detail: trees uprooted, creepers torn, pools broken open, terraces thrown down, hills of the pleasure-garden levelled, until the wood looks as though a forest fire had passed through it and the creepers hang like women in distress. The rākṣasa women sleeping there wake and see him standing in the ruin.
