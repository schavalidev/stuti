Blurb: This is a sarga of description, and nothing happens in it. Hanumān, bound and dragged forward, sees Rāvaṇa for the first time. The poet gives the crown of bright gold covered with a net of pearls, the ornaments set with diamonds, the costly silk, the red sandal paste, the wide and dreadful eyes, the sharp teeth, the ten heads like the peaks of Mount Mandara covered with serpents, the dark body like a heap of collyrium, and the stout arms like five-hooded serpents. He is seated upon a crystal throne, fanned by women, surrounded by four counsellors as the earth is surrounded by the four oceans. What makes the sarga memorable is Hanumān’s own thought at the end. He does not scorn what he sees. He says that the beauty, the firmness, the strength and the splendour are complete, and that if adharma were not strong in this one, he could be the protector of the world of the gods along with Indra himself. It is because of his cruel deeds alone that the worlds fear him.
