Blurb: The lowest point, and written throughout in a long metre. Sītā, terrified by Rāvaṇa's term, says that her heart must be firm indeed since it does not break into a thousand pieces like a mountain peak struck by lightning. She reproaches herself for sending Rāma after the golden deer and Lakṣmaṇa after him, and calls that deer Death itself. Then she says the hardest thing she says anywhere: that her vow of one husband, her patience, her bed on the ground and her restraint in dharma have all been fruitless, like kindness done to the ungrateful. She would end it with poison or a blade, but in a rākṣasa's house there is neither. So she takes the cord that binds her braid and reaches for a branch, intending to hang herself. The sarga stops there. In the last verse the omens begin — not omens born of grief, the poet says, but the good ones, marked before and found true.
