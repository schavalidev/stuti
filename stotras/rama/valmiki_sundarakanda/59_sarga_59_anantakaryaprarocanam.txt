Blurb: Hanumān speaks again to the vānaras. He tells them that Sītā is protected by her own austerity, and that Rāvaṇa who laid hands on her was not burnt only because she did not turn her anger upon him. He then urges that the remaining work is within their power. He says that he alone is enough to destroy Laṅkā with Rāvaṇa and his army, and that with them beside him it is a smaller thing still. He names Aṅgada the son of Vālin, Panasa, Nīla, and the two sons of the Aśvins, Mainda and Dvivida, who hold a boon of the Grandfather. He recalls that he burnt Laṅkā and proclaimed there that he was the servant of Rāma. He then describes Sītā as he found her: in the aśoka grove under a tree, surrounded by rākṣasīs, wasted by sorrow, in one soiled garment, her hair in a single braid, giving no thought to Rāvaṇa and resolved to die rather than yield.
