Blurb: Sītā's answer opens with the finest line of the exchange: what you have told me is nectar mingled with poison. She says two months of her term remain and the tenth is running. Hanumān then offers to end it at once — climb on my back and I will carry you across, I could carry Laṅkā itself with Rāvaṇa in it. She doubts that so small a creature could, and he takes it as a slight and grows to the size of a mountain to prove otherwise. Her refusal is then argued at length and entirely on practical grounds: his speed would make her faint and she would fall into the sea; the rākṣasas would pursue and he would have to fight while holding her; battles are uncertain and he might lose; if she were snatched back and hidden elsewhere the whole search would begin again; and above all Rāma's own fame would be lessened if another man recovered his wife. Only at the end does she give the reason that is not practical at all. She will not touch the body of any man but Rāma. That she came into Rāvaṇa's touch at all was by force, and helpless. If Rāma kills the ten-necked one and takes her from here himself, that would be worthy of him.
