Blurb: Akṣa has been killed, and Rāvaṇa turns to Indrajit, the son who defeated Indra. His speech is the speech of a father who is afraid. He recounts what has already been lost: the kiṅkaras, Jambumālī, the sons of the ministers, the five commanders, whole armies with their horses and elephants, and Akṣa his own dear son. He tells Indrajit to weigh his own strength against the enemy’s, and says plainly that sending him out is not the best of counsels. Indrajit goes, in a chariot yoked to four tigers, and the nāgas, yakṣas, sages and siddhas gather to watch. The two are evenly matched. The rākṣasa finds no weak point in Hanumān and Hanumān finds none in him, and the arrows fall in vain because the vānara moves in the spaces between them. Indrajit then uses the weapon of Brahmā. Hanumān falls bound, and at once understands what has happened. He is protected by the Grandfather, by Indra and by the wind, and there is no fear in him. He reasons that being seized is itself a gain, because it will bring him face to face with the lord of the rākṣasas. So he stops moving and lets it be. The rākṣasas then bind him with strips of bark and cords, and the moment they do so the weapon leaves him, for the weapon of Brahmā does not hold where a second bond is added. Indrajit sees this and knows the deed has been made useless, but Hanumān says nothing, and lets himself be beaten and dragged before the throne.
