Blurb: The persuasion turns to threat and then to appetite. The rākṣasa women tell Sītā that a human woman thinking much of a mere man is absurd, and that she should take the enjoyer of the wealth of the three worlds instead. She answers with the only argument she ever uses, and it is a list: as Śacī to Indra, Arundhatī to Vasiṣṭha, Rohiṇī to the moon, Lopāmudrā to Agastya, Sāvitrī to Satyavān, Damayantī to the king of Niṣadha — so am I to Rāma. Wretched or bereft of his kingdom, he who is my husband is my master. Then the threats begin in earnest. Vinatā argues that enough wifely love has been shown and everything done to excess turns to calamity. Vikaṭā promises to tear out her heart. Caṇḍodarī names the organs she craves. Praghasā proposes strangling her and telling the king she died of herself. Ajāmukhī wants the portions divided equally and drink brought. Śūrpaṇakhā agrees, and adds that they should dance before Nikumbhilā afterwards. Sītā's steadiness finally breaks, and she weeps.
