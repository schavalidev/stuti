Blurb: This is the last sarga of the Yuddhakāṇḍa, and it is the one people ask for by name. It opens with Bharata placing his folded palms on his head and giving the kingdom back. His argument is made entirely through images: a calf cannot carry the yoke a strong bull carries alone, an ass cannot follow the gait of a horse, a crow cannot follow a swan, and a tree grown too large inside a house flowers and withers without ever showing fruit. Rāma agrees. The barbers are called, the brothers and Sugrīva and Vibhīṣaṇa bathe, and Kausalyā herself adorns the wives of the vānaras. The chariot is yoked, and Rāma rides into Ayodhyā with Bharata holding the reins, Śatrughna the umbrella, and Lakṣmaṇa and Vibhīṣaṇa the fans. For the consecration the vānaras are sent to bring water, and they spring into the sky and return with the water of the four oceans and of five hundred rivers in golden jars. Vasiṣṭha seats Rāma with Sītā upon a jewelled seat, and eight named priests consecrate him while the gods stand in the sky. Then the gifts are given. Rāma gives Sītā a pearl necklace and tells her to give it to whomever she is pleased with, in whom valour and strength and understanding are found together. She looks at the vānaras, and looks at her husband, and gives it to Hanumān. After the vānaras take their leave comes the account of Rāma's reign, the passage known as Rāmarājya: no woman wept as a widow, the old did not perform the rites of the young, the rain came in season, the trees always bore flower and fruit, and the talk of the people was only "Rāma, Rāma, Rāma". The sarga closes with the phalaśruti, in which the poem speaks of itself and of what its reading brings.
