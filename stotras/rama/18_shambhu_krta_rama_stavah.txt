Title: Śrī Rāma Stavaḥ (Śambhu Kṛtam)
Devanāgarī: श्री राम स्तवः (शम्भु कृतम्)
Telugu: శ్రీ రామ స్తవః (శంభు కృతం)

Author: traditional attribution to Śambhu (Śiva); the closing verse calls the hymn īśvarokta, "spoken by the Lord", and ends "by my word may he be devoted to the service of your feet". No purāṇic locus is given in the source.

Source / recension: stotranidhi.com, fetched via curl with browser User-Agent — IAST text at https://stotranidhi.com/en/shambhu-krutha-sri-rama-stava-in-english/ and Devanāgarī text at https://stotranidhi.com/hi/shambhu-krutha-sri-rama-stava-in-sanskrit/. Both pages match verse-for-verse (10 verses plus a closing prose colophon); no variant readings found. Colophon: "iti śrīśaṁbhu kṛta śrīrāma stavaḥ."

Verse count: 10

Blurb: This hymn has nine verses of nothing but epithets, roughly a dozen to a verse. Each closes with the refrain: you I worship, Lord of the universe, wearing human form, joy of Raghu. A tenth verse names the form, calling the hymn śatanāmaka, meaning 'consisting of a hundred names'. What makes it strange is whose names they are. Verse 3 is Kṛṣṇa's life entire: Viṭhṭhala of Paṇḍharpur, dweller in Mathurā, killer of the washerman and of the elephant Kuvalayāpīḍa, killer of Baka and of Keśī the horse-demon, son of Nanda, son of Vasudeva, guardian of Vraja. The Kṛṣṇa material spills into verses 2, 4 and 6 as well, set beside Rāma's own deeds, namely Vālin, Mārīca the deer and Khara, without a seam. The address 'you who bear a human form' is doing the work. Śiva is not praising two avatāras but one person twice descended, and the hymn's refusal to distinguish them is its whole argument.

deva:
राघवं करुणाकरं भवनाशनं दुरितापहं

en: Rāghava, mine of compassion, destroyer of worldly existence, remover of evil; Mādhava, rider of the bird, of the form of water, supreme Lord; protector, ferryman of the people across, taker away of birth and death, slayer of foes — you I worship, Lord of the universe, wearing human form, delight of Raghu.

Recension note: The two stotranidhi editions (IAST and Devanāgarī) carry the identical 10-verse text with no variant readings. The hymn's self-description as śatanāmaka is borne out by the count: vv. 1–9 supply roughly a dozen epithets each, about a hundred in all, with three more repeated in the refrain. It is a stava in metrical verse, not a namāvalī — the names are governed by a single verb (bhaje) rather than set out as separate salutations. The Kṛṣṇa epithets of vv. 2–4 and 6 (Viṭhṭhala, Mathurāsthita, rajakāntaka, gajamāraka, bakamāraka, turagārdana, nandaja, vasudevaja, vrajapālaka, madhumāraka, gopriya, guruputrada) are given as printed, without emendation toward Rāma's cycle; the identification of Rāma with Kṛṣṇa is deliberate in this text and not a scribal confusion. In v. 8, kamalādṛśam is taken as "whom Kamalā (Lakṣmī) beholds"; it can also be read as "lotus-eyed".
