Source: *Śrāvaṇa Maṅgaḷagaurī Vratamu*, by **Challa Lakṣmī Nṛsiṁha Śāstri**, with the author's own Āndhra tātparya, printed at Machilipatnam **1958** (33 pp., 400 copies, four annas). No text layer of any kind — `pdftotext` returns only form feeds — so it is being read from page images rendered at 400 dpi and sliced.

**It is a different work from `26_sravana_mangalagauri_vrata_kalpam.txt` and `28_mangala_gauri_vrata_kalpam.txt`, not another copy of them.** Its sixteen services are carried by short anuṣṭubh verses, each followed by the author's Telugu tātparya, where those files carry the long Lalitā ṣoḍaśopacāra verses. Its main kathā is in Sanskrit ślokas with tātparya, spoken by Kṛṣṇa to Yudhiṣṭhira.

**Structure of the book, established from the running heads and sampled pages:**
- printed pp. 1–3 — the rite: saṅkalpa, dhyāna, the sixteen services, the aṅgapūjā, the aṣṭottara pointer, dhūpa to phalasamarpaṇa.
- printed p. 4 — the vāyana-dāna in three parts (to the brāhmaṇa, to the sumaṅgalīs, to the mother), the sixteen lamps, the collyrium, the vermilion, the feeding of sixteen sumaṅgalīs, then `కథా ప్రారంభము`.
- printed pp. 4–26 — the kathā. Kuṇḍina; **Dharmapāla**, a rich vaiśya, childless; an ascetic smeared with ash and wearing rudrākṣa comes daily for alms and will not take them; he tells the wife she is under a curse from a former birth; she is sent in black garments up a black hill into the forest to dig, and finds a temple of the goddess.
- printed pp. 29–31 — **శ్రావణ మంగళ గౌరీ వ్రత పాట**, the women's song, which the title page promises. It sings the serpent episode: the couple asleep, the black serpent, Maṅgaḷagaurī appearing in the wife's dream, the pearls.
- printed p. 32 — **శ్రావణ శుక్రవారపు పాట**, the Śrāvaṇa Friday song, on Cārumatī and Varalakṣmī. A different observance; relates to `25_varalakshmi_vrata_kalpam.txt`.

**Finding that corrects an earlier claim.** `26_sravana_mangalagauri_vrata_kalpam.txt` said there was no overlap at all between this book's narrative and the stotranidhi one. That was wrong and has been corrected in that file. There are two overlaps: the opening (Dharmapāla, rich and childless, and the ascetic who refuses the alms) and the whole serpent episode, which this book carries as its **song** rather than as its kathā.
