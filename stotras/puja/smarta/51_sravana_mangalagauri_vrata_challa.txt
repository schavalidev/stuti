Title: Śrāvaṇa Maṅgaḷagaurī Vrata Kalpamu — the 1958 Machilipatnam recension

Devanāgarī: श्रावण मंगळगौरी व्रत कल्पमु

Telugu: శ్రావణ మంగళగౌరీ వ్రత కల్పము

Author: The rite is anonymous and received. The Telugu explanation printed after the second verse is by Challa Lakṣmī Nṛsiṁha Śāstri, whose edition this is.

Type: vidhi (ritual manual) — not a stotra. Uses the one-extra-field vidhi format documented in `../../vidhi/madhyandina/01_sandhyopasana_vidhi.txt`: a `vidhi:` field carrying the ritual instruction, then deva / iast / en / tel / hi.

Recension / paddhati: **Smārta, South Indian, Telugu.** The vrata is kept by a married woman on the Tuesdays of Śrāvaṇa for five years from the year of her marriage, the first year in the house of her birth and then in her husband's house, and closed with the udyāpana.

**This is a different work from `26_sravana_mangalagauri_vrata_kalpam.txt` and `../vrata/03_mangala_gauri_vrata_kalpam.txt`, and neither supersedes the other.** Those two files are built on the stotranidhi page and carry the long Lalitā ṣoḍaśopacāra verses; this print carries a short anuṣṭubh verse of its own at every service, a different meditation in five verses, and the services of a married woman's rite that the other files do not have: the bodice, the things of good fortune, the golden upper cloth, the gift of money, the three vāyanas, the sixteen lamps and the feeding of sixteen married women. They agree on the frame: the five-year term, the nineteen names of the worship of the limbs, and the pointer at the aṣṭottara instead of printing it. **The three must never be merged, harmonised or collated against each other.**

**What this file holds, and what it does not yet.** This file carries the rite, printed pages 1 to 4 of the book, complete. The book then carries the story (printed pages 4 to 26, Sanskrit verses spoken by Kṛṣṇa to Yudhiṣṭhira, each followed by the author's Telugu explanation), the women's song of the vrata (pages 29 to 31) and the Śrāvaṇa Friday song (page 32). **Those are still to be transcribed and will be added to this file**, after the rite, as the next units. Until then the rite stands on its own, and the pointer in unit 34 to the hearing of the story has nothing in this file to point at. The Maṅgaḷagaurī aṣṭottara, which the print points at in unit 18 without printing, is not in this corpus either.

Source / recension: **Challa Lakṣmī Nṛsiṁha Śāstri, *Śrāvaṇa Maṅgaḷagaurī Vratamu*, with the author's Telugu tātparya, Machilipatnam, 1958, 33 pages**, held as the user's PDF of page images at 600 dpi with no text layer of any kind. **Every line was read from the page images and typed by hand** in Telugu script as the print has it, then converted mechanically to Devanāgarī with `../../bin/tel2dev.py`, and the `iast:` field from that with `../../bin/dev2iast.py`; no Sanskrit was composed. The print writes the homorganic nasal as the sunna throughout (మంగళ), and the Devanāgarī keeps it as the anusvāra (मंगळ), as `26` does from the same kind of source. The print's `ళ` is kept as `ळ`.

**The print is worn, and the reading is uncertain in twenty-four places across fifteen units, each marked ⟨?⟩ in the `deva:` and `iast:` fields.** The worst is the second verse, on where the vrata is kept each year, which carries six marks; the author's own explanation under it is clear and the translation follows the explanation. The others are single words: one in the resolution, two in the meditation, one each at the unbroken rice, one name of the worship of the limbs, the food-offering, the waving of the light, the circumambulation and the prayer, two each at the gift of money, the handful of flowers and the salutation, two for the bamboo tray in the vāyanas, and one in the closing instructions. A line between the prayer and the vāyana is worn beyond reading and is left out, and the `vidhi:` of unit 30 says so. **A second reading at higher magnification, or a second copy of the print, is wanted for these.** Nothing was reconstructed: where a word could not be read, the mark stands, and the translation gives the sense the readable words allow.

Independent collation: **none; this file rests on one printed edition.** No other copy of this work was found. The frame of the rite agrees with the stotranidhi page that `26` is built on (the five-year term, the nineteen names of the limbs in the same order, the pointer at the aṣṭottara, the vāyana), as `26` records; the service verses were searched for in that page and in this folder and stand nowhere else. The meditation's seed-syllables `ऐं ह्रीं श्रीं` and the formula `नमस्सर्वमंगळायै मंगळगौर्यै नमः` are the rite's own.

Defects in the base text, corrected with reasons: none. The print is followed as it stands, apart from the uncertain readings marked above.

Observed and deliberately **not** emended:
- **The verse at the water for the feet is headed `अर्घ्यम्`** (unit 6), and the print has no separate pādya. The verse itself says `पाद्यं`; the one offering stands for both, as printed.
- **`पुत्रपौत्राभिवृद्धये`** (unit 4) is printed `पुत्रि पौत्राभि`, a slip of the type for `पुत्र`; read as `पुत्र` and marked nowhere else, since the sense is not in doubt.
- **The dots** in the resolution (`आचम्य ... संकल्प्य ...`) and after each offering (`धूपम् ... मि`) are the print's own, standing for the full ācamana and saṅkalpa and for `समर्पयामि`; they are kept.
- **`शिवसुंदरी`, `सुरश्रेष्ठे`, `परमेश्वरि`** and the other vocatives are printed with long and short endings as they come, and are kept.

Accent: **none. This rite contains no Vedic mantra.** Every verse is paurāṇika, and the print carries no svara mark; 0 accent characters were counted in the units. This file can be used as it stands by any reciter, and no Paurāṇika twin is needed.

Blurb: This is the worship of Maṅgaḷagaurī kept on the Tuesdays of Śrāvaṇa, as printed in Machilipatnam in 1958. A married woman keeps it for five years from the year of her marriage, the first year in the house where she was born and afterwards in her husband's house, and closes it with the udyāpana. Every service has a short verse of its own. After the sixteen services come the worship of the limbs under nineteen names, the hundred and eight names, incense, the lamp, the food-offering, betel, the gift of money, the waving of the light, flowers, circumambulation, salutation and prayer. Then the vāyana is given three times, to the brāhmaṇa, to the married women and to the mother, sixteen lamps are lit, the story is heard, and sixteen married women and the mother are fed. The story and the two songs of this edition are not yet in this file.
