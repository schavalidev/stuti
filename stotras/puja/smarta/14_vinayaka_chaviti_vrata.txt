Devanāgarī: श्री वरसिद्धि विनायक व्रतम् — विनायक चविती पूजा विधानम् (स्मार्त पद्धतिः)
Telugu: శ్రీ వరసిద్ధి వినాయక వ్రతం — వినాయక చవితి పూజా విధానం (స్మార్త పద్ధతిః)

Author: Anonymous (received smārta vrata). No compiler is named and none should be inferred.

Type: vidhi (ritual manual) — not a stotra. Uses the one-extra-field vidhi format documented in `../../vidhi/madhyandina/01_sandhyopasana_vidhi.txt`: a `vidhi:` field carrying the ritual instruction, then deva / iast / en / tel / hi.

Recension / paddhati: **Smārta, South Indian (Telugu country).** Pūjā-vidhāna divides by *paddhati* the way sandhyāvandana divides by śākhā. The Śrīvaiṣṇava form of the opening is kept separately at `../vaishnava/01_purvanga_vidhanam.txt` and **the two must never be merged, harmonised or collated against each other.**

**This is a vrata, not a nitya pūjā, and it does not share the folder's pūrvāṅga.** Its ten siblings (`04_ganapati_nitya_puja.txt` through `13_navagraha_nitya_puja.txt`) all open with the same template units 1–29. This text does not: it has an opening of its own, a different twenty-four-name ācamana carrying explicit body-touch directions, its own saṅkalpa naming the day, a kalaśa pūjā that quotes **Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 6.29.1** by chapter and verse, and a **prāṇapratiṣṭhā** — the installation of breath in the image — which no nitya pūjā in this folder has. It is tied to one day of the year, the fourth of the bright fortnight of Bhādrapada.

**What is distinctive, and what it costs the reader to know:**

1. **The Puruṣasūkta is present only as bracketed incipits.** At seven services the source prints not the verse but its opening and closing words in parentheses — `(सहस्रशीर्षा ... दशाङ्गुलम्)`, `(पुरुष एवेदगं ... अतिरोहति)`, `(एतावानस्य ... दिवि)`, `(त्रिपादूर्ध्वः ... अभि)`, `(तस्माद्विराडजायत ... पुरः)`, `(यत्पुरुषेण ... हविः)`, `(सप्तास्यासन् ... पशुम्)`. **These are transcribed here exactly as abbreviated and have NOT been expanded**, even though full accented texts of those verses stand elsewhere in this corpus (`11_surya_nitya_puja.txt` units 32–33 and 50–51 carry four of them). Expanding them would be supplying text the source does not print, and the abbreviations are themselves information: they tell you this page expects the reciter to know the sūkta already.
2. **Three counted offerings of twenty-one**: twenty-one leaves (unit 37), twenty-one flowers (unit 38), and twenty-one pairs of dūrvā grass (unit 39), each with its own name for each offering. The number twenty-one governs this vrata as twelve governs the Sūrya rite.
3. **An eighteen-name aṅga-pūjā** (unit 36) and **a hundred and eight names printed in full** (unit 40) — the only page in this folder to print its aṣṭottara inline rather than pointing at it.
4. **A fourfold repeated guest-offering** (units 51–52), each verse answered by `इदमर्घ्यं इदमर्घ्यं इदमर्घ्यम्` three times over.
5. **The kathā, and the fact that no Sanskrit of it exists on the page.** See the note on languages below.
6. **A maṅgaḷācaraṇam in Telugu, not Sanskrit** (units 59–60). See the note below.

**Note on the languages of this page — important.** Three different languages appear, and the file marks which is which:

- The rite itself is **Sanskrit**, and is transcribed as such.
- The **Vighneśvara kathā and the Śyamantaka legend are printed by this source in English prose**, not in Sanskrit (units 57–58). Nothing has been back-translated into Sanskrit to fill the gap; the `deva:` of those units carries only the Sanskrit that is actually printed — the Śyamantaka verse `सिंहः प्रसेनमवधीत्` and its two following lines — and the narrative is given in the `en:` field, where the source put it. The second witness prints the same kathā in **Telugu** prose, also not Sanskrit. **No Sanskrit text of this kathā was available from either witness, and none has been invented.**
- The closing **maṅgaḷācaraṇam is Telugu-language verse printed in Devanāgarī script** (units 59–60) — utpalamāla and kanda metres, not Sanskrit ślokas. It uses the Dravidian short vowels `ॆ` (U+0946) and `ॊ` (U+094A), which Sanskrit does not have. **Those characters are correct here and must not be "corrected" away**: a corpus-wide scan that flags `ॆ`/`ॊ` as defects will flag these units, and it should not. The `tel:` field for those units gives the verse in Telugu script, which is its proper home.

**Accent.** The page carries 96 svara marks, and they sit in only five places: the śarīraśuddhi verse (unit 8), the prāṇāyāma and Gāyatrī and `आपो ज्योती` (units 11–13), **Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 6.29.1** at the kalaśa (unit 16, the longest accented passage on the page, and cited by the source itself as `तै. अर. 6.29.1`), `गणानां त्वा` (unit 18), and `यज्ञेन यज्ञमयजन्त देवाः` at the dismissal (unit 54). **Everything else — the seven Puruṣasūkta incipits included — is printed unaccented and has been left so.** Per `CLAUDE.md` accent is transcribed or absent, never inferred, and never imported from another page to fill a gap.

Udātta `॑` (U+0951), anudātta `॒` (U+0952), dīrgha svarita `᳚` (U+1CDA); the praṇava before a spirant takes the guttural nasal, written `ओग्ं` (`ogṁ`).

Source / recension: base text `https://vignanam.org/devanagari/ganesha-chaturthi-puja-vidhanam.html` (fetched 2026-09-12; 528 lines), a **native Devanāgarī edition**, so **nothing here was transliterated by us.**

**A caution about this particular page.** It is markedly more corrupt than the ten nitya pūjā pages from the same site, and the corruptions have a single cause: it has evidently been converted from Telugu script. The tell-tales are a **Latin `ā` inside a Devanāgarī word** (`झ्āम्बवता`), the Dravidian short vowels appearing inside Sanskrit words (`वामहस्तॆ`), `णमह` written twice where `नमः` belongs, and `यिदमर्घ्यं` with a Telugu glide. Readings from this page were therefore checked one by one rather than trusted.

Independent collation, and it is good here: against **stotranidhi's `श्री सिद्धि विनायक व्रतकल्पम्`, parts 1–3** (`https://stotranidhi.com/sri-vinayaka-vrata-kalpam-one/` and its continuations, fetched 2026-09-12), and against **Gītā Press, Gorakhpur, *Nitya Karma Pūjā Prakāśa***.

**Gītā Press has no Bhādrapada Caturthī vrata at all.** Searched and confirmed: no `वरसिद्धि`, no Śyamantaka legend, and `चतुर्थी` occurs only in a list of tithis and in a prohibition on plucking bilva leaves. Its `देहलीविनायक-पूजन` (p. 279) is a *different* rite — the threshold-Vināyaka worshipped as a limb of the Dīpāvalī Mahālakṣmī pūjā. So for the vrata itself this file rests on **two witnesses**; Gītā Press contributes only where it carries the same two stotras, and it is not made to speak beyond that.

Differences recorded and **not reconciled**:

- **The two aṅga-pūjās are not variants of each other; they are different lists.** This text gives eighteen names running `गणेशाय - पादौ`, `एकदन्ताय - गुल्फौ`, `शूर्पकर्णाय - जानुनी`, and so on. stotranidhi gives **twenty-nine**, built on alliteration between the name and the limb — `पार्वतीनन्दनाय - पादौ`, `गणेशाय - गुल्फौ`, `जगद्धात्रे - जङ्घे`, `जगद्वल्लभाय - जानुनी`, `उमापुत्राय - ऊरू`, `विकटाय - कटिं` — and it worships limbs this text omits altogether (the sides, the arms, the teeth, the chin, the lips, the cheeks, the hair). Neither has been merged into the other.
- **The twenty-one flowers and the twenty-one dūrvā offerings agree exactly** in both witnesses — same names, same order, same substances. That is a genuine agreement across two lineages and worth stating.
- The twenty-one **leaves** agree in substance but not in the names attached to them: this text opens `सुमुखाय - माचीपत्रं`, stotranidhi `उमापुत्राय - माचीपत्रं`. At the thirteenth this text has `देवदारुपत्रं` where stotranidhi has `आमलकीपत्रं`; at the seventeenth this text `गण्डकीपत्रं`, stotranidhi `गण्डवीपत्रं`; at the fourth this text offers a pair of dūrvā where stotranidhi offers a dūrvā leaf.
- Deity name: this text says `श्री वरसिद्धि विनायक स्वामिने`, stotranidhi `श्री सिद्धिविनायक स्वामिने`.
- stotranidhi has **no prāṇapratiṣṭhā** and **no Puruṣasūkta incipits**; this text has both.
- The **sixteen-name Vighneśvara stotra** (units 5–6) is a sixteen-name recension: `षोडशैतानि नामानि`, with `विघ्नराजो गणाधिपः`, `धूमकेतुर्गणाध्यक्षो फालचन्द्रो`, and `सङ्ग्रामे सर्व कार्येषु`. **Gītā Press prints a twelve-name recension** of the same verses — `द्वादशैतानि नामानि`, `विघ्ननाशो विनायकः`, `धूम्रकेतुर्गणाध्यक्षो भालचन्द्रो`, `सङ्ग्रामे संकटे चैव` — and also reads `गजकर्णकः` where this page has `गजकर्णिकः`. Two recensions of one stotra, recorded and kept apart.
- **Saṅkaṭanāśana Gaṇeśa Stotra** (units 55–56): Gītā Press reads `सप्तमं विघ्नराजेन्द्रं` where this text has `सप्तमं विघ्नराजं च`. Otherwise the two agree through all eight verses, including the colophon attributing it to the Nārada Purāṇa — a real collation of this stotra against the authority.
- The jewel's name: this text `श्यमन्तकः`, stotranidhi `शमन्तकः`. Both forms circulate; neither is corrected to the other.
- The closing declaration reads `श्री सिद्धि विनायकः स्वामि सुप्रीतो सुप्रसन्नो वरदो भवन्तु`, whose singular adjectives disagree with the plural verb. **Both witnesses print it so**, so it is the recension's own and is kept, not tidied.
- `यिदमर्घ्यं` for `इदमर्घ्यं`: this text prints the correct form three times and the glide form once; **stotranidhi prints the glide form all four times.** Since both witnesses carry it, it is recorded as the recension's orthography, and the Sanskrit form stands in the text (see corrections below).

Defects corrected, each with its reason and its support:

1. **`उपमश्रवस्तवम्` → `उपमश्रवस्तमम्`** (unit 18). Ṛgveda 2.23.1 reads `upamaśravastamam`, a superlative in *-tama-*; `-stavam` is not construable. The same slip stands on every deity page of this site and is corrected in each.
2. **`णमह` → `नमः`**, twice (unit 36), and **`श्कन्धौ` → `स्कन्धौ`**, **`हस्थौ` → `हस्तौ`** (unit 36). Conversion damage. **stotranidhi independently prints `नमः`, `स्कन्धौ` and `हस्तौ`** at the corresponding places, so these are the other witness's readings, not conjecture.
3. **`सर्वाण्य्ङ्गाणि` → `सर्वाण्यङ्गानि`** (unit 36). **stotranidhi prints `सर्वाण्यङ्गानि`.**
4. **`झ्āम्बवता` → `जाम्बवता`** (unit 58). The printed form contains a **Latin letter `ā`** inside a Devanāgarī word — plainly conversion damage. **stotranidhi's Telugu kathā prints `జాంబవతా`**, confirming the name.
5. **`यिदमर्घ्यं` → `इदमर्घ्यं`** (unit 52). The Sanskrit pronoun is `इदम्`; the initial `यि` is a Telugu glide. This page itself prints the correct `इदमर्घ्यं` three times and the glide once, so the correction is internal to the witness.
6. **`हृदयस्थोजनार्थनः` → `हृदयस्थो जनार्दनः`** (unit 3). The verse names Janārdana, `जनार्दन`; `जनार्थन` is not a word, and the two words were run together. No second witness carries this verse, and the correction rests on the standard form of the name and on sense.
7. **`सर्वविघ्नच्चिदेतस्मै` → `सर्वविघ्नच्छिदे तस्मै`** (unit 6). The compound is `सर्व-विघ्न-छिद्`, 'cutter of every obstacle', whose dative is `छिदे`; the following `तस्मै` was run into it and the aspirate lost. No second witness; corrected on grammar.
8. **`वशिष्ठाभ्यां` → `वसिष्ठाभ्यां`** and **`सितारामाभ्यां` → `सीतारामाभ्यां`** (unit 7). The sage is Vasiṣṭha and the name is Sītā. The same site's own ten nitya pūjā pages print both correctly, which is an internal witness within one lineage.
9. **`वामहस्तॆ` → `वामहस्ते`** (unit 9). The Dravidian short `ॆ` is not a Sanskrit vowel; the locative is `हस्ते`.

**Observed and deliberately NOT emended:** the hundred and eight names at unit 40 are printed with several visible corruptions — `नर्वसिद्दि-प्रदाय` (a garbled repetition of `सर्वसिद्धिप्रदाय`, which already stands at position 48), `बल्वान्विताय` for `बलान्विताय`, `बलोद्दताय` for `बलोद्धताय`, `विष्वग्दृशेनमः` run together, and `सख्यै नमः` with a feminine dative in a list of masculine names. **stotranidhi does not print its aṣṭottara on this page** — it refers the reader to a separate nāmāvalī — so there is no second witness for this list, and per `CLAUDE.md` these stand as printed with the defects recorded here rather than mended by conjecture.

Not collated: the paurāṇika verses of the opening, and this text's own upacāra verses, for which no critical edition exists.

Unit count: 60 units. Not a verse count in the stotra sense — the work is a prose order of service punctuated by mantras and stotras, and the numbering is this corpus's, not the source's.

Blurb: This is the Varasiddhi Vināyaka vrata, the worship kept on Vināyaka Cavitī — the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Bhādrapada — complete in sixty units. Unlike the daily pūjās in this folder it has an opening of its own, and it does something they never do: the breath is installed in the image, so that the god is present in it for as long as the worship lasts. Then come the sixteen services, and with them the three great counted offerings that give this vrata its shape — twenty-one leaves, twenty-one flowers, and twenty-one pairs of dūrvā grass, each offered under a name of its own. The hundred and eight names are printed here in full. The guest-offering is made four times over at the end, and the rite closes with the dismissal, the stotra that destroys distress, the story of how Gaṇeśa came to be lord of obstacles and why the moon must not be looked at on this day, and a set of auspicious verses in Telugu. At seven places the great hymn to the Person is not written out but named by its opening words, because the reciter is expected to know it.

--- unit 1 | section: प्रार्थन ---
vidhi:
The opening verse, to Viṣṇu, for the quieting of all obstacles; then the hour is declared a good hour.
deva:
शुक्लाम्बरधरं विष्णुं शशिवर्णं चतुर्भुजम् ।
प्रसन्न वदनं ध्यायेत्सर्व विघ्नोपशान्तये ॥
अयं मुहूर्तस्सुमुहूर्तोऽस्तु ।
iast:
śuklāmbaradharaṁ viṣṇuṁ śaśivarṇaṁ caturbhujam |
prasanna vadanaṁ dhyāyetsarva vighnopaśāntaye ||
ayaṁ muhūrtassumuhūrto'stu |
en: For the quieting of every obstacle one should meditate on Viṣṇu — robed in white, moon-coloured, four-armed, his face serene. May this hour be a good hour.
tel: సమస్త విఘ్నాలూ శాంతించడానికి — తెల్లని వస్త్రం ధరించినవాడు, చంద్రుని వన్నెవాడు, నాలుగు చేతులవాడు, ప్రసన్నమైన ముఖం కలవాడు అయిన విష్ణువును ధ్యానించాలి. ఈ ముహూర్తం సుముహూర్తమగుగాక.
hi: समस्त विघ्नों की शान्ति के लिये श्वेत वस्त्र धारण किये हुए, चन्द्रमा के समान वर्णवाले, चतुर्भुज, प्रसन्नमुख विष्णु का ध्यान करना चाहिये। यह मुहूर्त सुमुहूर्त हो।

--- unit 2 | section: प्रार्थन ---
vidhi:
That the hour itself is made auspicious by remembering the Lord. This page prints the verse as running prose rather than in four quarters, and closes it with a second `सुमुहूर्तोऽस्तु`.
deva:
तदेव लग्नं सुदिनं तदेव, ताराबलं चन्द्रबलं तदेव, विद्याबलं दैवबलं तदेव, लक्ष्मीपते तेङ्घ्रियुगं स्मरामि, सुमुहूर्तोऽस्तु ।
iast:
tadeva lagnaṁ sudinaṁ tadeva, tārābalaṁ candrabalaṁ tadeva, vidyābalaṁ daivabalaṁ tadeva, lakṣmīpate teṅghriyugaṁ smarāmi, sumuhūrto'stu |
en: That alone is the right moment, that alone the good day, that alone the strength of the stars and of the moon, that alone the strength of learning and of the gods — O consort of Lakṣmī, when I call your two feet to mind. May it be a good hour.
tel: ఓ లక్ష్మీపతీ! నీ పాదద్వయాన్ని స్మరించినప్పుడే అదే శుభలగ్నం, అదే సుదినం, అదే తారాబలం, చంద్రబలం, విద్యాబలం, దైవబలం. సుముహూర్తమగుగాక.
hi: हे लक्ष्मीपते! जब मैं आपके चरणयुगल का स्मरण करता हूँ, वही लग्न है, वही सुदिन, वही ताराबल और चन्द्रबल, वही विद्याबल और दैवबल। सुमुहूर्त हो।

--- unit 3 | section: प्रार्थन ---
vidhi:
Two verses: the remembering of Śiva and the Goddess together, and the promise that those who hold Janārdana in the heart cannot be defeated. The source prints `हृदयस्थोजनार्थनः`; the name is Janārdana and the words were run together, corrected here — see the header.
deva:
यश्शिवो नामरूपाभ्यां या देवी सर्वमङ्गला ।
तयोस्संस्मरणात् पुंसां सर्वतो जयमङ्गलम् ॥
लाभस्तेषां जयस्तेषां कुतस्तेषां पराभवः ।
येषामिन्दीवरश्यामो हृदयस्थो जनार्दनः ॥
iast:
yaśśivo nāmarūpābhyāṁ yā devī sarvamaṅgalā |
tayossaṁsmaraṇāt puṁsāṁ sarvato jayamaṅgalam ||
lābhasteṣāṁ jayasteṣāṁ kutasteṣāṁ parābhavaḥ |
yeṣāmindīvaraśyāmo hṛdayastho janārdanaḥ ||

The source glosses many of the plants with their **Telugu names in parentheses** — `मारेडु` for bilva, `गरिक` for dūrvā, `उम्मॆत्त` for datura, `रेगु` for jujube, `उत्तरेणि` for apāmārga, `आम्र` for mango, `गन्नेरु` for oleander, `दानिम्म` for pomegranate, `जम्मि` for śamī, `रावि` for the aśvattha, `मद्दि` for arjuna, `जिल्लेडु` for arka — and those glosses are kept, since they are how the worshipper actually finds the leaves. Note that `उम्मॆत्त` and `जिल्लेडु` are **Telugu words carrying the Dravidian short vowels `ॆ`/`ॊ`**, correct as printed and not to be normalised. Word-spacing has been regularised where the source runs `नमः` onto the preceding name. vidhi:
**The twenty-one flowers.** Here each name is a form of Gaṇapati — the five-faced, the great, the steadfast, Viṣvaksena, the fragrant, and so on — and to each its proper flower. **This list agrees exactly with stotranidhi's, name for name and flower for flower, in the same order**: a genuine agreement across two editorial lineages, and worth recording as such. --- unit 47 | section: षोडशोपचार पूजा ---
vidhi:
Circumambulation — three verses proper to this vrata, then the two of confession and refuge that the whole folder shares, closing with the vocative `गणाधिप`. The second quarter reads `मद्विघ्नं हरये शीघ्रं`; **both witnesses print `हरये`**, so it stands as transmitted rather than being mended to an imperative.
deva:

पापोऽहं पापकर्माऽहं पापात्मा पापसम्भवः ।
त्राहि मां कृपया देव शरणागतवत्सल ॥
अन्यथा शरणं नास्ति त्वमेव शरणं मम ।

vidhi:
**The story — and a plain statement of what is and is not here.** The source prints only the Devanāgarī heading; **the narrative itself it gives in English prose**, and the second witness gives it in **Telugu** prose. Neither prints it in Sanskrit. The `deva:` field therefore carries the heading alone, which is all the Devanāgarī there is, and the story is summarised in the translation fields where the sources put it. **Nothing has been back-translated into Sanskrit**; to compose Sanskrit for it would be to invent a text. The two witnesses also tell the story differently: this source begins with Sūta in the Naimiṣa forest and Kṛṣṇa's counsel to Yudhiṣṭhira, while stotranidhi's Telugu telling begins further back, with Gajāsura and the birth of Vināyaka. Both are recorded; neither is merged into the other.
deva:
विघ्नेश्वर कथा ॥
iast:
vighneśvara kathā ||
en: The story of Vighneśvara. The sages assembled in the Naimiṣa forest asked Sūta whether there is a vow by which work may be completed without obstacle and bear the fruit desired. He told them that during the war for Hastināpura, Yudhiṣṭhira asked Kṛṣṇa the same question, and Kṛṣṇa answered that on the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Bhādrapada one should keep the Varasiddhi Vināyaka vrata: bathing, putting on clean clothes, making an image of gold, silver, copper or clay — one-tusked, winnow-eared, four-armed, bearing noose and goad and a sweet dumpling and the gesture of fearlessness — and worshipping it with sandal in the name of Gaṇādhipa, flowers as Vināyaka, incense as Umāsuta, ghee-lamps as Rudrapriya, twenty-one modakas as Vighnanāśaka, and twenty-one blades of dūrvā with twenty-one names, ten of the modakas being given away to those in need. Śiva at the burning of the three cities, Rāma in the search for Sītā, Bhagīratha in bringing down the Gaṅgā, the gods and demons at the churning of the ocean, and Sāmba in his leprosy had all kept this vow. पुष्पजातुलु तॆच्चि पूजिन्तु नेनॆपुडु बहुबुद्धि गणपतिकि बागुगानु ॥ जय ॥

en: I have thought of the lord of the hosts; I have thought of the lord of obstacles, as of a task set. I have thought of Heramba, that the obstacles before me may be taken away. Flattened rice, pieces of coconut, a little jaggery, soaked lentils and the juice of sugarcane — a fine feast I make for the elder son of the forehead-eyed god, and I pray in my heart. O big-bellied Gaṇapati, I am your servant — send an army against the dumplings! Eating fragrant ghee and sweet thick lentils till your belly bursts, and rolling about. Victory and blessing, everlasting and auspicious blessing. In a silver dish, thousands upon thousands of pearls, and sapphires heaped like hills poured together; wearing garlands in plenty about your neck — abundantly I offer you the white waving of lights. To him whose form is worshipped, to him who is consciousness and bliss, to him of the shining hymns, to the eternal; to him whose eyes are the moon and the sun, to him of beautiful form, to him who takes what shape he will, to the lord of the hosts. The single tusk, the whole elephant face, the fine trunk, the beloved belly; and mounting the mouse briskly in turn — to the glorious lord of the hosts, even now. Water-lily, chrysanthemum, oleander in full bloom, lotuses and tangeḍu in plenty — bringing flowers of every kind I worship always, and worship well, the Gaṇapati of abundant wisdom.

hi: मैंने गणनाथ का स्मरण किया; मैंने विघ्नपति का स्मरण किया, मानो वही कार्य हो। मैंने हेरम्ब का स्मरण किया, कि मेरे विघ्न दूर हों। चिउड़ा, नारियल की गिरी, थोड़ा गुड़, भिगोयी दाल और ईख का रस — ललाटनेत्र के ज्येष्ठ पुत्र के लिये सुन्दर भोज बनाता हूँ और मन में प्रार्थना करता हूँ। हे बड़े पेटवाले गणपय्या, मैं आपका सेवक हूँ — मोदकों पर सेना भेजिये! सुगन्धित घी और गाढ़ी मीठी दाल पेट फूटने तक खाते और लोटते हुए। जय मङ्गल, नित्य शुभ मङ्गल। चाँदी के थाल में हजारों-हजार मोती, और पर्वत-सा नीलम एक साथ उँड़ेलकर; गले भर हार पहनकर — भरपूर आपको श्वेत आरती अर्पित करता हूँ। श्रीमूर्ति से वन्द्य को, चिन्मयानन्द को, भासुर स्तोत्रवाले को, शाश्वत को; चन्द्र-सूर्य नेत्रवाले को, सुन्दराकार को, कामरूप को, श्रीगणनाथ को। एक दन्त और सम्पूर्ण गजवदन, सुन्दर सूँड़, प्रिय उदर; और मूषक पर क्रम से चढ़ते हुए — भव्य देवगणपति को अब। चेंगल्व, चामन्ती, खिले हुए कनेर, कमल और तंगेडु प्रचुर मात्रा में — सब प्रकार के पुष्प लाकर मैं सदा बहुबुद्धि गणपति की भली-भाँति पूजा करता हूँ।
