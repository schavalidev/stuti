**One part of the rite has older and wider witnesses, and they are these.**

**The closing verse of unit 21 — three witnesses, and all three print it differently.** `गुह्यातिगुह्यगोप्ता त्वं गृहाण कृततर्पणम् । सिद्धिर्भवतु मे देव त्वत्प्रसादान्मयि स्थिरा` is the standard verse with which an offering is given over, and it is much older and wider than this rite.

- **Gītā Press, Gorakhpur, *Nitya Karma Pūjā Prakāśa*** (archive.org `nitya-karma-puja-prakash-gita-press-gorakhpur`, `_djvu.txt` fetched 2026-09-12; 370,963 Devanāgarī characters, zero Latin OCR noise) prints it in the masculine, at the close of a japa: `गुह्यातिगुह्यगोप्ता त्वं गृहाणास्मत्कृतं जपम्। सिद्धिर्भवतु मे देव! त्वत्प्रसादान्महेश्वर॥` — *receive the japa we have made*, and *by your grace, O Maheśvara*.
- **Gītā Press's printed *Durgā Saptaśatī***, read from the page images and held in this corpus at `../../devi/durga/40_navarna_vidhi.txt`, prints it in the feminine, to the goddess: `गुह्यातिगुह्यगोप्त्री त्वं गृहाणास्मत्कृतं जपम् । सिद्धिर्भवतु मे देवि त्वत्प्रसादान्मयि स्थिरा ॥` — **and its second line is word for word this file's**.
- `../../devi/varahi/03_vasyavarahi_stotram.txt` prints the feminine form ending `त्वत्प्रसादान्महेश्वरि`.

**So all four readings of the second half-line are attested, and this file's is one of them.** What is distinctive here is only `कृततर्पणम्` for `अस्मत्कृतं जपम्` — the offering of water in place of the recitation — which is what the verse must say in a tarpaṇa. Recension note: **the structure of the rite, set down because the text is repetitive and a reader should be able to check it.**

**(1) The count is exact and it works out.** The mūlamantra is offered twelve times at the start. Then each of fifty-four items is offered four times, and after each of them the mūlamantra is offered four times again. That is 12 + (54 × 8) = **444**, which is the figure the source prints at the last line. The name of the rite, *caturāvṛtti*, "four repetitions", is the four-fold offering of each item.

**(2) The fifty-four items are of two kinds.** The first twenty-eight are the syllables of the mūlamantra itself, taken in order — `ओं श्रीं ह्रीं क्लीं ग्लौं गं गं णं पं तं यें वं रं वं रं दं सं र्वं जं नं में वं शं मां नं यं स्वां हां`. The remaining twenty-six are the retinue of the god and their consorts, in pairs — Śrī and the Lord of Śrī, Girijā and her lord, Rati and her lord, the Earth and her lord, then Mahālakṣmī and Mahāgaṇapati, then Ṛddhi and Āmoda, Samṛddhi and Pramoda, Kānti and Sumukha, Madanāvati and Durmukha, Madadravā and Avighna, Drāviṇī and Vighnakartā, and last Vasudhārā with Śaṅkhanidhi and Vasumatī with Padmanidhi. **The pairing of a power with its lord is the mark of this school**, and it is why the list is of that length.

**(3) The syllable `गं` is offered twice in succession** at the sixth and seventh places, and `वं` and `रं` each appear twice in the series. That is not a printing fault: the mūlamantra itself has those syllables twice, in `गं गणपतये` and in `वरवरद` and `सर्वजनं`, and the offering follows the mantra syllable by syllable.

**(4) The nyāsas and the pañcopacāra are printed twice**, once before the offering and once after it, and the second time the binding of the quarters becomes their release, `दिग्विमोकः` for `दिग्बन्धः`. That is the ordinary shape of such a rite and both printings are transcribed.

**(5) The syllables are enclosed in typographic quotation marks in the source**, `“ ”`, to mark off the syllable being offered from the frame around it. Those are kept exactly as printed, because removing them would leave the line ambiguous.

Blurb: Tarpaṇa is the offering of water. This one is made to Mahāgaṇapati, and it is called the offering of four repetitions because every part of the mantra is offered four times over. The worshipper first asks the sun for the water of the holy places and invites the Gaṅgā into it. He places the syllables of the mantra on his hands and on his body, meditates on the god seated with Siddhalakṣmī on his left, and offers the five services. Then the offering itself begins. The whole mantra is offered twelve times; after that each single syllable of it is offered four times, and the whole mantra four times after each syllable, and then the same is done for each of the god's attendants and their consorts. The water released comes to four hundred and forty-four offerings in all. At the end the worshipper gives the whole of it over, asking that what has been gained stand firm in him.
