Title: Puruṣasūkta-sahita Śrīsūkta Vidhāna Pūjā

Devanāgarī: पुरुषसूक्त सहित श्रीसूक्त विधान पूजा

Telugu: పురుషసూక్త సహిత శ్రీసూక్త విధాన పూజ

Author: Anonymous (received smārta household rite). No compiler is named and none should be inferred. The mantras at its services are Vedic and have no human author.

Type: vidhi (ritual manual) — not a stotra. Uses the one-extra-field vidhi format documented in `../../vidhi/madhyandina/01_sandhyopasana_vidhi.txt`: a `vidhi:` field carrying the ritual instruction, then deva / iast / en / tel / hi.

Recension / paddhati: **Smārta, South Indian.** The Vaiṣṇava form of the opening is kept separately at `../vaishnava/01_purvanga_vidhanam.txt` and **the two must never be merged, harmonised or collated against each other.**

Note: **This rite is built on two sūktas at once, and that is what sets it apart in this folder.** Every one of the sixteen services is headed by two ṛcs recited one after the other — a ṛc of the Puruṣa Sūkta and then a ṛc of the Śrī Sūkta, each hymn taken in its own order, from `सहस्रशीर्षा पुरुषः` and `हिरण्यवर्णां हरिणीम्` at the invocation to `नाभ्या आसीदन्तरिक्षम्` and `तां म आवह … प्रभूतं` at the betel. At the light, the Puruṣa Sūkta gives its sixteenth ṛc and the Śrī Sūkta its closing verse `यः शुचिः प्रयतो भूत्वा`; at the mantra-flower the Puruṣa Sūkta gives `धाता पुरस्तात्`. The page names the plan in its own closing dedication, `पुरुषसूक्त सहित श्रीसूक्त विधान पूर्वक`, and the claim was checked service by service and holds. `18_mahalakshmi_visesha_shodashopachara_puja.txt` and `19_durga_shodashopachara_puja.txt` are built on the Śrī Sūkta alone and `22_surya_shodashopachara_puja.txt` on the Puruṣa Sūkta alone; this rite joins the two plans.

**It is written for no one deity.** Every offering reads `ओं श्री ______ नमः`, with the name left blank, and the closing dedication has two blanks, one for `भगवान् सर्वात्मकः` and one for `भगवती सर्वात्मिका` — the Lord and the goddess worshipped together, as the pairing of the two hymns suggests. The blanks are kept as printed; nothing was filled in.

**The bath in the five nectars is set out in full**, each substance with its own Vedic mantra: `आप्यायस्व` for milk, `दधिक्राव्णो अकारिषम्` for curd, `शुक्रमसि ज्योतिरसि` for ghee, the three `मधु वाता` verses for honey, `स्वादुः पवस्व` for sugar, `याः फलिनीः` for the water of fruits, and `आपो हि ष्ठा` at the plain bath. The same mantras stand as a rite of their own in `50_panchamrita_abhisheka_mantrah.txt`, from another page of the same publisher; the two pages differ in several readings, listed below, and **neither file is to be corrected from the other.**

What it does not have: **no pūrvāṅga, no saṅkalpa and no dhyāna verse of its own.** Where the meditation stands, the page prints only `{ध्यान श्लोकाः}`, a place for the deity's own meditation verses; that marker is described in unit 1's `vidhi:` and not printed as text. Perform `03_purvanga_vidhanam.txt` first. It has no prāṇapratiṣṭhā, no aṅga-pūjā and no aṣṭottara.

The Paurāṇika form of this rite, without the Vedic mantras, is `../pauranika/smarta/48_purushasukta_sahita_srisukta_vidhana_puja.txt`. Neither file supersedes the other.

**Most apparent differences are not differences of text.** The witnesses print the saṁhitā-pāṭha with the nasal assimilated and hyphenated (`श्रिय॑-न्दे॒वीम्`, `मुख॒-ङ्किम॑स्य`), and put the accent mark after the visarga where this page puts it before (`वः॑` against `व॑ः`); vignanam writes `माद्ध्वी` for `माध्वी` and `व्.ण्णो` for `व्णो`. These are printing habits within one tradition and were normalised away before comparing.

2. Puruṣa Sūkta ṛc 8: `प्रौक्षन्॑` here; the Puruṣa Sūktam file has no mark on the final syllable.
3. Śrī Sūkta ṛcs 1, 13 and 14: `म॒ आव॑ह` here (and `म॒ आवह`, with no udātta, at ṛc 14); the Śrī Sūktam file reads `म॒माव॑ह` in all three.
4. Śrī Sūkta ṛc 5: `शर॑णम॒हं` here; the Śrī Sūktam file has no anudātta on `म`.
5. Śrī Sūkta ṛc 6: `वृ॒क्षोऽथ बि॒ल्वः` here; the witness reads `वृ॒क्षो-ऽथ॑ बि॒ल्वः`. `18_mahalakshmi_visesha_shodashopachara_puja.txt`, from another page of this publisher, prints `ऽथ॑` and `तप॒सोऽधि॑ जा॒तो`, so the publisher's two pages disagree with each other here.
6. Śrī Sūkta ṛc 7: `की॒र्तिमृ॑द्धिं` here; the witness reads `की॒र्ति॒मृ॑द्धि-न्`.
7. Śrī Sūkta ṛc 8: `क्षुत्पि॑पा॒साम॑लां ज्ये॒ष्ठाम॑ल॒क्ष्मीं` here; the witness reads `क्षु॒त्पि॒पा॒साम॑ला-ञ्ज्ये॒ष्ठाम॒ल॒क्षी-र्` — different accent on two words, and `अलक्ष्मीम्` against the older `अलक्षीः`. The same ṛc reads `सर्वां॒` and `गृहा॑त्` here and `स॒र्वा॒-न्` and `गृहात्` there.
8. Śrī Sūkta ṛc 10: `प॒शू॒नां` here, `प॒शू॒नाग्ं` there.
9. Śrī Sūkta ṛc 15: `हि॑रण्यं॒ प्रभू॑तं॒` here; the witness reads `हिर॑ण्य॒-म्प्रभू॑त॒-ङ्`, and this page itself prints `हिर॑ण्यं` at ṛc 2. The mark in ṛc 15 is most likely a slip of this page, but no reading was changed on that ground.
10. The Śrī Sūkta's closing verse (at the light): `यः शुचि॒ः प्रय॑तो भू॒त्वा जु॒हुया᳚दाज्य॒ मन्व॑हम् । … प॒ञ्चद॑शर्चं॒ च … सत॒तं ज॑पेत्` here; the witness reads `यश्शुचिः॑ प्रयतो भू॒त्वा॒ जु॒हुया॑-दाज्य॒-मन्व॑हम् । … प॒ञ्चद॑शर्चं च … सत॒तं॒ ज॑पेत्`. This verse is the phala-śruti and is the least settled part of the hymn in print.
11. Curd: `कर॒त्प्राण॒` here; the Taittirīya Saṁhitā and this publisher's own abhiṣeka page (`50`) both read `कर॒त्प्रण॒` (`प्र णः`, 'our'). `प्राण` is a real word, so this is recorded as a reading and not emended.
12. Ghee: `तेजो॑सि` here without the avagraha; the Saṁhitā reads `तेजो॑-ऽसि`.
13. Honey: `स॒न्त्वौष॑धीः` here; the Saṁhitā and `50` read `स॒न्त्वोष॑धीः`. And `मु॒तोष॑सि॒` here with an udātta the Saṁhitā does not mark (`मु॒तोषसि॒`).
14. Sugar (Ṛgveda 9.85.6): `इन्द्रा᳚य`, `सु॒हवी᳚तु` and `मधु॑मां॒ अदा᳚भ्यः` here; the Ṛgveda reads `इंद्रा॑य`, `सु॒हवी॑तु` and `मधु॑माँ॒ अदा॑भ्यः`, and `50` reads `इन्द्रा॑य` and `सु॒हवी॑तु॒ नाम्ने᳚` and `मधु॑मा॒ग्ं अदा᳚भ्यः`. Three different printings of one verse; the dīrgha svarita marks here are not in the Ṛgveda.

Defects in the base text, corrected with reasons, because stotranidhi is a convenience source and not a critical edition:
1. **`आयूग्॑म्षि` → `आयूग्ं॑षि`** (unit 8, curd). The page's spelling puts the udātta inside the nasal and adds a `म्` that is not pronounced; it is not a possible form. The corrected reading is byte for byte the one this publisher's own abhiṣeka page prints, and it is the Saṁhitā's (`आयूग्ं॑षि`). The accent mark is on the same syllable before and after.
2. **`मुन्च॒न्त्व` → `मुञ्च॒न्त्व`** (unit 12, fruit water). `न्च` is not a Sanskrit conjunct; the verb is `मुञ्च`. `50` and the Saṁhitā both print `मुञ्च`. No accent mark was moved.
3. **`नमस्कारां` → `नमस्कारान्`** (unit 27). The accusative plural of `नमस्कार` is `नमस्कारान्`, as the page itself prints four lines earlier.
4. **`अकालमृत्यहरणं` → `अकालमृत्युहरणम्`** (unit 30). A dropped `u`; the corrected form stands in other files of this folder.
5. **`शीरसा` → `शिरसा`** (unit 30). A long `ī` for the short `i` of `शिरस्`.

Observed and deliberately **not** emended:
- **`रक्ष रक्ष जनार्दना रक्ष रक्ष सुरेश्वरी`** (unit 26), with long vowels for the vocatives `जनार्दन` and `सुरेश्वरि`. This is how the verse is commonly printed, and it calls on both deities of the rite.
- **`पापसम्भव`**, **`त्राहिमां`** and **`तदस्तुते`** (units 26 and 29), printed as the page has them.
- **`सुप्रीता सुप्रसन्ना वरदा भवन्तु`** (unit 29), where the adjectives are feminine singular and the verb plural, although the dedication names two deities. The same reading was left as printed in `18`.
- **The zero-width non-joiner** inside `प॒शूग्‌स्ताग्‌श्च॑क्रे` and `मधु॑माग्‌ं` (units 16 and 10). The page uses it to hold the conjunct together on screen, and it is kept as printed. The `iast:` field renders the Taittirīya nasal before a sibilant as `ṁ` (`paśūṁstāṁścakre`), as `../../veda/taittiriya/01_purusha_suktam.txt` does.

**A defect of the source's Veda pages, corrected throughout.** stotranidhi writes final visarga in its accented mantras as the ASCII colon `:` rather than as `ः` (U+0903). 28 such colons, all on accented lines, were mapped to `ः`.

Blurb: This is a worship in sixteen services in which two Vedic hymns are recited together. At every service the worshipper first says a verse of the Puruṣa Sūkta, the hymn of the cosmic Person, and then a verse of the Śrī Sūkta, the hymn to the goddess of fortune, so that both hymns are recited in order across the worship. The rite is not written for one deity. The name of the deity is said at each offering, and the closing prayer names both the Lord and the goddess. The bath is given in full: milk, curd, ghee, honey and sugar are each poured with their own Vedic mantra, then the water of fruits, then pure water. The worship ends with the mantra-flower, circumambulation, the royal services, a prayer for pardon, and the receiving of the water and the prasāda. The opening portion is not included, and is performed first.
