Title: Śrī Haridrā Gaṇapati Pūjā
Devanāgarī: श्री हरिद्रा गणपति पूजा
Telugu: శ్రీ హరిద్రా గణపతి పూజ

Author: Anonymous (received smārta household rite). No compiler is named and none should be inferred.

Type: vidhi (ritual manual) — not a stotra. Uses the one-extra-field vidhi format documented in `../../vidhi/madhyandina/01_sandhyopasana_vidhi.txt`: a `vidhi:` field carrying the ritual instruction, then deva / iast / en / tel / hi.

Recension / paddhati: **Smārta, South Indian.** The Vaiṣṇava form of the opening is kept separately at `../vaishnava/01_purvanga_vidhanam.txt` and **the two must never be merged, harmonised or collated against each other.**

**What this rite is.** Before any larger pūjā or vrata, Gaṇapati is worshipped in a small cone of turmeric paste, the `हरिद्राबिम्ब`, so that the work that follows is free of obstacles. This is that short worship. Several rites of this folder send the reciter to it by name before they begin — among them `19_durga_shodashopachara_puja.txt`, `43_rama_shodashopachara_puja.txt` and `44_shankaracharya_shodashopachara_puja.txt` (`श्री महागणपति पूजा (हरिद्रा गणपति पूजा) पश्यतु`).

**It is not the same rite as `04_ganapati_nitya_puja.txt` or `15_mahaganapati_shodashopachara_puja.txt`**, and none of the three is to be corrected from another. `04` is a full daily pūjā with its own pūrvāṅga; `15` is the Mudgala Purāṇa rite with a verse for every service. This one is brief: most services are the nāma-mantra and the offering alone, and the Vedic mantras stand at the placing of the breath, the meditation, the bath, the garment, the sacred thread, the sandal paste, the food, the light and the farewell. Its flower-worship is by the **sixteen names** of Gaṇapati (`सुमुखश्चैकदन्तश्च`), with a seventeenth, `सर्वसिद्धिप्रदाय`, added at the end, and the same sixteen names return as the verse of the mantra-flower with their phalaśruti.

What it does not have: **no pūrvāṅga of its own** — use `03_purvanga_vidhanam.txt` first — and **no second saṅkalpa**. It begins by invoking the Lord into the turmeric.

Defects in the base text, corrected with reasons, because stotranidhi is a convenience source and not a critical edition:

1. **`वनस्पत्युद्भविर्दिव्यैः` → `वनस्पत्युद्भवैर्दिव्यैः`** (the incense verse). `उद्भविः` is not a form; the instrumental plural `उद्भवैः` agrees with `दिव्यैः` and `गन्धैः`, and ten other files of this corpus print the verse so.
2. **`अन्यधा` → `अन्यथा`** (the circumambulation verse). A misprint of `ध` for `थ`; the Rāma page of the same site prints the verse with `अन्यथा`.
3. **A stray comma removed** from `कृ॒त्वाऽभि॒वद॒न्॒, यदास्ते᳚`. No accented edition has it; `../../veda/taittiriya/01_purusha_suktam.txt` and the Rāma page do not.
4. **`कपिलायनमः`, `लम्बोदरायनमः`, `गणाधिपायनमः` → `कपिलाय नमः`** and so on: a missing space only, in three of the seventeen names.

Observed and deliberately **not** emended:

- **`ओं श्री महागणपति नमः यथास्थानं उद्वासयामि`** at the farewell, where the dative `महागणपतये` is expected. No second witness prints this line.
- **`सुप्रीतो सुप्रसन्नो वरदो भवन्तु`** in the dedication — singular adjectives with a plural verb.
- **`यज्ञोपवीतार्थं अक्षतान् समर्पयामि । ।`** — the page gives the sacred thread no service number and prints an empty second daṇḍa where the number would stand. The sixteen numbered services run from meditation to the waving of the light, and the thread falls between the eighth and the ninth.
- The **blank `__________`** in the offering of food is the page's own: the worshipper names there the food being offered.
- The dhyāna verse `हरिद्राभं चतुर्बाहुं` is printed as **three half-lines, each closed with a single daṇḍa** and with no closing double daṇḍa — a verse of three lines, not a śloka of two. No second witness prints it, and it stands as printed.
- **`सुसम्युतः`, `सम्युक्तं`** for `सुसंयुतः`, `संयुक्तं`. This publisher writes anusvāra before a semivowel as `म्` throughout; an orthographic habit, not a defect.

Blurb: Before a larger worship or a vrata is begun, Gaṇapati is first worshipped in a small image shaped from turmeric paste, so that what follows may be free of obstacles. This is that short worship. The reciter first performs the opening portion. The Lord is then invoked into the turmeric, the life-breath is placed in it, and he is meditated upon as golden like turmeric, four-armed, holding the noose, the goad, a sweet and his tusk. The sixteen services follow, most of them brief. At the flowers he is worshipped by his sixteen names, and at the mantra-flower the same sixteen names are recited as a verse, with the promise that no obstacle arises for one who reads or hears them at the beginning of study, at a marriage, on entering or leaving a place, or in any undertaking. The rite closes with circumambulation, the asking of pardon, the receiving of the water from his feet, and the farewell.
