Author: No human author. Vedic mantras, arranged for the bath of a deity in a received smārta household rite.

Type: vidhi (ritual manual) — not a stotra. Uses the one-extra-field vidhi format documented in `../../vidhi/madhyandina/01_sandhyopasana_vidhi.txt`: a `vidhi:` field carrying the ritual instruction, then deva / iast / en / tel / hi.

Recension / paddhati: **Smārta, South Indian.** The Vaiṣṇava form of the opening is kept separately at `../vaishnava/01_purvanga_vidhanam.txt` and **the two must never be merged, harmonised or collated against each other.**

Note: **The mantras for bathing a deity in the five nectars, and after them in the water of fruits and in pure water.** Each substance has its own Vedic mantra, and each mantra suits its substance: `आप्यायस्व`, 'swell', for milk; `दधिक्राव्णो अकारिषम्`, which names curd, for curd; `शुक्रमसि ज्योतिरसि`, 'you are bright', for ghee; the three `मधु वाता` verses, each asking for sweetness, for honey; `स्वादुः पवस्व`, 'flow sweet', for sugar; `याः फलिनीः`, on the plants that bear fruit, for the water of fruits; and `आपो हि ष्ठा`, to the waters, for the plain bath. It is not a whole pūjā. It is the bath of one, to be used at the snāna service of any rite that calls for the five nectars.

The deity's name is left blank in every offering (`श्री _____ नमः`), and the blank is kept as printed.

The witness prints the saṁhitā-pāṭha with nasals assimilated and hyphenated (`अकारिष-ञ्जि॒ष्णोः`), puts the accent after the visarga where this page puts it before (`वः॑` against `व॑ः`), and writes `माद्ध्वी` and `व्.ण्णो`. These are printing habits and were set aside before comparing. **Differences that remain, recorded and not corrected:**
1. Honey: `मु॒तोष॑सि॒` here; the Saṁhitā has no udātta on `ष` (`मु॒तोषसि॒`).
2. Ghee: `तेजो॑सि` here without the avagraha; the Saṁhitā reads `तेजो॑-ऽसि`.
3. Sugar: `सु॒हवी॑तु॒ नाम्ने᳚` and `मधु॑मा॒ग्ं अदा᳚भ्यः` here; the Ṛgveda reads `सु॒हवी॑तुनाम्ने` and `मधु॑माँ॒ अदा॑भ्यः`. `48` reads `सु॒हवी᳚तु नाम्ने` and `मधु॑मां॒ अदा᳚भ्यः`.

Unit count: 7 units — one for each substance.

Sections:
पञ्चामृत स्नानम् — The bath in the five nectars (units 1–5)
फलोदकम् — The water of fruits (unit 6)
शुद्धोदक स्नानम् — The bath in pure water (unit 7)

Blurb: These are the Vedic mantras for bathing a deity in the five nectars. Milk, curd, ghee, honey and sugar are poured one after another, each with its own mantra, and each mantra speaks of what is being poured: milk with a verse asking the deity to swell with strength, honey with three verses asking for sweetness, sugar with a verse bidding it flow sweet. After the five nectars comes the water of fruits, with a verse on the plants that bear fruit and those that do not, and last the bath in pure water, with the three verses to the waters. The name of the deity is said at each offering. The mantras are used at the bath within a larger worship.
