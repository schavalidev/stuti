Title: Śrī Anaghāṣṭamī Vrata Kalpam — the worship of Anaghā and Anagha on the eighth day (Smārta Paddhati, South Indian)
Devanāgarī: श्री अनघाष्टमी व्रत कल्पम् — अनघादेवी समेत अनघस्वामी पूजा विधानम् (स्मार्त पद्धतिः)
Telugu: శ్రీ అనఘాష్టమి వ్రత కల్పం — అనఘాదేవి సమేత అనఘస్వామి పూజా విధానం (స్మార్త పద్ధతిః)

Author: Traditional. No compiler is named and none should be inferred. The kathā is spoken in the text by Kṛṣṇa to Yudhiṣṭhira, and reported by a teacher to his pupil Dīpaka.

Type: vidhi (ritual manual) — not a stotra. It uses the one-extra-field vidhi format of this folder: a `vidhi:` field carrying the instruction, then deva / iast / en / tel / hi.

Recension / paddhati: **Smārta, South Indian (Telugu country), within the Dattātreya tradition.** The rite directs the worshipper to the shared pūrvāṅga and the turmeric-Gaṇapati pūjā before it begins, and both stand in this folder — `03_purvanga_vidhanam.txt` and `04_ganapati_nitya_puja.txt`. The Śrīvaiṣṇava form of the opening is kept separately at `../vaishnava/01_purvanga_vidhanam.txt`, and **the two must never be merged, harmonised or collated against each other.**

Source / recension: base text `https://stotranidhi.com/sri-anagha-vratam-anaghashtami-vratham/` (fetched 2026-09-12, 399 lines), which is **Telugu script only**. **The Devanāgarī column is therefore transliterated by us**, mechanically, with `bin/tel2dev.py`, and `iast:` from that with `bin/dev2iast.py`; no Sanskrit in this file was typed by hand.

Independent collation, on two fronts, and both are real.

1. **A second printed edition of the whole rite**, supplied by the user: *Śrīśrīśrī Anaghāṣṭami Vratakalpamu*, published by the Vedabhārati Svacchanda Dhārmika Sevā Saṁstha, Nēreḍmeṭ (47 pp.), which states on its title page that it is drawn from the **second khaṇḍa of the Śrīpāda Śrīvallabha Caritāmṛta**. That is a different lineage from a stotra site, and it is a different attribution from the one the base text gives.
2. **The Puruṣa Sūkta ṛcs against this corpus's own accented Taittirīya copy**, `../../veda/taittiriya/01_purusha_suktam.txt`. Fifty-three lines of the base text were found there; **fifty are identical including every accent mark.** The three that differ are itemised below.

Recension note: the two editions are the same rite at different scope, and neither is a defective copy of the other.

1. **The base text points outward where the second edition prints inward.** It says only "see the pūrvāṅga" and "see the Mahāgaṇapati pūjā"; the Vedabhārati booklet sets both out in full before the vrata begins. The base text's way matches how this folder is built, which is why it is the base here.
2. **The second edition adds preparation directions the base text does not give** — what to place on which petal, the twenty-one rupee coins, the two betel leaves with an areca nut, a date and a coin in each, the two and a half measures of rice, and the writing of the year, month, fortnight, day, star, name and gotra on a sheet of white paper. It also gives fuller directions for the **toraṇa**: three strands of turmeric, white and red twisted together and set before the two pots.
3. **They agree exactly on all eight directions of the eight powers** — Aṇimā north-east, Laghimā south-east, Prāpti south-west, Prākāmya north-west, Īśitva south, Vaśitva north, Kāmāvasāyitā west, Mahimā east. That is a genuine agreement across two lineages and worth stating.
4. **They differ on the central pots, and this is not reconciled.** The base text installs **Dattātreya** in the first central pot, its verse naming him Dattātreya, teacher, whose self is Viṣṇu, Brahmā and Īśa. The Vedabhārati booklet installs **Anagha** in the first and **Anaghādevī** beside him, and does not name Dattātreya there. The kathā itself says Anagha is the form of Viṣṇu and that Datta is Kṛṣṇa's own portion, so the two may well be one thing under two names; but the editions print differently and both readings are kept.
5. **The kathā's own attribution could not be confirmed.** The base text states that the greatness of this vrata is told in the **Uttara-parva of the Bhaviṣya Mahāpurāṇa**. Three Bhaviṣya Purāṇa scans on archive.org were searched — the largest carrying 1,641,197 Devanāgarī characters — and **not one contains the word `अनघा` at all**. A search for any Sanskrit Anaghāṣṭamī text returned nothing, and the Bhaviṣyottara items on archive.org cover other vratas but not this one. **So no Sanskrit of the kathā was found, and none has been composed.** The narrative is the source's own Telugu prose, and it sits in the translation fields where the source put it; the `deva:` of those units carries the section heading alone.
6. **Kṛṣṇa's own directions in the kathā differ from the rite printed above it, in two places, and neither is reconciled.** He directs that the worship be made **with the ṛcs of the Ṛgveda**, where the rite uses the Puruṣa Sūkta; and he directs that the eight sons be thought of as **Pradyumna and the rest, named in the Harivaṁśa**, where the rite installs them as the eight yogic attainments. Both stand as the source prints them.
7. **Three differences from the corpus's accented Puruṣa Sūkta, recorded and not reconciled.** At `यदन्नेनातिरोहति` this source carries an anudātta on the first syllable which that copy does not. At `आरण्यान्ग्राम्याश्च ये` the two differ only in a word space. And the closing ṛc `यज्ञेन यज्ञमयजन्त देवाः` is **printed here without any accent**, where that copy carries it accented and divides `प्रथमान्यासन्` where this source writes `प्रथमा न्यासन्`. Per the rule in the repo-root `CLAUDE.md`, accent is transcribed or absent and never imported to fill a gap, so the closing ṛc is left unaccented exactly as this source prints it.

Not collated: the hundred and eight names. At its aṣṭottara the rite points at two nāmāvalis rather than printing them, one for Anagha Deva and one for Anaghā Devī. Both are published separately and **neither is in this corpus yet**; they are work to follow, not part of this file.

Accent: **present throughout the Vedic portions, 322 marks**, which is correct — the Puruṣa Sūkta is Vedic and this source prints it accented, in the Taittirīya tradition that a Telugu smārta rite follows. Udātta `॑` (U+0951), anudātta `॒` (U+0952), dīrgha svarita `᳚` (U+1CDA). The rite's own verses, being stotra, are unaccented and are left so.

Note on defects repaired in the base text, each counted. **The largest is systematic: the dīrgha svarita `᳚` is printed as a Latin right double quotation mark `”` (U+201D).** Twenty-five of those were converted. **Twelve further `”` in the same page are genuine closing quotation marks in the Telugu prose and were deliberately left alone** — the mark does both jobs on this page, and converting all of them would have put svaritas inside the story. The reading `”` = `᳚` is not a guess: `../../veda/taittiriya/01_purusha_suktam.txt` prints `सर्वम्᳚`, `भव्यम्᳚`, `पादो᳚ऽस्य॒` and `तस्मा᳚द्वि॒राड॑जायत` at exactly these places. Also repaired: **six Latin `o` for the anusvāra `ం`** inside Telugu words (`భూమి॑o`, `అమృత॑o`, `పురు॑ష॒o`, `ముఖ॒o`, `వరే᳚ణ్య॒o`), the same defect class as the twenty-nine repaired in `../../vidhi/taittiriya/01_krishna_yajurveda_sandhyavandanam.txt`; **two `గ్` followed by a zero-width joiner before a spirant**, which spell the guttural nasal and would otherwise have read as a bare `g`; **six places where the accent mark was written before the anusvāra** rather than after it, normalised to the order that same accented copy uses; and **twenty ASCII colons standing for visarga**: six of them `bin/tel2dev.py` maps on its way from the Telugu, and **fourteen more stood in the Devanāgarī of the Puruṣa Sūkta** (`पुन॑:`, `परि॒धय॑:`, `स॒मिध॑:`, `स॑र्व॒हुत॑:` twice, `ऋच॒:`, `अ॑जा॒वय॑:`, `रा॑ज॒न्य॑:`, `यद्वैश्य॑:`, `चक्षो॒:`, `भूर्भुव॒स्सुव॑:`, `धियो॒ यो न॑:`, `दिश॒:`, `धीर॑:`) where the conversion had not reached them. Counted off the file as it now stands, not off the plan. One further defect is corrected in the text and flagged at its unit: **the verse naming the eight powers prints `మహిమా` twice and omits `లఘిమా`**; the second word is `लघिमा` and is written so here.

Unit count: 53 units. The rite falls into the second resolve, the installing of the eight powers with the two principal pots and the installing of breath, the meditation, the sixteen services with the aṅga-pūjās and the hundred and eight names, the binding of the thread, the kathā in six parts, and the closing. The Puruṣa Sūkta is distributed one ṛc to each service, which is what the resolve itself declares.

Blurb: This is the vrata of Anaghā and Anagha, kept on the eighth day of the dark fortnight of Mārgaśīrṣa. Anagha is Dattātreya, the son of Atri and Anasūyā, and Anaghā is his wife. The rite is built on an eight-petalled lotus drawn in rice flour, turmeric and vermilion. A pot is set on each petal, and on each the worshipper installs one of the eight powers that yoga gives — minuteness, lightness, attainment, freedom of will, greatness, mastery, control, and the fulfilment of desire. Each is called in by a verse that names it as a child of the divine pair. In the middle of the lotus the pair themselves are installed. The sixteen services follow, and each one is headed by a single verse of the Puruṣa Sūkta, taken in order from the first to the last. The limbs of both are then worshipped by sixteen names apiece, and a thread of three strands is tied. The story is told in six parts. Its heart is the answer to a pupil's question: when the demons came, Datta did not fight them. He stopped them with the fire of his meditation, Anaghā drained their strength by her austerity, and the gods struck them down. The name is then explained — agha is sin, arising through mind, speech and deed, and Anagha is he who destroys it.

vidhi:
**The answer to Dīpaka's question, and it is not the answer he expected.** The gods, driven from heaven, take refuge at the hermitage on the Vindhya. The demons follow and lay hands on Anaghā. Datta stops them with the fire of his meditation and Anaghā drains their strength by her austerity; only then do the gods strike them down, and Indra kills Jambha. So Datta did not fight: he and Anaghā made the demons powerless, and others finished it.
deva:
जम्भासुर युद्धम्
iast:
jambhāsura yuddham
en: The gods came to be afflicted by a demon named Jambha. By a boon from Brahmā, Jambha went to Amarāvatī and made war for a hundred divine years. The demons of the nether world joined that battle, and it was fought with numberless armies of dance and demon. Indra and the Marut hosts, defeated, left their places and were driven to the ten directions. The gods fled in fear before, and Jambha followed behind; the demons fought with volleys of arrows and with club, pestle and mace. Mounted on wild buffaloes, on buffaloes, tigers, monkeys and other beasts, they pursued the gods, raining down boulders and shafts. So the gods reached the hermitage on the Vindhya where the couple Anagha and Anaghā dwelt, and there they begged for refuge: "God of gods, lord of the world, bearer of conch, discus and mace — save us, save us. Give refuge to us who are in danger. We have been defeated at the hand of the demon Jambha. For gods and devotees there is no other course but your lotus feet. Therefore, holy one, we gods have come to your shelter." Hearing their lament, the lord Ātreya looked lightly towards his goddess Anaghā, and gave his assent to all the gods, who had come with their fever gone, and satisfied them. Meanwhile the demons came on, striking. Seeing the brahmin woman there — Anaghā Devī — and overcome by her beauty, with minds full of arrogance, they began to carry her off, saying they would give her flowers and fruit. Datta, seeing this, made them motionless by the fire of his meditation, and Anaghā Devī made the demons powerless by the strength of her austerity. Seeing the demons drained of light and of strength, the gods struck at them. As the gods attacked with swords, spears, tridents and clubs, the demons cried out, unable to bear that pain. The demons were destroyed by the weapons of the gods, and Jambha by Indra. **How the vrata came to men.** Kārtavīryārjuna served Datta in his austerity and asked four boons — a thousand arms, guidance to keep to dharma, the winning of the whole earth by dharma, and death only at the hand of a great warrior in battle. The last sentence is the point of the whole story: it was through him that the Anaghāṣṭamī vrata became known in the world of mortals.

en: Kṛṣṇa continued: "That Datta brought good to all the worlds by his deeds, his mind and his speech. That great ascetic, arms raised, motionless as a log or a stone, keeping the observances, performed austerity; gazing with his eyes at the place between the brows, he stood firm in austerity for three thousand divine years. To him, standing in yoga, came Kārtavīryārjuna, king of Māhiṣmatī. Night and day, without weariness, he served him humbly — worship with the body, contemplation with the mind, keeping the whole observance, doing these attendances gladly. Datta, pleased with him, told him to ask for boons, and Kārtavīryārjuna asked four: first a thousand arms; second, right guidance to hold to dharma and not walk the unrighteous road; third, to win the whole earth by the way of dharma and to rule it righteously; fourth, to die a hero's death only at the hand of a great warrior in battle. Datta gladly gave the boons, and granted him a wide kingdom and a knowledge of yoga without equal. Thereafter that king gained sovereignty and the eight attainments, and by the power of his thousand arms won righteously the whole earth with its seven continents and its mountains. Performing ten thousand sacrifices in all the continents, that great-armed one gave rich fees and gifts to all. He had all the sacrificial platforms adorned with golden pillars, and the sacrificial grounds shone with the daily singing and dancing of gandharvas and apsarases. Seeing the life and the greatness of that lion among kings, it seemed there was no other in the world beyond Kārtavīrya. By sacrifices, by gifts and by austerities he was praised as a man of valour. With his sword and his arrows, by his power of yoga, he went about the seven continents and watched over them. By that king's power there was no loss of wealth, no grief and no sickness in the kingdom, and the people were guarded by dharma. Ruling for eighty-five thousand years, that lord of men governed cattle and fields as an emperor. By Arjuna's power of yoga Parjanya gave rain. With his thousand arms he shone like a thousand suns drying up the seas. He conquered the city where Karkoṭaka was. Swimming for sport against the current of the Narmadā and blocking the river, the water, pressed by his force, drew away like a beautiful woman — it flowed backwards. By the beating of his thousand arms the ocean was thrown into tumult, great sea-monsters died, and the demons in the nether world were struck still. He overcame Rāvaṇa and held him bound in Māhiṣmatī; afterwards, honouring the request of the sage Pulastya, he released him — but Rāvaṇa, once freed, reviled Pulastya. Once, when Citrabhānu — the god of fire — came begging out of hunger, he gave him as alms the crops, the creepers and the trees of the seven continents. Such qualities came to Kārtavīryārjuna only by the grace of Anagha Deva, the teacher of yoga. And it was through Kārtavīrya, who had received the boon of such a yogin, that the Anaghāṣṭamī vrata became known in the world of mortals."

ప్రశంసింపబడెను. తన ఖడ్గముతో, శరములతో తన యోగశక్తిచే ఏడు ద్వీపములు తిరుగుచూ చూచుచుండెను. ఆ రాజు ప్రభావము చేత రాజ్యమున ద్రవ్యనష్టము, శోకము, అనారోగ్యములు లేక ప్రజలు ధర్మము వలన రక్షింపబడుచుండిరి. ఎనభైఅయిదు వేల సంవత్సరములు పాలించుచూ ఆ నరాధిపుడు (రాజు) చక్రవర్తివలె పశువులను, క్షేత్రములను, పాలించెను. ఆ అర్జునుడి యోగశక్తి వలన పర్జన్యుడు వృష్టిని ఇచ్చెను (వానలు కురిసెను). తన సహస్ర బాహువులతో సముద్రములను శోషింపజేయు వేయి సూర్యులవలె ప్రకాశించెను. కర్కోటకుడు ఉన్న పురమును జయించెను. క్రీడావిలాసమున నర్మదా నదిలో ఎదురీత చేయుచూ నదిని ఆడ్డగింప, ఆతని ధాటికి జలము పీడనమునకు గురి అయి అందమైన స్త్రీ వలె దూరముగా పులస్త్య ఋషి అభ్యర్థనను గౌరవించి రావణాసురుని విడిచెను. కానీ విడుదల అయిన రావణుడు (అవమానభారముతో) పులస్త్యుని నిందించెను. ఒకసారి ఆకలి వలన భిక్షకు వచ్చిన చిత్రభానునికి (అగ్నిదేవునికి) సప్తద్వీపములలోని పంటలను, లతలను, వృక్షములను భిక్షగా ఇచ్చెను. ఇటువంటి గుణములు కార్తవీర్యార్జునునకు కేవలము యోగాచార్యుడైన అనఘదేవుని కృప వలన మాత్రమే కలిగెను. అలాంటి యోగి వరము లభించిన కార్తవీర్యుని వలన మర్త్యలోకమున అనఘాష్టమీ వ్రతము ప్రసిద్ధి చెందెను.
hi: श्रीकृष्ण ने कहा: "उन दत्त ने अपने कर्म, मन और वाक् से सर्व लोकों का शुभ किया। वे महातपस्वी ऊर्ध्व बाहु किये, काष्ठ अथवा शिला के समान अचल, नियमों का पालन करते तपस्या करने लगे; नेत्रों से भ्रूमध्य स्थान को देखते हुए तीन हजार दिव्य वर्ष स्थिर तपस्या की। योगस्थिति में स्थित उनके पास माहिष्मती के राजा कार्तवीर्यार्जुन आये। रात-दिन अथक विनयपूर्वक शुश्रूषा करने लगे — गात्र से पूजा, मानसिक चिन्तन, सम्पूर्ण नियमों का पालन करते आनन्द से सपर्या की। उससे सन्तुष्ट दत्त ने उसे वर माँगने को कहा, और कार्तवीर्यार्जुन ने चार वर माँगे: पहला सहस्र बाहु; तदनन्तर पुलस्त्य ऋषि की अभ्यर्थना का आदर कर रावण को छोड़ा — किन्तु मुक्त रावण ने पुलस्त्य की निन्दा की। एक बार भूख से भिक्षा के लिये आये चित्रभानु — अग्निदेव — को सप्त द्वीपों की फसलें, लताएँ और वृक्ष भिक्षा में दिये। ऐसे गुण कार्तवीर्यार्जुन को केवल योगाचार्य अनघदेव की कृपा से ही प्राप्त हुए। और ऐसे योगी का वर पाये कार्तवीर्य के कारण ही मर्त्यलोक में अनघाष्टमी व्रत प्रसिद्ध हुआ।"

vidhi:
**The name explained, and it explains the rite.** Agha is sin; it arises in three ways, through mind, speech and deed; and he who destroys such sin is Anagha. The eight powers worshipped on the eight petals are named again here, and the passage closes with Kṛṣṇa saying that Anagha is his own portion.
deva:
अनघ नाम विवरणम्
iast:
anagha nāma vivaraṇam
en: "Agha means sin. In the world agha arises in three ways — through the mind, through speech and through deeds. Know that he who destroys such agha is Anagha. To one who worships Anagha according to the rule there come the eight qualities and the eight kinds of wealth. By worshipping the eight yogic attainments — aṇimā, laghimā, prāpti, prākāmya, mahimā, īśitva, vaśitva and kāmāvasāyitā — the marks of liberation are gained. Therefore for one who takes up the name of Datta with devotion, the aghas come to an end. Know that Anagha, who makes the world free of agha, is my own portion."

--- unit 50 | section: व्रतकथा ---
vidhi:
**Kṛṣṇa's own directions, and they are the authority for the rite printed above.** The day is the eighth of the dark fortnight of Mārgaśīrṣa. The pair are installed with their many sons on ground first made clean, in pots on an eight-petalled figure, or on lotuses, or on darbha grass. Note two things the rite above does differently: here the worship is directed to be made **with the ṛcs of the Ṛgveda**, where the rite above uses the Puruṣa Sūkta; and the sons are to be thought of as **Pradyumna and the rest, as named in the Harivaṁśa**. Both are recorded and neither is reconciled.
deva:
अनघाष्टमी व्रत विधानम्
iast:
anaghāṣṭamī vrata vidhānam
en: Hearing this, Yudhiṣṭhira said: "Lotus-eyed one, tell me what vow this Arjuna, king of all, performed — its mantras, its times, in what season and on what days it is to be kept. Tell me, Keśava." Kṛṣṇa then said: "On the eighth day of the dark fortnight in the month of Mārgaśīrṣa, the couple Anagha and Anaghā, together with their many sons, are to be installed and worshipped on ground first made quiet and clean — in pots upon an eight-petalled figure, or upon lotuses, or upon darbha grass. Having first bathed, with flowers and fragrances, meditating on Viṣṇu, one should worship with the ṛcs of the Ṛgveda. Anagha, who is a portion of Vāsudeva, and Anaghā, who is a portion of Lakṣmī, are to be worshipped, thinking of Pradyumna and the rest, named in the Harivaṁśa, as their company of sons. Having first saluted the śūdras and the brahmins, one should offer the good fruits of that season, bulbs, water-chestnuts, jujubes and other such offerings of food, with sandal, incense and lamps. Afterwards one should take food with the twice-born, with connections and with kinsmen. At the close of the vow one should urge at least one other person to keep it. I say this truth: this vow is worth keeping for a lifetime. It should be kept for at least one year. That night one should keep vigil with acting, dance and song of holy things, and at dawn on the ninth the images are to be given into water. Whoever keeps it thus with devotion and care every year is released from all sins. His family prospers. Viṣṇu becomes gracious to him. He has health for seven births, and thereafter gains the highest state. Son of Kuntī, those who keep this Anaghāṣṭamī vrata, which quiets sin, in the way I have told, become renowned as Kṛtavīrya's son was."
