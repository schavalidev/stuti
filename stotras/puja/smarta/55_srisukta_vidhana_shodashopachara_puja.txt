Note: **This rite is built on the Śrī Sūkta alone.** Each of the sixteen services is headed by one ṛc of the hymn, taken in the hymn's own order, from `हिरण्यवर्णां हरिणीम्` at the invocation to `तां म आवह … प्रभूतं` at the betel; the light takes the closing verse `यः शुचिः प्रयतो भूत्वा`, which names the hymn's fifteen ṛcs. The page names the plan in its closing dedication, `श्रीसूक्त विधान पूर्वक`, and the claim was checked service by service and holds. **The mantra-flower has no mantra**: the page prints only the offering line there.

**It is the Śrī Sūkta half of `48_purushasukta_sahita_srisukta_vidhana_puja.txt`.** That rite recites a ṛc of the Puruṣa Sūkta and then a ṛc of the Śrī Sūkta at every service; this one recites the Śrī Sūkta ṛc only. The two pages come from the same publisher and share every other line: the five-nectar bath with its seven mantras, the mantras over the food, the circumambulation, the royal services and the asking of pardon. The Puruṣa Sūkta half is `54_purushasukta_vidhana_shodashopachara_puja.txt`. `18_mahalakshmi_visesha_shodashopachara_puja.txt`, `19_durga_shodashopachara_puja.txt` and `37_kubera_puja_vidhanam.txt` are Śrī Sūkta rites written each for one deity. **None of these files supersedes another.**

**It is written for no one goddess.** Every offering reads `ओं श्री ______ नमः`, with the name left blank, and the closing dedication has one blank, for `भगवती सर्वात्मिका`. The verses of pardon and of circumambulation address the deity in the feminine (`देवी`, `परमेश्वरी`, `महेश्वरी`, `सुरेश्वरी`), and the royal services are offered as `राज्ञीयोपचारान् देव्योपचारान्`, the services due to a queen and to a goddess. The blanks are kept as printed; nothing was filled in.

**The bath in the five nectars is set out in full**, each substance with its own Vedic mantra: `आप्यायस्व` for milk, `दधिक्राव्णो अकारिषम्` for curd, `शुक्रमसि ज्योतिरसि` for ghee, the three `मधु वाता` verses for honey, `स्वादुः पवस्व` for sugar, `याः फलिनीः` for the water of fruits, and `आपो हि ष्ठा` at the plain bath. The same mantras stand as a rite of their own in `50_panchamrita_abhisheka_mantrah.txt`. **At the sandal-paste the page adds a second offering**, of turmeric, kuṅkuma, collyrium, musk and gorocanā, which `48` does not have.

What it does not have: **no pūrvāṅga, no saṅkalpa and no dhyāna verse of its own.** Where the meditation stands, the page prints only `{ध्यानश्लोकाः}`, a place for the goddess's own meditation verses; that marker is described in unit 1's `vidhi:` and not printed as text. Perform `03_purvanga_vidhanam.txt` first. It has no prāṇapratiṣṭhā, no aṅga-pūjā and no aṣṭottara.

Blurb: This is a worship in sixteen services built on the Śrī Sūkta, the Vedic hymn to the goddess of fortune. At every service the worshipper first recites one verse of the hymn, so that the whole hymn is recited in order across the worship. The rite is not written for one goddess. Her name is said at each offering, and the closing prayer names the goddess. The bath is given in full: milk, curd, ghee, honey and sugar are each poured with their own Vedic mantra, then the water of fruits, then pure water. The worship ends with the mantra-flower, circumambulation, the royal services, a prayer for pardon, and the receiving of the water and the prasāda. The opening portion is not included, and is performed first.
