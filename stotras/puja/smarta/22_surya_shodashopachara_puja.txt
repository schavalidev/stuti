Recension / paddhati: **Smārta, South Indian.** The Vaiṣṇava form of the opening is kept separately at `../vaishnava/01_purvanga_vidhanam.txt` and **the two must never be merged, harmonised or collated against each other.**

**This is a second recension of the Sūrya pūjā, beside `11_surya_nitya_puja.txt`.** Both are smārta sixteen-service rites for the same deity, from two different publishers, and **neither file is to be corrected from the other.** Of the 286 lines of this page, **183 do not stand in `11` in any form** — 103 do. That is the closest of the five pairs written on 2026-09-12, and the reason is that **both rites carry the dvādaśārghya**, the twelve offerings of water to the twelve Ādityas, which is a long passage and is much the same in both.

**This rite is built on the Puruṣa Sūkta, and `11` is not.** The page's second saṅkalpa says the worship is performed `पुरुषसूक्त विधानेन`, its closing dedication says so again, and the claim holds: the sixteen services are headed by the ṛcs of the Puruṣa Sūkta in the hymn's own order, one to each service, from `सहस्रशीर्षा पुरुषः` at the invocation to `नाभ्या आसीदन्तरिक्षम्` at the betel. **The ṛc at the waving of the light is the one that names him of the colour of the sun, beyond the darkness** — the fittest verse of the hymn for this deity, and the page puts it there.

This is the third such plan in the folder, and the three should be read together: `17_shiva_shodashopachara_puja.txt` is built on the Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad, `18_mahalakshmi_visesha_shodashopachara_puja.txt` and `19_durga_shodashopachara_puja.txt` on the Śrī Sūkta, and this one on the Puruṣa Sūkta.

**What else this rite has that `11` does not:** a **prāṇapratiṣṭhā**; a **meditation in three verses**, in which the Lord within the disc of the sun is Nārāyaṇa himself with conch and discus; a **second verse at the bath in the five nectars** naming each substance in turn, down to coconut water and the juice of the sugar-cane; and the **worship with the twelve names** as a service of its own, before the hundred and eight.

**Its second resolution is the most detailed in this folder, and it names the illnesses by name** — fever, wasting, jaundice, skin disease, colic, dysentery, the wasting of the tissues, sores, urinary disease and fistula. The salutation with eight limbs at the close asks the sun in so many words to take away the disease of the heart. This is a rite resolved upon for health.

What it does not have: **no directions at the top.** Like the Sarasvatī page, this one prints no line sending the worshipper to the pūrvāṅga; it begins at the second resolution. Perform `03_purvanga_vidhanam.txt` before it.

**What that collation showed.** The ṛcs agree throughout. The differences found are of four kinds, and none of them is a difference of reading:

1. **The visarga written as an ASCII colon** — a defect of this page's typesetting, corrected here; see below. It accounts for most of the apparent mismatches.
2. **Whether the doubled avagraha is written**: this page prints `वसन्तो अस्यासीदाज्यम्` and `सप्तास्यासन्परिधयः` where the Puruṣa Sūktam file prints `अस्याऽऽसीत्` and `सप्तास्याऽऽसन्`. The sound is the same; only the mark differs.
3. **How the sandhi at a pāda boundary is printed**: `ग्रीष्म इध्मश्शरद्धविः` here against `ग्रीष्म इध्मः शरद्धविः` there; `यज्ञात्सर्वहुतः` here against `यज्ञात् सर्वहुतः` there; `सम्भृतं` here against `संभृतं` there. These are printing habits, not variants.
4. **One genuine difference of accent**: this page reads **`य॒दन्ने॑नाति॒रोह॑ति`** with the anudātta on the first syllable, and the Puruṣa Sūktam file reads **`यदन्ने॑नाति॒रोह॑ति`** without it. Both are recorded and **neither is corrected from the other.** Nothing was moved between the files.

The **Sūrya Gāyatrī** at the mantra-flower (unit 28) was collated separately against the **Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad** (sa.wikisource.org, the text of Taittirīya Āraṇyaka prapāṭhaka 10), where it stands in the garland of Gāyatrīs. The Upaniṣad reads `तन्नो आदित्य्यः प्रचोदयात्`; this page reads `तन्नो आदित्यः प्रचोदयात्`. Recorded, not corrected.

Defects in the base text, corrected with reasons, because stotranidhi is a convenience source and not a critical edition:

1. **`ध्येयःसदा` → `ध्येयः सदा`** (unit 3). Two words run together with no space between them.
2. **`प्राणप्रतिष्ठ` → `प्राणप्रतिष्ठा`** (unit 2), **`प्रार्थन` → `प्रार्थना`** (unit 33), **`क्षमा प्रार्थन` → `क्षमाप्रार्थना`** (unit 34) and **`समर्पण` → `समर्पणम्`** (unit 35), all in section headings, where the final vowel or the case ending is dropped.
3. **`अकालमृत्यहरणं` → `अकालमृत्युहरणम्`** (unit 36). A dropped `u`. The corrected form stands in eleven other files of this corpus.

Observed and deliberately **not** emended:

- **`मुखौ पूजयामि`** in the worship of the limbs (unit 18). `मुख` is neuter and its dual is `मुखे`; the other rites of this folder print `मुखं`. `11` has a different list of names, so there is no witness to correct this from.
- **`सर्वकामप्रदो भवः`** (unit 17) and **`सर्वसौख्यकरो भवः`** (unit 27), where `भवः` stands for the imperative `भव`.
- **`ताभ्यं स्नानार्थमाहृतम्`** (unit 12), where the form wanted is `ताभिः`, and **`सुगन्धोश्चन्दनादिभिः`** (unit 15), which does not construe. Neither verse has a second witness.
- **`शरणागतवत्सला`** (unit 29) and **`सुप्रीता सुप्रसन्ना वरदा भवन्तु`** (unit 35), where the adjectives are feminine although the deity is masculine. The same reading was left as printed in `16_anjaneya_shodashopachara_puja.txt`, where the other publisher's rite reads the masculine.
- **The zero-width joiner** inside `प॒शूग्‍स्ताग्‍श्च॑क्रे` (unit 15). The source uses it to hold the conjunct together on screen. The Puruṣa Sūktam file writes the same syllables without it. It is kept as the source prints it.
- **`सम्युक्तं`, `सम्युतम्`** for `संयुक्तं`, `संयुतम्`. This publisher writes anusvāra before a semivowel as `म्` throughout. It is an orthographic habit of the edition, not a defect.

**A defect of the source's Veda pages, corrected throughout.** stotranidhi writes final visarga in its accented mantras as the ASCII colon `:` rather than as `ः` (U+0903) — `धीर॑:` for `धीर॑ः`. It looks right on the page and is wrong in the file: it breaks search, breaks collation against any real edition, and transliterates as a colon. Every such colon inside a `deva:` field here has been mapped to `ः`.

Accent: **present on the Vedic material** — the sixteen ṛcs of the Puruṣa Sūkta, the prāṇapratiṣṭhā mantra, the verse `धाता पुरस्तात्` of the Uttaranārāyaṇa, the Gāyatrī, the offerings to the five breaths and the closing śānti. Udātta `॑` (U+0951), anudātta `॒` (U+0952), dīrgha svarita `᳚` (U+1CDA). **The Sūrya Gāyatrī at unit 28 is printed unaccented and is left unaccented.** The paurāṇika verses are unaccented, which is normal and not a defect. **No accent was inferred, derived or reconstructed.**

Blurb: This is the worship of Śrī Sūryanārāyaṇa in sixteen services, offered to him together with Uṣā, Padminī and Chāyā. It is a second and different form of the rite kept at `11_surya_nitya_puja.txt`, published by a different house, and the two should not be mixed. It is a rite resolved upon for health: the second resolution names the illnesses to be removed one by one, and the salutation near the close asks the sun to take away the disease of the heart. The life-breath is placed in the image, and the Lord is meditated upon in three verses — within the disc of the sun as Nārāyaṇa with conch and discus, then as red Aruṇa upon the red lotus, then simply as Ravi drawn by seven horses held with seven reins. Every one of the sixteen services that follow is headed by a verse of the Puruṣa Sūkta, taken in the order of that hymn. His limbs are worshipped under twenty names, and he is then worshipped under the twelve names of the twelve months. At the close, water is offered twelve times over, once to each of those twelve names, in a golden vessel and reddened with saffron.
