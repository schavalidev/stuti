Devanāgarī: श्री शिव षोडशोपचार पूजा
Telugu: శ్రీ శివ షోడశోపచార పూజ

Author: Anonymous (received smārta household rite). No compiler is named and none should be inferred.

Type: vidhi (ritual manual) — not a stotra. Uses the one-extra-field vidhi format documented in `../../vidhi/madhyandina/01_sandhyopasana_vidhi.txt`: a `vidhi:` field carrying the ritual instruction, then deva / iast / en / tel / hi.

Recension / paddhati: **Smārta, South Indian.** The Vaiṣṇava form of the opening is kept separately at `../vaishnava/01_purvanga_vidhanam.txt` and **the two must never be merged, harmonised or collated against each other.**

**This is a second recension of the Śiva pūjā, beside `05_shiva_nitya_puja.txt`.** Both are smārta sixteen-service rites for the same deity, from two different publishers, and **neither file is to be corrected from the other.** `05` itself recorded that this second recension existed and queued it as a file of its own.

**The two are far apart.** Of the 256 lines of this page, 214 do not stand in `05` in any form. That is a much greater distance than between the two Hanumān recensions at `12` and `16`, and it comes from one structural difference:

**This rite is built on the Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad, and `05` is not.** Every one of the sixteen services here is headed by a short Vedic mantra printed in round brackets, and those mantras are anuvākas 17 to 22 of the Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad (Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10.43 to 10.48), taken in the order the Āraṇyaka has them:

- **Anuvāka 17**, the Sadyojāta formula, is divided across the first five services — invocation, seat, water for the feet, water for the hands, water for sipping.
- **Anuvāka 18**, the eleven names from `वामदेवाय` to `मनोन्मनाय`, gives one name to each of the next eleven services — bath, garment, sacred thread, ornament, sandal-paste, unbroken rice, flowers, incense, lamp, food-offering and betel.
- **Anuvāka 19** (`अघोरेभ्यः`) is the waving of the light, **anuvāka 20** (`तत्पुरुषाय विद्महे`) the mantra-flower, **anuvāka 21** (`ईशानः सर्वविद्यानाम्`) the circumambulation, and **anuvāka 22** (`नमो हिरण्यबाहवे`) the prayer.

**Three more things this rite has that `05` does not:** worship of the liṅga under the twenty-two names of anuvāka 16 of the same Upaniṣad; worship and then the offering of water to the **eight forms of Rudra each with his consort**, a service `05` lacks entirely; and the **asking of blessings** from Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa 3.5.10.4, which the source cites by name. It also worships Śiva **in a liṅga** rather than in an image, and it worships him as **Umāmaheśvara**, with Umā beside him throughout, where `05` addresses `ॐ नमः शिवाय`.

What it does not have: **no pūrvāṅga of its own.** The page opens with three directions, sending the worshipper to the pūrvāṅga, to the short Mahāgaṇapati pūjā and to the short nyāsa. Use `03_purvanga_vidhanam.txt` and `15_mahaganapati_shodashopachara_puja.txt` before this file.

Source / recension: base text `https://stotranidhi.com/hi/sri-shiva-shodasopachara-puja-in-sanskrit/` (fetched 2026-09-12), a Devanāgarī page carrying accent marks on its Vedic mantras.

Independent collation: against the **Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad itself** (sa.wikisource.org, `महानारायणोपनिषत्`, the text of Taittirīya Āraṇyaka prapāṭhaka 10), which is a genuinely different editorial lineage and the source from which the page's own Vedic material comes. All six anuvākas the page uses were located and compared word by word. The page's claim on its own authority — that the rite follows the Sadyojāta rule — was checked against the Āraṇyaka and it holds. Two further passages were checked against files of this corpus written from other sources: the Kātyāyanī Gāyatrī at unit 34 stands in `../../veda/taittiriya/06_gayatri_mala_mantrah.txt`, and the Puruṣa Sūkta to which unit 17 sends the worshipper stands at `../../veda/taittiriya/01_purusha_suktam.txt`.

**Gītā Press, Gorakhpur, is the authority where it has published the text**, and its *Nitya Karma Pūjā Prakāśa* gives Śiva-pūjā as the worked example of its `देवी-देव-पूजाका विधान` chapter. That chapter was compared while `05` was written and is a **different regional rite**: it expands the bath into eight separate abhiṣekas, uses different upacāra verses, and addresses `श्रीभगवते साम्बशिवाय नमः`. Nothing has been imported from it here, and nothing here is corrected from it.

**Where the page and the Āraṇyaka differ, and what was done:**

1. **The pādya and arghya mantras divide one sentence of the Āraṇyaka in two.** Anuvāka 17 reads `भवे भवे नातिभवे भवस्व माम्`. The page gives `भवे भ॑वे॒न` to the water for the feet and `अति॑ भवे भवस्व॒मां` to the water for the hands, so the word `नातिभवे` is cut between the two services. This is a **division of the mantra for ritual use**, not a corruption, and it is kept exactly as the page prints it. A reciter should know that `भवे भवेन` on its own is not the Āraṇyaka's reading.
2. **`बल॑ प्रमथनाय॒ नमः` at the lamp (unit 29); the Āraṇyaka reads `बलप्रमथाय नमः`.** Both forms are printed in editions of this passage. The page's reading is kept as it stands and the other is recorded here rather than set in brackets in the line, because the page's form carries accent and the Āraṇyaka's does not, and a bracket would leave the reader unable to tell which marking belonged to which reading.
3. **`सर्वे᳚भ्यः सर्व॒शर्वे᳚भ्यो` at the waving of the light (units 33 and 38); the Āraṇyaka reads `सर्वतः शर्व सर्वेभ्यो`.** A real difference of reading in anuvāka 19. Both are recorded and neither is corrected from the other.
4. **`आत्माय॒ नमः` in the liṅga names (unit 26)** stands where the grammar would want `आत्मने`. The Āraṇyaka prints `आत्माय` as well, so it is the received reading of the passage and is **not** emended.

Defects in the base text, corrected with reasons, because stotranidhi is a convenience source and not a critical edition:

1. **`ब्रहाण्डाय` → `ब्रह्माण्डाय`** (unit 24). A dropped `म्`. The word is `ब्रह्माण्ड`, the egg of Brahmā, that is, the universe; `ब्रहाण्ड` is not a word.
2. **`चमक म्` → `चमकम्`** (unit 17). A space inside the word, plainly a typesetting slip.

Observed and deliberately **not** emended:

- **`हेरम्ब`** in the nīrājana verse (unit 33). `हेरम्ब` is a name of Gaṇapati, not of Śiva; the verse has been carried over from the Gaṇapati pūjā unchanged. `05_shiva_nitya_puja.txt`, from a different publisher, prints the same word in the same verse, so the reading is received in both lineages and is not ours to repair.
- **`नमस्तेस्तु`** for `नमस्तेऽस्तु` (unit 13) and **`अक्षतोसि`** for `अक्षतोऽसि` (unit 22). These are the common printing habit of leaving the avagraha unwritten. `05` prints `नमस्तेस्तु` likewise.
- **`जङ्घौ`** in the worship of the limbs (unit 24). `जङ्घा` is feminine and its dual is `जङ्घे`. No second witness prints this list at all — `05` has a different list of fifteen names — so there is nothing to correct it from, and it stands as printed.
- **`सम्युतम्`** for `संयुतम्` (unit 33). This publisher writes anusvāra before a semivowel as `म्` throughout. It is an orthographic habit of the edition, not a defect.
- The blank `___` in the food-offering (unit 30). That is the source's own slot, to be filled with the name of whatever food is offered. It is reproduced as a slot and has not been filled in with any dish.

Accent: **present throughout the Vedic material**, and heavily — udātta `॑` (U+0951), anudātta `॒` (U+0952), dīrgha svarita `᳚` (U+1CDA). The accented mantras are Taittirīya, which is the tradition this page carries, so nothing has been brought across a śākhā boundary. The paurāṇika upacāra verses are printed unaccented, which is normal and not a defect. **No accent was inferred, derived or reconstructed.**

**A defect of the source's Veda pages, corrected throughout.** stotranidhi writes final visarga in its accented mantras as the ASCII colon `:` rather than as `ः` (U+0903) — `चक्षु॒:` for `चक्षु॒ः`. It looks right on the page and is wrong in the file: it breaks search, breaks collation against any real edition, and transliterates as a colon. Every such colon inside a `deva:` field here has been mapped to `ः` and the affected `iast:` fields regenerated.

**Never copy stotranidhi's IAST.** Its pages mark anusvāra `ṃ` and avagraha `’` where this corpus uses `ṁ` and `'`. Every `iast:` field here was generated from the Devanāgarī by `../../bin/dev2iast.py`.

Unit count: 44 units. Not a verse count in the stotra sense — the work is a prose order of service punctuated by mantras, and the numbering is this corpus's, not the source's.

Blurb: This is the pūjā of Śiva in sixteen services, worshipped in a liṅga as Umāmaheśvara, with Umā beside him. It is a second and different form of the rite kept at `05_shiva_nitya_puja.txt`, published by a different house, and the two should not be mixed. This page has no opening portion of its own. The worshipper first performs the pūrvāṅga, the short Gaṇapati pūjā and the short nyāsa, to which the page sends him. Śiva is then invoked into the liṅga, the life-breath is placed, and he is meditated upon as Mṛtyuñjaya, who conquers death. What makes this rite different is that every one of the sixteen services is headed by a mantra from the Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad, taken in the order of that text. The liṅga is worshipped under twenty-two names from the same Upaniṣad. The eight forms of Rudra are then worshipped, each with his consort, and water is offered to each of them. The worshipper stands before the Lord, repeats the Rudra Gāyatrī according to his ability, and asks for long life, good children and a divine abode in the words of the Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa. The rite closes with the royal services, the asking of pardon, and the receiving of the water from Śiva's feet.
