Recension / paddhati: **Smārta, South Indian.** This is important and is not a formality. Pūjā-vidhāna divides by *paddhati* the way sandhyāvandana divides by śākhā, and the divisions are the practice of different communities rather than variant readings of one text. The Vaiṣṇava, that is Śrīvaiṣṇava, form is a genuinely different rite. It is kept separately at `../../vaishnava/01_purvanga_vidhanam.txt`. There Viṣvaksena, and not Gaṇapati, is the remover of obstacles. The ācamana opens on a different triad of names. There is a four-syllable āsana (अं अनन्तासनाय, रं कूर्मासनाय, विं विमलासनाय, पं पद्मासनाय) with no smārta counterpart. And the saṅkalpa locates the worshipper at Śrīraṅga, where this one says Śrīśaila. **The two must never be merged, harmonised or collated against each other.** A user who follows the Vaiṣṇava rite should not use this file.

Blurb: The Vedic mantras are recited only by one for whom the upanayana saṁskāra has been performed. This form of the rite leaves them out, and where the rite needs a verse in their place, the Paurāṇika verse is given. The worship so performed is complete. The form with the Vedic mantras is kept separately, for those who have had the upanayana. It is a complete order of service. The worshipper first gathers the materials. He then performs the opening acts: purification, prayer, ācamana, the lighting of the lamp, the dispelling of the bhūtas, prāṇāyāma and the saṅkalpa. He then worships the water-vessel, the conch and the bell. He worships Gaṇapati first, so that obstacles may be removed. Only then does he perform the sixteen services, the ṣoḍaśopacāra, for the chosen deity. These run from the invocation and the seat, through water for the feet, the bath, garments, sandal paste, flowers, incense, the lamp, food and betel, to the waving of the lamp. At the end he asks pardon for what was done wrongly or left undone, takes leave of the deity, and receives the prasāda. The file is a template: the name of the chosen deity is left to be filled in.

Whatever I do with body, speech, mind or senses, with intellect or self or by the nature of my own disposition, all of it I offer to the supreme Nārāyaṇa. By this sixteen-fold worship beginning with meditation and invocation, may my chosen deity be well pleased, gracious, and a giver of boons.

--- unit 52 | section: उद्वासनम् ---
vidhi:
Dismissal, and the taking of the prasāda. The rite closes with the threefold peace and the surrender of the act to Brahman.
deva:
उद्वासनम् — ॐ श्री इष्ट देवतायै नमः । यथास्थानं उद्वासयामि । शोभनार्थे क्षेमाय पुनरागमनाय च ॥
प्रसाद ग्रहणम् — अकालमृत्युहरणं सर्वव्याधिनिवारणम् ।
समस्तपापक्षयकरं देवता पादोदकं शुभम् ॥
श्री इष्ट देवता प्रसादं शिरसा गृह्णामि ॥
ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः ॥ ॐ तत्सत् ब्रह्मार्पणमस्तु ॥
iast:

prasāda grahaṇam — akālamṛtyuharaṇaṁ sarvavyādhinivāraṇam |
samastapāpakṣayakaraṁ devatā pādodakaṁ śubham ||
śrī iṣṭa devatā prasādaṁ śirasā gṛhṇāmi ||

en: Dismissal — salutation to my chosen deity. I dismiss you to your own place, for beauty, for well-being, and for your coming again. The taking of the prasāda — the water from the deity's feet is auspicious: it takes away untimely death, wards off every disease, and destroys all sin. I receive the prasāda of my chosen deity upon my head. Oṁ, peace, peace, peace. Oṁ, that is the truth: let this be an offering to Brahman.
