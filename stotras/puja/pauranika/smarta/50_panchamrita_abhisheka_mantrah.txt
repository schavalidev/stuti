Title: Pañcāmṛta Abhiṣeka Mantrāḥ — Paurāṇika Form, without Vedic Mantras

Devanāgarī: पञ्चामृत अभिषेक मन्त्राः — पौराणिक पद्धतिः

Telugu: పంచామృత అభిషేక మంత్రాః — పౌరాణిక పద్ధతి

Author: Anonymous. The verses are those of the received Paurāṇika order of worship.

Type: vidhi (ritual manual) — not a stotra. The one-extra-field vidhi format: a `vidhi:` field carrying the ritual instruction, then deva / iast / en / tel / hi.

Recension / paddhati: **Smārta, South Indian.** The Vaiṣṇava form of the opening is kept separately at `../../vaishnava/01_purvanga_vidhanam.txt` and **the two must never be merged, harmonised or collated against each other.**

Note: **This is the Vedic-removed form, for a reciter for whom the upanayana saṁskāra has not been performed. The Vedic form is `../../smarta/50_panchamrita_abhisheka_mantrah.txt`, kept unchanged for the initiated. Neither file supersedes the other, and neither is ever to be deleted in favour of the other** (user, 2026-09-17). Built on 2026-09-17 from the same declared unit plan as the Vedic form; not one Sanskrit word was composed. All seven Vedic mantras were removed with their translations. What is left of the page is its headings and its name-offerings.

**The rite is almost wholly Vedic, so the question was whether anything meaningful remains.** It does, because Gītā Press prints its own Paurāṇika verse for each of the same baths. 6 of the seven units take that verse: milk, curd, ghee, honey, sugar and pure water. **The water of fruits has no verse in Gītā Press** and is offered with the name alone. The line offering the whole bath in the five nectars had no mantra on the page either, and stays as it was; Gītā Press's `पञ्चामृतस्नान` verse was not added there, because nothing was removed from that line.

**The gender of the verses is a decision for review.** Gītā Press gives these verses in a masculine set, worked as a Śiva-pūjā, and in a feminine set for Durgā. This page names no deity. The masculine set was used, and the only word it affects is `देव` in the curd verse (unit 2), for which a worshipper of a goddess says `देवि`, as Gītā Press's Durgā-pūjā does. The two sets also differ in the wording of the honey and pure-water verses; the masculine set's wording was kept throughout.

Each was lifted from the archive's OCR of that volume and checked against the page image; the OCR matched the page in five, and in the pure-water verse it ran the heading into the text and read `गृह्याताम्`, both corrected from the image. `॥` is written for Gītā Press's `।।`. Offering with the name alone where no verse is given is the rule of the same section: `केवल नाममन्त्रसे … 'नैवेद्य' आदि चढ़ाना चाहिये`.

Blurb: The Vedic mantras are recited only by one for whom the upanayana saṁskāra has been performed. This form of the rite leaves them out, and where the rite needs a verse in their place, the Paurāṇika verse is given. The worship so performed is complete. The form with the Vedic mantras is kept separately, for those who have had the upanayana. This is the bath of a deity in the five nectars. Milk, curd, ghee, honey and sugar are poured one after another, each with its own verse, then the water of fruits, and last pure water. The name of the deity is said at each offering. The verses are used at the bath within a larger worship.
