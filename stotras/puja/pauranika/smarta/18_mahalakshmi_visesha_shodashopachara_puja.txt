Blurb: The Vedic mantras are recited only by one for whom the upanayana saṁskāra has been performed. This form of the rite leaves them out, and where the rite needs a verse in their place, the Paurāṇika verse is given. The worship so performed is complete. The form with the Vedic mantras is kept separately, for those who have had the upanayana. This is the worship of Śrī Mahālakṣmī in sixteen services. The goddess is meditated upon as she who is seated on the lotus and bathed by the elephants of heaven from golden vessels. Besides the usual services she is offered red sandal-paste, vermilion, saffron and scented oil. The eight attainments are then worshipped in the eight directions, and after them the eight Lakṣmīs. The rite ends with a portion for Dīpāvalī: the lamp is itself worshipped as a form of Brahman, the row of lamps is offered, and the worshipper prays through eleven verses for wealth, grain, long life and children, and asks the night of ease to carry his misfortune away.

The prayer for the night of Dīpāvalī, verses seven to eleven. Here the worshipper asks plainly for what he needs.
deva:
शङ्खचक्रगदाहस्ते शुभ्रवर्णे शुभानने ।
