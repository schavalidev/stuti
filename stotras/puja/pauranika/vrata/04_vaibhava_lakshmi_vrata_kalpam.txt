Blurb: The Vedic mantras are recited only by one for whom the upanayana saṁskāra has been performed. This form of the rite leaves them out, and where the rite needs a verse in their place, the Paurāṇika verse is given. The worship so performed is complete. The form with the Vedic mantras is kept separately, for those who have had the upanayana. This is the vrata of Vaibhavalakṣmī, the Lakṣmī who gives prosperity. It is kept in the evening, after the sun has set. It is not fixed to any month. The worshipper resolves upon a number of weeks — four, or nine, or eleven, or twenty-one — and keeps the vrata on that many Thursdays or Fridays. She fasts until the evening. Then she spreads a new cloth in that corner, pours rice upon it, and sets a pot on the rice. She fills the pot with water, drops in coins according to her means, adds the leaves of fruit trees, and places a coconut on top. Vaibhavalakṣmī is invited into that pot, and the sixteen services are offered to her. Her limbs are then worshipped by ten names, from the feet upward to the head, and the hundred and eight names of Lakṣmī are recited. Sweet food is offered. The story is told after the worship. In it Parameśvara tells Pārvatī why wealth comes to some and not to others: of Bhṛgu, whose anger drove Lakṣmī from Vaikuṇṭha into the ocean of milk; of Suśarma, a learned and poor man whose want came from the deeds of an earlier birth; and of four sisters whose husbands grew proud and said their wealth was their own doing, and lost it. An old woman came to the sisters and taught them this vrata, and their prosperity returned. On the last Friday the udyāpana is spoken. The vāyana and the tāmbūla are given to at least five married women.

--- unit 33 | section: व्रतकथा ---
vidhi:
The story begins. Parameśvara tells it to Pārvatī on Kailāsa.
deva:
व्रतकथा प्रारम्भः
iast:
vratakathā prārambhaḥ
en: Long ago on Kailāsa, Pārvatī Devī was speaking kindly with Parameśvara. She said, "Lord of my life, in the world of men all people suffer great hardship to earn wealth. She is the deity of all wealth and prosperity. Those who despise wealth do not obtain it." Hearing this, Pārvatī Devī prayed, "Tell me more about this Vaibhavalakṣmī." Then Parameśvara spoke as follows.

--- unit 34 | section: व्रतकथा ---
vidhi:
Bhṛgu's austerity, and the birth of Lakṣmī as his daughter.
deva:
व्रतकथा प्रारम्भः
iast:
vratakathā prārambhaḥ
en: Once the great sage Bhṛgu performed austerity for the sake of the supreme Śakti. Pleased with his devotion, she appeared before him and told him to ask for what he wished. Bhṛgu prayed, "Mother, be born as my daughter in that form of yours which brings prosperity, and give me your grace." The supreme Śakti was pleased with his devotion and faith, granted the boon with the word "So be it," and disappeared. Thus the wealth-giving portion of the supreme Śakti was born as the daughter of the great sage Bhṛgu. Bhṛgu was glad with the love of a father for his child, and gave her in marriage to Śrī Mahāviṣṇu. By the command of Śrī Hari she gave prosperity to all the gods, and was worshipped as Vaikuṇṭhalakṣmī.
tel: ఒకానొకప్పుడు భృగు మహర్షి ఆ పరాశక్తిని గూర్చి తపస్సు చేయుచుండగా, ఆ భక్తికి మెచ్చి పరాశక్తి ప్రత్యక్షమై ఏమి కావలెనో కోరుకొమ్మని చెప్పినది. భృగు మహర్షి, "తల్లీ, వైభవము కలిగించు నీ రూపముతో నా కుమార్తెగా జన్మించి నన్ను అనుగ్రహింపవలసినది" అని ప్రార్థింపగా, ఆ పరాశక్తి ఆతని భక్తిశ్రద్ధలకు మెచ్చి "తథాస్తు" అని వరమిచ్చి అంతర్ధానమాయెను. అందువలన పరాశక్తి యొక్క సంపత్కర అంశము భృగు మహర్షి కుమార్తెగా అవతరించినది. పుత్రికా వాత్సల్యముతో సంతోషించిన భృగువు ఆమెను శ్రీమహావిష్ణువునకు ఇచ్చి వివాహము చేసెను. ఆ శ్రీహరి ఆనతి మేరకు ఆమె దేవతలందరికి వైభవమును ప్రసాదించి వైకుంఠలక్ష్మిగా ఆరాధింపబడుచుండెను.

--- unit 35 | section: व्रतकथा ---
vidhi:
Bhṛgu's journey through the three worlds, and why Lakṣmī left Vaikuṇṭha.
deva:
व्रतकथा प्रारम्भः
iast:
vratakathā prārambhaḥ
en: After this, the sages of the world of men wished to perform a great sacrifice. They asked Bhṛgu to decide to whom the oblation should be given. Bhṛgu agreed and set out through the three worlds. In the world of Brahmā he saw Brahmā speaking kindly with Sarasvatī. Angry that his coming was not honoured, he went on to Kailāsa. There too Maheśvara was deep in meditation and did not notice his coming. With still greater anger Bhṛgu left that place. At last he reached Vaikuṇṭha, and seeing Śrī Mahāviṣṇu at rest there, he was overcome with anger that his coming was not honoured, and he struck the breast of Viṣṇu with his foot. Viṣṇu calmed the anger of Bhṛgu and sent him away. Śrī Mahālakṣmī saw this. Her own dwelling is the breast of her husband, and he had said nothing when it was struck with a foot. In her displeasure she left Vaikuṇṭha and went into the ocean of milk. With this the prosperity of Indra and all the gods declined. They all went to Śrī Mahāviṣṇu and prayed to him, and he commanded them to churn the ocean of milk. While they were churning it with the mountain Mandhara, Śrī Mahālakṣmī chose to favour them. en: In the same way, wealth comes to people also from the deeds of former births. Hear the story of Suśarma as an example of this. In a certain village there lived Suśarma, a man learned in all the śāstras. Through his gurukula he taught the śāstras to the townspeople, to kings and to wealthy men. Yet there was poverty in his house. Seeing this, Suśānta, the wife of Suśarma, said, "Husband, among the pupils you have taught there are some who are wealthy. Go to them and bring some money for the needs of our house." Suśarma replied, "It is not proper to beg, and it is not good to set one's hope upon wealth. Still, since the wealth we need can be given by Śrī Mahālakṣmī, who is prosperity itself, I shall perform a sacrifice for her." So saying, he began the sacrifice. Śrī Mahālakṣmī was pleased with the sacrifice Suśarma performed. She appeared before him, granted the boon, "Tonight I shall rain gold coins in your house," and disappeared. Suśarma told this joyfully to Suśānta, and they kept watch through the whole night. So they watched until dawn came. When Suśarma understood that no rain of wealth had come, he grieved greatly that he had kept his wife in poverty, and set out from the house intending to fast until death. Outside the house he saw a woman in soiled clothes, without brightness. Suśarma asked who she was, and the woman said, "I came, pleased with your sacrifice, to rain gold in your house. But Jyeṣṭhādevī, who is in your house, stopped me. She said that because you did no meritorious deeds and no acts of giving in your former births, wealth does not come to you in this birth." Hearing this, Suśarma understood that if he were not there, wealth would come to his wife and children, and he took the life of a renunciant. He prayed to Śrī Mahālakṣmī to show compassion in return. She was pleased with his devotion, and Śrī Vaibhavalakṣmī granted prosperity to that house.

en: The four sisters grieved for the fallen state of their husbands and for their own poverty. Seeing them, Śrī Mahālakṣmī felt compassion and chose to favour them. Hear the manner of it. Keep the vrata on four, or nine, or eleven, or twenty-one Thursdays or Fridays. On the last Friday speak the udyāpana, and give the vāyana and the tāmbūla to at least five married women whose husbands are living. The vrata is to be begun in the evening, after the setting of the sun. On the day of the vrata rise in the morning, finish what is to be done, bathe, clean the north-east corner of the house, smear it with cow dung, draw the muggu, and prepare the place of worship. Fast until evening. In the north-east spread a new blouse cloth, pour rice upon it, set the pot upon the rice, pour water into it, put in coins according to your means, put in the leaves of fruit trees, place a coconut on top, and establish the pot. Invite Śrī Vaibhavalakṣmī into that pot, worship her, and offer sweet foods. On the day the udyāpana is spoken, after the worship is done, pour the water of that pot at the root of the nīm, the tulasī or another sacred tree. Those who wish for wealth should take a little of that water as tīrtha. Keep the coin from the pot safely in the house. Give the rice from under the pot to the birds." Having told the manner of the vrata, she went away.
