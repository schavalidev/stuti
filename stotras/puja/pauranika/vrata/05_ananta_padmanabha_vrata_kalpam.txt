Blurb: The Vedic mantras are recited only by one for whom the upanayana saṁskāra has been performed. This form of the rite leaves them out, and where the rite needs a verse in their place, the Paurāṇika verse is given. The worship so performed is complete. The form with the Vedic mantras is kept separately, for those who have had the upanayana. This is the vrata of Ananta Padmanābha, kept on the fourteenth tithi of the bright fortnight of Bhādrapada. It is kept for fourteen years, and the fruit stated for it is the going away of poverty, the increase of sons and grandsons, and happiness in this world. The worship of Yamunā Devī is done first, as a limb of the rite. A pure place is smeared with cow dung and a maṇḍala of eight petals is made on it. A pot of water is set on the south side. In the middle the image of the Lord is made of darbha grass, and he is invited into it as one who dwells in the white island, with seven hoods, tawny eyes and four arms, bearing the conch, the discus and the mace. The sixteen services are offered to him, and his limbs are worshipped by seventeen names from the feet upward to the head. The central act of this vrata is the toram. It is a strong thread wetted with kuṅkuma, and fourteen knots are tied in it — fourteen for the fourteen years the story gives, and for the fourteenth tithi on which the day falls. The toram is set up before the Lord early, before the services begin, which most vratas do not do. Each of its knots is then worshipped by one name, and the thread is bound on the right hand. Twenty-eight cakes are made, half are given away and half eaten, and all the materials of the worship are counted in fourteens. The story is told last. In it Kṛṣṇa tells Dharmarāja of Śīlā, the daughter of Sumanta, who learned the vrata from the women at the lake on her wedding journey and kept it. Her husband Kauṇḍinya tore off her toram in anger and threw it into the fire, and everything was lost. He wandered in the forest asking the tree, the cow, the bull, the lakes, the donkey and the elephant for Ananta, and none of them knew him.

en: "Dharmarāja, listen. In the ages gone by there was a brāhmaṇa named Sumanta, born in the Vasiṣṭha line and full of the learning of the Veda and the śāstras. His wife was Dīkṣādevī, the daughter of the great sage Bhṛgu. After they had lived together a long time, Dīkṣādevī conceived and bore a daughter of good qualities. The girl was given the name Śīlā.
After some days Dīkṣādevī died of a burning fever. Sumanta then feared that his Vedic duties would fall away, and married a girl named Karkaśā. That Karkaśā was very hard of heart, quarrelsome, and given to strife. Meanwhile Śīlā, the daughter of the first wife, grew up in her father's house. She drew pictures in bright colours on the walls and the doorways, and made designs like conches and lotuses in the hall and elsewhere, and she was devoted to God.
When the time came for Śīlā to be married, Sumanta set about arranging it. Just then the great sage Kauṇḍinya, who had performed austerity for some days and now wished to marry, was travelling through many lands and came to Sumanta's house. Sumanta honoured Kauṇḍinya with arghya and pādya, and on an auspicious day gave him his daughter Śīlā in marriage. After the marriage Sumanta wished to give his son-in-law some gift. He went to his wife Karkaśā and said, "Dear one! We must give our son-in-law some gift. What may we give?" At once Karkaśā got up, went inside, bolted the door, and said there was nothing here, and that he should go away. Sumanta grieved much. He thought it was not right to send them off without even provision for the road, so he gave them the parched flour that had been left over from the wedding, and sent his daughter away with her husband. Kauṇḍinya set out for his hermitage in a cart with his wife, who kept to good conduct. As it was noon, he got down from the cart and went to a lake to perform the sandhyā and the other rites. That day was the Ananta Padmanābha caturdaśī. In one place there many women, wearing red garments and full of devotion, were worshipping Ananta Padmanābha Svāmi, each apart. Śīlā, the wife of Kauṇḍinya, saw this. She went quietly to those women and prayed to them: "Noble women! Which god are you worshipping? What is the name of this vrata? Tell me all of it." And those faithful wives said this."

The *Āru Vratālu* print gives the same rule in its Sanskrit verses, and the two agree, including that of the twenty-eight cakes half are given away and half eaten.

en: "Good woman, we shall tell you. Listen. This is the vrata of Ananta Padmanābha Svāmi. He who keeps this vrata obtains fruit without end. On the fourteenth tithi of the bright fortnight of Bhādrapada, go to the bank of a river, bathe, and put on clean garments. Smear a pure place with cow dung and make on it the maṇḍala called sarvatobhadra, which is like a lotus of eight petals. Adorn it all round with designs of five colours and with white rice flour, and draw many designs. On the south side of that seat place a pot filled with water. In the middle of the seat make with darbha grass the image of Ananta Padmanābha Svāmi, who fills everything, and invite him into it. Meditate upon him as dwelling in the white island, as tawny-eyed, as having seven hoods, and as bearing the conch, the discus and the mace. Perform the sixteen services in the manner the kalpa lays down, walk round him and bow to him. Then take a new toram, tied with fourteen knots and wetted with kuṅkuma, place it near Padmanābha Svāmi and worship it. With five palas of wheat flour make twenty-eight atirasa cakes and offer them as food. Bind on the toram. Give fourteen of the atirasas to the brāhmaṇas along with gifts of pāyasa, and eat the rest yourself. All the materials of the worship should be in fourteens. Then feed the brāhmaṇas, and remain meditating upon Ananta Padmanābha Svāmi. Śīlā, in this way the vrata is to be completed."
When they had said this, Śīlā, the wife of the sage Kauṇḍinya, at once bathed, performed the vrata with the help of those women, and bound on the toram. She gave as vāyana the parched flour that had been brought for the journey, ate of it herself, and was content. Her husband too was satisfied with the meal, and they went up into the cart and returned to the hermitage.

en: By the greatness of the Ananta vrata that Śīlā had kept, the whole hermitage became of gold, and the house was filled with the eight kinds of wealth. One day, as the two were sitting together, Kauṇḍinya of the unhappy mind saw the toram on Śīlā's arm and said, "Lady! You have bound a toram on your arm. For what have you bound it? Is it to bring me under your power, or to bring someone else under your power?" Then Śīlā said, "My lord! I wear Ananta Padmanābha Svāmi. It is by the grace of that god that we have this wealth of money and grain." When she had spoken the truth, Kauṇḍinya grew very angry. His eyes turned red, he abused the god and asked who Ananta was, tore off the toram, and threw it into the blazing fire. Śīlā cried out, ran, took the toram back, dipped it in milk and set it aside.

en: After some days, because Kauṇḍinya had done this wrong, all his wealth was lost. His cattle went to thieves and his house to fire. Whatever was set down anywhere in the house was destroyed on the spot. Wherever he tried to speak, a quarrel arose. Wherever he went, no one would speak to him.
Then Kauṇḍinya, not knowing what to do and afflicted by poverty, entered the thickets of the forest. Tormented by hunger, he remembered Ananta Padmanābha Svāmi and went on, meditating in his mind on how he might see that great god. He came upon a great mango tree full of flowers and fruit, and was amazed to see that not one bird would settle on it. He said to the tree, "Lord of trees! Have you seen the god whose name is Ananta?" The tree said it did not know.
Kauṇḍinya went further and saw a cow with its calf turning this way and that in green grass. He asked, "Kāmadhenu! Have you seen Ananta Padmanābha Svāmi?" It too said it did not know.
Then he went further and saw a bull standing in grass that reached its knees. He asked, "King of bulls! Have you seen Ananta Padmanābha Svāmi?" It said it did not know who Ananta Padmanābha Svāmi was.
Going further still, he came to a place where there were two lakes, beautiful and pleasing, full of waves, covered with lotuses and water lilies of many kinds, and with swans, karaṇḍavas and cakravākas; and the water of one lake was flowing over into the other. He asked, "Lakes of lotuses! Have you seen Ananta Padmanābha Svāmi?" Those pools said they did not know. Kauṇḍinya went further and came to a place where a donkey and an elephant were standing. "Best of brāhmaṇas! In former times a brāhmaṇa learned all the branches of knowledge, but out of pride taught them to no one; therefore he was born as a mango tree in the forest, of use to no one. The close of the story: the fourteen years kept, and the others who kept this vrata.
deva:
