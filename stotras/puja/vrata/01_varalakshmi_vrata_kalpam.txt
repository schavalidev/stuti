Title: Śrī Varalakṣmī Vrata Kalpam — the worship of Varalakṣmī on the Friday before the full moon of Śrāvaṇa (Smārta Paddhati, South Indian)
Devanāgarī: श्री वरलक्ष्मी व्रत कल्पम् — श्रावण शुक्ल पूर्णिमा पूर्व शुक्रवार व्रत विधानम् (स्मार्त पद्धतिः)
Telugu: శ్రీ వరలక్ష్మీ వ్రత కల్పం — శ్రావణ శుక్ల పూర్ణిమకు ముందు వచ్చే శుక్రవారం నాటి వ్రత విధానం (స్మార్త పద్ధతిః)

Author: Traditional. No compiler is named and none should be inferred. The kathā is spoken in the text by Parameśvara to Pārvatī, and reported by Sūta to Śaunaka and the other sages.

Type: vidhi (ritual manual) — not a stotra. It uses the one-extra-field vidhi format of this folder: a `vidhi:` field carrying the instruction, then deva / iast / en / tel / hi.

Recension / paddhati: **Smārta, South Indian (Telugu country).** The rite directs the worshipper to the shared pūrvāṅga and the Gaṇapati pūjā before it begins, and both stand in this folder — `../smarta/03_purvanga_vidhanam.txt` and `../smarta/15_mahaganapati_shodashopachara_puja.txt`. The Śrīvaiṣṇava form of the opening is kept separately at `../vaishnava/01_purvanga_vidhanam.txt`, and **the two must never be merged, harmonised or collated against each other.** Śākhā does not apply to this genre; what divides vrata texts is paddhati and region.

Source / recension: base text `https://stotranidhi.com/sri-varalakshmi-vrata-kalpam-in-telugu/` (fetched 2026-09-12, 164 lines), which is **Telugu script only**. **The Devanāgarī column is therefore transliterated by us**, mechanically, with `bin/tel2dev.py`, and `iast:` from that with `bin/dev2iast.py`; no Sanskrit in this file was typed by hand.

Independent collation: **a printed Telugu edition of a different lineage — *Āru Vratālu*, Paṇḍita Pariṣkṛtamu, Gollapudi Veeraswamy Son, Rajahmundry 1999**, read from the archive.org scan `snmc-aaru-vratalu-pandita-parishkrutamu-telugu-and-sans`, where the Varalakṣmī kalpa runs from line 1494 to line 2020 of the OCR text. This is a genuinely independent witness: a printed book from a different publishing house, not the same site in another script. The whole rite was compared verse by verse. It agrees with the base text on the entire body of the sixteen services, on the toram mantra, on the vāyana verse and on the frame of the story.

Recension note: the two editions are the same rite, and six differences were found. None of them is reconciled by silently choosing one.

1. **They differ in the order of two services.** The base text gives the arghya before the pādya. The *Āru Vratālu* print gives the pādya before the arghya. Both orders are in use and the base text's order is kept here.
2. **They differ in which service stands eleventh.** The base text gives the **māṅgalya**, the marriage thread of gold. The *Āru Vratālu* print gives the **upavīta**, the sacred thread, in that place, with the verse `ఉపవీతమిదం దేవి గృహాణ త్వం శుభప్రదే`. The base text's reading is kept. The difference is a real one and is worth knowing, because a vrata kept by married women is more likely to offer the māṅgalya.
3. **They differ in the number of knots in the toram, and the base text is the one that is internally consistent.** The base text worships **nine** knots and ends with `హరివల్లభాయై నమః నవమ గ్రంథిం పూజయామి`. The *Āru Vratālu* print worships only **eight** and stops at the eighth — yet its own binding mantra, printed immediately below, says `నవసూత్రం`, a thread of nine. The kathā in both editions also says nine. The base text's nine knots are kept. Whether the print omits the ninth line or the scan dropped it could not be settled from the OCR.
4. **They differ by one name in the worship of the limbs.** The base text has fourteen names and includes `ఓం శ్రియై నమః ఓష్ఠౌ పూజయామి`, the worship of the lips. The *Āru Vratālu* print does not have it. The base text's fourteen are kept.
5. **They differ in one word of the vāyana mantra, and this one changes the sense.** The base text reads `ఇందిరా ప్రతిగృహ్ణాతు ఇందిరాయై దదాతి చ` — Indirā receives, and it is given to Indirā. The *Āru Vratālu* print reads `ఇందిరా వై దదాతి చ` — and Indirā indeed gives. **This is not bracketed as a variant, because it is not a matter of spelling; the two readings say different things about who gives and who receives.** The base text's reading stands in the verse and both are recorded here.
6. **The *Āru Vratālu* print carries a kalaśa pūjā which the base text does not print.** Before the sixteen services it establishes the pot with `కుక్షౌ తు సాగరాః సర్వే సప్తద్వీపా వసుంధరా` and `గంగే చ యమునే చైవ గోదావరి సరస్వతి`, which are the standard verses of that rite. The base text leaves the kalaśa to the pūrvāṅga, which this folder also does.

Not transcribed, and said plainly: **the *Āru Vratālu* print carries the story in Sanskrit as well as in Telugu**, marked `అథ కథాప్రారంభః` at line 1823 of its OCR, in the form of a dialogue of Gaurī and Īśvara. **That Sanskrit was not written into this file, because the OCR of those pages is too damaged to transcribe with confidence** — whole lines of the questions are missing, and the readings that survive are broken (`శరణ్యో త్రి జగద్వంద్యే` for `శరణ్యే త్రిజగద్వంద్యే`). **Nothing was reconstructed from memory.** The Sanskrit kathā is a real gap in this file and closing it needs the page images, not the OCR. The story as it stands here is the base text's own Telugu prose, and it sits in the translation fields where the source put it; the `deva:` of those units carries the section heading alone.

Not collated: the hundred and eight names. At its aṣṭottara the rite points at the Lakṣmī nāmāvaḷi rather than printing it. That nāmāvaḷi is in this corpus at `../../devi/lakshmi/36_lakshmi_ashtottarashatanamavali.txt`, and it was not compared against this rite's own list of names.

Accent: **none, and none is expected.** This rite contains no Vedic mantra. Every verse in it is stotra, and stotra is not accent-marked. No accent was added and none was inferred.

Note on defects repaired in the base text, each counted and each confirmed against the *Āru Vratālu* print rather than corrected from judgement. Six in all: `ఓం వరలక్ష్మై నమః` to `ఓం వరలక్ష్మ్యై నమః` and `మహాలక్ష్మై నమః` to `మహాలక్ష్మ్యై నమః`, both missing the `య` of the feminine dative; `నారయణప్రియే` to `నారాయణప్రియే` in the mantrapuṣpa verse, which the same page prints correctly in the meditation nine lines above; `తోరగ్రంధి` to `తోరగ్రంథి`; `ప్రథమ గంథిం` to `ప్రథమ గ్రంథిం`, where the following eight lines all read `గ్రంథిం`; and `స్వశక్తతః` to `స్వశక్తితః` in the vāyana verse. No other character of the base text was altered.

Unit count: 39 units. The rite falls into the resolve, the sixteen services with the worship of the limbs and the hundred and eight names, the worship of the nine knots and the binding of the toram, the vāyana, the story in six parts, and the closing.

Blurb: This is the vrata of Varalakṣmī, kept on the Friday that falls before the full moon of the bright fortnight of Śrāvaṇa. It is kept by women, and the fruit stated for it is a fortunate married life, good children, and the well-being of the whole household. A maṇḍapa is made on ground cleaned with cow dung. A seat is placed on it, new rice is poured on the seat, and a pot is set up with five kinds of fresh shoots. Varalakṣmī is invited into that pot, and the sixteen services are offered to her. Her limbs are then worshipped by fourteen names, from the feet upward to the head, and the hundred and eight names of Lakṣmī are recited. The central act of this vrata is the toram. It is a thread of nine strands with nine knots tied in it. Each knot is worshipped by one name of the goddess, and the thread is then bound on the right hand. Twelve apūpas are given as the vāyana. The story is told last. In it Parameśvara tells Pārvatī of Cārumatī of the city of Kuṇḍina, a woman who served her elders, did the work of her house, and spoke little and kindly. Lakṣmī came to her in a dream and named the day. Cārumatī kept the vrata with the other women of the city, and as they walked around the goddess the ornaments came upon them, once at the feet, once at the hands, and the third time in full.
