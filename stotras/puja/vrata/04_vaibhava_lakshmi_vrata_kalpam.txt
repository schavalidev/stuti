Title: Śrī Vaibhava Lakṣmī Vrata Kalpam — the evening worship of Vaibhavalakṣmī, kept on Thursdays or Fridays and closed with the udyāpana (Smārta Paddhati, South Indian)
Devanāgarī: श्री वैभवलक्ष्मी व्रत कल्पं — गुरुवारमु लेक शुक्रवारमु सायंकालमुन चेयु व्रत विधानं (स्मार्त पद्धतिः)
Telugu: శ్రీ వైభవలక్ష్మీ వ్రత కల్పం — గురువారము లేక శుక్రవారము సాయంకాలమున చేయు వ్రత విధానం (స్మార్త పద్ధతిః)

Author: Traditional. No compiler is named and none should be inferred. The kathā is spoken in the text by Parameśvara to Pārvatī.

Type: vidhi (ritual manual) — not a stotra. It uses the one-extra-field vidhi format of this folder: a `vidhi:` field carrying the instruction, then deva / iast / en / tel / hi.

Recension / paddhati: **Smārta, South Indian (Telugu country).** The rite directs the worshipper to the shared pūrvāṅga and the Gaṇapati pūjā before it begins, and both stand in this folder — `../smarta/03_purvanga_vidhanam.txt` and `../smarta/15_mahaganapati_shodashopachara_puja.txt`. The saṅkalpa states the method of the worship itself: the sixteen services are performed **by the Śrī Sūkta**, one ṛc of the sūkta standing before the verse of each service. Śākhā does not apply to this genre; what divides vrata texts is paddhati and region.

**How this vrata differs from the Mārgaśira Lakṣmīvāra vrata, with which it is often confused.** The Mārgaśira Lakṣmīvāra vrata is a separate work and is not written in this file. It is kept only on the five Lakṣmīvāras — the Thursdays — of the month of Mārgaśira, in that month and no other; it prints no pūjā of its own but sends the worshipper to the Mahālakṣmī viśeṣa ṣoḍaśopacāra pūjā, which stands in this folder at `../smarta/18_mahalakshmi_visesha_shodashopachara_puja.txt`; it names no udyāpana and no vāyana; and its kathā is the story of Suśīla and her stepmother. The Vaibhava Lakṣmī vrata is tied to no month. It is kept on four, nine, eleven or twenty-one Thursdays or Fridays, as the worshipper resolves; it carries its own full ṣoḍaśopacāra pūjā by the Śrī Sūkta; it ends on the last Friday with the udyāpana and with the vāyana given to at least five married women; and its kathā is the story of Bhṛgu, of Suśarma, and of the four sisters. **The two must not be merged or harmonised.**

Source / recension: base text `https://stotranidhi.com/sri-vaibhava-lakshmi-vratham-puja-katha-in-telugu/` (fetched 2026-09-14, 229 lines), which is **Telugu script only**. **The Devanāgarī column is therefore transliterated by us**, mechanically, with `bin/tel2dev.py`, and `iast:` from that with `bin/dev2iast.py`; no Sanskrit in this file was typed by hand.

Independent collation: **a printed Telugu booklet of an entirely different lineage — *Śubhaprada Vaibhavalakṣmī Pūjā Vidhānam*, compiled by Śrīnidhi Koṭṭampalle Prakāśarāo, published by Mahesh Prakashan, Yeshwantpur, Bengaluru, printed at Sri Sathyananda Press, Bangalore**, read from the archive.org scan `vqye-shubhaprada-vaibhava-lakshmi-pooja-vidhana-by-srin` (13,188 Telugu characters of OCR, measured, not estimated). This is a genuinely independent witness: a printed book from another house in another state, not the same site in another script. It was compared verse by verse against the base text. Two further printed witnesses of the northern lineage were read for the date and the descent of the observance and are described below.

Recension note: **the Bengaluru booklet and the base text are not the same rite, though they worship the same goddess under the same name.** The agreements and the differences were both counted.

1. **They agree on the verses they share.** Both carry the Śrī Sūkta ṛc `హిరణ్యవర్ణాం హరిణీం సువర్ణరజతస్రజామ్` and `గంధద్వారాం దురాధర్షాం`; both carry the dhyāna `యా సా పద్మాసనస్థా విపులకటితటీ`; both carry `యాని కాని చ పాపాని జన్మాంతరకృతాని చ`; both close with `యస్య స్మృత్యా చ నామోక్త్యా` and `మంత్రహీనం క్రియాహీనం`. The shared body of the worship is therefore attested twice.
2. **They differ in the whole shape of the rite.** The base text is a full ṣoḍaśopacāra pūjā performed upon a pot, with the sixteen services, the worship of the limbs, the mantrapuṣpa and the vāyana. The Bengaluru booklet is a short evening pūjā performed upon a picture of the goddess and upon a small pot into which one or two gold or silver ornaments have been placed; it has a dhūpa, a dīpa, an ārati sung in Telugu verse, a naivedya of wheat pāyasam, and the hundred and eight names. **Neither was made from the other, and neither reading was carried across.**
3. **They differ in the number of weeks, and this is a difference of rule, not of wording.** The base text prescribes four, nine, eleven or twenty-one Thursdays or Fridays. The Bengaluru booklet says plainly `ఈ పూజకు ఇన్ని శుక్రవారములని నియమం లేదు` — for this pūjā there is no rule of so many Fridays — and leaves the worshipper to continue as long as she wishes. The base text's rule stands in this file and the booklet's is recorded here.
4. **They differ in one vocative and one pronoun of the closing verse, and the first of these changes the sense.** The base text reads `భక్తిహీనం మహేశ్వరి` and `పరిపూర్ణం తదస్తు తే` — O Maheśvarī, let it be complete for you. The Bengaluru booklet reads `భక్తిహీనం హరిప్రియే` and `పరిపూర్ణం తదస్తు మే` — O beloved of Hari, let it be complete for me. **These are not bracketed as spelling variants, because the goddess is addressed by a different name and the completeness is asked for a different person.** The base text's readings stand in the verse and both are recorded here.
5. **They differ in one word of the dhyāna.** Where the base text reads `స్నాపితా హేమకుంభైః` — bathed from golden pots — the Bengaluru booklet reads `వాసితా హేమకుంభైః`. The base reading is kept. It is recorded and not adopted, because the booklet's OCR is damaged in the two words immediately before it.
6. **They differ entirely in the prārthanā.** The base text prays `శ్రియం దేహి సుఖం దేహి సౌభాగ్యం సంతతిం ముదా`. The booklet prays `దేవి దేహి పరం జ్ఞానం దేవి దేహి పరం సుఖం`. Both are recorded; only the base text's stands in the file.
7. **They differ in the kathā, and this is the sharpest difference of the two.** The base text tells a purāṇic story — Pārvatī questions Parameśvara; Bhṛgu's austerity and the birth of Lakṣmī as his daughter; Bhṛgu's anger, the striking of Viṣṇu, and the churning of the ocean; Suśarma and his wife Suśānta; and the four sisters Śīla, Suśīla, Guṇaśīla and Viśāla. **The Bengaluru booklet has no purāṇic kathā at all.** In its place it prints a series of modern testimonies — a ring lost and found in Pune, money taken by a pickpocket in Bombay, a transfer from Davanagere to Ahmedabad, the postmaster of Jayanagar in Bangalore, a man in Mangalore cured of a pain, a marriage settled in Dharwad. The two lineages are recorded here side by side and neither was written into the other.

**What this text is, said plainly.** The Vaibhava Lakṣmī vrata is a **modern printed observance of the twentieth century, not a classical one**, and the evidence for that was gathered rather than assumed.

- *Vratha Ratnākaram* volumes 1 and 2, the older Telugu vrata compendia, were read in full from the archive.org scans `VrathaRathnakaramu1` and `VrathaRatnakaramu2` (126,949 and 365,404 Telugu characters of OCR, measured). **Neither volume contains this vrata.** The word `వైభవలక్ష్మీ` does not occur in either. Their OCR is broken in places, but it is readable enough to serve as a coverage map, and the coverage is plain.
- *Āru Vratālu*, Paṇḍita Pariṣkṛtamu, Gollapudi Veeraswamy Son, Rajahmundry 1999, does not contain this vrata either. It was consulted only as a printed witness for the wording of two shared ritual verses.
- The observance does not appear in the classical nibandhas.
- The northern printed lineage can be dated. *Vaibhav Lakshmi Vrat*, Sāhitya Saṅgam, Surat, read from the archive.org scan `pxdz_vaibhav-lakshmi-vrat-surat-sahitya-sangam` (26,297 Devanāgarī characters), carries a Government of India registration number `एल-११६३९/८८` — that is, 1988 — and states that the same booklet is published in Gujarati, Marathi, Tamil, Kannada, Malayalam, Oriya, Bengali, Sindhi, English and German. Its rule is eleven or twenty-one Fridays with an udyāpana, and its kathā is the story of Śīlā. A Delhi reprint of the same work, *Śrī Vaibhav Lakshmi Vrat Kathā*, compiled by Narendra Sharma for Lakshmi Publication, read from the scan `BwbU_shri-vaibhav-lakshmi-vrat-katha-compiled-by-narendra-sharma-lakshmi-publication-delhi` (32,866 Devanāgarī characters), gives twenty-one or fifty-one Fridays and feeds seven girls at the end.
- The Bengaluru Telugu booklet itself states that the vrata is kept in Maharashtra and northern Karnataka, and it directs the worshipper to give eight copies of the booklet to eight married women, so that `8 × 8 = 64` women may have worshipped. **A rite that propagates by the distribution of its own printed booklet is a rite of the age of cheap printing.**

**The base text's Telugu recension is therefore a South Indian reworking, not the Gujarati booklet in translation.** It has supplied the observance with a full Śrī Sūkta pūjā and with a purāṇic kathā that the northern booklets do not have. That is a real finding about this text and it is set down here without dressing the rite in an authority it does not hold.

Not collated: the hundred and eight names. At its aṣṭottara the base text prints no names; it points to the Mahālakṣmī and the Lakṣmī nāmāvaḷis and leaves the choice open. The Lakṣmī nāmāvaḷi is in this corpus at `../../devi/lakshmi/36_lakshmi_ashtottarashatanamavali.txt`. The Bengaluru booklet does print a hundred and eight names, but its OCR of those pages is broken name by name (`ఓం ప్రకృత నమః` for `ఓం ప్రకృత్యై నమః`, `ఓం విద్యాయై నమః` sound but `ఓం MOF నమః` unreadable), and **no comparison of the two lists was attempted, because a list cannot be collated against a list that cannot be read.** This is a real gap and closing it needs the page images.

Not found, and each named: `vignanam.org` was searched through its own `sitemap.xml`, all 27,096 entries. **It carries no Vaibhava Lakṣmī vrata in any of its scripts.** The only pages whose names contain `vaibhava` are the Devī Vaibhava Āścarya nāmāvaḷi and the Raghuvīra Gadyam, which are different works. `stotranidhi.com` is the base text and is not counted as a second witness.

Accent: **the base text prints the Śrī Sūkta ṛcs with their Vedic accent marks, and those marks are kept here exactly as the source prints them.** They were lifted from the fetched file by machine and were not typed. No accent was added anywhere, and no accent was inferred. The verses of the services themselves are stotra and carry no accent, and none was given to them. **The Bengaluru booklet prints the same Śrī Sūkta lines without any accent at all; its unaccented reading was not used to strip anything from this file.**

Note on defects repaired in the base text. **50 in all, of two kinds, and every one of them counted.**

- **39 occurrences of a Latin letter `o` standing in the place of the Telugu anusvāra `ం`, and 9 occurrences of an ASCII colon `:` standing in the place of the visarga `ః`.** These are an encoding fault of the source, and they fall only on the accented lines — the ordinary lines of the same page write `ం` and `ః` correctly. The repair was applied **only to lines that already carry an accent mark**, so that a real colon in ordinary prose could not be touched. It is confirmed twice over: by the base text's own unaccented spellings, and by the Bengaluru booklet, which prints `హిరణ్యవర్ణాం` and `సువర్ణరజతస్రజాం` with the anusvāra.
- **2 single-word repairs, each confirmed against the *Āru Vratālu* print and not corrected from judgement.** `శూభాన్` to `శుభాన్` in the akṣata verse, which the *Āru Vratālu* print spells `శుభాన్` in both of its two occurrences of the same verse; and `తంబూలం` to `తాంబూలం` in the naming of the betel service, which the verse printed immediately above it in the base text itself spells `తాంబూలం`.

**Left standing, and not repaired, because no printed witness confirms a correction:** `ప్రార్థనయాహృదమ్` in the pādya verse, `గృహీత్వ` for an expected `గృహీత్వా` in the upavīta verse, `శీతోత్థగమితం` in the snāna verse, and `అకాలమృత్యహరణం` in the closing tīrtha verse. Each of these looks like a printing slip, and each was searched for in the *Āru Vratālu* print and in the Bengaluru booklet without result. **They are left exactly as the source prints them.** No other character of the base text was altered.

A note on the Telugu prose of the story: the source's narrative has several plain typing slips (`వృద్ధి స్త్రీ` for `వృద్ధ స్త్రీ`, `ఆచరించరింపుము`, `భద్రప్రచుకొనవలెను`). **The story is prose and lives in the translation fields, not in `deva:` or `iast:`, so nothing was transcribed from those lines; only their sense was carried over.**

Blurb: This is the vrata of Vaibhavalakṣmī, the Lakṣmī who gives prosperity. It is kept in the evening, after the sun has set. It is not fixed to any month. The worshipper resolves upon a number of weeks — four, or nine, or eleven, or twenty-one — and keeps the vrata on that many Thursdays or Fridays. On the day of the vrata she rises, bathes, cleans the north-east corner of the house, smears it with cow dung and draws the muggu there. She fasts until the evening. Then she spreads a new cloth in that corner, pours rice upon it, and sets a pot on the rice. She fills the pot with water, drops in coins according to her means, adds the leaves of fruit trees, and places a coconut on top. Vaibhavalakṣmī is invited into that pot, and the sixteen services are offered to her. Each service is offered with a verse of the Śrī Sūkta before it. Her limbs are then worshipped by ten names, from the feet upward to the head, and the hundred and eight names of Lakṣmī are recited. Sweet food is offered. The story is told after the worship. In it Parameśvara tells Pārvatī why wealth comes to some and not to others: of Bhṛgu, whose anger drove Lakṣmī from Vaikuṇṭha into the ocean of milk; of Suśarma, a learned and poor man whose want came from the deeds of an earlier birth; and of four sisters whose husbands grew proud and said their wealth was their own doing, and lost it. An old woman came to the sisters and taught them this vrata, and their prosperity returned. On the last Friday the udyāpana is spoken. The vāyana and the tāmbūla are given to at least five married women. The water of the pot is poured at the root of the nīm or the tulasī, the coin is kept safely in the house, and the rice from under the pot is given to the birds.

iast:
vratakathā prārambhaḥ
en: After this, the sages of the world of men wished to perform a great sacrifice. They asked Bhṛgu to decide to whom the oblation should be given. Bhṛgu agreed and set out through the three worlds. In the world of Brahmā he saw Brahmā speaking kindly with Sarasvatī. Angry that his coming was not honoured, he went on to Kailāsa. There too Maheśvara was deep in meditation and did not notice his coming. With still greater anger Bhṛgu left that place. At last he reached Vaikuṇṭha, and seeing Śrī Mahāviṣṇu at rest there, he was overcome with anger that his coming was not honoured, and he struck the breast of Viṣṇu with his foot. Viṣṇu calmed the anger of Bhṛgu and sent him away. Śrī Mahālakṣmī saw this. Her own dwelling is the breast of her husband, and he had said nothing when it was struck with a foot. In her displeasure she left Vaikuṇṭha and went into the ocean of milk. With this the prosperity of Indra and all the gods declined. They all went to Śrī Mahāviṣṇu and prayed to him, and he commanded them to churn the ocean of milk. While they were churning it with the mountain Mandhara, Śrī Mahālakṣmī chose to favour them. en: In the same way, wealth comes to people also from the deeds of former births. Hear the story of Suśarma as an example of this. In a certain village there lived Suśarma, a man learned in all the śāstras. Through his gurukula he taught the śāstras to the townspeople, to kings and to wealthy men. Yet there was poverty in his house. Seeing this, Suśānta, the wife of Suśarma, said, "Husband, among the pupils you have taught there are some who are wealthy. Go to them and bring some money for the needs of our house." Suśarma replied, "It is not proper to beg, and it is not good to set one's hope upon wealth. Still, since the wealth we need can be given by Śrī Mahālakṣmī, who is prosperity itself, I shall perform a sacrifice for her." So saying, he began the sacrifice. Śrī Mahālakṣmī was pleased with the sacrifice Suśarma performed. She appeared before him, granted the boon, "Tonight I shall rain gold coins in your house," and disappeared. Suśarma told this joyfully to Suśānta, and they kept watch through the whole night. So they watched until dawn came. When Suśarma understood that no rain of wealth had come, he grieved greatly that he had kept his wife in poverty, and set out from the house intending to fast until death. Outside the house he saw a woman in soiled clothes, without brightness. Suśarma asked who she was, and the woman said, "I came, pleased with your sacrifice, to rain gold in your house. But Jyeṣṭhādevī, who is in your house, stopped me. She said that because you did no meritorious deeds and no acts of giving in your former births, wealth does not come to you in this birth." Hearing this, Suśarma understood that if he were not there, wealth would come to his wife and children, and he took the life of a renunciant. He prayed to Śrī Mahālakṣmī to show compassion in return. She was pleased with his devotion, and Śrī Vaibhavalakṣmī granted prosperity to that house.
