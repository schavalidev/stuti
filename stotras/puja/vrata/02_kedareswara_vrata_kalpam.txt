Recension note: the two editions are the same rite, and seven differences were found. None of them is reconciled by silently choosing one.

1. **They differ in which name stands over the twenty-first knot, and this changes the sense.** The base text worships the last knot with `ఓం మృత్యుంజయాయ నమః`. The *Āru Vratālu* print worships it with `ఓం కేదారేశ్వరాయ నమః`, so that the series closes on the name of the deity of the vrata itself. **This is not bracketed as a variant, because it is not a matter of spelling; the two readings name two different forms of Śiva.** The base text's reading stands in the verse and both are recorded here.
2. **They differ in what is printed before the sixteen services.** The base text prints the establishing of the pot in full: the Ṛgvedic verse `ఓం కద్రుద్రాయ ప్రచేతసే` for the invitation of Rudra, the verse `ఓం గౌరీర్మిమాయ సలిలాని తక్షతి` for the invitation of Mahāgaurī, and the prāṇapratiṣṭhā. The *Āru Vratālu* print gives only the direction `ఆదౌ గణాధిపతి పూజాం, కలశ పూజాంచ కుర్యాత్` and prints none of those mantras. The base text's reading is kept.
3. **They differ in the bathing.** The base text gives the full series of baths with their Vedic mantras — milk, curd, ghee, honey, sugar and fruit water, and `ఆపో హిష్ఠా మయోభువః` before the bath in pure water. The *Āru Vratālu* print goes straight from the bath in the five nectars to the bath in pure water and prints only the verse `నదీజలం సమాయుక్తం`. The base text's fuller reading is kept.
4. **They differ in how much of the closing apparatus is printed.** The base text carries the mantras spoken while the food is offered, the further worship in which the services of a king are offered, and the mantrapuṣpa with the five faces of Śiva. The *Āru Vratālu* print has none of these three. It is the shorter book at this point, and nothing in it contradicts the base text.
5. **They differ over the hundred and eight names.** The base text does not print them. It directs the worshipper to the Śiva nāmāvaḷi. The *Āru Vratālu* print gives the hundred and eight names in full, set in three columns on one page. **Those names were not transcribed and were not collated**, because the OCR of a three-column page interleaves the columns and its readings cannot be trusted line by line.
6. **They differ in one word of the worship of the limbs.** The base text reads `నేత్రాని పూజయామి`. The *Āru Vratālu* print reads `నేత్రాన్ పూజయామి`. The base text's reading is kept. The base reading looks like a defect for `నేత్రాణి`, but the printed witness does not give that form, so nothing was altered; see the note on defects below.
7. **They differ in one word of the sixth knot, and the difference is only in that word.** The base text reads `షష్ట గ్రంథిం` and the *Āru Vratālu* print reads `షష్టమ గ్రంథిం`. This is a one-word difference inside a line that is otherwise the same, so it is printed in the bracket form `షష్ట(షష్టమ)`, written identically in `deva:` and `iast:`.

Not transcribed, and said plainly: **the *Āru Vratālu* print carries the story in Sanskrit as well as in Telugu**, marked `కేదారేశ్వర వ్రత కథారంభః (మూలము)` at line 3491 of its OCR, in the form `సూత ఉవాచ`. **That Sanskrit was located and was not written into this file, because the OCR of those pages is too damaged to transcribe with confidence** — Latin characters and stray marks stand in the middle of half the lines, and words are broken across the damage (`హ్యుజ్ఞయినీపతి:`, `TL: కార్పాసకైర్వాపి`). **Nothing was reconstructed from memory.** The Sanskrit kathā is a real gap in this file and closing it needs the page images, not the OCR. The story as it stands here is the base text's own Telugu prose, and it sits in the translation fields where the source put it; the `deva:` of those units carries the section heading alone. As far as the damaged text can be read, it agrees with the Telugu on every point of the rule — twenty-one threads, the eighth day of the bright fortnight of Bhādrapada to the new moon, twenty-one brāhmaṇas, and twenty-one kinds of food.

Not collated: the hundred and eight names, for the reason given above; and the Śiva nāmāvaḷi to which the rite points was not compared against the list in the *Āru Vratālu* print.

Accent: **none, and none is expected.** The Vedic verses used here for the baths and for the mantrapuṣpa are printed unaccented in both editions, and every other verse in the rite is stotra, which is not accent-marked. No accent was added and none was inferred.

Note on defects repaired in the base text, each counted and each confirmed against the *Āru Vratālu* print rather than corrected from judgement. Three in all: `ఓం విరూపాక్ష్యాయ నమః` to `ఓం విరూపాక్షాయ నమః` in the worship of the limbs; `వృషభద్వజాయ` to `వృషభధ్వజాయ` at the fourth knot; and `చతుర్ధశ గ్రంథిం` to `చతుర్దశ గ్రంథిం` at the fourteenth knot, where the surrounding lines all read `ద`. No other character of the base text was altered.

Note on two suspected defects left standing, because no witness confirms a correction. The base text reads `నేత్రాని పూజయామి` where `నేత్రాణి` would be expected, and `అన్యధా శరణం నాస్తి` where `అన్యథా` would be expected. The *Āru Vratālu* print gives a different word in the first place and does not carry the verse at all in the second. Both were therefore left exactly as the base text prints them.

Note: the base text prints the reference of the verse `ఓం గౌరీర్మిమాయ సలిలాని తక్షతి` as `(ఋ.౧.౧౬౧.౪౧)`. The reference was left exactly as printed. No witness was found that confirms it and nothing was altered.

Blurb: This is the vrata of Kedāreśvara, who is Śiva worshipped together with Gaurī. It is not a rite of one day. It begins on the eighth day of the bright fortnight of the month of Bhādrapada and is kept every day until the new moon, which is twenty-one days, and the rite given here is the worship performed on the last of those days. It may be kept by anyone. The fruit stated for it is long life, health, wealth and, at the end, union with Śiva. A full pot is set upon a heap of grain in a clean place in the house, wrapped with twenty-one threads and covered with silk. Kedāreśvara is invited into that pot along with Gaurī, and the sixteen services are offered to him. His limbs are then worshipped by seventeen names, from the feet upward to the crown of the head, and the hundred and eight names of Śiva are recited. The central act of this vrata is the toram. It is a thread in which twenty-one knots are tied, one for each day of the vrata. Each knot is worshipped by one name of Śiva, and the thread is then bound upon the hand. The vāyana is given, and the image that was worshipped is given away. The story is told last. In it Pārvatī, ashamed at being passed over by the devotee Bhṛṅgiriṭi, draws back her own power and leaves Kailāsa. The sage Gautama teaches her this vrata, and by keeping it she obtains half the body of Śiva. She then asks that whoever keeps it shall obtain what they desire, and Śiva grants it.

वह चलकर आ रहा था कि मार्ग में बिना प्रयत्न के पहले खोया हुआ सारा धन उसे मिल गया। वह प्रसन्न होकर सब लेकर काञ्ची नगरी में प्रवेश कर रहा था, तभी चतुरङ्ग सेना के साथ उसके पिता उसे लेने आये। बालक और उसकी माता को साथ लेकर वे अपने नगर को गये। तब वह राजकुमार माता-पिता के साथ सुख से रहने लगा।
इसके पश्चात् माता भाग्यवती और पिता चोलराज उस समय से यह व्रत करते हुए अविच्छिन्न समस्त सम्पदाओं का भोग करते रहे।"

--- unit 54 | section: व्रतकथा ---
vidhi:
The fruit of hearing the story, and the close. The source names the Skānda Purāṇa as the place where this account is given.
deva:
व्रत कथा प्रारंभमु
iast:
vrata kathā prāraṁbhamu
en: "Whoever hears or reads the greatness of this vrata with devotion, in the way it has been told, obtains by the grace of Śrī Mahādeva unending life, health and wealth, enjoys happiness, and obtains union with Śiva." This was said by the great sage Gautama, and Sūta told it to Śaunaka and the others. Śrī Vyāsa Bhaṭṭāraka set it out in the Skānda Purāṇa.
tel: "ఎవరైనను చెప్పిన ప్రకారము ఈ వ్రత మహాత్మ్యమును భక్తితో వినినను, చదివినను, అట్టివారందరూ శ్రీ మహాదేవుని అనుగ్రహము వలన అనంతమైన ఆయురారోగ్య ఐశ్వర్యములను పొంది, సుఖములను అనుభవించి శివసాయుజ్యమును పొందుతారు\" అని గౌతమ మహర్షిచేత చెప్పబడినదని సూతుడు శౌనకాదులకు చెప్పినట్లుగా శ్రీ వ్యాస భట్టారకుడు స్కాంద పురాణమునందు వర్ణించెను.
hi: "जो कोई कहे गये प्रकार से इस व्रत के माहात्म्य को भक्तिपूर्वक सुनते हैं अथवा पढ़ते हैं, वे सब श्री महादेव के अनुग्रह से अनन्त आयु, आरोग्य और ऐश्वर्य पाकर सुख भोगते हैं और शिवसायुज्य प्राप्त करते हैं" — ऐसा गौतम महर्षि ने कहा, और सूत ने शौनक आदि से कहा, जैसा श्री व्यास भट्टारक ने स्कान्द पुराण में वर्णन किया है।

न्यूनं संपूर्णतां याति सद्योवंदे महेश्वरं ||

यत्पूजितं मयादेव परिपूर्णं तदस्तुते ||
iast:
samarpaṇaṁ –
yasya smṛtyāca nāmoktyā tapaḥ pūjā kriyādiṣu |
nyūnaṁ saṁpūrṇatāṁ yāti sadyovaṁde maheśvaraṁ ||
maṁtrahīnaṁ kriyāhīnaṁ bhaktihīnaṁ sureśvara |
yatpūjitaṁ mayādeva paripūrṇaṁ tadastute ||
en: Offering. By remembering him and by speaking his name, whatever is lacking in austerity, in worship and in the acts of the rite at once becomes complete. To that Maheśvara I bow.

tel: సమర్పణం. ఎవరిని స్మరించుట చేతను, ఎవరి నామమును ఉచ్చరించుట చేతను తపస్సు పూజ క్రియ మొదలైన వాటిలో లోపించినది వెంటనే సంపూర్ణమగునో, ఆ మహేశ్వరునికి నమస్కరిస్తున్నాను.
