Blurb: This is a different kavaca from the Trailokyavijaya armour already in this collection. It is drawn from the Kālikā-kalpa of the Rudrayāmala. It is framed as a dialogue, in which the Devī asks Bhairava for the means to destroy enemies and to secure supreme sovereignty. Unusually for a kavaca, it supplies a full ritual apparatus before the body-protecting verses begin. There is a formal ṛṣi-chandas-devatā formula. There is a six-part hand-nyāsa and a six-part heart-nyāsa, keyed to the syllables Krāṁ, Krīṁ, Krūṁ, Kraiṁ, Krauṁ and Kraḥ. There is a dhyāna of four-armed Kālī laughing upon a corpse-seat. Its centrepiece is verse 18, an extended garland of mantras, without metre, addressed to a blood-drinking Kālikā. It commands her in the imperative to devour and destroy enemies, and to bring kings, officials and wealth under the reciter's control. A phalaśruti follows. It describes an aggressive rite of sorcery, in which the names of enemies are written in cremation-ground charcoal, the life-breath is installed in an effigy, and the effigy is struck with a weapon-mantra. Beside this stand the more usual promises of protection, prosperity and success.

--- verse 23 | section: Phalaśruti ---
