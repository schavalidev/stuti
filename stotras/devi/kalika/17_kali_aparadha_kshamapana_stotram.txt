Sections:

Blurb: This is an apology hymn, an aparādha-kṣamāpaṇa, of the kind that Śaṅkara's Devyaparādhakṣamāpaṇa made well known. It is pitched squarely within Kaula practice. It has fifteen long verses in the Sragdharā metre, every one closing with the same plea: forgive my offence, O you of the unveiled face, O Kāmarūpā, O terrible one. Its spine is a life told as a sequence of failures to worship. Childhood was lost to play and to a mind stupefied by wanting. Youth was lost to senses like venomous snakes, and to an appetite for other men's wives and wealth. Middle age was lost to the daily arithmetic of feeding wife and children, asking where shall I get it and where shall I go. Old age was lost to deafness, blindness, fallen teeth, asthma and dysentery, with nothing left to think about but dying. This occupies verses 2 to 5. Around that spine the petitioner keeps totalling up what he never did: no water offered, no flower, no food-offering, no nyāsa, no telling of her qualities, no repetition of her mantra, and no place among the assemblies of the Kaulas. This is in verses 6 and 10. Only in the last third does the manner change. The same speaker recognises her as the elements, as prakṛti, and as the self itself. He then runs through the ten Mahāvidyās by name: Kālī, Tārā, Sundarī, Bhairavī, Durgā, Chinnamastā, Bhuvaneśvarī, Dhūmāvatī, Mātaṅgī and Bagalā. In the closing verse he asks to be pardoned as a son.

deva:

तेनाद्या कीर्तिवर्गेर्जठरजदहनैर्बाद्ध्यमानो बलिष्ठैः ।
क्षिप्त्वा जन्मान्तरान्नः पुनरिहभविता क्वाश्रयः क्वापि सेवा

iast:

kṣiptvā janmāntarānnaḥ punarihabhavitā kvāśrayaḥ kvāpi sevā
kṣantavyo me'parādhaḥ prakaṭita vadane kāmarūpe karāle ||
en: Set in a former body, I neither took refuge at your two feet nor worshipped them; and so today I am bound fast by fierce, powerful burnings born in the belly. Flung through birth after birth, where in this world shall I find refuge again, where any chance of service at all? Forgive my offence, O you of the unveiled face, O Kāmarūpā who takes what form she wills, O terrible one.

en: Grown to full manhood, a beggar of alms, scrambling after food and the rest for son and daughter and wife — "Where shall I get it? Forgive my offence, O you of the unveiled face, O Kāmarūpā, O terrible one.
