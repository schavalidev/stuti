Blurb: This hymn has an unusual speaker and an unusual occasion. Mahākāla tells Maheśānī that when Rāvaṇa set out to conquer the Kālaka asuras, wishing to defeat them all by the strength of his own arms, this is what he said. What follows is nineteen unbroken verses in the bhujaṅgaprayāta metre, forming a single sentence of accusatives. It is one enormous act of seeing, which never pauses for a finite verb until verse 22 at last supplies it: thus do I ever remember Kāmakālī. She is seen first in her place: in a cremation ground rinsed by waves from an ocean of blood, on a corpse-seat in the form of Śiva, ringed by ḍākinīs whose hair falls to their feet, who have drunk wine and flesh and blood, and who are laughing with her. Then comes the body. She has ten faces and twenty-seven eyes, counted exactly: two each in the three front faces, and three each in the other seven. Her faces are shaped like those of leopard, lion, jackal, monkey, camel, bear, Garuḍa, elephant and crocodile. Her tongue lolls between two long tusks. Her breasts are washed in the blood that leaps from her mouth. The serpent-king composes hymns to her out of terror at the weight of her tread. Six verses then simply list what her fifty-four hands hold. First come a jewel-garland, a dry skull, a hide, a noose, a spear, a khaṭvāṅga, a severed head, a bow, a discus, a bell, an infant, a preta, mountains, a human skeleton, a flute of frenzy, a fire-pit and a ḍamaru. Then come a javelin, a spiked axe, a śataghnī, a jackal cub, a chopping-knife, a sword, a threatening forefinger and a trident. Then come five Pāśupata arrows, a pārijāta flower, a vulture, a bud, a piece of flesh, a sacrificial ladle, a citron, a needle, an axe, a mace and the vajra-fist. Last come a corpse, and after the corpse, a caress. Four closing verses dress her entirely in living snakes: yellow kumbhīnasas for bangles, pale-red cobras for armlets, an ash-grey serpent for a waist-cord, a white one for a sacred thread, and a crimson one strung with skulls and bells about her hips.

mahākāla uvāca |

en: Mahākāla said: Now I shall declare, O Maheśānī, the unsurpassed hymn of the Goddess — by the mere remembrance of which all obstacles turn their faces away and go.
tel: మహాకాలుడు పలికెను: ఓ మహేశానీ, ఇప్పుడు దేవి యొక్క అనుత్తమమైన స్తోత్రమును చెప్పెదను — దేనిని స్మరించినంత మాత్రముచేతనే విఘ్నములు ముఖము త్రిప్పుకొని పోవునో.
hi: महाकाल ने कहा: हे महेशानि, अब मैं देवी का अनुत्तम स्तोत्र कहूँगा — जिसके स्मरण मात्र से विघ्न पराङ्मुख होकर चले जाते हैं।

Recension note: The two stotranidhi.com pages used (IAST on the /en/ page, Devanāgarī on the /hi/ page) present an identical 24-verse text and were cross-checked verse for verse; no variant readings were found, and both slugs were confirmed against stotranidhi.com's post sitemaps. Verse 1 is in anuṣṭubh and carries the frame; verses 2–24 are in bhujaṅgaprayāta without exception, the meter the title names, and the narrative tag "mahākāla uvāca" is kept at the head of verse 1 rather than dropped, since the whole attribution to Rāvaṇa hangs on it. Verses 3 through 21 form a single unbroken string of accusatives governed by the verb that does not arrive until verse 22 (saṁsmarāmi, "I remember"); the translation supplies a bracketed "[I remember her]" at verse 3 to make the construction readable and lets the accusatives run from there. Three printed readings are kept as they stand. In verse 22 both sources print ईदृशों / īdṛśoṁ where the accusative feminine īdṛśīṁ is required by both sense and meter. In verse 18, avaddhanaddha stands without the hyphenated line-break that the surrounding verses use, and the doubled participle is redundant. In verse 20, avaśobhā has been read as an-avaśobhā, "of unsurpassed splendor," which the meter supports. In verses 7 and 14 the Devanāgarī prints महाट्‍टाट्‍टहासैः and पट्‍टिशं with a zero-width joiner inside the ṭṭ conjunct, rendered on the IAST page as literal hyphens; these are typographic and the words are written normally above. The eye-count of verse 10 is internally consistent and worth recording as a check on the text: ten faces, of which the front, right and left carry two eyes each and the remaining seven carry three each, giving exactly the twenty-seven the verse states; verse 11 then names nine animal faces, the tenth being the human one already described in verses 8 and 9, and verse 12 gives twenty-seven arms on each side, fifty-four in all, which is what the inventory of verses 12–17 fills. The Kaula and cremation-ground content — the ocean of blood, the corpse-seat in the form of Śiva, the ḍākinīs who have drunk wine and flesh and blood, the ornaments made from dead Brahmās, the infant and the preta and the corpse among the hand-attributes — is translated as it stands, in the same manner as the Śrī Kālī Karpūra Stotram already in this folder. The colophon places the hymn in the tenth paṭala of the Mahākālasaṁhitā and names Rāvaṇa as its author, matching the narrative occasion given in verse 2. Stotranidhi carries no prose translation on either page; the en/tel/hi renderings above were made for this corpus directly from the Sanskrit.
