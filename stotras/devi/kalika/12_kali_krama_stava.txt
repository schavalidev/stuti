Sections:
నమస్కారః — Namaskāraḥ, the opening salutations (vv. 1–3)
పాదపద్మస్తుతిః — Pādapadmastutiḥ, praise of the Goddess's feet and what the gods gained from them (vv. 4–15)
ధ్యానజపః — Dhyānajapaḥ, japa performed while holding her form in mind (vv. 16–25)
మంత్రక్రమః — Mantrakramaḥ, the ordered recitation of the bīja-groups (vv. 26–32)
స్తుతిః ఫలశ్రుతిశ్చ — Stutiḥ and Phalaśrutiḥ, the closing identifications and the fruits of recitation (vv. 33–35)

Blurb: The word krama in the title is not ornamental. Verses 26 to 30 walk the root mantra of Dakṣiṇakālikā through in order, group by group. Each cluster is named by its technical name, and a distinct fruit is attached to each: first the three bījas, then the pair of kūrca syllables, then the pair of lajjā syllables, then the three bījas again, the two kūrcas again, and the two lajjās again. That is exactly the skeleton of the twenty-two-syllable vidyā, and reading those five verses in sequence is the nearest thing the hymn has to an argument. Two other movements sit around that spine. The first is a long meditation upon her feet, in verses 4 to 15, which becomes a list of debts. Hari became the prosperity of the worlds by them. Śiva got something higher than the highest. The stations of Prajāpati and of Indra, and even Viṣṇu's lordship of the discus, are all called the fruit of her lotus feet. The second movement, verses 19 to 25, is a set of instructions for what to hold in the mind while reciting. It is the cremation-ground Kālī without any softening: the garland named 'of skulls', the severed human head and the sword in the left hands with the gestures of boon and fearlessness on the right, the sky-clad naked form, the ring of howling jackals, the śṛṅgāra-rasa, and the hair flung loose. Each image is paired with what the reciter becomes, from one who knows the savour of poetry to a lord among the gods. The text is old and has been damaged in transmission. Several of its lines have come down bent out of shape, and the translation follows them where it can rather than straightening them.

--- verse 1 | section: Namaskāraḥ ---
deva:
नमामि कालिका देवीं कलिकल्मषनाशिनीम् ।
नमामि शम्भुपत्नीं च नमामि भवसुन्दरीम् ॥
iast:
namāmi kālikā devīṁ kalikalmaṣanāśinīm |
namāmi śambhupatnīṁ ca namāmi bhavasundarīm ||
en: I salute the goddess Kālikā, destroyer of the defilements of the Kali age; I salute the wife of Śambhu; I salute her who is the beauty of Bhava.
tel: కలియుగపు కల్మషములను నశింపజేయు కాళికాదేవికి నమస్కరించుచున్నాను; శంభుని పత్నికి నమస్కరించుచున్నాను; భవసుందరికి నమస్కరించుచున్నాను.
hi: कलि के कल्मष का नाश करने वाली कालिका देवी को मैं नमस्कार करता हूँ; शंभु की पत्नी को नमस्कार करता हूँ; भवसुंदरी को नमस्कार करता हूँ।

en: If with these thirty-three verses one recites the mantra and offers praise, bowing to them one by one — this hymn that is like the supreme nectar and gives joy — then attainment will come in purity; and in this world your pair of feet, to be bowed to with the head, to be longed for, is spread out as the object of worship by the best of the gods.
tel: ఈ ముప్పదిమూడు శ్లోకములతో ఎవడు మంత్రమును జపించి స్తుతించునో, వీటిని ఒక్కొక్కటిగా నమస్కరించునో — పరమామృతముతో సమానమైన, సుఖమునిచ్చు ఈ స్తవమును — వానికి శుద్ధియందు సిద్ధి కలుగును; జగత్తునందు శిరస్సుతో ప్రణమిల్లదగిన, ప్రకామ్యమైన నీ పాదయుగము శ్రేష్ఠ దేవతలచే పూజ్యముగా విస్తరించియున్నది.

Recension note: The two stotranidhi.com pages used (IAST on the /en/ page, Devanāgarī on the /hi/ page) carry an identical text — identical unit for unit, including every hyphenated line-break and every irregular reading — so they descend from one transcription and give no independent check; both slugs were confirmed against stotranidhi.com's post sitemaps. Two structural points. Verse 3 is a single hemistich, not a full śloka, and is printed under its own number in both scripts; it has been kept as its own unit. And verse 35 calls the hymn "these thirty-three verses" (trayastriṁśaiḥ ślokaiḥ), against the 35 numbered units actually printed. Counting the half-verse 3 as part of verse 2 and the closing phalaśruti verse 35 as apparatus rather than as one of the stava's own verses reconciles the two figures exactly; but since both sources print 35 numbered units, the file records 35 and the discrepancy is noted here rather than resolved by renumbering. This text has come down damaged. Verses 7, 8, 13, 16, 17, 18, 31, 32 and 34 contain pādas that neither scan nor construe cleanly — among them vidhunvajjaṅghā me sakalagaṇam (v. 7), nārībhūtaṁ (v. 8), sadaśīrmurāreḥ (v. 17), the whole of verse 18, and pūjyavititam in the last line (v. 35), where the /en/ page's transliteration reads pūjyavitatim against the Devanāgarī पूज्यवितितम्, the single point where the two pages diverge and evidently a transcription slip rather than a variant reading. These have been translated as closely as the received wording permits, with no emendation and no silent smoothing; where the sense had to be guessed, the translation stays as literal as it can. The mantra sequence of verses 26–30 is the one part of the hymn that is perfectly clear, and it is what gives the stava its name: bījatraya, then kūrcadvaya, then lajjādvaya, then bījatraya again, kūrcayuga again, lajjādvaya again — the group-by-group skeleton of the Dakṣiṇakālikā vidyā, in which the bījas are the three krīṁ, the kūrcas the two hūṁ, and the lajjās the two hrīṁ. Verse 31's "dakṣiṇa pada" names the vocative segment of that same mantra. The Kaula and cremation-ground material of verses 19–25 — the garland of severed heads, the human head and sword in the left hands, the naked digambarā form, the ring of howling jackals, the śṛṅgāra-rasa held in the mind during japa — is translated as it stands, in the same manner as the Śrī Kālī Karpūra Stotram already in this folder. Stotranidhi carries no prose translation on either page; the en/tel/hi renderings above were made for this corpus directly from the Sanskrit.
