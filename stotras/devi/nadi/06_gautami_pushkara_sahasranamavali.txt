Title: Śrī Gautamī Puṣkara Sahasranāmāvalī — the thousand names of the Godāvarī in her Puṣkara
Devanāgarī: श्रीगौतमीपुष्करसहस्रनामावळिः
Telugu: శ్రీగౌతమీపుష్కరసహస్రనామావళిః

Author: Traditional. The source names no composer. Its colophon assigns the work to the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa; on that attribution see the Recension note.

Accent: **none.** The print carries no svara marks anywhere. This is a nāmāvalī and not Vedic chant, so no accent belongs to it, and under the standing rule none was inferred or supplied.

Source / recension: base text and sole witness is the Telugu-script print *Gautamī Puṣkara Kṛtya*, archive.org item `gautamipushkarakritya`, where the nāmāvalī occupies PDF pages 18–31 (printed pages ౧౫–౨౮), three columns to a page, read down each column. **The item's OCR is unusable and was not used for any reading** — it is old Telugu type read as Latin, 19,975 Latin characters and not one Telugu character. Every name was transcribed from the page images, rendered at 1200 dpi and cropped column by column, with a Telugu OCR of all 42 columns kept only as a second opinion. Devanāgarī was produced from the Telugu by `bin/tel2dev.py` and IAST from the Devanāgarī by `bin/dev2iast.py`; neither tool changes a word, and no name was transliterated by hand.

Independent collation: **none exists, and this was established rather than assumed.** The colophon's own attribution was tested first. GRETIL's Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa (1,935,427 characters) carries no such work: its three mentions of the Godāvarī are river-list geography, its one mention of the Gautamī is the Sātavāhana king-list, and its one occurrence of `sahasranām` is a forward reference to the Lalitā Sahasranāma. Two further Brahmāṇḍa editions (2,926,189 and 1,129,040 Devanāgarī characters) give nothing. Two Veṅkaṭeśvara 1906 prints of the *Brahma Mahāpurāṇa with Gautamī Māhātmya* (994,761 and 1,076,094 characters) give nothing. sanskritdocuments.org, stotranidhi.com and vignanam.org's 27,096-entry sitemap hold no Godāvarī sahasranāma of any kind — only an eight-verse Godāvarī Aṣṭakam. **This file therefore rests on one edition, and nothing in it has been checked against a second.**

Recension note: **the colophon's attribution is not borne out.** It assigns the work to the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, but three Brahmāṇḍa texts totalling about 5.9 million characters carry nothing of it, while the Gautamī Māhātmya — the body of Godāvarī material the names draw on — sits in the **Brahma** Purāṇa, at its chapters 70 to 175, where GRETIL's text yields 159 mentions of the Gautamī. The attribution is recorded here as the source's own claim and is not repeated as fact.

**On the count: 1001 names, not 1000.** The source's closing numeral is ౧౦౦౦ and its last name is `క్షమాయై`, but the pages yield one name more than the printer counted. Under the standing rule the count reported is the one the source actually yields, and it is not normalised to the famous number. The discrepancy was traced rather than absorbed. The print numbers its names in the margin every ten, and those numerals are demonstrably unreliable: the numeral ౭౮౦ is absent altogether, where it should have fallen on a name that runs over two lines; the compositor then prints the off-round ౭౫౧ and ౮౩౧ to re-synchronise after gaining; and ౮౫౦ is set one line early. Every numeral from ౮౦౦ to ౧౦౦౦ runs one below the true count and was never re-synchronised. Two intervals were re-counted line by line against the page images, distinguishing a new name, which is set flush at the left margin, from a continuation, which is indented: the interval the numerals enclose as sixty holds sixty-one complete names, and the interval they enclose as ten holds eleven. **No name was deleted, merged or invented to reach a round number.**

**109 of the 1001 names carry a reading that could not be settled**, because the lithograph is faulty or worn at that point. They are given as the glyphs stand. Where the printed form yields no Sanskrit word that can be stood behind, the English rendering is left as an em dash rather than filled with a guess — this is the case at 22 names. A number of further names are legible but hard to construe, and those carry a literal rendering marked as uncertain. Nothing was emended.

**Readings that look like printer's errors were reported and left alone.** `कीतिकाकृते` for an expected `कीर्तिकाकृत्`, `षट्वांग` for `खट्वाङ्ग`, `यवाद्य` for `यमाद्य`, and a name in the ḍa-block that begins `दि-` where the alphabetical order requires `डि-`. In an akṣara-ordered nāmāvalī the order is itself an argument, but an argument is not a witness, and with no second edition to check against, every one of these stands as printed.

**Four names are printed without a dative ending** where all their neighbours have one, and two more end in what reads as a masculine dative. They are given as printed and glossed as printed. One of them appears to be a line of verse that has been cut into the list.

**The spelling convention.** The Devanāgarī was converted from a Telugu-script original, so it carries the anusvāra spellings the Telugu print uses (`अनंता`, not `अनन्ता`) throughout, in the names and in the nominative forms alike.

Blurb: When Jupiter enters Siṁha the Puṣkara of the Godāvarī begins, and these are the thousand names by which the river is worshipped through those days. She is called Gautamī here, the river of the sage Gautama. The names are arranged by the alphabet, and they praise her in three ways at once. She is a river — full of waves, cool, swift, holding lotuses, carrying the boats of men. She is the Goddess herself — Gāyatrī, Bhavānī, the mother of Skanda, the power that upholds the three worlds. And she is what bathing in her is held to give — the destroyer of the guilt of the worst deeds, the healer of sickness, the one who grants release. Each name is spoken in the dative, in the manner of an archana, so that the worshipper offers a flower or a grain of rice at each one.
