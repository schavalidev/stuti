Blurb: The second chapter opens the madhyama carita and begins the war with Mahiṣāsura. After a hundred-year battle the gods are beaten, Mahiṣa takes Indra's place and usurps the offices of sun, fire, wind, moon, Yama and Varuṇa, and the dispossessed devas come to Viṣṇu and Śiva. Anger pours out of them as light; the massed tejas of all the gods fuses into a woman, limb by limb, and each god arms her with his own weapon. Her roar shakes the earth and brings Mahiṣa's army out. The rest is the fight itself: Cikṣura, Cāmara, Udagra, Mahāhanu, Asiloma, Bāṣkala, Biḍāla and their numberless troops cut down by trident, mace, sword and noose, until rivers of blood run and only Mahiṣa is left.

Recension note: Verse numbering follows the Saptaśatī's own ritual division, in which standalone "uvāca" speaker-lines are counted as verses and some ślokas are split, so that the thirteen adhyāyas total exactly 700 — the saptaśatī of the title. GRETIL's Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa prints the same words divided into 587 verses across its adhyāyas 81–93, so the two cannot be aligned by verse number and were collated by content. Gītā Press prints the 700-division followed here. This file was re-collated against the printed Gītā Press edition after an earlier round had used only a degraded archive.org OCR of it. That pass confirmed 62 of the 63 readings the OCR round had adopted and reversed one, and corrected further readings, front matter and verse divisions besides; where the print and the other witnesses disagree, the print is followed unless our reading is the one Gītā Press itself prints and the alternative sits in its footnotes. The Gītā Press scan settled more here than its OCR quality would suggest, but almost entirely through its verse-division and its editorial footnotes rather than its letterforms. The Devanāgarī is badly degraded — र/ल and श/ष/स confused, conjuncts collapsed (रुलेन for शूलेन, प्रहासुराः for महासुराः, इतेः for शतैः), visarga and vowel signs unreliable, and the Hindi commentary bleeding into the verse lines — so it was never quoted as clean text and no reading was adopted on a single degraded character alone. What it did settle: its per-verse numbering (अ० २।१ through २।६९) confirms the base's division and its colophon tally उवाच १, श्लोकाः ६८, एवम् ६९, एवमादितः १७३ confirms both the 69-verse count and the cumulative total after chapter 1's 104. Its pāṭhāntara footnotes were decisive at four points: they demote उग्रदर्शनः (v. 43), °वृष्टिभिः (v. 55), तस्यै चर्म (v. 24) and सेनानु°/शल्यानु°/डोलानु° (v. 60), and they place the ततो देवा ददुस्तस्यै … / ऊचुर्जयजयेत्युच्चैः … couplet outside the main text as an addition of some manuscripts — which is precisely the line the base brackets. Three readings were corrected against it (vv. 20, 22, 24 and 60, the first two being one variant repeated). 4 divergence groups were left as printed, being orthographic or damage in the comparison text — v. 43 — परिवारितः vs उग्रदर्शनः: GRETIL's anekair ugradarśanaḥ is exactly the reading Gītā Press records as a pāṭhāntara ("१. पा०—कैरुग्रदर्शनः") while printing अनेकैः परिवारितः in the main text — and it even glosses परिवारितः etymologically in the same footnote. Base kept. Same verse: बाष्कल is the Gītā Press main reading against GRETIL's वाष्कल; v. 55 — शक्तिऋष्टिभिः vs शक्तिवृष्टिभिः: Gītā Press prints the ṛṣṭi (a weapon in the list of trident, mace, ṛṣṭi, sword) and footnotes °वृष्टिभिः as the variant; GRETIL's śaktivṛṣṭibhiḥ with śaḍgādibhiḥ for खड्गादिभिः is the damaged side of the comparison. Base kept; GRETIL's extra half-verses (after v. 45: वृतः कालो रथानां …; after v. 64: रुधिरौघविलुप्ताङ्गाः संग्रामे लोमहर्षणे): Neither appears in the Gītā Press text, whose verse tally of 68 ślokas matches the base's verse division exactly. Base kept; Orthographic and damage-only differences across the chapter: All kept as they stand: anusvāra vs conjunct nasal (सम्मानिता / सम्युगे against GRETIL's saṁ°), the ZWJ inside पट्‍टिश and साट्‍टहास, केसरी vs GRETIL केशरी, and विधा/ज्ञ orthography — pure spelling. The bracketed half-line ततो देवा ददुस्तस्यै … is retained as bracketed: Gītā Press keeps it out of the main text and records it in a footnote as an addition found in some manuscripts (with the following ऊचुर्जयजयेत्युच्चैः … half-line, which GRETIL carries in full). The parallel replacement at v. 22 is वज्रमिन्द्रः समुत्पाद्य कुलिशादमराधिपः ।
ददौ तस्यै सहस्राक्षो घण्टामैरावताद्गजात् ॥; v. 24: Gītā Press has तस्याश्चर्म in the main text and demotes तस्यै चर्म to a pāṭhāntara footnote ("४. पा०— तस्यै चर्म"); GRETIL reads tasyāś carma. Both scan; the edition's own choice settles it; v. 60: The strongest case in the chapter. Gītā Press prints शैलानुकारिणः (the asuras giving up their lives "like mountains") and its footnote lists three demoted variants — पा०—सेनानु°, शल्यानु°, डोलानु° — so the base's सेनानुकारिणः is explicitly the reading it rejects. GRETIL independently reads śailānukāriṇaḥ. Section divisions in the header are editorial — the Sanskrit carries no internal headings — and are given so a passage can be located; the numbered verses, the front matter and the colophon are as printed in the source.
