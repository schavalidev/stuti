Blurb: With Niśumbha slain, Śumbha accuses the Devī of fighting only by the strength of others. She answers that there is no second beside her, and the śaktis led by Brahmāṇī dissolve back into her body, so that Ambikā stands alone. The single combat that follows is fought before the gods and the asuras. He sends showers of arrows and divine astras, which she shatters with a huṅkāra. She cuts away his bow, his śakti, his hundred-mooned sword, his shield, his horses and his charioteer, and then his mudgara. He strikes her breast with his fist, and she fells him with her palm. He seizes her and fights on in mid-air, until she whirls him down and drives her trident through his chest. The earth shakes, the evil omens subside, and the gods and gandharvas rejoice.

Recension note: Verse numbering follows the Saptaśatī's own ritual division, in which standalone "uvāca" speaker-lines are counted as verses and some ślokas are split, so that the thirteen adhyāyas total exactly 700 — the saptaśatī of the title. GRETIL's Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa prints the same words divided into 587 verses across its adhyāyas 81–93, so the two cannot be aligned by verse number and were collated by content. Gītā Press prints the 700-division followed here. This file was re-collated against the printed Gītā Press edition after an earlier round had used only a degraded archive.org OCR of it. That pass confirmed 62 of the 63 readings the OCR round had adopted and reversed one, and corrected further readings, front matter and verse divisions besides; where the print and the other witnesses disagree, the print is followed unless our reading is the one Gītā Press itself prints and the alternative sits in its footnotes. The Gītā Press scan settled a good deal here — this stretch of the OCR is better than most, with the verse text broken cleanly by its (अ० १०।n) verse numbers, so verse division and the chapter's shape could be read off directly, and the colophon tally (उवाच ४, अर्धश्लोकः १, श्लोकाः २७, एवम् ३२, एवमादितः ५७५) came through legibly and confirms the base's 32 verses and its four standalone speaker lines. Its editorial pāṭhāntara footnotes were the decisive evidence in three of the four replacements: at v. 3 it prints बलावलेपाद् दुष्टे and demotes the compound; at v. 16 it prints अभ्यधावत्तदा and demotes 'वत तां हन्तुं'; at v. 25 it prints वेगितः and demotes वेगवान्; and at v. 14 it prints सापि while demoting 'सा च', which is what saves the base there. The Devanāgarī itself remains unusable as text — खड्ग appears as खड, शूल as श्यूल/शुक, अस्त्र as अखर, ब्रह्माणी and भानुमत् lines mangled, and the Hindi footnote apparatus bleeds into the verse block — so it was read for readings only and is nowhere quoted.
