Author: Traditionally ascribed to Mārkaṇḍeya, as Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa adhyāyas 81-93; the narration is nested — Mārkaṇḍeya to his pupil Krauṣṭuki, and within that the ṛṣi Medhas to King Suratha and the merchant Samādhi — and this chapter, with 4, 5 and 11, forms the Adhyāya Catuṣṭayam, the four hymn-bearing chapters the tradition holds may be recited without upadeśa, on ordinary śuci alone.

Blurb: The first carita opens the Devī Māhātmyam by asking how Sāvarṇi became the eighth Manu, and answers with a frame story: King Suratha, stripped of his kingdom by enemies and then by his own ministers, meets Samādhi, a merchant cast out by wife and sons — and both find they still love what has ruined them. The ṛṣi Medhas names that pull Mahāmāyā, and illustrates her with the cosmogonic tale of Viṣṇu asleep in yoganidrā at the dissolution, Brahmā threatened by Madhu and Kaiṭabha, and Brahmā's hymn rousing the goddess so that Hari wakes and slays them. That hymn (vv. 72-87) circulates separately as the Tantrokta Rātri Sūktam, catalogued in this corpus as file 14.

hi: ऐसा ज्ञान होने पर भी इन पक्षियों को देखो — स्वयं भूख से पीड़ित होते हुए भी मोहवश अपने बच्चों की चोंचों में दाने डालने में ही लगे रहते हैं।

hi: फिर भी वे ममता के भँवर में, मोह के गड्ढे में गिरा दिए जाते हैं — संसार की स्थिति बनाए रखने वाली महामाया के प्रभाव से।

--- verse 54 | section: Medhas teaches Mahāmāyā ---

iast:

en: So no wonder need be felt at this: she is the Yoganidrā of the lord of the world, she is the Mahāmāyā of Hari, and by her the world is thrown into delusion.
tel: కాబట్టి దీనికి ఆశ్చర్యపడవలసిన పని లేదు: ఆమె జగత్పతి యొక్క యోగనిద్ర, ఆమెయే హరి యొక్క మహామాయ; ఆమెచేతనే ఈ జగత్తు మోహంలో ముంచబడుతున్నది.
hi: इसमें आश्चर्य नहीं करना चाहिए: वह जगत्पति की योगनिद्रा है, वही हरि की महामाया है; उसी के द्वारा यह सारा जगत् मोह में डाल दिया जाता है।

--- verse 55 | section: Medhas teaches Mahāmāyā ---

hi: वही भगवती देवी — महामाया है; वह ज्ञानियों के चित्त को भी बलपूर्वक खींचकर मोह के अधीन कर देती है।

--- verse 56 | section: Medhas teaches Mahāmāyā ---

hi: चराचर यह सम्पूर्ण विश्व उसी के द्वारा रचा जाता है; और वही प्रसन्न होने पर वरदायिनी बनकर मनुष्यों की मुक्ति का कारण होती है।

Recension note: Verse numbering follows the Saptaśatī's own ritual division, in which standalone "uvāca" speaker-lines are counted as verses and some ślokas are split, so that the thirteen adhyāyas total exactly 700 — the saptaśatī of the title. GRETIL's Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa prints the same words divided into 587 verses across its adhyāyas 81–93, so the two cannot be aligned by verse number and were collated by content. Gītā Press prints the 700-division followed here. This file was re-collated against the printed Gītā Press edition after an earlier round had used only a degraded archive.org OCR of it. That pass confirmed 62 of the 63 readings the OCR round had adopted and reversed one, and corrected further readings, front matter and verse divisions besides; where the print and the other witnesses disagree, the print is followed unless our reading is the one Gītā Press itself prints and the alternative sits in its footnotes. The Gītā Press scan carries the full chapter with the viniyoga, dhyāna, bīja and per-verse (अ० १।n) references, plus its own editorial footnotes recording pāṭhāntaras — and those footnotes turned out to be the single most useful thing in the file, settling ममत्वाकृष्टचेतनः/मानसः, यत्प्रभावा/यत्स्वभावा, and the disputed प्रीतौ स्वस्तव युद्धेन half-verse outright. The OCR itself is badly degraded — conjuncts and vowel signs are routinely wrong (सुनिना for मुनिना, पनुः for मनुः, कोडो for कोशो, शुरहस्ती with the vowel lost), visarga and daṇḍa drop out, and the Devanāgarī of the interleaved Hindi commentary bleeds into the verse text — so it was read for readings and verse division only, never quoted as clean text, and a handful of loci (notably हार्दी at v. 42 and the शूरो हस्ती word-division) were too mangled to settle and fell back on the vulgate. 7 divergence groups were left as printed, being orthographic or damage in the comparison text — v. 13, शूरो हस्ती vs śūrahastī: Gītā Press and GRETIL both compound it (शूरहस्ती); the base splits it. This is word-division/sandhi orthography, not a difference of text or sense — kept per house rule, and flagged here only so the compound reading is on record; vv. 51-52, महामाया हरेश्चैषा तया सम्मोह्यते जगत्: GRETIL's हरेश्चैतत्तथा संमोह्यते जगत् is an isolated variant. The base agrees with the Saptaśatī vulgate and with the Gītā Press text, and this is one of the chapter's most-quoted lines; v. 57, यत्प्रभावा च सा देवी: GRETIL reads यत्स्वभावा. Gītā Press prints प्रभावा in the main text and records स्वभावा only as a footnoted pāṭhāntara (its footnote also records कर्म चास्याश्च for the preceding pāda). The base follows the main reading; v. 75, त्वयैतद्धार्यते विश्वं त्वयैतत्सृज्यते जगत्: GRETIL reads त्वयैव धार्यते सर्वं. The Gītā Press line is legible and matches the base exactly, so this settles in the base's favour; and so on. 3 readings were corrected — v. 12: Gītā Press prints ममत्वाकृष्टचेतनः in the main text and relegates ममत्वाकृष्टमानसः to a footnoted pāṭhāntara; GRETIL independently reads cetanaḥ. Two independent witnesses, one of them the authority edition, against the base. Note that मानसः is genuine and widely circulated — it also stands undisputed at v. 43, ममत्वाकृष्टमानसौ, where all three witnesses agree; v. 49: Gītā Press reads किं तु (OCR 'कि तु ते नहि केवलम्', legible), as does GRETIL. किं तु is also the better sense — 'men are knowers, true, but they are not the only ones' — where किं नु leaves the adversative unmarked before the following यतो हि; v. 98: Gītā Press reads एतावद्धि वृतं मम, and GRETIL agrees. मया is the easier reading and looks like a grammatical smoothing of मम; the harder reading with the authority edition behind it is preferred. Part of this chapter also exists in the corpus as a standalone stotra — `devi/durga/14_tantrokta_ratri_suktam.txt` (Brahmā's stuti of Yoganidrā) — because the tradition recites and anthologises it separately as well as in situ. Neither file supersedes the other; the renderings were aligned so the two do not disagree on the same verse. Section divisions in the header are editorial — the Sanskrit carries no internal headings — and are given so a passage can be located; the numbered verses, the front matter and the colophon are as printed in the source.
