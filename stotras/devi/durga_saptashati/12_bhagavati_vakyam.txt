Blurb: With Śumbha and Niśumbha slain, the Devī answers the gods' hymn in her own voice, and this chapter is her speech. She states the terms on which her Māhātmyam works. Recited on the eighth, ninth and fourteenth days, at the autumn festival, and at offerings, fire-rites and processions, it dissolves debt, plague, evil portent, hostile planets, bad dreams, the possession of children, and the strife that splits a clan. She promises her presence wherever it is read, and rescue for anyone who remembers it in forest, in shipwreck, in prison or in battle. She then disappears before the assembled gods. The gods take back their shares of the sacrifice, and the surviving daityas flee to Pātāla. Medhas closes by naming her as Mahākālī: the making, the keeping and the unmaking of the world, Lakṣmī in the house and Alakṣmī in its ruin.
