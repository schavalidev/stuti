Blurb: With Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa slain, Śumbha orders out every remaining clan of the daityas, and the Devī answers with her bowstring and her lion. Here the śaktis of Brahmā, Śiva, Skanda, Viṣṇu, Varāha, Narasiṁha and Indra step out of the gods' bodies, in the gods' own forms and shapes. Śiva himself is sent as envoy, and this earns the Devī the name Śivadūtī. The mātṛs scatter the host until Raktabīja comes. Every drop of his blood that touches the ground stands up as another Raktabīja, so that wounding him only multiplies him. The turn comes when Cāmuṇḍā widens her mouth. She drinks the blood before it falls, and the bloodless demon dies.

Recension note: Verse numbering follows the Saptaśatī's own ritual division, in which standalone "uvāca" speaker-lines are counted as verses and some ślokas are split, so that the thirteen adhyāyas total exactly 700 — the saptaśatī of the title. GRETIL's Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa prints the same words divided into 587 verses across its adhyāyas 81–93, so the two cannot be aligned by verse number and were collated by content. Gītā Press prints the 700-division followed here. This file was re-collated against the printed Gītā Press edition after an earlier round had used only a degraded archive.org OCR of it. That pass confirmed 62 of the 63 readings the OCR round had adopted and reversed one, and corrected further readings, front matter and verse divisions besides; where the print and the other witnesses disagree, the print is followed unless our reading is the one Gītā Press itself prints and the alternative sits in its footnotes. The Gītā Press scan settled more here than its OCR quality would suggest. The Devanāgarī verse body is heavily degraded — conjuncts, vowel signs and visarga are unreliable (सेन्य for सैन्य, राक्ति for शक्ति, मातुः for मातॄः, रास्र for शास्त्र), the running heads and the interleaved Hindi commentary bleed into the verse column, and no line of it can be quoted as clean text. But two features survived legibly and did the real work. First, the per-verse markers "(अ० ८। N)" printed after each verse gave the edition's own verse division intact, letting the 63 units of the base be aligned one-to-one against GP, and the closing tally (उवाच १, अर्धश्लोकः १, श्लोकाः ६१, एवम् ६३, एवमादितः ५०२) confirmed both the count and the half-verse at v. 57. Second, the five pāṭhāntara footnotes — at vv. 9, 18 (two), 28, 61 and 62 — are decisive, because each names a variant GP put *below* the line, and in four of those places the demoted variant is exactly GRETIL's reading; that turns a dozen apparent disagreements into settled questions in the base's favour. Only at v. 9 does a GP footnote run the other way, demoting the base's तान्नादम्, and at v. 15 GP's main text sides with GRETIL with no footnote at all. Those two are the chapter's only substantive changes. 3 divergence groups were left as printed, being orthographic or damage in the comparison text — Four readings GP explicitly demotes to pāṭhāntara footnotes (vv. 18, 28, 62): GRETIL's divergences here are precisely the variants Gītā Press prints in its footnotes while keeping the base reading in the main text: v. 18 यज्ञवाराह॰ (fn. जज्ञे वाराह॰) and बिभ्रतो (fn. ती); v. 28 जग्मुर्यत्र (fn. जग्मुर्यतः); v. 62 शस्त्रसङ्घसमाहतः (fn. शास्त्रसंहतितो हतः). The authority puts our base in the text and GRETIL's reading below the line, so all four stand; vv. 16, 36, 47, 61 — candrarekhā, digambarā, gadayā tāḍayāmāsa, vajreṇa: GP's main text legibly supports the base at each: चन्दरेखा॰ (v. 16, vs GRETIL candralekhā), नादापूर्णदिगम्बरा (v. 36, vs digantarā), गदया ताडयामास (v. 47, vs dāḍayāmāsa — GRETIL's form is not a word), and देवी शूलेन वज्रेण (v. 61), where GP puts चक्रेण in a footnote as the demoted variant. GP also footnotes at v. 61 that some copies insert an extra ऋषिरुवाच line there; it is not in GP's own text and is not added; Bulk orthographic and damage-only divergences across the chapter: The remaining collation_diffs are either corpus orthography (anusvāra for conjunct nasal, ś/ṣ, avagraha and sandhi splitting, the ZWJ in चण्डाट्टहास) or plain corruption in GRETIL's file that never appears in Gītā Press: bhiṣaṇam, bhoṣaṇaiḥ, devo śarīrāt, vaiṣṇāvī/veṣṇāvī, kaumāro, susutrāva/sustrāva, mātṛbir, buvi, soṇitam, gadāpāṇipa, loke'smiṁmas, mātaḥ for मातॄः, sahasraśau. None is a Sanskrit form; the base is kept throughout. 2 readings were corrected — v. 9: Gītā Press prints तन्नादम् in the main text and demotes the base's reading to a footnote (२. पा०—तान्नादानम्बिका), and GRETIL independently has tannādam. Sense confirms it: the Devī swells *that* roar (the lion's, just mentioned) with her bell. Only the pronoun is adopted; GP keeps चोपबृंहयत् against GRETIL's cāpyabṛṁhayat, so the base's second half stands. GP's footnote १ (पा०—स च) shows तत: सिंहो is GP's main text and GRETIL's sa ca siṁho is the demoted variant — base kept there; v. 15: Both witnesses agree against the base: Gītā Press's main text reads साभिधीयते (legible even through the OCR damage, ब्रह्माणी सा॰), with no pāṭhāntara footnote offered, and GRETIL has brahmāṇī sābhidhīyate. The base's iti-clause is the lectio facilior; सा resumes शक्तिः naturally and the metre is unaffected. Section divisions in the header are editorial — the Sanskrit carries no internal headings — and are given so a passage can be located; the numbered verses, the front matter and the colophon are as printed in the source.
