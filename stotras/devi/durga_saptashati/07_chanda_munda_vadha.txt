Blurb: Śumbha's generals Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa lead a four-fold army up the golden peak to seize the Devī, who sits on her lion faintly smiling. Her anger blackens her face, and from her knotted brow Kālī steps out — gaunt, tiger-skinned, garlanded with heads, sword and noose in hand. She eats the army: elephants with their riders and bells thrown into her mouth, horses, chariots, charioteers, weapons crunched between her teeth. Muṇḍa buries her under thousands of discs; she laughs and rides down Caṇḍa, shearing off his head, then strikes Muṇḍa to the ground. Carrying both heads to Caṇḍikā, she is told the killing of Śumbha and Niśumbha is the Devī's own, and is given the name Cāmuṇḍā.

hi: ओम्। इस प्रकार श्रीमार्कण्डेयपुराण के सावर्णिक मन्वन्तर में देवीमाहात्म्य का "चण्ड-मुण्ड वध" नामक सप्तम अध्याय समाप्त हुआ।

Recension note: Verse numbering follows the Saptaśatī's own ritual division, in which standalone "uvāca" speaker-lines are counted as verses and some ślokas are split, so that the thirteen adhyāyas total exactly 700 — the saptaśatī of the title. GRETIL's Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa prints the same words divided into 587 verses across its adhyāyas 81–93, so the two cannot be aligned by verse number and were collated by content. Gītā Press prints the 700-division followed here. This file was re-collated against the printed Gītā Press edition after an earlier round had used only a degraded archive.org OCR of it. That pass confirmed 62 of the 63 readings the OCR round had adopted and reversed one, and corrected further readings, front matter and verse divisions besides; where the print and the other witnesses disagree, the print is followed unless our reading is the one Gītā Press itself prints and the alternative sits in its footnotes. The Gītā Press pages for adhyāya 7 are legible enough to be decisive on structure and on several readings, though never quotable as clean text: conjuncts and vowel signs are routinely mangled (काल्ीमतिभीषणाम्, दषा for दृष्ट्वा, शुष्णं for शुम्भं, दरीपिचर्म for द्वीपिचर्म), and the Hindi commentary bleeds in. What it does settle: its per-verse numbers (अ० ७।२ through ७।२७) confirm the 27-verse division against GRETIL's 26, and its closing tally उवाच २, श्लोकाः २५, एवम् २७, एवमादितः ४२३ confirms the count exactly. Its footnotes were the most valuable part — one demotes GRETIL's रणे at v15 to a pāṭhāntara, and another confines the extra śloka छिन्ने शिरसि दैत्येन्द्रः… to the Śāntanavī commentator rather than the text. It is also readable enough to back °ग्राहि at v10, करालवक्त्रान्तर् at v19, and vocative देवि at v27. It could not adjudicate anusvāra, visarga or ś/ṣ anywhere, and the dhyāna printed above the chapter is too damaged to transcribe. 5 divergence groups were left as printed, being orthographic or damage in the comparison text — v15b दन्ताग्राभिहतास्तथा / °हता रणे: GRETIL's रणे is exactly the pāṭhāntara Gītā Press records in a footnote ("पा०—ता रणे") while printing °हतास्तथा in its main text. The authority demotes it; base stands; v16 क्षणेन तद्बलं सर्वम् / तन्महासैन्यम्, and the following half-line: GRETIL's तन्महासैन्यम् and its दृष्ट्वा चण्डोऽभिमैर्भोमाक्षीं… are a variant plus an eye-skip that duplicates v17's शरवर्षैर्महाभीमैर्भीमाक्षीं. Gītā Press prints क्षणेन तद् बलं सर्वम्… दृष्ट्वा चण्डोऽभिदुद्राव तां कालीमतिभीषणाम्, i.e. the base; Extra śloka after v20 (छिन्ने शिरसि दैत्येन्द्रश्चक्रे नादं सुभैरवम्…): GRETIL carries this line as its verse 20. Gītā Press confines it to a footnote, noting only that the Śāntanavī commentator counted one śloka more here; it is not in the printed text and its inclusion would break the 27-verse tally the colophon states. Not admitted; GRETIL corruptions: भोमाक्षीं for भीमाक्षीं (v17), घतयन्ती for घातयन्ती (v9), शस्त्रणि for शस्त्राणि (v13), चणाडमुण्डौ for चण्डमुण्डौ (v26), शिरञ्चण्डस्य for शिरश्चण्डस्य (v23), सत्समीपगाः for तत्समीपगाः (v4): Non-words or plain typing damage in the comparison text; none is a Sanskrit form. Gītā Press supports the base at every one of these points where its OCR is legible; and so on. 3 readings were corrected — v. 10: Gītā Press main text and GRETIL agree on °ग्राहि°, the regular in-compound form of the agent noun grāhin ("the fighters who hold the goad and the rear"); the base's °ग्राह° leaves the compound with a bare stem. Genuine improvement with GP behind it; v. 19: Both Gītā Press and GRETIL read करालवक्त्रान्तर्दुर्दर्शदशनोज्ज्वला — "blazing with teeth terrible to look on inside her gaping mouth", which alone gives दुर्दर्शदशनोज्ज्वला something to sit in. The base's करालवदना is a mechanical repetition of v6a; v. 27: Vocative देवि, read by Gītā Press and GRETIL alike: Caṇḍikā is addressing Kālī directly (भविष्यसि, 2nd person). The base's nominative देवी cannot construe with a second-person verb. Section divisions in the header are editorial — the Sanskrit carries no internal headings — and are given so a passage can be located; the numbered verses, the front matter and the colophon are as printed in the source.
