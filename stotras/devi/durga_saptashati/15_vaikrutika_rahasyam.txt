Blurb: This is the second of the three Rahasyams appended to the Saptaśatī, and it is doctrinal rather than narrative. Having set out the Devī's undifferentiated prakṛti, the ṛṣi now describes her vikṛtis, that is, the forms she takes on. Mahākālī is tāmasī, with ten faces and ten arms; she is the Yoganidrā whom Brahmā praised. Mahālakṣmī rose from the bodies of all the gods and has eighteen arms; her weapons are listed in order, beginning from the lower right hand. Mahāsarasvatī was born of Gaurī's body and has eight arms; she killed Śumbha and Niśumbha. The rest of the chapter is practical. It states where each image stands, which pairs and attendant deities accompany which central form, what is offered, how the caritas are recited, and what daily worship brings, and what its neglect brings.

Recension note: The three Rahasyams — Prādhānika, Vaikṛtika and Mūrti — are appended to the Saptaśatī and stand outside the Devī Māhātmya's thirteen adhyāyas and its seven-hundred-verse count. They are doctrinal rather than narrative, expounding the Devī's prakṛti, her vikṛtis and her mūrtis, and share a single viniyoga covering the rahasya-traya as a set. Recensions of these three diverge more than the Māhātmya's own text does, so Gītā Press was given decisive weight. This file was re-collated against the printed Gītā Press edition after an earlier round had used only a degraded archive.org OCR of it. That pass confirmed 62 of the 63 readings the OCR round had adopted and reversed one, and corrected further readings, front matter and verse divisions besides; where the print and the other witnesses disagree, the print is followed unless our reading is the one Gītā Press itself prints and the alternative sits in its footnotes. The scan is the same badly degraded run as elsewhere: vowel signs and conjuncts are unreliable (सात्विको for सात्त्विकी, हा्वां for शर्वा, दरापादाञ्चनपभा for daśapādāñjanaprabhā, विराल्या for viśālayā), visarga and anusvāra come and go, and running heads, page numbers and stray marginal glyphs drop into the verse block.
