Title: Śrī Durgā Saptaśatī — Prādhānika Rahasyam
Devanāgarī: श्री दुर्गा सप्तशती — प्राधानिकं रहस्यम्
Telugu: శ్రీ దుర్గా సప్తశతీ — ప్రాధానికం రహస్యమ్

Author: No author is named. The Prādhānika Rahasyam is the first of the three Rahasyams appended to the Durgā Saptaśatī and transmitted with the Devī Māhātmya of the Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa, though it stands outside the Purāṇa proper; it continues the Māhātmya's own frame, the ṛṣi (Medhas) answering King Suratha, here on the Devī's prakṛti — her primordial nature — as the Vaikṛtika and Mūrti Rahasyams continue with her vikṛtis and her mūrtis. The viniyoga of this base text assigns the ṛṣitva of the three Rahasyams to Nārāyaṇa; the sanskritdocuments witness instead names Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Rudra as ṛṣis, which is itself liturgical convention rather than an attribution of authorship.

Blurb: Suratha, having heard of the Devī's deeds, now asks what she actually is: which form he should worship, and by what rite. The ṛṣi answers with doctrine rather than with story. Mahālakṣmī is the primordial threefold Parameśvarī, both perceptible and imperceptible. She fills the void with her own light. From tamas she produces a dark woman with four arms, whom she names Mahākālī, and from purified sattva a woman bright as the moon, whom she names Mahāsarasvatī. The three then generate three couples: Brahmā and Trayī, Rudra and Gaurī, and Viṣṇu and Śrī. Their names are listed and their consorts assigned, and from their union the cosmic egg and all creation follow. The text closes by refusing to give her any single name.

Recension note: The three Rahasyams — Prādhānika, Vaikṛtika and Mūrti — are appended to the Saptaśatī and stand outside the Devī Māhātmya's thirteen adhyāyas and its seven-hundred-verse count. They are doctrinal rather than narrative, expounding the Devī's prakṛti, her vikṛtis and her mūrtis, and share a single viniyoga covering the rahasya-traya as a set. Recensions of these three diverge more than the Māhātmya's own text does, so Gītā Press was given decisive weight. This file was re-collated against the printed Gītā Press edition after an earlier round had used only a degraded archive.org OCR of it. That pass confirmed 62 of the 63 readings the OCR round had adopted and reversed one, and corrected further readings, front matter and verse divisions besides; where the print and the other witnesses disagree, the print is followed unless our reading is the one Gītā Press itself prints and the alternative sits in its footnotes. The scan covers this Rahasyam from verse 17 to the closing इति प्राधानिकं रहस्यं सम्पूर्णम् ।, then runs on into the Vaikṛtika Rahasyam; verses 1-16 are on a page not present in the OCR, so the first half of the text had no Gītā Press check at all. Where it is present the Devanāgarī is badly degraded — श्रियम्/स्त्रियम् appears as च्रियम्, खियम् and स्त्रियं indifferently, प for म and र्य for श्य throughout, and सत्त्व prints as सत्व — so it was read only for readings and division, never quoted. What it did settle: the nominative महाकाली at v. 20, the word order of v. 18d, नु पश्यन्ति at v. 24, and शिवा against the second witness's शुभा at v. 23. What it could not settle: v. 21d, where its own line is too corrupt to trust. Its most consequential contribution is structural rather than textual — its parenthesised verse numbers are legible where the verse text is not, and they run to 31 against this recension's 29, exposing a half-line (संजहार जगत्सर्वं सह गौर्या महेश्वरः) and a variant v. 28cd that the second witness also lacks. No editorial pāṭhāntara footnotes ("१. पा०—…") appear on these pages. 5 divergence groups were left as printed, being orthographic or damage in the comparison text — v. 21d — भाषा स्वराक्षरा (base) vs Gītā Press's apparent भाषाक्षरा स्वरा: The Gītā Press line here is among the worst-damaged in the scan (खा सरी for सा स्त्री in the same pāda), so its word order cannot be trusted against a metrically and syntactically sound base reading. Kept, flagged as unresolved; vv. 5, 18, 23, 26 — substantive second-witness variants: मातुलिङ्गं for मातुलुङ्गं, विरञ्च- for विरिञ्च- (twice), शुभा for शिवा: All four are sanskritdocuments singulars against Gītā Press, which prints मातुलुङ्गं (the citron, the standard attribute), विरिञ्च- and शिवा. शुभा for शिवा in a Devī name-list is the only tempting one and is still isolated. Base kept throughout; Anusvāra and vowel-length trivia across vv. 6, 8, 10, 13, 20, 29 — dropped ṁ in दंष्ट्रा-, तामसीं, महाकालीं, नामान्तरैः, रक्तबाहुं, चन्द्र-; तप्तकंचन for तप्तकाञ्चन; इन्दुप्रभ्रं for इन्दुप्रभं: Every one is a dropped nasal, a lost vowel sign, or an intrusive र् in the comparison text — damage and orthographic noise, not readings. Grouped and kept; Front matter and colophon present in the second witness only: sanskritdocuments prefixes a Gaṇeśa salutation and a longer viniyoga (ṛṣis Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Rudra; navadurgā-mahālakṣmī bīja; śrīṁ śakti) and appends the Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa colophon. The base already carries a viniyoga for the Rahasya-traya; the divergent one is recorded here rather than substituted, and the colophon corresponds to Gītā Press's इति प्राधानिकं रहस्यं सम्पूर्णम् ।; and so on. 3 readings were corrected — v. 18: Gītā Press's line is legible enough here (माता च तां खियम् in the OCR, the ख a routine स्त्र misread) and gives the smoother pāda; adopted on its authority. Sense is unchanged; v. 20: Mahākālī is the subject of जनयामास, generating the blue-throated man and a white woman; the base's accusative makes her her own product. Gītā Press prints the nominative and sanskritdocuments agrees. A clear grammatical correction, not orthography; v. 24: Gītā Press reads नु as a separate particle (OCR चक्षुष्मन्तो नु परयन्ति; र्य for श्य is its standard confusion, but the free-standing नु is not the sort of thing OCR invents). Gītā Press outranks the second witness, which agrees with the base. Sense is effectively the same — only the seeing perceive it.
