Blurb: The Devī Māhātmyam closes by returning to the two men of its opening frame. Medhas ends his teaching: the same Viṣṇumāyā that upholds the world is what deludes king and merchant and the discerning alike, and the one remedy is refuge in her, who when worshipped gives enjoyment, heaven and release. Suratha and Samādhi go to a river sandbank, make an earthen image, and worship three years without food, offering blood drawn from their own bodies; Caṇḍikā appears and tells them to ask. The king asks his kingdom back, unfailing here and in the next birth; the merchant, done with "mine" and "I", asks only for the knowledge that cuts attachment. Both are granted, and Suratha is promised rebirth from the Sun as Sāvarṇi Manu.

Recension note: Verse numbering follows the Saptaśatī's own ritual division, in which standalone "uvāca" speaker-lines are counted as verses and some ślokas are split, so that the thirteen adhyāyas total exactly 700 — the saptaśatī of the title. GRETIL's Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa prints the same words divided into 587 verses across its adhyāyas 81–93, so the two cannot be aligned by verse number and were collated by content. Gītā Press prints the 700-division followed here. This file was re-collated against the printed Gītā Press edition after an earlier round had used only a degraded archive.org OCR of it. That pass confirmed 62 of the 63 readings the OCR round had adopted and reversed one, and corrected further readings, front matter and verse divisions besides; where the print and the other witnesses disagree, the print is followed unless our reading is the one Gītā Press itself prints and the alternative sits in its footnotes. The Gītā Press pages for adhyāya 93 came through unusually well by the standards of this scan, and settled more here than in most chapters. Its verse division matches the base exactly — 29 units, with the same six standalone उवाच lines and the same eleven half-verses standing alone — and its own colophon tally (उवाच ६, अर्धश्लोकाः ११, श्लोकाः १२, एवम् २९, एवमादितः ७००) independently confirms both the chapter count and the seven-hundred total; the "११" and one "१३" chapter reference are OCR'd as "१९"/"९३", but the arithmetic is unambiguous. Its footnote apparatus was decisive at v23, where "१. पा०—मनुर्नाम।" shows it puts सावर्णिको नाम मनुर्भवान् in the main text and demotes the base's order, and it is clear enough at v9 to confirm संस्थितः. Elsewhere the Devanāgarī is the usual wreck — पहाभागं, तपुषिं, रोसितत्रतम्, वैद्यो/वेरयश्च for वैश्य-, मूर्वि महीयीम्, यताहारौ, पुष्पधूपाथितर्पणः — so it could not arbitrate v8 (संशित/शंसित) and was read for readings and division only, never quoted.
