Blurb: With Mahiṣa's army broken, his commanders come at the Devī one by one. Cikṣura empties his quiver, loses horses, bow, banner and finally his sword-arm, and dies on his own hurled spear; Cāmara rides in on an elephant and is torn down by the lion; Udagra, Karāla, Bāṣkala, Andhaka, Ugrāsya, Biḍāla, Durdhara and Durmukha follow. Then Mahiṣa himself takes buffalo shape, trampling and goring the gaṇas, flinging mountains, lashing the sea with his tail. The turn comes when Ambikā ropes him: bound, he starts changing form — lion, swordsman, elephant, buffalo again — and she cuts each away, drinks, laughs, and pins his throat underfoot as he is half out of the buffalo mouth, beheading him there.

Recension note: Verse numbering follows the Saptaśatī's own ritual division, in which standalone "uvāca" speaker-lines are counted as verses and some ślokas are split, so that the thirteen adhyāyas total exactly 700 — the saptaśatī of the title. GRETIL's Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa prints the same words divided into 587 verses across its adhyāyas 81–93, so the two cannot be aligned by verse number and were collated by content. Gītā Press prints the 700-division followed here. This file was re-collated against the printed Gītā Press edition after an earlier round had used only a degraded archive.org OCR of it. That pass confirmed 62 of the 63 readings the OCR round had adopted and reversed one, and corrected further readings, front matter and verse divisions besides; where the print and the other witnesses disagree, the print is followed unless our reading is the one Gītā Press itself prints and the alternative sits in its footnotes. The Gītā Press scan settled more here than usual, because this chapter's decisive evidence lies in its footnotes rather than in its degraded Devanāgarī. Four pāṭhāntara notes are cleanly legible and each one names which reading Gorakhpur puts in the verse and which it demotes: तेन तच्छतधा नीतं at v10, खण्डखण्डं at v27, and the two blocks of surplus ślokas (कालं च कालदण्डेन ... after v20, एवं स महिषो नाम ... after v42) marked as found only in some manuscripts — all four of which run against GRETIL and confirm or correct the base. The verse body itself is unusable as text: conjuncts and vowel signs are wrong throughout (शक्तिं appears as रक्ते, शूलं as श्यूलं, हुङ्कार as हुकार, शिरश्छित्त्वा as शिररिछन््वा), whole words drop out of v36, and the देव्युवाच line vanished entirely; it was read for readings and verse division only, never quoted. 4 divergence groups were left as printed, being orthographic or damage in the comparison text — The two extra ślokas after v20 (कालं च कालदण्डेन ... जयक्ष्वेडाकृतोत्सवैः): GRETIL carries these lines inside its vv. 19-20. Gītā Press does not: it prints them only in a footnote, prefaced 'इसके बाद किसी-किसी प्रतिमें ... ये दो श्लोक अधिक हैं'. As the authority puts them outside the text, the base's omission stands — and keeping them out is what makes the 44-verse tally come out; The two extra ślokas after v42 (एवं स महिषो नाम ... सदेवासुरमानवैः): Same verdict on the same grounds: GRETIL prints them as its vv. 41-42, Gītā Press relegates them to a footnote as a 'किसी-किसी प्रतिमें' surplus. The base text rightly moves straight from the beheading to ततो हाहाकृतं सर्वं; Orthographic and sandhi trivia across the chapter: Kept as mere spelling variance, all with Gītā Press behind the base: अवलोक्य (not GRETIL's avalākya), छिन्नधन्वानम् without post-vocalic doubling, तुण्डप्रहारेण (GRETIL's prihāreṇa is a typo), चान्याञ्छृङ्गाभ्यां with the base's correct sandhi against GRETIL's split cānyān śṛṅgā-, निःश्वास- with visarga, उच्चांश्चिक्षेप, आपतन्तं, विषाणाभ्यां (GRETIL's viṣāṇābyāṁ is not a word), and the anusvāra-vs-conjunct-nasal alternation throughout; v15 युद्ध्यमानौ / yudhāyamāno: GRETIL's yudhāyamāno with a following masculine singular is ungrammatical beside तौ ... गतौ; the base's dual युद्ध्यमानौ is required by the syntax and is what the Gītā Press line, damaged as its OCR is, evidently reads. 6 readings were corrected — v. 10: The base text carries शतधा तेन शूलं स च महासुरः in v10 and then hangs the variant तेन तच्छतधा नीतं as a bracketed extra line on v11. Gītā Press settles this: its main text reads नीतं in the verse itself, and demotes तेन तच्छतधा नीतं to the footnote (१. पा०—तेन तच्छतधा नीतं). GRETIL agrees with the main reading. Adopting GP's main text also clears the intrusive bracketed line off v11 — see the paired verdict below; v. 11: Consequent on the v10 verdict: with the pāṭhāntara restored to its footnote status, v11 stands as the clean two-line śloka Gītā Press and GRETIL both print.
