Blurb: Suratha, having heard how Raktabīja fell, asks what Śumbha and Niśumbha did next, and Medhas answers with the death of the younger brother. Niśumbha charges with the main asura force, Śumbha behind him with his own; the two brothers and the Devī trade arrow-storms like rain-clouds. Niśumbha wounds her lion, and she then cuts away everything he raises — sword, eight-mooned shield, śakti, spear, mace, axe — until he drops. Śumbha rushes in eight-armed on his chariot; conch, bowstring, bell, lion and Kālī and Śivadūtī answer him with noise, and a hurled śakti is turned aside. He falls stunned; Niśumbha revives, fights again, and is speared through the heart. A second man steps out of the wound crying halt, and the Devī laughs and takes his head. The Mātṛs then destroy what is left of the army.

Recension note: Verse numbering follows the Saptaśatī's own ritual division, in which standalone "uvāca" speaker-lines are counted as verses and some ślokas are split, so that the thirteen adhyāyas total exactly 700 — the saptaśatī of the title. GRETIL's Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa prints the same words divided into 587 verses across its adhyāyas 81–93, so the two cannot be aligned by verse number and were collated by content. Gītā Press prints the 700-division followed here. This file was re-collated against the printed Gītā Press edition after an earlier round had used only a degraded archive.org OCR of it. That pass confirmed 62 of the 63 readings the OCR round had adopted and reversed one, and corrected further readings, front matter and verse divisions besides; where the print and the other witnesses disagree, the print is followed unless our reading is the one Gītā Press itself prints and the alternative sits in its footnotes. The Gītā Press scan settled a great deal here — unusually much for this corpus. Its Devanāgarī OCR for chapter 9 is degraded in the expected ways (शुम्भ appears as चुम्भ/जुम्भ/गुण्भ, निशुम्भ as निरुम्भ/निदुम्भ, शूल as रुठ/श्यूल, visarga and vowel signs unreliable), so it was read for readings and verse division and never quoted as clean text. But three things came through cleanly. First, its own colophon tally — उवाच २, श्लोकाः ३९, एवम् ४१, एवमादितः ५४२ — confirms the 41-verse division and the counting of both uvāca lines, matching the base exactly and against GRETIL's 39. Second, its verse boundaries are legible throughout and confirm that निशुम्भो निशितं खड्गं and ताडिते वाहने देवी are two separate verses, exposing GRETIL's repetition at its 9/10 as dittography. Third, and most valuable, four of its editorial pāṭhāntara footnotes fall exactly on disputed loci and state which reading it puts in the main text: आयान्तं demoted at v. 14, अथादाय demoted at v. 15, दोग्रदंष्ट्रा demoted at v. 37, खण्डखण्ड demoted at v. 40. Two of those confirm the base and two overturn it, which is the cleanest kind of evidence this edition can give. It also carries the plain reading हृदयान्निःसृतोऽपरः at v. 35, where the base has simply lost a word. Two further footnotes (आशु शरोत्करैः at v. 10, तथोपदिशो at v. 21) record variants the base does not share and needed no action. 4 divergence groups were left as printed, being orthographic or damage in the comparison text — vv. 1, 4 — the standalone speaker lines राजोवाच and ऋषिरुवाच, absent from GRETIL: GRETIL simply does not number uvāca lines; Gītā Press numbers both (उवाच २ in its own colophon tally) and its numbering runs 1 = राजोवाच, 4 = ऋषिरुवाच. The base agrees with Gītā Press and with the 700-verse convention this corpus follows; Orthographic and sandhi trivia across the chapter — देव्याः/देव्या (v. 9), मूर्छितो/मूर्च्छितो (v. 28), the ZWJ inside अट्‍टाट्‍टहास (v. 23), joined vs split तथाऽपरे (v. 40), anusvāra vs conjunct nasal throughout: None of these is a difference of reading.
