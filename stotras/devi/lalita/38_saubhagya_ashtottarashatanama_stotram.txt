Title: Śrī Saubhāgya Aṣṭottaraśatanāma Stotram
Devanāgarī: श्रीसौभाग्या-अष्टोत्तरशतनामस्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ సౌభాగ్యా అష్టోత్తరశతనామ స్తోత్రం

Author: The colophon places it in the **Tripurārahasya**. The sanskritdocuments witness gives the location as the Māhātmya-khaṇḍa, adhyāya 26. **Dattātreya** teaches it to **Paraśurāma** (Jāmadagnya, Bhārgava). The embedded viniyoga (v. 9) names **Śiva** as seer, anuṣṭubh as metre and **Śrī Lalitāmbikā** as deity.

Source / recension: Primary text: **Gītā Press, Gorakhpur — Śatanāma Stotra Saṅgraha**, code 1850, pp. 187–192 (stotra pp. 187–190, nāmāvalī pp. 191–192; PDF pp. 188–193), transcribed from the page images of the printed edition. Entry 36 of the volume's forty. **Collated against sanskritdocuments.org `saubhAgya108str.itx`** (encoded by Sridhar Seshagiri, proofread by Sridhar Seshagiri and Avinash Sathaye) and its companion **`saubhAgyAShTottarashatanAmAvalI.itx`** (proofread by Sridhar Seshagiri and Shriramana Sharma), both from the *Tripurārahasya*, Māhātmya-khaṇḍa 26. This is the **same text**, with the same Dattātreya–Paraśurāma frame, the same initial-letter scheme and the same Tripurārahasya source. The witness is independent of Gītā Press. Stotra and nāmāvalī are paired in one file as the publisher prints them, and were checked against each other name by name. No independent witness of the dhyāna was found.

Recension note: **Gītā Press followed; the sanskritdocuments witness (SD) is recorded, not adopted.** **Name count 100, not 108 — Gītā Press prints a hundred names under a "hundred and eight" title, and its stotra and nāmāvalī agree on the hundred.** The shortfall is two half-verses that SD has and Gītā Press does not. SD v. 13b `atilokacaritrā'tisundaryatiśubhapradā` and v. 14a `aghahantryativistārā'rcanatuṣṭā'mitaprabhā` carry seven names, SD nāmāvalī nos. 18–24: *atilokacaritrā, atisundarī, atiśubhapradā, aghahantrī, ativistārā, arcanatuṣṭā, amitaprabhā*. Gītā Press v. 13 joins SD 13a directly to SD 14b. SD also counts `ekā` (`ekasyai`) as a name inside `एकैकभावतुष्टा` where Gītā Press keeps the compound whole (no. 22). With those eight SD reaches 108. This file keeps the hundred as printed and does not import SD's names. **Please check this against another Gītā Press printing if one is to hand. A dropped line in the print is possible, since Gītā Press's own title promises 108.** Because of the missing half-verses, Gītā Press's verse numbers run one behind SD from v. 13 on. **SD's phalaśruti is longer.** After Gītā Press's last verse (SD v. 32) SD has four and a half more verses of prayoga (vv. 33–36 and a half-line). They cover fire-offerings with red karavīra and white flowers, feeding suvāsinīs, worship in the Śrīcakra, and a closing promise of Lalitā's favour. Gītā Press does not print them. Other differences (Gītā Press → SD): v. 8 `सारतरं` → `sāratamaṁ`; v. 17 `लयकर्त्री` → `layakarṇī` (SD's nāmāvalī gives `layakartryai` as a variant); v. 23 `अन्तःस्थरूपिणी` → `antaḥsvarūpiṇī` (SD's nāmāvalī gives `antaḥstharūpiṇyai` as a variant); v. 12 `कमलाभारतीसेव्या`, one name in both nāmāvalīs, is split `kamalā bhāratīsevyā` in SD's stotra. **The letter scheme.** The names run in groups by initial letter: ka, a, e, ī, la, ha, ra, a, sa. V. 4 says that "the letters of the Saubhāgya-vidyā are drawn out" in the hymn. The groups plainly echo the ka–e–ī–la and ha–sa groups of the Śrīvidyā mantra. How exactly they map to it is not stated in the print and is not asserted here. **Prefix.** Gītā Press prints `ॐ` before each name; this corpus prints `श्री` for every nāmāvalī without exception (user, 2026-09-08). No. 28 `एनोऽद्रिशक्रायुधसमस्थित्यै` is broken across two lines in the print but is one name. **Cross-reference:** the Lalitā sahasranāma and the other Lalitā texts in `devi/lalita/` are different works; v. 31 names this hymn as a substitute for the Sahasranāma.

Blurb: A list of names built on letters. They run in groups by initial letter, which the hymn says draw out the syllables of the Saubhāgya-vidyā. Dattātreya gives it to Paraśurāma as the secret by which Kāma won his power to enchant the worlds. He calls it obligatory for every Śrīvidyā worshipper, and worth a hundred recitations of the Thousand Names.
