Title: Śrī Tripurasundarī Daṇḍakam
Devanāgarī: श्री त्रिपुरसुन्दरी दण्डकम्
Telugu: శ్రీ త్రిపురసుందరీ దండకం

Author: traditionally ascribed to Śrī Dīpakanātha Siddha (one manuscript witness expands this to "Dīpakanātha Siddha Mahāyogin"). The text's own closing colophon states "iti śrī dīpakanātha siddha viracitaṁ śrī tripurasundarī daṇḍakam" ("thus [ends] the Śrī Tripurasundarī Daṇḍakam, composed by Śrī Dīpakanātha Siddha"), and the phalaśruti verse (v.27) itself contains what looks like a deliberate signature-pun: it calls the poem a "mahāpraṇati dīpakam," a "lamp (dīpaka) of great obeisance," echoing the author's own name. No separate viniyoga, ṛṣi, or chandas note is given in either source consulted; the invocatory verse (v.1) and the two closing verses (vv.28-29) are in a different, shorter meter from the daṇḍaka's own compound-prose body.

Source / recension: primary source is stotranidhi.com's "Śrī Tripurasundarī Daṇḍakam" page, checked across its Devanāgarī/Sanskrit, Telugu (with a running Telugu-prose paraphrase), and English/IAST editions — all three carry the identical underlying Sanskrit text, numbered continuously 1-29 in every edition. This was independently cross-checked against te.wikisource.org's "శ్రీ త్రిపురసుందరీ దండకం" page (Telugu script, with a parallel English/IAST transliteration credited to "edited by Chandra Machiraju," itself citing an unnamed print source), whose running text and verse numbering (1-29) match stotranidhi's exactly. No verified traditional word-by-word (padaccheda/tātparya) commentary could be located for this text on any source consulted; stotranidhi's own Telugu paraphrase (a modern editorial summary, not a classical commentary) was consulted as a secondary aid in checking the sense of individual compounds, but the en/tel/hi renderings below are this transcriber's own compound-by-compound reading of the Sanskrit, offered with that caveat rather than false certainty, given how unusually dense the daṇḍaka's compounding is even by kāvya standards.

Verse count: 29 numbered verse-units, exactly as numbered in both sources consulted (1 through 29, continuous, no gaps or repeats), followed by a one-line prose colophon that is not itself numbered. Verse 1 is a four-line invocatory stanza; verses 2-26 are the daṇḍaka proper, a single continuous chain of compound description broken by the source only at its own comma-pauses and closed with 25 numbered ॥ n ॥ markers of widely varying length (some spanning several lines, some a single short phrase); verse 27 is a phalaśruti stanza in a different, end-rhymed meter; verses 28-29 are two closing śaraṇāgati verses in yet another, shorter meter.

Sections:
పీఠికా శ్లోకం — Invocatory Verse (v.1: names the three kūṭas — Vāgbhava, Kāmarāja, Śakti — of the Pañcadaśī/Śrīvidyā mantra and links each to sun, moon and fire)
దండకం — Daṇḍakam proper (vv.2-26: one unbroken chain of compound epithets portraying the Goddess from her dancing feet upward to her crown, then naming her four principal seats)
ఫలశ్రుతి — Phalaśruti (v.27: the fruit promised for reciting the daṇḍaka)
శరణాగతి శ్లోకాలు — Refuge Verses (vv.28-29: two closing verses of surrender, in a different meter from both the invocation and the daṇḍaka body)
సమాప్తి వాక్యం — Colophon (the closing prose sentence naming the work and its author)

Blurb: This is a daṇḍaka. It builds one unbroken portrait of Tripurasundarī as a single long compound. It begins with her dancing feet, on which the anklets ring, and rises to the crescent moon bound in her hair. It then becomes technical. It encodes the three kūṭas of her Pañcadaśī mantra, which are Vāgbhava, Kāmarāja and Śakti. It treats the Sanskrit alphabet itself as divine, in her form as Mātṛkā. It traces the unfolding of the primal triangle as Vāmā, Jyeṣṭhā and Raudrī, from which she takes the name Tripurasundarī. At the end it places her at each of the four Kaula pīṭhas, which are Kāmarūpa, Jālandhara, Pūrṇagiri and Oḍḍiyāṇa, under the name she bears at each seat.

अथ सरस समुद्यत् कामतत्त्वानुभावा ।
तदनु परमधाम ध्यानसंलक्ष्य मोक्षा

en: Victorious is the glory of the Vāgbhava-kūṭa (the first of the three syllable-clusters that make up the Pañcadaśī/Śrīvidyā mantra), sprung from her own nectarous waters; then the rasa-laden, ever-rising glory that is the very experience of the Kāmarāja-kūṭa (the second cluster); and after that, the glory of the Śakti-kūṭa (the third cluster), whose supreme abode is grasped only in meditation and which bestows liberation — appearing, in turn, as sun, moon and sacrificial fire: hail to this mantra-power of Tripurā (traipurī mantraśakti), the Śrīvidyā itself!
