Blurb: This is a hymn of mental worship in 127 verses, traditionally ascribed to Śaṅkara. It takes Tripurasundarī through a whole imagined day of service. The worshipper approaches her jewelled mansion in Maṇidvīpa. He offers her a full bath, then dressing, then ornaments from head to foot. He accompanies her in procession to her throne room. There he performs a complete navāvaraṇa-pūjā of the Śrīcakra, naming all nine enclosing cakras in turn, each with its groups of guardian yoginīs. He then offers flowers and incense, and a generous offering of food. At the end he asks for her grace. The whole of this worship is performed nowhere except in the worshipper's own mind.

--- verse 1 | section: Pratijñā ---
deva:
मम न भजनशक्तिः पादयोस्ते न भक्तिर्न च विषयविरक्तिर्ध्यानयोगे न सक्तिः ।
इति मनसि सदाहं चिन्तयन्नाद्यशक्ते
रुचिरवचनपुष्पैरर्चनं सञ्चिनोमि ॥
iast:
mama na bhajanaśaktiḥ pādayoste na bhaktirna ca viṣayaviraktirdhyānayoge na saktiḥ |
iti manasi sadāhaṁ cintayannādyaśakte
ruciravacanapuṣpairarcanaṁ sañcinomi ||
en: I have no strength to serve you, no true devotion at your feet, no dispassion toward the objects of sense, no steadiness in the discipline of meditation — thinking this always in my mind, O Ādyaśakti (the primal Power), I gather an offering of worship made of lovely flowers of words.
tel: నాకు నిన్ను సేవించే శక్తి లేదు, నీ పాదాల యెడ నిజమైన భక్తి లేదు, విషయాల పట్ల వైరాగ్యం లేదు, ధ్యానయోగంలో స్థిరత్వం లేదు — ఇలా మనసులో ఎల్లప్పుడూ తలచుకుంటూ, ఓ ఆద్యశక్తీ, మనోహరమైన మాటల పుష్పాలతో అర్చనను సమకూర్చుకుంటున్నాను.
hi: मुझमें तुम्हारी सेवा करने की शक्ति नहीं, तुम्हारे चरणों में सच्ची भक्ति नहीं, विषयों के प्रति वैराग्य नहीं, ध्यानयोग में स्थिरता नहीं — ऐसा सदा मन में सोचते हुए, हे आद्यशक्ते, मैं सुंदर वचन-पुष्पों से अर्चना जुटाता हूँ।

--- verse 2 | section: Maṇidvīpa-varṇanam ---
deva:
व्याप्तं हाटकविग्रहैर्जलचरैरारूढदेवव्रजैः
पोतैराकुलितान्तरं मणिधरैर्भूमीधरैर्भूषितम् ।
आरक्तामृतसिन्धुमुद्धुरचलद्विचीचयव्याकुलव्योमानं परिचिन्त्य सन्ततमहो चेतः कृतार्थीभव ॥
iast:
vyāptaṁ hāṭakavigrahairjalacarairārūḍhadevavrajaiḥ
potairākulitāntaraṁ maṇidharairbhūmīdharairbhūṣitam |
āraktāmṛtasindhumuddhuracaladvicīcayavyākulavyomānaṁ paricintya santatamaho cetaḥ kṛtārthībhava ||
en: Meditate always, O my mind, on that crimson ocean of nectar — its waters filled with golden-bodied sea-creatures and with boats bearing companies of gods, its expanse crowded and adorned by gem-laden mountains, its vast surface restless with towering, shifting waves — and be fulfilled.
tel: ఓ మనసా, ఆ ఎర్రని అమృత సముద్రాన్ని సదా ధ్యానించు — అందులో బంగారు శరీరాలు గల జలచరాలు, దేవతా సమూహాలు ఎక్కిన నావలు నిండి ఉన్నాయి; రత్నభరిత పర్వతాలతో అలంకృతమై ఉంది; ఎత్తుగా లేచి కదిలే అలలతో దాని విశాలత్వం అల్లకల్లోలంగా ఉంది — దీన్ని ధ్యానించి కృతార్థుడవు కమ్ము.
hi: हे मन, उस लाल अमृत-सागर का सदा ध्यान कर — जिसमें स्वर्ण-वर्ण जलचर तथा देवगणों से भरी नावें भरी हैं, जो रत्नमय पर्वतों से अलंकृत है, जिसका विस्तार ऊँची उठती हिलती लहरों से व्याकुल है — इसका ध्यान करके कृतार्थ हो जा।

--- verse 3 | section: Maṇidvīpa-varṇanam ---
deva:
तस्मिन्नुज्ज्वलरत्नजालविलसत्कान्तिच्छटाभिः स्फुटं
कुर्वाणं वियदिन्द्रचापनिचयैराच्छादितं सर्वतः ।
उच्चैःशृङ्गनिषण्णदिव्यवनिताबृन्दाननप्रोल्लसद्गीताकर्णननिश्चलाखिलमृगं द्वीपं नमस्कुर्महे ॥
iast:
tasminnujjvalaratnajālavilasatkānticchaṭābhiḥ sphuṭaṁ
kurvāṇaṁ viyadindracāpanicayairācchāditaṁ sarvataḥ |

en: We bow to that island (Maṇidvīpa) — made brilliant there by the flashing radiance of clusters of gleaming jewels, covered on every side by rainbows in the sky, where every deer stands motionless, listening to the songs pouring from the mouths of divine women seated on its high peaks.
tel: ఆ ద్వీపానికి (మణిద్వీపానికి) నమస్కరిస్తున్నాము — అక్కడ ప్రకాశించే రత్నసమూహాల కాంతితో స్పష్టంగా వెలుగొందుతున్నది; ఆకాశంలో ఇంద్రధనుస్సుల సమూహాలతో అన్నివైపులా కప్పబడి ఉంది; దాని ఎత్తైన శిఖరాలపై కూర్చున్న దివ్యస్త్రీల ముఖాలనుండి వెలువడే గీతాలను వింటూ అన్ని జింకలు కదలకుండా నిలిచి ఉన్నాయి.
hi: उस द्वीप (मणिद्वीप) को हम नमस्कार करते हैं — जो वहाँ चमकते रत्नसमूहों की कांति से स्पष्ट भासित होता है, आकाश में इंद्रधनुषों के समूह से चारों ओर से आच्छादित है, जिसकी ऊँची चोटियों पर बैठी दिव्य स्त्रियों के मुख से निकलते गीतों को सुनते हुए सभी मृग निश्चल खड़े हैं।

क्वाहं मन्दमतिः क्व चेदमखिलैरेकान्तभक्तैः स्तुतं
ध्यातं देवि तथापि ते स्वमनसा श्रीपादुकापूजनम् ।
कादाचित्कमदीयचिन्तनविधौ सन्तुष्टया शर्मदं
स्तोत्रं देवतया तया प्रकटितं मन्ये मदीयानने ॥
iast:
kvāhaṁ mandamatiḥ kva cedamakhilairekāntabhaktaiḥ stutaṁ
dhyātaṁ devi tathāpi te svamanasā śrīpādukāpūjanam |
kādācitkamadīyacintanavidhau santuṣṭayā śarmadaṁ
stotraṁ devatayā tayā prakaṭitaṁ manye madīyānane ||
en: Where am I, of dull mind, and where is this worship of your sacred sandals, praised and meditated upon only by your most single-minded devotees? Even so, O Devī, I believe that this beneficent hymn — arising now and again in the course of my own meditation — has been revealed by that very Goddess, pleased with me, into my own mouth.
tel: నేను మందబుద్ధిని, ఏకాంతభక్తులచేత మాత్రమే ధ్యానించి స్తుతించబడే నీ శ్రీపాదుకా పూజ ఎక్కడ? అయినప్పటికీ, నా చింతనా క్రమంలో అప్పుడప్పుడు కలిగే, సంతుష్టురాలైన ఆ దేవతచేతనే నా నోటిలో ప్రకటించబడిన శుభకరమైన ఈ స్తోత్రమని భావిస్తున్నాను.
hi: मैं मंदमति, और यह श्रीपादुका-पूजन जो केवल एकांतभक्तों द्वारा ध्यात-स्तुत है — कहाँ मैं, कहाँ वह? फिर भी, हे देवी, मेरे चिंतन-क्रम में कभी-कभार उठने वाला, संतुष्ट हुई उसी देवता द्वारा मेरे मुख में प्रकट किया गया यह कल्याणकारी स्तोत्र है, ऐसा मानता हूँ।

--- verse 127 | section: Śaraṇāgatiḥ ---
deva:
नित्यार्चनमिदं चित्ते भाव्यमानं सदा मया ।
निबद्धं विविधैः पद्यैरनुगृह्णातु सुन्दरी ॥
iast:
nityārcanamidaṁ citte bhāvyamānaṁ sadā mayā |
nibaddhaṁ vividhaiḥ padyairanugṛhṇātu sundarī ||
en: May the Beautiful One graciously accept this daily worship, ever contemplated by me in my heart, composed in these various verses.
tel: నా చిత్తంలో సదా భావించబడుతూ, వివిధ పద్యాలతో బంధించబడిన ఈ నిత్యార్చనను ఆ సుందరి అనుగ్రహించుగాక.
hi: मेरे चित्त में सदा भावित, विविध पद्यों में निबद्ध यह नित्यार्चन उस सुंदरी द्वारा अनुगृहीत हो।

--- verse none | section: Colophon ---
deva:
इति श्रीमत्परमहंसपरिव्राजकाचार्यस्य श्रीगोविन्दभगवत्पूज्यपादशिष्यस्य
श्रीमच्छङ्करभगवतः कृतौ त्रिपुरसुन्दरी मानसपूजा स्तोत्रम् ।
iast:
iti śrīmatparamahaṁsaparivrājakācāryasya śrīgovindabhagavatpūjyapādaśiṣyasya
śrīmacchaṅkarabhagavataḥ kṛtau tripurasundarī mānasapūjā stotram |
en: Thus ends the Tripurasundarī Mānasapūjā Stotram, found in the work of the blessed Śaṅkara Bhagavān — glorious paramahaṁsa and wandering (parivrājaka) preceptor, disciple of the blessed and venerable Govinda Bhagavatpāda.
tel: ఇది శ్రీమత్పరమహంస పరివ్రాజకాచార్యులు, శ్రీగోవింద భగవత్పూజ్యపాదుల శిష్యులు అయిన శ్రీమచ్ఛంకర భగవానుల కృతిలోని త్రిపురసుందరీ మానసపూజా స్తోత్రం.
hi: यह श्रीमत्परमहंस परिव्राजकाचार्य, श्रीगोविंद भगवत्पूज्यपाद के शिष्य श्रीमच्छंकर भगवान की कृति में त्रिपुरसुंदरी मानसपूजा स्तोत्र है।

Recension note: This file was completed across three passes (two earlier sessions were interrupted before reaching verse 28; this pass supplied verses 28-127 and this closing note). The whole 127-verse text rests on stotranidhi.com's paired IAST and Devanāgarī editions (fetched with a browser-style User-Agent to work around the site's HTTP 406 response to default fetches; en.wikipedia-style stotranidhi transliteration uses ē/ō and r̥ for ṛ, all silently normalized here to the corpus's plain-e/o, ṛ, and ṁ conventions), cross-checked verse-by-verse against sanskritdocuments.org's "tripurasundarīmānasapūjāstotram" (encoded by Sunder Hattangadi). Both sources agree on 127 verses under one title, with no gaps or repeated numbers, and the same closing colophon content.

For verses 28-127, the sanskritdocuments comparison text was read from a live browser fetch and then retyped into a working file for line-by-line diffing against stotranidhi, rather than saved directly from the page; this cross-check is therefore real but carries a small added risk of transcription slips on my part, mainly in anusvāra-vs-class-nasal spelling (e.g. -aṁk-/-aṅk-) and b/v spelling, which are exactly the kind of detail easy to miscopy by hand. With that caveat, the diff turned up two categories of difference. First, a long tail of minor spelling variants of the kind normal between independently maintained e-texts — anusvāra vs. class nasal (saṅkṣobhaṇe/saṃkṣobhaṇe, sañjña/saṃjña), b/v spelling (bṛnda/vṛnda), long/short vowel (mṛdupaṭī/mṛdupaṭi, yāntībhiḥ/yantībhiḥ), and the like, scattered across roughly a dozen verses (e.g. vv.30, 31, 36, 45, 48, 61, 63, 64, 65, 75, 87, 88, 91, 92, 94, 102, 104, 105, 107) — none of which change the sense; stotranidhi's reading is followed throughout as the designated base source, and these are not itemized verse-by-verse above. Second, two variants that are not mere spelling: at v.67, stotranidhi's āveditān (accusative plural, agreeing with the gods being announced) reads more coherently than sanskritdocuments' āveditāt; and at v.98, stotranidhi's sā pātu māṁ (the feminine sā agreeing with yoginī) is kept over sanskritdocuments' sa māṁ pātu, a word-order-and-gender variant. v.109's dugdham ("milk," stotranidhi) vs. digdham ("smeared," sanskritdocuments) was also checked directly against the page text and confirmed as a genuine reading difference; dugdham is kept as it fits the surrounding list of dairy offerings (milk, curd, molasses, śikhariṇī).

One genuine defect is confirmed rather than merely repeated from the header: sanskritdocuments.org's verse 122 preserves only the first half (the first two pādas) of what is, in stotranidhi's edition, a complete four-pāda verse — sanskritdocuments breaks off after "...parihṛtaviṣayāsaṅgamākarṇyamānam" and proceeds directly to verse 123, silently dropping the second half-verse ("gītajñābhiḥ prakāmaṁ madhurasamadhuraṁ vāditaṁ kiṁnarībhirvīṇājhaṅkāranādaṁ kalaya paraśivānandasandhānahetoḥ"). Since stotranidhi's Devanāgarī and IAST editions agree with each other on the full four-pāda text, and hariome.com and stotram.co.in (checked as further mirrors of the Sanskrit only, not for translation) also carry the complete verse, the fuller reading is treated as correct and printed above; sanskritdocuments' curtailed verse is flagged here as its own scribal defect, not silently smoothed over.

The colophon itself differs slightly between the two sources: stotranidhi ends with a single daṇḍa after "...tripurasundarī mānasapūjā stotram," while sanskritdocuments closes the same sentence with a double daṇḍa and the additional word "sampūrṇam" ("[is] complete"). Both are recording the same fact; stotranidhi's shorter form is kept as the base reading here since it is the corpus's designated primary source, and the variant is noted rather than silently merged in.

The Sections scheme in the header (Pratijñā through Puṣpikā) is supplied by this corpus entry, not printed as such by either source — both present the 127 verses as one continuous, unheaded sequence. The divisions follow the mānasapūjā's own narrative sequence of imagined services (approach and description of Maṇidvīpa; gate deities; entry into the jewel-mansion; the day's first courtesies; bath; dressing; ornamentation head to foot; procession to the assembly hall; ascent of the throne; the nine-fold āvaraṇa-pūjā of the Śrīcakra itself, with each cakra's guardian yoginī-group named in turn per the standard navāvaraṇa scheme — Prakaṭā, Guptā, Guptatarā, Sampradāya, Kulakaulā/Kulottīrṇā, Nigarbhā, Rahasyā, and the three central deities Kāmeśvarī-Vajreśvarī-Bhagamālinī, ending in the Bindu's Parāpara-rahasya Yoginī; flowers; incense, lamp and a full food-offering culminating in Annapūrṇā; worship of the cakra's attendant deities; contemplative absorption; bali; betel and ārātrika; and finally surrender) and are stated as such rather than being mistaken for the source's own apparatus.

The English, Telugu and Hindi glosses for every verse (1-127) are original renderings made for this corpus; no published translation of this stotra was located to check them against, and this is stated plainly rather than implying a translation source exists. Two verses called for interpretive judgment beyond straightforward description and are flagged as such, though neither is a source-variant issue: v.71 and v.87 both use an unusual compound built on "-traya" (oghatraye / moghatrayam) in a context that most naturally reads as "the threefold instrument of mind, speech and body" whose habitual futility (mogha-) is ended by the guru's grace; this is a defensible reading consistent with the surrounding guru-paramparā language of vv.71-87, but the compound is genuinely elliptical and is noted here as an interpretive rendering rather than a certain one. All Śrī Vidyā technical vocabulary — the four Pīṭhas, the sixteen Nityās, the nine āvaraṇas and their yoginī-group names, the pañcabāṇa/pāśa/aṅkuśa weapons, the kāmakalā diagram of v.103 — is kept in its technical form and glossed in the en field rather than translated away, per the corpus's Śākta-handling brief. Anusvāra is ṁ (U+1E41) throughout; Sanskrit iast uses plain e/o only. No verse was left unverified: verses 1-127 and the colophon were all checked against at least two independently maintained editions.
