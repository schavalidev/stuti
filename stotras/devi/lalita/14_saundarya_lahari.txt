Title: Saundaryalaharī
Devanāgarī: सौन्दर्यलहरी
Telugu: సౌందర్యలహరీ

Author: traditionally ascribed to Ādi Śaṅkarācārya. The colophon printed at the end of the text itself states it is "the work of the venerable Śaṅkara Bhagavat, disciple of the venerable Govinda Bhagavatpāda, [teacher] of the paramahaṁsa-parivrājaka order" (iti śrīmatparamahaṁsaparivrājakācāryasya śrīgovindabhagavatpūjyapādaśiṣyasya śrīmacchaṅkarabhagavataḥ kṛtau saundaryalaharī sampūrṇā). A well-known parallel tradition, recorded by several commentators, holds that only the first 41 verses (the Ānandalaharī) came from Śiva himself, dictated to Śaṅkara on Kailāsa, and that Nandi/Gaṇeśa erased or intercepted the rest before Śaṅkara could carry it away, so that verses 42-100 (the Saundaryalaharī proper) are Śaṅkara's own composition, written to recover what was lost — this legend is the traditional explanation for why the two halves carry separate names.

Source/recension: primary text collated from two independently maintained electronic editions that agree with each other almost word for word: vignanam.org's "śuddha devanāgarī" edition (vignanam.org/devanagari/soundarya-lahari.html) and sanskritdocuments.org's doc_devii/saundaryalahari.itx (transliterated by Anshuman Pandey, proofread by Sunder Hattangadi; that edition's own note states it was itself collated from ten printed editions and gives 103 verses as "what the majority of the printed editions have also listed"). Both give the same 100 numbered verses in the same order, the same closing colophon, and the same three appendix verses (101-103); both mark the identical variant readings at the identical points (verse 11's "catuścatvāriṁśad-" vs. "trayaścatvāriṁśad-" and "śaraṇakoṇāḥ" vs. "caraṇakoṇāḥ"/"bhavanakiṇāḥ"; verse 15's "bhaṇitayaḥ" vs. "phaṇitayaḥ"; verse 30's "nirājanavidhim" vs. "nīrājanavidhim"; verse 44's "bṛndaiḥ" vs. "vṛndaiḥ"; verse 51's "-jananī" vs. "-jayinī"; verse 101's "bhayādāsyād" vs. "bhayādāsya snigdhastimita" vs. "bhayādāsyasyāntaḥstimita"; verse 102's "janayanti sma" vs. "janayāmāsa madano" vs. "janayantaḥ samatulāṁ" vs. "janayantā suvadane") — all reproduced at the relevant verse below and listed again in the recension note. vignanam.org's own romanized "plain-english" page was checked as a further informal cross-check on the letter text and matches throughout, though its dedicated word-meaning page is marked "Coming soon" and so was not usable for translation. stotranidhi.com was confirmed via its own site search to carry this text (indexed under దేవీ స్తోత్రాలు → శ్రీ లలితా స్తోత్రాలు → సౌందర్యలహరీ, opening శివః...) but its page could not be retrieved in full in this session — it blocks automated fetches and both direct curl attempts to it and to its mirror hariome.com failed — so it supports rather than independently verifies the collation; this is flagged as reduced (two-source, not three-source) verse-by-verse cross-checking. The English, Telugu, and Hindi renderings below are composed directly from the collated Sanskrit, since no verse-meaning translation was available on either primary source; verse 14's element/mind numerology (56+52+62+54+72+64) and verse 32's mantra-letter identification follow the standard, widely reproduced interpretation of those two verses (Lakṣmīdhara-tradition commentary), flagged as interpretive rather than literally spelled out by the verse text itself.

Verse count: 103 numbered verses — the core 100 (1–41 Ānandalaharī, 42–100 Saundaryalaharī proper) plus three appendix verses numbered 101–103, preceded by one unnumbered prefatory verse (bhūmau skhalitapādānām…), a stock invocatory verse asking forgiveness for faults of recitation, which is kept here as a separate unnumbered block. After verse 100 there follows an unnumbered phalaśruti couplet (saundaryalaharī mukhyastotraṁ…), then the closing colophon, then the three further numbered verses 101–103, which stand as an appendix and are widely held to be later additions; they are kept visibly outside the core 100. Where a single long compound straddles a pāda boundary it is kept unbroken on one line, so a few verses appear as three lines rather than four.

Sections:
మంగళాచరణం — Maṅgalācaraṇam (the unnumbered prefatory verse asking forgiveness for faults of recitation)
ఆనందలహరీ — Ānandalaharī (vv. 1-41: the dense Śrī Vidyā/tantric first part — ṣaṭcakra and kuṇḍalinī description, the Goddess's mantra decoded syllable by syllable, the navayoni triangles of the Śrī Cakra counted out, praise of her feet, anklet, and earring, and the merit of worshipping her)
సౌందర్యలహరీ — Saundaryalaharī proper (vv. 42-100: the head-to-foot keśādipādānta description of the Goddess's physical beauty, crown to feet)
ఫలశ్రుతి, కొలోఫన్ — Phalaśruti and colophon (unnumbered closing verse and prose attribution)
అనుబంధః — Anubandha (vv. 101-103: three further verses widely printed with the text but identified by editors as later additions)

Blurb: This is a hymn of one hundred verses to Tripurasundarī, in the śikhariṇī metre. Its first half is called Ānandalaharī. That half goes through her Śrī Cakra and through the working of kuṇḍalinī. Verse 9 places the five elements and the mind upon the six cakras, from mūlādhāra to sahasrāra. Verse 10 describes kuṇḍalinī coiled like a sleeping serpent. Verse 11 counts out the navayoni triangles of the Śrī Yantra. Verse 32 sets out her garland-mantra of fifty-one syllables, letter by letter. The second half is the Saundaryalaharī proper. It describes her body in the classical order from head to foot, called keśādipādānta. It takes in the crown, the hair, the forehead, the brows, the eyes, the cheeks, the nose, the lips, the teeth, the chin, the neck, the arms, the breasts, the waist, the navel, the thighs, the knees and the feet. Each limb is described through a series of familiar poetic comparisons, such as the sliver of the moon, the bow of Kāma, the elephant's trunk and the lotus. Sanskrit poets have drawn upon these comparisons for a thousand years since.

Recension note: The Sanskrit text (Devanāgarī) was collated directly from vignanam.org's "śuddha devanāgarī" edition of the Saundaryalaharī, cross-checked verse by verse against sanskritdocuments.org's doc_devii/saundaryalahari.itx (transliterated by Anshuman Pandey, proofread by Sunder Hattangadi, itself stated to be collated from ten printed editions). The two sources agree almost word for word across all 103 verses, the unnumbered prefatory verse, the closing colophon, and the section headings (Ānandalaharī/Saundaryalaharī); stotranidhi.com was confirmed by site search to carry the same text under దేవీ స్తోత్రాలు → శ్రీ లలితా స్తోత్రాలు but its page could not be retrieved in this session (blocks automated fetches; its mirror hariome.com also failed to a plain curl with a browser User-Agent), so this is a two-source rather than three-source collation — flagged as reduced cross-checking per the corpus spec. No verse-meaning translation was available from either primary source (vignanam.org's own "Meaning" page for this text is marked "Coming soon"), so the English, Telugu, and Hindi glosses throughout are composed directly from the collated Sanskrit rather than adapted from a published translation.

Textual variants recorded by the sources (both reproduced at the relevant verse above): v.11 "catuścatvāriṁśad-" (44) vs. "trayaścatvāriṁśad-" (43), and "śaraṇakoṇāḥ" vs. "caraṇakoṇāḥ"/"bhavanakiṇāḥ"; v.15 "bhaṇitayaḥ" vs. "phaṇitayaḥ"; v.30 "nirājanavidhim" vs. "nīrājanavidhim"; v.44 "bṛndaiḥ" vs. "vṛndaiḥ"; v.51 "-saubhāgyajananī" vs. "-saubhāgyajayinī"; v.71, where the two sources genuinely diverge rather than merely offering a bracketed variant — vignanam.org prints "lākṣārasachaṇam" (a form this translator could not parse grammatically) where sanskritdocuments.org prints "lākṣāruṇadalam" ("[lotus] petal reddened by lac," which parses cleanly as a compound modifying "kamalam"); the latter is followed in the main text as the better-attested reading, with vignanam's divergent reading noted at that verse; v.101 "bhayādāsyādantaḥstimita-" vs. "bhayādāsya snigdhastimita-" vs. "bhayādāsyasyāntaḥstimita-"; v.102's third-quarter verb "janayanti sma" vs. "janayāmāsa madano" vs. "janayantaḥ samatulāṁ" vs. "janayantā suvadane." Verse 82's second half was also checked with care: an earlier, less careful pass had mis-set its verb as "vijigye" ("conquered"); both source editions in fact read "vidhijñe... asi" ("O knower of the rite... you are"), which is what is printed here.

Numbering and structural notes: the unnumbered prefatory verse (bhūmau skhalitapādānām, a stock invocation used before many Devī stotras generally, not exclusive to this text) is kept as its own unnumbered block rather than folded into verse 1. The 100 core verses are numbered continuously and without gaps or repeats in both sources. After verse 100, vignanam.org prints an additional unnumbered phalaśruti couplet (saundaryalaharī mukhyastotraṁ...) that sanskritdocuments.org's file does not carry as a separate item; it is kept here as its own unnumbered block, with the difference flagged at that point. Both sources then give the identical prose colophon, followed by exactly the same three additional numbered verses (101-103), which sanskritdocuments.org's own editorial note explicitly identifies as later interpolations "included because the majority of printed editions" carry them; they are reproduced here under a separate "Anubandha" section heading rather than merged into the count of 100, per that same editorial judgement.

Formatting and transliteration choices: anusvāra is rendered ṁ (U+1E41) throughout per this corpus's convention, never ṃ; Sanskrit iast uses plain e and o only. Devanāgarī compound-word hyphens that vignanam.org's page inserts purely as a print-width line-wrap aid (not marking any real pāda break) have been removed and the compound rejoined on one line, per the rule that a soft wrap is not a pāda break; a small number of verses (11, 23, 24, 31, 33, 91) consequently print as three lines rather than the usual four, because their third and fourth pādas are joined by sandhi in a way this translator could not responsibly un-sandhi and split without altering the printed reading. Elsewhere, where a compound genuinely straddles a real pāda boundary (a common and metrically legitimate feature of śikhariṇī verses with long samāsas), the line break is kept at that boundary without inserting an artificial hyphen (e.g. verses 44, 46, 50, 58, 70, 78, 81, 85, 89, 90, 101). Two verses carry brief interpretive glosses beyond a literal rendering, flagged inline: verse 14's element/mind numerology (56+52+62+54+72+64, summing to 360) follows the standard, widely reproduced reading of that verse; verse 32, which encodes the Pañcadaśākṣarī (fifteen-syllable) Śrī Vidyā mantra acrostically through code-words, is translated by preserving those code-words rather than supplying the mantra's actual syllables, in keeping with the tradition's own practice of transmitting it only through initiation. Verse 9's cakra-element correspondence (mūlādhāra-earth, maṇipūra-water, svādhiṣṭhāna-fire, anāhata-air, viśuddhi-space, ājñā-mind) is this text's own non-standard mapping, differing from the more common maṇipūra-fire/svādhiṣṭhāna-water scheme found in many other Śrī Vidyā sources; verses 39 and 40 are translated consistently with this text's own scheme rather than "corrected" to the more familiar one. No verse was left unverified in the sense of the corpus spec's escape clause — every one of the 103 core-plus-appendix verses, the prefatory verse, the phalaśruti couplet, and the colophon is attested identically (barring the noted variants) in both primary sources.
