Verse count: 16. Each verse is keyed to one syllable of the Goddess's own mantra: the fifteen syllables of the Pañcadaśī (ka e ī la hrīṁ / ha sa ka ha la hrīṁ / sa ka la hrīṁ, the three kūṭas of Śrīvidyā) supply the opening sound of verses 1-15 in that exact order, and a sixteenth verse keyed to śrīṁ — the additional bīja that turns the fifteen-syllable Pañcadaśī into the sixteen-syllable Ṣoḍaśī mantra — closes the stava.

Sections:
విజయస్తవం — Vijaya Stavaḥ (vv. 1–16, each verse keyed in turn to one syllable of the Pañcadaśī/Ṣoḍaśī mantra)

Blurb: This is a victory hymn, called a vijaya-stava, in sixteen verses. It is built as a kūṭākṣara-nyāsa. Each verse opens with the next syllable of the Goddess's own Pañcadaśī mantra, in strict order. Every verse closes with the same refrain: 'śrīcakrādhinivāsinī vijayate śrīrājarājeśvarī', meaning 'victorious is Śrī Rājarājeśvarī, who dwells enthroned in the Śrīcakra'. Between the mantra syllable and the refrain, most verses give one line describing her form from head to foot, naming her earrings, her forehead, her breasts, her waist, her thighs, and her feet graced with anklets, in the ornate style of a formal Devī stuti. The sequence is not only ornamental. Verse 9 names the battle with Bhaṇḍāsura directly, which is the same episode that frames the Aṣṭakārikā Stotram in this folder, and it calls her Lalitā for the first time. Verse 10 states plainly that every name given to her in a body, whether Lakṣmī or Vāc or Lalitā, is a concession of grace, since the Goddess is properly beyond all form. The sixteenth and closing verse is keyed to the additional Śrīṁ that completes the Ṣoḍaśī mantra. It names her as Lalitā a second time, as the secret companion of Kāmeśa, and as one worshipped by Guha. A few verses are openly tantric. Verse 15 invokes the three Hrīṁ syllables central to Śrīvidyā, together with the identification 'haṁsa so'ham', meaning 'I am That', which is found also in the Rājñī Stotram in this folder.

en: Adorned with a bodily radiance like the fierce sun risen at the end of the aeon; bearing a mass of hair dark as a raincloud; an ocean of compassion; worshipped through the three shining kūṭas (syllable-clusters) of the sovereign mantra marked out by the letter ka — victorious is Śrī Rājarājeśvarī, who dwells enthroned in the Śrīcakra! This first verse opens, fittingly, on ka, the very first syllable of the Pañcadaśī mantra that structures the whole stava.
