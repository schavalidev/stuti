Title: Śrī Kāmākṣī Stotram 3 (Brahma-kṛtam)
Devanāgarī: श्री कामाक्षी स्तोत्रम् ३ (ब्रह्म कृतम्)
Telugu: శ్రీ కామాక్షీ స్తోత్రం ౩ (బ్రహ్మ కృతం)

Author: Explicitly framed, within the text itself, as spoken by Brahmā — it opens with the narrative tag "brahmovāca" ("Brahmā spoke") and closes with an internal colophon naming him as composer: "iti śrī brahmāṇḍamahāpurāṇe uttarabhāge lalitopākhyāne ekonacatvāriṁśo'dhyāye brahmakṛta śrī kāmākṣī stotram" — "thus [ends] the Śrī Kāmākṣī Stotram composed by Brahmā (brahmakṛta), in the thirty-ninth chapter of the Lalitopākhyāna section of the Uttarabhāga of the glorious Brahmāṇḍa Mahāpurāṇa." Worth flagging honestly: the word "Kāmākṣī" itself never occurs inside the twenty-two verses — throughout the stotra proper she is addressed only as Devī, Jaganmātā, Tripurasundarī, Jagadambā, Śrīsarvamaṅgalā and Mahādevī; the "Kāmākṣī" naming appears solely in the page heading and in this closing colophon, which is how the Kāñcī-Kāmākṣī tradition has received and titled it. sanskritdocuments.org's doc_devii catalog separately lists a differently-titled "kāñcīkāmākṣīstotram (brahmakṛtaṁ, lakṣmīnārāyaṇīyasaṁhitāntargatam)" — a Brahmā-attributed Kāmākṣī stotra sourced from the Lakṣmīnārāyaṇīya-saṁhitā rather than the Brahmāṇḍa Mahāpurāṇa. That catalog entry offers only page-scan images (no transcribed text), so it could not be read, verified, or compared against this text; it is not known whether the two are the same composition under two source-attributions or two genuinely different Brahmā-attributed Kāmākṣī hymns. The text keyed here is the one stotranidhi.com itself titles, in its own numbering, "Kāmākṣī Stotram 3 (Brahma Krutam)" — an exact match to this assignment's title — and it independently satisfies the disambiguation test given for this assignment (an internal "brahmovāca" frame plus an explicit brahmakṛta colophon), so it is used as the primary text; see the recension note for the full account of what could and could not be cross-checked.

Blurb: This is a litany of twenty-two verses, each using the words namo namaḥ. According to its own colophon, Brahmā speaks it to the Goddess in the Lalitopākhyāna section, chapter 39, of the Brahmāṇḍa Mahāpurāṇa. The verses themselves never once call her Kāmākṣī. They describe her as the Parabrahman, whom the Veda, the āgamas and the yogi cannot reach. They describe her as Śrīvidyā. They describe her as the source of the thirty-six tattvas of Śaiva teaching, that is, the twenty-five tattvas together with the five coverings of māyā, and the further unfolding into Śiva, Śakti, Īśvara, Sadvidyā and Sadāśiva. They describe her also as the hidden power behind the creating of Brahmā, the protecting of Viṣṇu, the destroying of Rudra, the earth-bearing of Ādiśeṣa, and the burning of fire, sun and wind. At the end, Brahmā, having praised her, bows and takes refuge in her.

Recension note: This text could be located, after real effort, only on stotranidhi.com, in three parallel editions (English/IAST, Devanāgarī, Telugu-with-meaning) that agree with each other letter for letter, with a single exception in verse 21: the Devanāgarī and English/IAST editions both print "हे प्रतिभटाकारा" / "he pratibhaṭākārā" (literally "O you of matching/rival form," which reads oddly as praise without a negation), while the Telugu edition's own Sanskrit line instead reads "अप्रतिभटाकारा" / "apratibhaṭākārā" ("of unequalled/matchless form," i.e. with the negative prefix a- and no separate vocative "he"), and its own Telugu tātparyaṁ explicitly glosses this as "సాటిలేని రూపము కలది" ("she whose form has no match"). The two readings likely encode the same underlying sense — "he" plus an elided "a-" (he 'pratibhaṭākārā, with the avagraha simply dropped in transmission) would yield exactly the Telugu edition's meaning — but since no independent source could be found to adjudicate, the majority Devanāgarī/English print ("he pratibhaṭākārā") is kept as the deva/iast text here exactly as printed by two of the three stotranidhi editions, with the Telugu variant and the likely intended sense noted in the verse's en field and here. All source e/o vowels, printed by stotranidhi with macrons throughout (ē, ō) per the Telugu-influenced transliteration convention that marks Sanskrit's inherently-long e/o, have been silently normalized in the iast field to this corpus's plain e/o convention (e.g. source "brahmovāca," "devi," "tripurasundari" appear here as "brahmovāca," "devi," "tripurasundari"); the Devanāgarī, which does not distinguish e/ē or o/ō, needed no such change. No other source repository could be confirmed to carry this specific text: it is not on vignanam.org (whose only Kāmākṣī-stotram page is the different, unattributed text already keyed as this corpus's item 1); it is not on hariome.com despite that site carrying the neighboring "Kāmākṣī Stotram 2" and "Stotram 4 (Paramācārya Krutam)" (its equivalent "Stotram 3" URLs, in both English and Telugu, returned 404 Page Not Found); and sanskritdocuments.org's doc_devii catalog, while confirming that a Brahmā-attributed Kāñcī-Kāmākṣī stotra is separately catalogued there, sources its entry to the Lakṣmīnārāyaṇīya-saṁhitā rather than to the Brahmāṇḍa Mahāpurāṇa's Lalitopākhyāna named in this text's own colophon, and offers only scanned page images rather than a transcribed text — so it could not be read to determine whether it is the same composition as this one or a second, distinct Brahmā-attributed Kāmākṣī hymn. This item's title, "Kāmākṣī Stotram 3 (Brahma Krutam)," and its content (an internal "brahmovāca" frame and an explicit "brahmakṛta ... stotram" colophon) match this assignment's brief precisely, so it is presented here as the intended text, with the Lakṣmīnārāyaṇīya-saṁhitā item flagged above as a possible, unverified duplicate-or-alternate under a different source-attribution. No skipped or repeated verse numbers were found in the 22 numbered verses; the unnumbered opening frame and closing colophon are reproduced in their own blocks as the source prints them, per this corpus's convention for unnumbered ritual/narrative material. An editorial note printed by stotranidhi immediately under its Telugu-language title (not part of the Sanskrit text or its colophon, and not attested in Devanāgarī or English) describes the stotra's dramatic setting and claims recitation benefits (clarity of intellect, creative power, removal of sins); this is stotranidhi's own website framing rather than a phalaśruti transmitted with the verses, so it is reported here in this note rather than keyed as a text block.
