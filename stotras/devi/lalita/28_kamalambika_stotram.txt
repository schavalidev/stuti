Title: Śrī Kamalāmbikā Stotram
Devanāgarī: श्री कमलाम्बिका स्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ కమలాంబికా స్తోత్రం

Author: Unknown by name. The text signs itself indirectly in verse 8, where the Goddess is called śrīmad-vedapurīśa-dāsa-vinutām, "praised by the servant of the blessed Vedapurīśa" — a phrase that, on the ordinary convention of the poet's mudrā, embeds the composer's own self-description as a devotee of a Śiva named Vedapurīśa, "Lord of the Veda-city." No name is given on the source page, and the identification of that Vedapurīśa with any particular shrine is not attempted here.

That placement was re-tested against the verse text itself on 2026-09-05 and holds: verse 2 seats her śrīkāmeśvarapīṭhamadhyanilayāṁ and names her śrīrājarājeśvarīṁ outright, verse 6 has ṣaṭkoṇasaṁvāsinīm and places her above the six cakras in nāda and bindu, verse 8 has hrīṅkārasandhālayām, and verse 4 has kaulinīṁ — every term the note claims is present in the received text. The decisive evidence is negative as well as positive: Lakṣmī never appears here as the goddess addressed but only in her train — verse 2 has Śrī and Vāṇī attending her feet, verse 7 has her granting boons to Viṣṇu alongside Brahmā and Śambhu, verse 5 calls her gaṇeśajananīṁ and verse 4 kālāripriyanāyikāṁ, the beloved of Śiva. One strand of that should be held loosely: verse 2's śrīvāṇīparisevitāṅghriyugalāṁ is read here as a dvandva, Śrī and Vāṇī, but it equally admits a single-epithet reading, "whose pair of feet is served by Śrī-Vāṇī," that is by Sarasvatī alone (the verse's three other śrī-s are honorific prefixes) — on which reading Lakṣmī is not named in verse 2 at all rather than named in the retinue. The dvandva is defensible and is the reading used consistently below, but the routing does not rest on verse 2: verses 4, 5 and 7 carry the negative case independently, and the Śrīvidyā vocabulary carries the positive one. The source site's own shelving under Daśamahāvidyā, and the "Śrī Lakṣmī Stotranidhi" printing, are anthology placements keyed to the name Kamalā- in the title and are outweighed by the internal evidence. Sections:
స్తోత్రం — Stotram, the refrain-verse praise proper (vv. 1–8)
ఫలశ్రుతి — Phalaśruti, the fruits of recitation (v. 9)

Blurb: This hymn has eight verses in the śārdūlavikrīḍita metre. Each closes with the same line: 'vande'haṁ kamalāmbikām anudinaṁ vāñchānukūlāṁ śivām', meaning 'day after day I bow to Kamalāmbikā, the auspicious one who inclines towards what is wished for'. A phalaśruti follows, promising sons and prosperity, the eight siddhis, and the end of anxiety. The name Kamalāmbikā belongs to the Goddess of Tiruvārūr, and it is best known from Muttusvāmi Dīkṣitar's Kamalāmbā Navāvaraṇa cycle. The deity described here, however, is expressly Lalitā. Verse 2 seats her in the middle of the Kāmeśvara-pīṭha and calls her Rājarājeśvarī directly. Verse 6 places her in the hexagram and above the six cakras, in nāda and bindu. Verse 8 makes her dwelling the juncture of the syllable hrīṁ. The hymn is built upon sound as carefully as upon doctrine. Verse 4 runs on the sounds bā, sā and kā. Verse 6 repeats the word ṣaṭ, meaning six, five times, and then fills it out with six different sixes: the six cakras, the six śāstras, the hexagram, the six daily times of worship, and the six inner enemies that she cuts away. Its most constant claim is that it refuses to choose between opposites. She grants both enjoyment and release (verse 1). She is worshipped by the sages and is one with them (verse 3). She is Bālā the girl-child and also the unborn (verse 4). And at the last she is free of difference and of non-difference alike (verse 8).

--- verse 1 | section: Stotram ---
deva:
बन्धूकद्युतिमिन्दुबिम्बवदनां बृन्दारकैर्वन्दितां
मन्दारादि समर्चितां मधुमतीं मन्दस्मितां सुन्दरीम् ।
बन्धच्छेदनकारिणीं त्रिनयनां भोगापवर्गप्रदां
वन्देऽहं कमलाम्बिकामनुदिनं वाञ्छानुकूलां शिवाम् ॥
iast:
bandhūkadyutimindubimbavadanāṁ bṛndārakairvanditāṁ
mandārādi samarcitāṁ madhumatīṁ mandasmitāṁ sundarīm |
bandhacchedanakāriṇīṁ trinayanāṁ bhogāpavargapradāṁ
vande'haṁ kamalāmbikāmanudinaṁ vāñchānukūlāṁ śivām ||
en: She whose lustre is that of the scarlet bandhūka flower and whose face is the orb of the moon; worshipped by the gods; honoured with mandāra and other celestial blossoms; sweet as honey, softly smiling, beautiful; who cuts through every bond; three-eyed; the giver of both enjoyment and liberation — day after day I bow to Kamalāmbikā, the auspicious one who bends toward what is wished for. The last epithet of the third line states the hymn's characteristic move at once: bhoga and apavarga, worldly enjoyment and release from the world, are named together as her single gift rather than as alternatives.
tel: బంధూక పుష్పము వంటి ఎఱ్ఱని కాంతి కలది, చంద్రబింబము వంటి ముఖము కలది, దేవతలచే వందించబడునది, మందారము మొదలైన దివ్యపుష్పములతో అర్చించబడునది, మధురమైనది, మందహాసము కలది, సుందరి, బంధములను ఛేదించునది, త్రినేత్రి, భోగమును మోక్షమును రెంటినీ ప్రసాదించునది — ఆ కమలాంబికను, కోరికలకు అనుకూలయైన శివాదేవిని, ప్రతిదినము నేను వందించుచున్నాను. మూడవ పాదపు చివరి విశేషణమే ఈ స్తోత్రపు ప్రత్యేకతను చాటుతున్నది — భోగము, అపవర్గము రెండునూ ఆమె ఇచ్చు ఏకైక వరముగా చెప్పబడ్డాయి; ఒకదానికి బదులు మఱొకటి అని కాదు.
hi: जिनकी आभा बन्धूक-पुष्प की लालिमा जैसी है और जिनका मुख चन्द्र-बिम्ब है; जो देवताओं से वन्दित हैं; जो मन्दार आदि दिव्य पुष्पों से अर्चित हैं; जो मधुमयी, मन्दस्मिता और सुन्दरी हैं; जो समस्त बन्धनों का छेदन करती हैं; जो त्रिनयना हैं; जो भोग और अपवर्ग — दोनों प्रदान करती हैं — उन कमलाम्बिका को, इच्छाओं के अनुकूल शिवा को, मैं प्रतिदिन प्रणाम करता हूँ। तीसरे चरण का अन्तिम विशेषण ही स्तोत्र की विशेषता कह देता है — भोग और अपवर्ग दोनों को उनका एक ही वरदान कहा गया है, विकल्प नहीं।

--- verse 2 | section: Stotram ---
deva:
श्रीकामेश्वरपीठमध्यनिलयां श्रीराजराजेश्वरीं
श्रीवाणीपरिसेविताङ्घ्रियुगलां श्रीमत्कृपासागराम् ।
शोकापद्भयमोचिनीं सुकवितानन्दैकसन्दायिनीं
वन्देऽहं कमलाम्बिकामनुदिनं वाञ्छानुकूलां शिवाम् ॥
iast:
śrīkāmeśvarapīṭhamadhyanilayāṁ śrīrājarājeśvarīṁ
śrīvāṇīparisevitāṅghriyugalāṁ śrīmatkṛpāsāgarām |
śokāpadbhayamocinīṁ sukavitānandaikasandāyinīṁ
vande'haṁ kamalāmbikāmanudinaṁ vāñchānukūlāṁ śivām ||
en: She who dwells at the centre of the blessed Kāmeśvara throne; Śrī Rājarājeśvarī; whose two feet are attended by Śrī and Vāṇī; an ocean of blessed compassion; who releases from grief, calamity and fear; the one giver of the joy of fine poetry — day after day I bow to Kamalāmbikā, the auspicious one who bends toward what is wished for. This is where the identification is made explicit: the Kamalāmbikā of the title is seated on the Kāmeśvara-pīṭha and named Rājarājeśvarī, that is, Lalitā.
tel: శ్రీకామేశ్వరపీఠము మధ్యలో నివసించునది, శ్రీరాజరాజేశ్వరి, లక్ష్మీ సరస్వతులచే పాదద్వయము సేవించబడునది, శ్రీమంతమైన కృపాసాగరము, శోకము ఆపద భయము వీటినుండి విముక్తి కలిగించునది, ఉత్తమ కవిత్వానందమును ఒసగు ఏకైక దాత — ఆ కమలాంబికను, కోరికలకు అనుకూలయైన శివాదేవిని, ప్రతిదినము నేను వందించుచున్నాను. ఇక్కడే గుర్తింపు స్పష్టమవుతున్నది — శీర్షికలోని కమలాంబిక కామేశ్వరపీఠమునందు ఆసీనురాలై, రాజరాజేశ్వరి అని పేర్కొనబడినది; అనగా లలిత.
hi: जो श्री कामेश्वर-पीठ के मध्य में निवास करती हैं; जो श्री राजराजेश्वरी हैं; जिनके चरण-युगल की सेवा लक्ष्मी और सरस्वती करती हैं; जो श्रीमती कृपा-सागरा हैं; जो शोक, आपत्ति और भय से मुक्त करती हैं; जो सुकविता के आनन्द की एकमात्र दात्री हैं — उन कमलाम्बिका को, इच्छाओं के अनुकूल शिवा को, मैं प्रतिदिन प्रणाम करता हूँ। यहीं पहचान स्पष्ट हो जाती है — शीर्षक की कमलाम्बिका कामेश्वर-पीठ पर आसीन हैं और राजराजेश्वरी कही गई हैं, अर्थात् ललिता।

--- verse 3 | section: Stotram ---
deva:
माया मोहविनाशिनीं मुनिगणैराराधितां तन्मयीं
श्रेयःसञ्चयदायिनीं गुणमयीं वाय्वादि भूतां सताम् ।
प्रातःकालसमानशोभमकुटां सामादि वेदैस्तुतां
वन्देऽहं कमलाम्बिकामनुदिनं वाञ्छानुकूलां शिवाम् ॥
iast:
māyā mohavināśinīṁ munigaṇairārādhitāṁ tanmayīṁ
śreyaḥsañcayadāyinīṁ guṇamayīṁ vāyvādi bhūtāṁ satām |
prātaḥkālasamānaśobhamakuṭāṁ sāmādi vedaistutāṁ
vande'haṁ kamalāmbikāmanudinaṁ vāñchānukūlāṁ śivām ||
en: She who destroys māyā and delusion; who is worshipped by the throngs of sages and is of one substance with them; who gives the virtuous their whole store of good; who is made of the guṇas and has become the elements beginning with wind; whose crown shines like the light of early morning; who is praised by the Vedas beginning with the Sāma — day after day I bow to Kamalāmbikā, the auspicious one who bends toward what is wished for. The word tanmayīm, "consisting of that," is placed immediately after "worshipped by the sages," so that she is at once the object of their worship and the very stuff of the worshippers; the genitive satām in the second line is read here with the giving of good rather than with the elements.
tel: మాయను మోహమును నశింపజేయునది, మునిగణములచే ఆరాధించబడి వారి స్వరూపమే అయినది, సజ్జనులకు శ్రేయస్సుల రాశిని ఒసగునది, గుణమయి, వాయువు మొదలైన భూతముల రూపమైనది, ప్రాతఃకాలపు కాంతివంటి కిరీటము కలది, సామము మొదలైన వేదములచే స్తుతించబడునది — ఆ కమలాంబికను, కోరికలకు అనుకూలయైన శివాదేవిని, ప్రతిదినము నేను వందించుచున్నాను. --- verse 4 | section: Stotram ---
deva:
बालां भक्तजनौघचित्तनिलयां बालेन्दुचूडाम्बरां
सालोक्यादि चतुर्विधार्थफलदां नीलोत्पलाक्षीमजाम् ।
कालारिप्रियनायिकां कलिमलप्रध्वंसिनीं कौलिनीं
वन्देऽहं कमलाम्बिकामनुदिनं वाञ्छानुकूलां शिवाम् ॥
iast:
bālāṁ bhaktajanaughacittanilayāṁ bālenducūḍāmbarāṁ
sālokyādi caturvidhārthaphaladāṁ nīlotpalākṣīmajām |
kālāripriyanāyikāṁ kalimalapradhvaṁsinīṁ kaulinīṁ
vande'haṁ kamalāmbikāmanudinaṁ vāñchānukūlāṁ śivām ||
en: Bālā, the girl; who dwells in the minds of the flood of her devotees; who wears the crescent moon on her crest; who grants the fourfold fruit beginning with sālokya; whose eyes are blue lotuses; the unborn; the beloved consort of the enemy of Death; the destroyer of the filth of the Kali age; the Kaulinī — day after day I bow to Kamalāmbikā, the auspicious one who bends toward what is wished for. The four fruits headed by sālokya are the classical four grades of liberation: dwelling in the god's world, nearness to him, likeness of form, and full union. Note the unresolved pairing that runs through the verse: she is the girl-child and the unborn, the destroyer of Kali-age impurity and the mistress of the Kaula path.

en: Whoever recites always this hymn so dear to Śrī Kamalāmbikā — which grants children and prosperity, yields the eight siddhis, and is the seat of the destruction of anxiety — attains the state of Brahman: his own, without any likeness, without stain, without parts, hard to reach even for the greatest of yogins; and he attains, besides, the supreme ending of all care. The phalaśruti names the ordinary rewards first and the state of Brahman last, but the word it chooses for that state is nijam, "his own": what is attained was never anything other than the reciter himself.
