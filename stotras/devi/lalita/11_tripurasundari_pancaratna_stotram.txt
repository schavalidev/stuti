Title: Tripurasundarī Pañcaratna Stotram
Devanāgarī: त्रिपुरसुन्दरी पञ्चरत्न स्तोत्रम्
Telugu: త్రిపురసుందరీ పంచరత్న స్తోత్రం

Author: No traditional author or ṛṣi/chandas/devatā viniyoga is given in either source consulted. The closing couplet (verse 6) functions as the text's own colophon: addressing the goddess as "Bāle" (O young goddess/Bālā), the unnamed poet says he has "just now" (samprati) composed this fresh (navīna) pañcaratna after meditating on her feet, and asks that it be worn there — a self-referential dedication rather than a named signature.

Source / recension: Base text is stotranidhi.com's Devanāgarī and romanized-IAST editions of "Tripura Sundari Pancharatna Stotram" (stotranidhi.com/hi/tripura-sundari-pancharatna-stotram-in-sanskrit/ and stotranidhi.com/en/tripura-sundari-pancharatna-stotram-in-english/), which agree word for word with each other (stotranidhi's own romanization marks long e/o with macrons in Telugu-transliteration style; those have been normalized here to the corpus's plain e/o convention for Sanskrit). This was cross-checked against sanskritdocuments.org's independently prepared edition (doc_devii/tripurasundarIpancharatnastotram.itx, transliterated and proofread by PSA Easwaran, last updated January 18, 2020), fetched in raw itrans form via the browser tools. vignanam.org does not carry this specific pañcaratnam: direct probes of vignanam.org/english|devanagari|telugu/tripura-sundari-pancharatna-stotram.html all returned "Access Denied," and web search under vignanam.org surfaces only its "Tripura Sundari Ashtakam" pages (a different, already-catalogued text, corpus file 08) — so cross-checking here rests on the two independently maintained sources above rather than on vignanam. The two sources agree closely across all six verses; three real variant readings surfaced and are detailed in the recension note at the end of this file.

Verse count: 6 numbered verses in both sources — the five "ratna" (jewel) verses of bodily praise proper (1-5), each closing on the same refrain, plus one further verse (6) in a different meter that is a closing dedicatory couplet rather than a sixth "jewel." Both independent sources number this couplet 6 rather than leaving it unnumbered, so it is kept as verse 6 here rather than silently reassigned to "verse none," per the corpus's numbering rule. One unnumbered one-line prose colophon ("iti ...") follows.

Sections:
స్తోత్రం — Stotra proper (the five pañcaratna verses of bodily praise, vv.1-5, each in Vasantatilakā metre, each closing on the refrain "tvāṁ sāmprataṁ tripurasundari devi vande")
సమర్పణ శ్లోకః — Samarpaṇaślokaḥ, the closing dedicatory verse (v.6, in anuṣṭubh, offering the newly composed pañcaratnam at the goddess's feet)
పుష్పిక — Puṣpikā (the closing one-line prose colophon)

Blurb: This is a meditation in five verses in the Vasantatilakā metre. It describes the form of Tripurasundarī from head to foot. It names her dark curling hair, her face like the moon, her nose like the campaka flower, her lotus eyes, her teeth like jasmine buds, her jewelled throat, her full breasts and her waist dark as a bee, her thighs smooth as the plantain, and her feet, which the crowns of Indra and the other gods touch. It names her golden garment and her sword. Last, it places her lotus feet within the heart of Koṭīśvara. Each verse closes with the same words of homage. A sixth verse follows in a different metre. In it the poet addresses her as Bālā, and offers this newly composed pañcaratnam as an ornament to be worn at her two feet.

--- verse 1 | section: స్తోత్రం ---
deva:
नीलालकां शशिमुखीं नवपल्लवोष्ठीं
चाम्पेयपुष्पसुषमोज्ज्वलदिव्यनासाम् ।
पद्मेक्षणां मुकुरसुन्दरगण्डभागां
त्वां साम्प्रतं त्रिपुरसुन्दरि देवि वन्दे ॥
iast:
nīlālakāṁ śaśimukhīṁ navapallavoṣṭhīṁ
campeyapuṣpasuṣamojjvaladivyanāsām |
padmekṣaṇāṁ mukurasundaragaṇḍabhāgāṁ
tvāṁ sāmprataṁ tripurasundari devi vande ||
en: I now bow to you, O Tripurasundarī, O Devī — you whose curling locks are dark, whose face is moon-bright, whose lower lip is like a fresh tender shoot, whose divine nose is radiant with the beauty of a campaka flower, whose eyes are lotuses, and whose cheeks are as lovely as a mirror.
tel: నల్లని ముంగురులు కలదానా, చంద్రుని వంటి ముఖము కలదానా, లేత చిగురు వంటి క్రింది పెదవి కలదానా, సంపెంగ పువ్వు అందముతో ప్రకాశించు దివ్యమైన నాసిక కలదానా, పద్మముల వంటి కన్నులు కలదానా, అద్దము వలె సుందరమైన చెక్కిళ్ళు కలదానా త్రిపురసుందరీ దేవీ, నిన్ను ఇప్పుడు నమస్కరించుచున్నాను.
hi: हे त्रिपुरसुन्दरि देवि! मैं अभी तुम्हें प्रणाम करता हूँ — जिसकी घुँघराली अलकें श्याम हैं, जिसका मुख चन्द्रमा-सा है, जिसका निचला ओष्ठ कोमल नवपल्लव-सा है, जिसकी दिव्य नासिका चम्पक-पुष्प की शोभा से उज्ज्वल है, जिसके नेत्र कमल-सदृश हैं, और जिसके कपोल दर्पण-से सुन्दर हैं।

--- verse 2 | section: స్తోత్రం ---
deva:
श्रीकुन्दकुड्मलशिखोज्ज्वलदन्तबृन्दमन्दस्मितद्युतितिरोहितचारुवाणीम् ।
नानामणिस्थगितहारसुचारुकण्ठीं
त्वां साम्प्रतं त्रिपुरसुन्दरि देवि वन्दे ॥
iast:
śrīkundakuḍmalaśikhojjvaladantabṛndamandasmitadyutitirohitacāruvāṇīm |
nānāmaṇisthagitahārasucārukaṇṭhīṁ
tvāṁ sāmprataṁ tripurasundari devi vande ||
en: I now bow to you, O Tripurasundarī, O Devī — you whose teeth gleam like a row of jasmine (kunda) buds just opening, your sweet voice all but veiled (tirohita) by the shimmer of your gentle smile, and whose lovely throat is hung with a necklace inset with many gems. (Sanskritdocuments.org's independently proofread edition divides this same compound differently — "...dantavṛndāṁ mandasmitadyutitirāhitacāruvāṇīm" — reading "tirāhita" where stotranidhi.com, the base text used here, reads "tirohita"; "tirohita," 'hidden/eclipsed,' is the more grammatically sound reading and is kept here.)
tel: శ్రేష్ఠమైన మల్లెమొగ్గల వంటి ప్రకాశించు దంతపంక్తులు కలదానా, మృదుమందహాసపు కాంతిచే మరుగుపడిన మధురవాణి కలదానా, నానారత్నఖచితమైన హారముతో అందమైన కంఠము కలదానా త్రిపురసుందరీ దేవీ, నిన్ను ఇప్పుడు నమస్కరించుచున్నాను.
hi: हे त्रिपुरसुन्दरि देवि! मैं अभी तुम्हें प्रणाम करता हूँ — जिसके दाँत खिलते हुए कुन्द-कली-समूह-से उज्ज्वल हैं, जिसकी मधुर वाणी कोमल मन्द मुस्कान की द्युति से मानो ढक गई है, तथा जिसका सुन्दर कण्ठ नाना मणियों से जड़े हार से सुशोभित है।

--- verse 3 | section: స్తోత్రం ---
deva:
पीनस्तनीं घनभुजां विपुलाब्जहस्तां
भृङ्गावलीजितसुशोभितरोमराजिम् ।
मत्तेभकुम्भकुचभारसुनम्रमध्यां
त्वां साम्प्रतं त्रिपुरसुन्दरि देवि वन्दे ॥
iast:
pīnastanīṁ ghanabhujāṁ vipulābjahastāṁ
bhṛṅgāvalījitasuśobhitaromarājim |
mattebhakumbhakucabhārasunamramadhyāṁ
tvāṁ sāmprataṁ tripurasundari devi vande ||
en: I now bow to you, O Tripurasundarī, O Devī — you whose breasts are full, whose arms are firm, whose broad hands are lotus-soft, whose lovely romarājī (the fine line of body-hair below the navel, a conventional mark of beauty) outshines even a row of black bees, and whose waist bends gracefully under the weight of breasts as full as a rutting elephant's temples.
tel: పుష్టిగా ఉన్న స్తనములు కలదానా, దృఢమైన బాహువులు కలదానా, విశాలమైన పద్మము వంటి హస్తములు కలదానా, తుమ్మెదల వరుసను జయించిన శోభాయమానమైన రోమరాజి (నాభి క్రింది సన్నని రోమపంక్తి, సౌందర్యచిహ్నం) కలదానా, మదించిన ఏనుగు కుంభస్థలముల వంటి స్తనభారముచే వంగిన నడుము కలదానా త్రిపురసుందరీ దేవీ, నిన్ను ఇప్పుడు నమస్కరించుచున్నాను.
hi: हे त्रिपुरसुन्दरि देवि! मैं अभी तुम्हें प्रणाम करता हूँ — जिसके स्तन पीन हैं, भुजाएँ सुदृढ़ हैं, विशाल हाथ कमल-सम कोमल हैं, जिसकी रोमराजि (नाभि के नीचे की पतली रोमपंक्ति, सौन्दर्य-चिह्न) भृंगावली को भी मात करती हुई शोभित है, तथा जो मत्त गज के कुम्भस्थल-सम स्तनों के भार से सुन्दर झुकी कमर वाली है।

--- verse 4 | section: స్తోత్రం ---
deva:
रम्भोज्ज्वलोरुयुगलां मृगराजपत्रामिन्द्रादिदेवमकुटोज्ज्वलपादपद्माम् ।
हेमाम्बरां घनघृताञ्चितखड्गवल्लीं
त्वां साम्प्रतं त्रिपुरसुन्दरि देवि वन्दे ॥
iast:
rambhojjvaloruyugalāṁ mṛgarājapatrāmindrādidevamakuṭojjvalapādapadmām |
hemāmbarāṁ ghanaghṛtāñcitakhaḍgavallīṁ
tvāṁ sāmprataṁ tripurasundari devi vande ||
en: I now bow to you, O Tripurasundarī, O Devī — you whose pair of thighs gleam splendidly like plantain trees; "mṛgarājapatrām," an epithet of uncertain precise sense (literally "lion/deer-king-leaved," perhaps naming a particular garment or ornament — reproduced as printed rather than guessed at); whose lotus feet are radiant with the touch of the crowns of Indra and the other gods; who wears a garment of gold; and who bears a splendid, richly gleaming sword named khaḍgavallī ("sword-creeper," a poetic name for a slender curved blade). Sanskritdocuments.org's independently proofread edition instead reads "karadhṛtāñcita" ("held aloft and adorned in the hand") where stotranidhi.com, used as the base text here, reads "ghanaghṛtāñcita"; the sanskritdocuments variant parses more cleanly and is flagged here and in the closing recension note.
tel: అరటిచెట్టు వలె ప్రకాశించు తొడలజంట కలదానా; "మృగరాజపత్రాం" అనునది ఖచ్చితమైన అర్థం నిశ్చయము కాని పదబంధము (బహుశా ఏదైనా వస్త్రము లేదా ఆభరణము పేరు కావచ్చు — మూలంలో ఉన్నట్లుగానే ఇక్కడ ఉంచబడింది); ఇంద్రాది దేవతల కిరీటముల స్పర్శచే ప్రకాశించు పాదపద్మములు కలదానా; బంగారు వస్త్రము ధరించినదానా; ప్రకాశవంతముగా మెరయు ఖడ్గవల్లి (వంకర కత్తికి కావ్యనామం) అనే ఖడ్గమును ధరించినదానా త్రిపురసుందరీ దేవీ, నిన్ను ఇప్పుడు నమస్కరించుచున్నాను.
hi: हे त्रिपुरसुन्दरि देवि! मैं अभी तुम्हें प्रणाम करता हूँ — जिसकी जंघाएँ केले के वृक्ष-सी उज्ज्वल हैं; "मृगराजपत्राम्" — जिसका ठीक-ठीक अर्थ अनिश्चित है (सम्भवतः किसी वस्त्र या आभूषण का नाम — मूल में जैसा है वैसा ही यहाँ रखा गया है); जिसके चरणकमल इन्द्रादि देवताओं के मुकुटों के स्पर्श से उज्ज्वल हैं; जो स्वर्ण-वस्त्र धारण करती है; तथा जो चमकीली, "खड्गवल्ली" (वक्र तलवार का काव्यात्मक नाम) नामक तलवार धारण करती है।

--- verse 5 | section: స్తోత్రం ---
deva:
मत्तेभवक्त्रजननीं मृडदेहयुक्तां
शैलाग्रमध्यनिलयां वरसुन्दराङ्गीम् ।
कोटीश्वराख्यहृदिसंस्थितपादपद्मां
त्वां साम्प्रतं त्रिपुरसुन्दरि देवि वन्दे ॥
iast:
mattebhavaktrajananīṁ mṛḍadehayuktāṁ
śailāgramadhyanilayāṁ varasundarāṅgīm |
koṭīśvarākhyahṛdisaṁsthitapādapadmāṁ
tvāṁ sāmprataṁ tripurasundari devi vande ||
en: I now bow to you, O Tripurasundarī, O Devī — mother of the elephant-faced one (mattebhavaktrajananī, i.e. Gaṇeśa); you who share the body of Mṛḍa (Śiva, "the gracious one"); who dwell amid mountain peaks; whose limbs are supremely lovely; and whose lotus feet are enshrined in the heart of the one called Koṭīśvara (an epithet or tīrtha-name of Śiva, "lord of ten million [worlds/liṅgas]").
tel: మదగజ ముఖము గల గణేశుని తల్లివి, మృడుని (శివుని) దేహముతో ఏకమైనదానా, పర్వతశిఖరాగ్రములలో నివసించుదానా, శ్రేష్ఠమైన సుందరాంగములు కలదానా, కోటీశ్వరుడు అను పేరుగల (శివుని) హృదయమున నెలకొన్న పాదపద్మములు కలదానా త్రిపురసుందరీ దేవీ, నిన్ను ఇప్పుడు నమస్కరించుచున్నాను.
hi: हे त्रिपुरसुन्दरि देवि! मैं अभी तुम्हें प्रणाम करता हूँ — गजमुख गणेश की जननी, मृड (शिव) के देह से युक्त, पर्वत-शिखरों के मध्य निवास करने वाली, अत्यन्त सुन्दर अंगों वाली, तथा कोटीश्वर नामक (शिव के) हृदय में प्रतिष्ठित चरणकमलों वाली।

--- verse 6 | section: సమర్పణ శ్లోకః ---
deva:
बाले त्वत्पादयुगलं ध्यात्वा सम्प्रति निर्मितम् ।
नवीनं पञ्चरत्नं च धार्यतां चरणद्वये ॥
iast:
bāle tvatpādayugalaṁ dhyātvā samprati nirmitam |
navīnaṁ pañcaratnaṁ ca dhāryatāṁ caraṇadvaye ||
en: O Bālā (young goddess) — meditating on your pair of feet, this fresh pañcaratna (set of five jewels, i.e. the five verses just recited) has just now been composed; may it be worn upon your two feet, like an anklet of five jewels.
tel: ఓ బాలా! నీ పాదయుగళమును ధ్యానించి ఇప్పుడే ఈ నూతనమైన పంచరత్నము రచింపబడినది; ఇది నీ రెండు పాదములయందు ఆభరణముగా ధరింపబడుగాక.
hi: हे बाले! तुम्हारे चरणयुगल का ध्यान करके अभी-अभी यह नवीन पञ्चरत्न रचा गया है; यह तुम्हारे दोनों चरणों में आभूषण के रूप में धारण किया जाए।

--- verse none | section: Colophon ---
deva:
इति श्री त्रिपुरसुन्दरी पञ्चरत्न स्तोत्रम् ।
iast:
iti śrī tripurasundarī pañcaratna stotram |
en: Thus ends the Śrī Tripurasundarī Pañcaratna Stotram. (Sanskritdocuments.org's edition closes instead with "iti śrī tripurasundarī pañcaratna stotraṁ sampūrṇam" — adding sampūrṇam, "[it] is complete" — a minor variant noted here and in the recension note below.)
tel: ఇది శ్రీ త్రిపురసుందరీ పంచరత్న స్తోత్రము ముగిసినది.
hi: इस प्रकार श्री त्रिपुरसुन्दरी पञ्चरत्न स्तोत्रम् समाप्त होता है।

Recension note: Base text is stotranidhi.com's Devanāgarī and IAST editions of "Tripura Sundari Pancharatna Stotram" (Dasa Mahavidya section), which agree word for word with each other; stotranidhi's own transliteration uses macrons over long e/o (ē, ō) in its Telugu-influenced romanization style, which have been normalized to the corpus's plain-e/o Sanskrit convention throughout. This was cross-checked against sanskritdocuments.org's independently transliterated and proofread edition (doc_devii/tripurasundarIpancharatnastotram.itx, by PSA Easwaran, updated January 18, 2020), retrieved in raw itrans form via the browser tools (WebFetch alone tends to summarize .itx files rather than quote them). vignanam.org, this corpus's usual primary source, does not carry this particular pañcaratnam under its standard /devanagari|telugu|english/ slug pattern — direct URL probes returned "Access Denied" and web search surfaced only vignanam's "Tripura Sundari Ashtakam" (already corpus file 08, a distinct eight-verse text with a different opening line, "kadambavanacāriṇīṁ..."). Cross-checking therefore rests on the two independently maintained sources named above.

The two sources agree closely. Three concrete variants surfaced: (1) verse 2 — stotranidhi reads the compound ending "...tirohitacāruvāṇīm" ("veiled/eclipsed sweet voice"), which is grammatically the more sensible reading and is kept as the base text; sanskritdocuments' itx has "...tirāhitacāruvāṇīm," almost certainly an OCR/typing slip, and also divides the preceding word as "dantavṛndāṁ" (with -āṁ) rather than stotranidhi's fused "dantabṛnda-" continuing directly into the next word. (2) Verse 4 — stotranidhi reads "ghanaghṛtāñcitakhaḍgavallīm" ("a sword richly gleaming, as if oiled"); sanskritdocuments reads "karadhṛtāñcitakhaḍgavallīm" ("a sword held aloft and adorned in the hand"), which parses more cleanly; stotranidhi's reading is kept as the base text per this corpus's normal source order, with the variant flagged in the verse's own en field. Also in verse 4, the compound "mṛgarājapatrām" (literally "lion/deer-king-leaved") resists confident glossing on either source; it is reproduced exactly as printed (identically in both sources) rather than guessed at. (3) The closing colophon — stotranidhi ends "iti śrī tripurasundarī pañcaratna stotram," sanskritdocuments adds "sampūrṇam" ("is complete"); stotranidhi's shorter form is used as the base colophon line here, with the sanskritdocuments variant noted in that block's en field.

No content verse was left unverified: all six numbered verses and the closing colophon are attested identically in substance across both independently sourced editions. Both sources number the closing dedicatory couplet as verse 6 (not as an unnumbered coda), so it is kept as verse 6 here rather than silently renumbered or demoted to "verse none," even though only verses 1-5 are the titular "five jewels." This stotram contains no bīja/mantra syllables, nyāsa, or dhyāna apparatus of its own — it is, in its five main verses, itself already written in dhyāna-verse register (a running bodily description), a pattern also seen in this corpus's Tripurasundaryaṣṭakam (file 08).
