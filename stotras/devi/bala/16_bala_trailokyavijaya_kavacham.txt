Blurb: These are forty verses from the Rudrayāmala, and the usual roles of the tantric dialogue are reversed. Here it is the Devī who asks and Bhairava who yields. He calls this armour of the three-syllabled Bālā his whole treasure, hidden even from the thirty gods, and he admits that he gives it up only out of affection for her. What follows is unusual in shape. The first third is not protection at all but a list of debts owed to the kavaca. Nārāyaṇa slew the daitya kings by it, Indra slew Bala, Kumāra slew Tāraka, Rāma slew Rāvaṇa, and Agastya swallowed Vātāpi. More than that, it claims that Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Śiva, Indra, the sun, the moon, fire, Varuṇa, Vāyu, Yama, Kubera and the ocean each became what they are by this kavaca. Only then does the armour itself begin, and it is built entirely out of the mātṛkā. The whole Sanskrit alphabet is laid across the body in order: the vowels on the head and face, the five series of stops down the trunk and arms, the semivowels on the back, the sibilants at the navel, and laṁ and kṣaṁ at the hips. The three bījas aiṁ, klīṁ and sauḥ are permuted afresh at every joint. The eight Mātṛkās then hold the eight directions, and four guardians hold the four watches of the day, so that no seam of space or of time is left open. The closing verses promise sons to the barren, wealth to the poor and victory in battle. They end by ordering that the text be guarded as one guards one's own origin.

Recension note: The two stotranidhi.com pages used (IAST on the /en/ page, Devanāgarī on the /hi/ page) present an identical 40-unit text; they were cross-checked verse for verse and no variant readings were found. Both slugs were confirmed against stotranidhi.com's post sitemaps. Three points of structure are worth recording. First, verse 32 is a single hemistich rather than a full śloka: it carries the main verb for the long list of dangers begun in verse 31, and both sources print it under its own number, so it is kept as verse 32 here rather than merged into 31. Second, the ṛṣyādi and viniyoga (vv. 14–15) are metrical here, not the usual prose formula, and verse 15 runs to three hemistichs in both sources; since they are numbered verses of the text itself they remain in the sequence, and no prose apparatus of any kind appears on either page. Third, in verse 19 both pages print लुं and लूं (luṁ, lūṁ) each followed by an asterisk keyed to a footnote identifying them as the vocalic ḷ and ḹ of the mātṛkā series — a standard editorial flag, since these two syllables have no independent Devanāgarī vowel sign in common use; the asterisks have been removed and the readings kept exactly as printed. The mātṛkā-nyāsa of verses 17–27 covers the full alphabet in order (16 vowels, then ka- through pa-varga, then the semivowels, sibilants, and finally laṁ kṣaṁ) with no syllable omitted and none repeated, which is a useful internal check that the text is complete. Verses 24 and 25 place the nyāsa on the liṅga, the bhaga and the thighs, in the plain Kaula manner; these are translated as they stand. The colophon reads "iti śrīrudrayāmale śrī bālā trailokyavijaya kavacam," assigning the text to the Rudrayāmala — the same source tantra given for Śrī Bālā Kavacam 2 already in this folder, though the two are entirely different texts with no shared verses. Stotranidhi carries no prose translation on either page; the en/tel/hi renderings above were made for this corpus directly from the Sanskrit.
