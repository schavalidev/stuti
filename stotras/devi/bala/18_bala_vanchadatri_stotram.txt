Verse count: 13

Blurb: This hymn has thirteen verses, and it is not one dhyāna but thirteen, each cut to a different wish. That is what the title claims: vāñchādātrī means 'she who gives what is wanted'. The hymn says so as it goes. Verse 1 is worshipped for mastery of speech. Verse 5 is for the winning of your own form. Verse 9 is for the attainment of wealth. Verse 10 is for the gaining of knowledge. Verse 11 is for health. Verse 12 is for gladness of mind. The goddess changes colour to match each request. She is white as jasmine and strung with pearls for eloquence. She is gold upon a lotus for wealth. She is spotless white for health. She is red as the bandhūka flower in the long śārdūlavikrīḍita portrait at the centre. Her hands change with her, from citron and skull-bowl and bow to the plain book and rosary. Between these images sits verse 6, the one wholly inward verse. It drops the description of her form and places the three bījas along the spine instead: the vāgbhava at the base, the kāma bīja red as a scarlet mite in the lotus of the heart, and the śākta syllable blazing white at the crown-aperture. It promises that those who meditate on these three go to the subtle state. The metres shift with the moods: upajāti, then śārdūlavikrīḍita, then plain anuṣṭubh for the short petitions, and a single āryā at the end that asks for nothing but speed, saying 'quicken my mind at once'.

पद्मासनगतां बालां ध्यायामि धनसिद्धये ॥
iast:
mātuliṅgapayojanmahastāṁ kanakasannibhām |
padmāsanagatāṁ bālāṁ dhyāyāmi dhanasiddhaye ||
en: With the citron and the lotus in her hands, her hue like gold, seated on the lotus throne — I meditate on Bālā for the attainment of wealth.
tel: మాతులింగము (మాదీఫలము), పద్మము చేతులయందు కలదానిని, బంగారమువలె ప్రకాశించునదానిని, పద్మాసనమునందున్న బాలను ధనసిద్ధి కొరకు నేను ధ్యానించుచున్నాను.
hi: मातुलिंग और कमल हाथों में लिए, स्वर्ण के समान वर्ण वाली, पद्मासन पर स्थित बाला का मैं धन-सिद्धि के लिए ध्यान करता हूँ।

--- verse 10 ---
deva:
वरपीयूषकलशपुस्तकाभीतिधारिणीम् ।
सुधां स्रवन्तीं ज्ञानाप्त्यै ब्रह्मरन्ध्रे विचिन्तये ॥
iast:
varapīyūṣakalaśapustakābhītidhāriṇīm |
sudhāṁ sravantīṁ jñānāptyai brahmarandhre vicintaye ||
en: Bearing the boon-gesture, the vessel of nectar, the book, and the gesture of fearlessness, and streaming nectar — on her, at the brahmarandhra, I meditate for the gaining of knowledge.
tel: వరముద్రను, అమృతకలశమును, పుస్తకమును, అభయముద్రను ధరించినదానిని, అమృతమును స్రవించునదానిని — జ్ఞానప్రాప్తి కొరకు బ్రహ్మరంధ్రమునందు నేను చింతించుచున్నాను.
hi: वर-मुद्रा, पीयूष-कलश, पुस्तक और अभय धारण करने वाली, सुधा बरसाती हुई — ज्ञान की प्राप्ति के लिए ब्रह्मरंध्र में मैं उनका चिंतन करता हूँ।

--- verse 11 ---
deva:
शुक्लाम्बरां शशाङ्काभां ध्यायाम्यारोग्यदायिनीम् ।
सृणिपाशधरां देवीं रत्नालङ्कारभूषिताम् ॥
iast:
śuklāmbarāṁ śaśāṅkābhāṁ dhyāyāmyārogyadāyinīm |
sṛṇipāśadharāṁ devīṁ ratnālaṅkārabhūṣitām ||
en: Clothed in white, radiant as the moon, holding the goad and the noose, adorned with ornaments of jewels — I meditate on the goddess who gives health.
tel: శుక్లాంబరము ధరించినదానిని, చంద్రునివలె ప్రకాశించునదానిని, సృణి (అంకుశము) పాశములను ధరించినదానిని, రత్నాలంకారములచే భూషితమైనదానిని — ఆరోగ్యమునిచ్చు ఆ దేవిని నేను ధ్యానించుచున్నాను.
hi: श्वेत वस्त्र धारण किए, चंद्रमा के समान आभा वाली, सृणि (अंकुश) और पाश धारण किए, रत्नालंकारों से भूषित — आरोग्य देने वाली उस देवी का मैं ध्यान करता हूँ।

--- verse 12 ---
deva:
अकारादिक्षकारान्तवर्णावयवशालिनीम् ।
प्रसन्नामरुणामीक्षे सौमनस्यप्रदां शिवाम् ॥
iast:
akārādikṣakārāntavarṇāvayavaśālinīm |
prasannāmaruṇāmīkṣe saumanasyapradāṁ śivām ||
en: Whose very limbs are the letters from a to kṣa — gracious, ruddy, the auspicious one who bestows gladness of mind — on her I gaze.
tel: అకారము మొదలు క్షకారము వరకు గల వర్ణములే అవయవములుగా శోభిల్లునదానిని; ప్రసన్నురాలిని, అరుణవర్ణమైనదానిని, సౌమనస్యమునిచ్చు శివను నేను వీక్షించుచున్నాను.
hi: अकार से लेकर क्षकार तक के वर्ण ही जिसके अवयव हैं, ऐसी शालिनी; प्रसन्ना, अरुणा, सौमनस्य देने वाली शिवा को मैं देखता हूँ।

--- verse 13 ---
deva:
पुस्तकजपवटहस्ते वरदाभयचिह्नबाहुलते ।
कर्पूरामलदेहे वागीश्वरि चोदयाशु मम चेतः ॥
iast:
pustakajapavaṭahaste varadābhayacihnabāhulate |
karpūrāmaladehe vāgīśvari codayāśu mama cetaḥ ||
en: O you who hold the book and the rosary in your hands, whose creeper-arms bear the signs of boon-giving and fearlessness, whose body is spotless as camphor — O mistress of speech, quicken my mind at once.
tel: పుస్తకమును జపమాలను చేతులయందు ధరించినదానా, వరద అభయ చిహ్నములు కల లతలవంటి బాహువులు కలదానా, కర్పూరమువలె నిర్మలమైన దేహము కలదానా — ఓ వాగీశ్వరీ, నా చిత్తమును వెంటనే ప్రేరేపించుము.
hi: हे पुस्तक और जपमाला हाथों में धारण करने वाली, वरद और अभय के चिह्नों से युक्त लता-सी भुजाओं वाली, कर्पूर के समान निर्मल देह वाली — हे वागीश्वरि, मेरे चित्त को शीघ्र प्रेरित करो।

Recension note: The two stotranidhi.com pages used (IAST on the /en/ page, Devanāgarī on the /hi/ page) present an identical 13-verse text and were cross-checked verse for verse; no variant readings were found, and both slugs were confirmed against stotranidhi.com's post sitemaps. The only discrepancy between the pages is the category breadcrumb — devī stotrāṇi on the /en/ page against śrī bālā stotrāṇi on the /hi/ page — which affects navigation only, not the text; the hymn itself names Bālā in verses 1, 3, 4, 7 and 9 and identifies her with Tripurā in verse 4. The hymn has no sections: it is a chain of independent dhyānas in four different meters — upajāti (vv. 1–4, 7), śārdūlavikrīḍita (vv. 5–6), anuṣṭubh (vv. 8–12) and āryā (v. 13) — with no dialogue frame, no viniyoga and no phalaśruti, so no Sections block is given above. Six of the thirteen verses name their own purpose in the text itself: vāṅmayasiddhi (v. 1), possession of the goddess's own form (v. 5), the subtle state (v. 6), dhanasiddhi (v. 9), jñānāpti (v. 10) and ārogya (v. 11), with saumanasya added in verse 12 — which is what earns the title vāñchādātrī, "giver of what is wished." Two printed readings are kept as they stand. In verses 1 and 2 a compound is broken by a hyphen across the pāda boundary in both scripts (vidyākṣamālāsukapālamudrā- / -rājatkarāṁ, and uddīptaratnā- / -''sane, the latter carrying the double avagraha ऽऽ); this is stotranidhi's line-breaking convention and has been reproduced rather than silently joined, matching how Śrī Bālā Mānasa Pūjā Stotram is handled in this folder. In verse 4, bhaje'khilarghyai is metrically short and grammatically strained in both sources where an ablative or dative of akhilārghyā would be expected; it is translated as "to whom the offering of all is due" and left unemended. The three bījas of verse 6 are named descriptively rather than spelled out — vāgbhava, manmatha and śākta, which are the three syllables of the Bālā tryakṣarī — and have been translated by their names rather than resolved into the syllables themselves, following the text. Stotranidhi carries no prose translation on either page; the en/tel/hi renderings above were made for this corpus directly from the Sanskrit.
