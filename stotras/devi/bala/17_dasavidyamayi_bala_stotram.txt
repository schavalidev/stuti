Blurb: This is a hymn from the Merutantra, built on a single claim: that the ten Mahāvidyās are not ten goddesses standing beside Bālā, but ten faces of Bālā herself. The opening verse names all ten in one breath. The hymn then takes them one at a time in ten dhyānas in the śārdūlavikrīḍita metre. Kālikā is dark as a raincloud. Bagalāmukhī is robed in yellow and grips the tongue of every enemy. Ṣoḍaśī holds noose and bow. Dhūmāvatī is gaunt, with the crow upon her banner, in soiled clothes and with a winnowing basket. Bhairavī is garlanded with the heads of daitya kings. Mātaṅgī bends over her vīṇā. Bhuvaneśvarī is crowned with the moon. Kamalā is golden and bathed by snow-white elephants. Chinnamastā holds her own severed head and feeds her companions on the blood from her own throat. Tārā is green as dūrvā grass and carries her chopping knife. Every one of the ten verses closes with the same two words, 'bālāṁ saṅkaṭanāśinīm', meaning 'Bālā, destroyer of danger'. The refrain therefore performs the very identification that the hymn argues for. The metre then changes to śikhariṇī and the ten are placed upon the body: Mātaṅgī on the face, Tārā on the eyes, Kālī within, Bhairavī between the brows, Chinnamastā at the hips, Kamalā at the breasts, and Bagalāmukhī at the feet. The last verse leaves her with her crystal rosary and her book, three lines at her throat, unmatched in song, and the wine she has always drunk.

deva:
श्रीकाली बगलामुखी च ललिता धूमावती भैरवी
मातङ्गी भुवनेश्वरी च कमला श्रीर्वज्रवैरोचनी ।
तारा पूर्वमहापदेन कथिता विद्या स्वयं शम्भुना
लीलारूपमयी च देशदशधा बाला तु मां पातु सा ॥

śrīkālī bagalāmukhī ca lalitā dhūmāvatī bhairavī

deva:

sadā pītā hālā jayati kila bālā daśamayī ||
en: The slopes of her breast graced with a shining garland of mandāra blossoms; her crystal rosary a rescue from every terror, the book her chief treasure; three lines at her throat; unequalled in gamaka, in movement and in song; the wine forever drunk — victorious indeed is Bālā, who is made of the ten.
tel: మందారవృక్షపు పూలహారముచే ప్రకాశించు వక్షస్థల ప్రాంతము కలది; సమస్త భయమునుండి రక్షించు స్ఫటికమాల కలది, పుస్తకమే శ్రేష్ఠమైన సంపదగా కలది; కంఠమునందు మూడు రేఖలు కలది; గమకమునందు, గతియందు, గానమునందు అసమానమైన నైపుణ్యము కలది; ఎల్లప్పుడూ హాలను (మద్యమును) సేవించినది — దశమయియైన బాల నిజముగా జయించుచున్నది.
hi: मंदार वृक्ष के पुष्पों के हार से सुशोभित वक्षस्थल वाली; समस्त त्रास से त्राण करने वाली स्फटिक-माला वाली, पुस्तक ही जिसकी श्रेष्ठ संपदा है; कंठ पर तीन रेखाएँ; गमक, गति और गीत में अद्वितीय निपुण; सदा हाला (मद्य) का पान किए हुए — दशमयी बाला की निश्चय ही जय होती है।

---
Recension note: The two stotranidhi.com pages used (IAST on the /en/ page, Devanāgarī on the /hi/ page) present an identical 13-verse text and were cross-checked verse for verse; no variant readings were found, and both slugs were confirmed against stotranidhi.com's post sitemaps. Verses 1–11 are in śārdūlavikrīḍita and verses 12–13 in śikhariṇī, the change of meter coinciding exactly with the change of function from dhyāna to nyāsa. Four readings are worth flagging as printed rather than emended. In verse 1, deśadaśadhā is metrically sound but semantically strained; it has been rendered "standing forth tenfold," and both scripts agree on it. In verse 4, bhaskarāṁ is printed with a short first vowel in both sources where bhāsvarāṁ or bhāskarāṁ would be expected. In verse 5 both sources close the third pāda with a daṇḍa as well as the second; since it marks no structural division it has not been reproduced in the verse body. In verse 12, bhairavi is printed with a short final i in both scripts, giving what looks like a vocative inside a list of locatives; the sense required is the nominative Bhairavī, and it is translated that way. The ten vidyās of the dhyāna verses are, in order of appearance: Kālikā (2), Bagalāmukhī (3), Ṣoḍaśī (4), Dhūmāvatī (5), Bhairavī (6), Mātaṅgī (7), Bhuvaneśvarī (8), Kamalā (9), Chinnamastā (10) and Tārā (11) — a complete Daśamahāvidyā set, though the opening roll-call of verse 1 lists them in a different order and names Chinnamastā by her esoteric title Vajravairocanī and Ṣoḍaśī as Lalitā. That mention of Lalitā is a name within the ten, not a claim on the Lalitā corpus: the refrain of every dhyāna verse subordinates each vidyā to Bālā, not the reverse. The colophon reads "iti śrīmerutantre śrī daśavidyāmayī bālā stotram," assigning the text to the Merutantra. The Kaula details of verses 10 and 13 — Chinnamastā feeding her companion on her own blood, and Bālā as the one who has always drunk wine — are translated as they stand. Stotranidhi carries no prose translation on either page; the en/tel/hi renderings above were made for this corpus directly from the Sanskrit.
