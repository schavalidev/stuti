Verse count: 16

Sections:
ధ్యానమ్ — Dhyānam, the meditation verse (v. 1)
ఉపచారాః — Upacārāḥ, the offerings made in the mind (vv. 2–16)

Blurb: A mānasa-pūjā is worship performed entirely in the mind. It is called the antaryāga, the inner rite. This one runs the full sequence of services without a single physical object changing hands. It begins with a dhyāna verse in the śārdūlavikrīḍita metre. Fifteen verses in a uniform metre of eleven syllables then present the offerings one by one. They give a seat of moonstone with golden feet, water for the feet, the arghya in golden vessels, and water to sip. They give a bath in water consecrated by mantra, the ornaments being respectfully set aside first. They give a pair of saffron-red silks, and then crown, bangles, anklets and rings. They give sandal paste blended with musk and camphor. They give kadamba, jasmine, campaka and ketakī flowers. They give incense of the eight fragrances, and lamps fed with cow's ghee and shaped like campaka buds. They give milk-rice and a thousand cakes of black gram, and betel with powdered pearl. At the last they give an añjali made not of flowers but of the mantras themselves. The hymn also hides a mantra within its own structure: the opening syllables of the offering verses spell out the Śrīvidyā vidyā. See the recension note for this. The last verse closes not with a request but simply with namaste, addressed to the goddess who wears the body of a girl.

Recension note: The three stotranidhi.com script versions (Devanāgarī, IAST and Telugu) were compared verse by verse and agree word for word throughout; no variant readings were found, and the verse numbering runs 1–16 without irregularity. The hymn is a mantragarbha as well as a pūjā: the opening syllables of the fifteen offering verses (2–16) read in order as e · ī · la · hrīṁ | ha · sa · ka · ha · la · hrīṁ | sa · ka · la · hrīṁ | śrīṁ. That is the Śrīvidyā ṣoḍaśākṣarī lacking only its very first syllable ka, and verse 15 names the vidyā outright as the Pañcadaśī together with its sixteen-syllabled form. Whether a ka-initial verse has fallen out of this recension between verses 1 and 2 — which would yield sixteen offering verses for sixteen syllables, matching the sixteen upacāras that the Telugu headnote says the hymn performs — or the omission is deliberate, cannot be settled from this source, since all three script pages print the identical fifteen. The upacāras actually present are, in order: āsana, pādya, arghya, ācamana, snāna, vastra, ābharaṇa, gandha, puṣpa, dhūpa, dīpa, naivedya, tāmbūla, mantrapuṣpāñjali and namaskāra. Verse 1 is in śārdūlavikrīḍita and verses 2–16 in a uniform eleven-syllable meter. The second pāda of verse 11 is compressed to the point of obscurity in all three versions and is translated here following the Telugu gloss.
