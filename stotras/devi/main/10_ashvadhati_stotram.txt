Title: Devī Aśvadhāṭī (Ambā Stuti)
Devanāgarī: देवी अश्वधाटी (अम्बा स्तुति)
Telugu: దేవీ అశ్వధాటీ (అంబా స్తుతి)

Author: disputed, and the sources consulted here do not agree — reported rather than resolved. vignanam.org heads its text "(कालिदास कृतम्)" and files it under its Kālidāsa category, i.e. Kālidāsa. stotranidhi.com closes with the colophon "iti mahākavi kālidāsa kṛta devī aśvadhāṭi stotram," i.e. Kālidāsa. sanskritdocuments.org, which carries the same poem's eight-verse core under the title ambāṣṭakam (athavā navaratnamañjarī), closes it with "iti śrīmacchaṅkarācāryaviracitam ambāṣṭakaṁ sampūrṇam," i.e. Śaṅkarācārya — but its own file header says "This is also attributed to Kalidasa or Vivekananda by some. It is difficult to prove the authorship," and its trailing note states outright that the Śaṅkara colophon was included only "due to its reference as composed by Shankaracharya in some places." A thread on the bhāratīya-vidvat-pariṣat list on the same poem asks whether any reliable source actually supports the Kālidāsa attribution and receives no answer. The poem itself contains no poet's signature in any of the thirteen verses, so nothing internal decides it. Note also that "aśvadhāṭī" in the title is the name of the METRE, not of any deity or of the author — see the recension note.

Source / recension: Base text is vignanam.org's śuddha-devanāgarī edition (vignanam.org/samskritam/devi-aswadhati-amba-stuti.html), collated word for word against vignanam's own Telugu-script edition and its IAST edition, and then against two independent sources: stotranidhi.com's IAST edition (stotranidhi.com/en/devi-pranava-sloki-stuti-in-english/), which carries all thirteen verses in exactly the same order, and sanskritdocuments.org's doc_devii file ambAShTakam.itx, which carries eight of these verses in a different order under a different title with a different colophon plus five more appended at the end. Cross-checking here is genuinely three-source and was fully achieved for all thirteen verses; the sources differ on many individual readings, and the substantive disagreements are itemised in the recension note. vignanam's Devanāgarī was fetched through the browser tools after WebFetch returned it empty, as expected; stotranidhi was fetched by curl with a browser User-Agent; sanskritdocuments' .itx was fetched through the browser tools to get raw text.

Verse count: 13, numbered 1–13 continuously in both vignanam.org and stotranidhi.com with no gaps or repeats. The count is itself a recension question: sanskritdocuments prints eight of these as a self-contained aṣṭaka and appends the remaining five separately, and its editorial note records that the poem also circulates as devī praṇavaślokī stutiḥ or daśaślokī "because there are 10 verses in some of the versions," with "some add three more to it." The 13-verse form followed here is the one current in the Telugu tradition.

Sections:
అశ్వధాటీ స్తోత్రం — Aśvadhāṭī Stotram (the thirteen verses; the text has no viniyoga, nyāsa, dhyāna or phalaśruti, and no section divisions of its own)

Blurb: The word Aśvadhāṭī names the metre of this poem, not its subject and not its deity. It is a galloping line of twenty-two syllables, named after the gait of a horse. The hymn is built to display that metre. Every one of its fifty-two pādas places the same rhyming syllable in the second, ninth and sixteenth positions, so that an internal rhyme recurs every seven syllables. The rhyme changes from verse to verse: ṭī, pā, lī, lā, mbā, sā, ṅkā, mbhā, kṣā, ndā, trā, lā and ndhā. Twice the poet asks Pārvatī for exactly this gift. In verse 1 he asks for poetic skill whose charge outruns a mare's hoof. In verse 10 he asks for speech that passes the swiftness of the celestial Gaṅgā. The request, the metre and the demonstration of it are therefore one and the same thing.

--- verse 1 | section: Aśvadhāṭī Stotram ---
deva:
चेटी भवन्निखिल खेटी कदम्बवन वाटीषु नाकि पटली
कोटीर चारुतर कोटी मणीकिरण कोटी करम्बित पदा ।
पाटीरगन्धि कुचशाटी कवित्व परिपाटीमगाधिप सुता
घोटीखुरादधिक धाटीमुदार मुख वीटीरसेन तनुताम् ॥
iast:
ceṭī bhavannikhila kheṭī kadambavana vāṭīṣu nāki paṭalī
koṭīra cārutara koṭī maṇīkiraṇa koṭī karambita padā |
pāṭīragandhi kucaśāṭī kavitva paripāṭīmagādhipa sutā
ghoṭīkhurādadhika dhāṭīmudāra mukha vīṭīrasena tanutām ||
en: In the kadamba-grove bowers of every hamlet the whole host of heaven-dwellers becomes her serving-maid; her feet are flecked with crores of rays from the gems at the lovely tips of their bowed crowns; her breast-cloth is fragrant with sandal (pāṭīra). May that daughter of the mountain-lord grant me a poetic craft (kavitva-paripāṭī) whose charge (dhāṭī) outruns a mare's hoof, by the juice of the betel-roll (vīṭī) in her generous mouth. The request is self-referential: a "charge faster than a mare's hoof" is precisely the aśvadhāṭī metre the verse is written in, and every pāda here rhymes on -ṭī at its 2nd, 9th and 16th syllable.
tel: ప్రతి పల్లెలోని కదంబవన వాటికలలో దేవతల సమూహమంతా ఆమెకు దాసీలుగా మారుతుంది; వంగిన ఆ దేవతల కిరీటాల అందమైన కొనలలోని మణుల నుండి వెలువడే కోట్ల కిరణాలు ఆమె పాదాలపై పరచుకున్నాయి; ఆమె కుచశాటి చందనగంధంతో పరిమళిస్తోంది. ఆ పర్వతరాజపుత్రిక తన ఉదారమైన ముఖంలోని తాంబూలరసంతో, ఆడగుర్రపు గిట్టల పరుగును మించిన ధాటి గల కవిత్వపరిపాటిని నాకు ప్రసాదించుగాక. ఈ కోరిక తనను తానే సూచించుకునేది — "గుర్రపు గిట్ట కంటే వేగవంతమైన ధాటి" అనేదే ఈ శ్లోకం రాయబడిన అశ్వధాటీ ఛందస్సు; ఇక్కడ ప్రతి పాదమూ రెండవ, తొమ్మిదవ, పదహారవ అక్షరాల వద్ద -టీ అనే ప్రాసను పలుకుతుంది.
hi: हर गाँव के कदंब-वन के कुंजों में समस्त देवगण उनकी दासी बन जाते हैं; झुके हुए उन देवताओं के मुकुटों की सुंदर नोकों में जड़ी मणियों से निकलती करोड़ों किरणें उनके चरणों पर बिखरी हैं; उनकी कुच-शाटी चंदन की सुगंध से महकती है। वह पर्वतराज-पुत्री अपने उदार मुख के ताम्बूल-रस से मुझे ऐसी कवित्व-परिपाटी प्रदान करें जिसकी धाटी घोड़ी के खुर से भी तेज़ हो। यह याचना स्वयं-निर्देशक है — "घोड़ी के खुर से तेज़ धाटी" ही वह अश्वधाटी छंद है जिसमें यह श्लोक रचा गया है, और यहाँ प्रत्येक पाद अपने दूसरे, नवें और सोलहवें अक्षर पर -टी की तुक मिलाता है।

--- verse 2 | section: Aśvadhāṭī Stotram ---
deva:
द्वैपायन प्रभृति शापायुध त्रिदिव सोपान धूलि चरणा
पापापह स्वमनु जापानुलीन जन तापापनोद निपुणा ।
नीपालया सुरभि धूपालका दुरितकूपादुदञ्चयतु माम्
रूपाधिका शिखरि भूपाल वंशमणि दीपायिता भगवती ॥
iast:
dvaipāyana prabhṛti śāpāyudha tridiva sopāna dhūli caraṇā
pāpāpaha svamanu jāpānulīna jana tāpāpanoda nipuṇā |
nīpālayā surabhi dhūpālakā duritakūpādudañcayatu mām
rūpādhikā śikhari bhūpāla vaṁśamaṇi dīpāyitā bhagavatī ||
en: The dust of her feet is the stairway to the third heaven for Dvaipāyana (Vyāsa) and the other sages whose weapon is the curse; she is expert at driving off the torment of people absorbed in the repetition (jāpa) of her own sin-destroying manu — manu here means her mantra, so the verse is prescribing her own mantra-japa. She dwells in the nīpa (kadamba) tree, her locks fragrant with incense; she surpasses all in beauty; she has become the jewel-lamp of the lineage of the mountain-king. May that Bhagavatī draw me up out of the well of my misdeeds.
tel: శాపమే ఆయుధంగా గల ద్వైపాయనుడు (వ్యాసుడు) మొదలైన మునులకు ఆమె పాదధూళియే స్వర్గానికి సోపానం; పాపాలను హరించే తన మనువును (మంత్రాన్ని) జపిస్తూ లీనమైన జనుల తాపాన్ని పోగొట్టడంలో ఆమె నిపుణురాలు — ఇక్కడ "మను" అంటే ఆమె మంత్రమే, కనుక ఈ శ్లోకం ఆమె మంత్రజపాన్నే విధిస్తోంది. నీపవృక్షంలో నివసించేది, సురభిళమైన ధూపంతో పరిమళించే అలకలు గలది, రూపంలో అందరినీ మించినది, పర్వతరాజ వంశానికి మణిదీపమైనది — ఆ భగవతి నన్ను దురితకూపం నుండి పైకి లేపుగాక.
hi: शाप ही जिनका आयुध है, उन द्वैपायन (व्यास) आदि मुनियों के लिए उनके चरणों की धूलि ही स्वर्ग की सीढ़ी है; पाप-नाशक अपने मनु (मंत्र) के जाप में लीन जनों के ताप को दूर करने में वे निपुण हैं — यहाँ "मनु" का अर्थ उनका मंत्र ही है, अतः यह श्लोक उनके मंत्र-जप का ही विधान करता है। नीप (कदंब) वृक्ष में निवास करने वाली, सुगंधित धूप से महकते केशों वाली, रूप में सबसे अधिक, पर्वतराज के वंश की मणि-दीप बनी हुई — वह भगवती मुझे दुरित के कूप से ऊपर उठा लें।

--- verse 3 | section: Aśvadhāṭī Stotram ---
deva:
यालीभि रात्मतनुतालीनकृत्प्रियक पालीषु खेलति भवा
व्याली नकुल्यसित चूली भरा चरण धूली लसन्मणिगणा ।
याली भृति श्रवसि ताली दलं वहति यालीक शोभि तिलका
साली करोतु मम काली मनः स्वपद नालीक सेवन विधौ ॥
iast:
yālībhi rātmatanutālīnakṛtpriyaka pālīṣu khelati bhavā
vyālī nakulyasita cūlī bharā caraṇa dhūlī lasanmaṇigaṇā |
yālī bhṛti śravasi tālī dalaṁ vahati yālīka śobhi tilakā
sālī karotu mama kālī manaḥ svapada nālīka sevana vidhau ||
en: Bhavā, who plays with her companions (ālī) among the rows (pālī) of priyaka trees where her own body dissolves away; a mongoose to the serpents, bearing a mass of dark tresses, the cluster of gems on her feet glittering through the dust; she wears a tālī-leaf on the ear that bears its ornament, and her brow (alīka) is graced with a tilaka — may that Kālī make my mind a bee (ālī) at the rite of attending upon the lotus (nālīka) of her own feet. The verse turns on ālī meaning both "woman companion" and "bee," and on nālīka meaning "lotus." The first pāda is the least stable in the whole poem across the three sources; see the recension note.
tel: ప్రియకవృక్షాల పాళీలలో తన శరీరమే లీనమయ్యేలా చెలికత్తెలతో (ఆలీలతో) ఆడుకునే భవా; సర్పాలకు ముంగిస వంటిది, నల్లని కురుల భారం గలది, ధూళిలోనూ మెరిసే పాదమణుల సముదాయం గలది; ఆభరణం ధరించిన చెవిపై తాళీదళాన్ని ధరించినది, అలీకంపై (నుదుటిపై) శోభించే తిలకం గలది — ఆ కాళి నా మనస్సును తన పాదనాళీకాన్ని (పాదపద్మాన్ని) సేవించే విధిలో తుమ్మెదగా (ఆలీగా) చేయుగాక. ఇక్కడ "ఆలీ" అంటే చెలికత్తె అనీ, తుమ్మెద అనీ రెండర్థాలు; "నాళీక" అంటే పద్మం. ఈ కావ్యంలో మొదటి పాదమే మూడు మూలాల మధ్య అత్యంత అస్థిరమైనది — రిసెన్షన్ నోట్ చూడండి.
hi: प्रियक वृक्षों की पालियों में, जहाँ उनका अपना शरीर ही लीन हो जाता है, सखियों (आली) के साथ क्रीड़ा करने वाली भवा; सर्पों के लिए नकुली, काले केशों का भार धारण करने वाली, धूलि में भी चमकते चरण-मणियों वाली; आभूषण धारण किए कान पर ताली-दल पहने हुए, अलीक (ललाट) पर शोभित तिलक वाली — वह काली मेरे मन को अपने चरण-नालीक (चरण-कमल) की सेवा-विधि में भ्रमर (आली) बना दें। यहाँ "आली" के दो अर्थ हैं — सखी और भ्रमर; "नालीक" का अर्थ कमल है। इस काव्य का यही प्रथम पाद तीनों स्रोतों में सर्वाधिक अस्थिर है — रिसेंशन नोट देखें।

--- verse 4 | section: Aśvadhāṭī Stotram ---
deva:
बालामृतांशु निभ फालामना गरुण चेला नितम्ब फलके
कोलाहल क्षपित कालामराकुशल कीलाल शोषण रविः ।
स्थूलाकुचे जलद नीलाकचे कलित वीला कदम्ब विपिने
शूलायुध प्रणति शीला दधातु हृदि शैलाधि राज तनया ॥
iast:
bālāmṛtāṁśu nibha phālāmanā garuṇa celā nitamba phalake
kolāhala kṣapita kālāmarākuśala kīlāla śoṣaṇa raviḥ |
sthūlākuce jalada nīlākace kalita vīlā kadamba vipine
śūlāyudha praṇati śīlā dadhātu hṛdi śailādhi rāja tanayā ||
en: Her brow like the young moon, a faintly ruddy garment across the slope of her hips; by her war-cry (kolāhala) she has done away with Kāla, a sun that dries up the blood (kīlāla) of those who wish the immortals ill; full of breast, cloud-dark of hair, at play in the kadamba grove, habitual in bowing to the trident-bearer — may the daughter of the king of mountains take up residence in my heart. The second half of this verse recurs almost verbatim as verse 12; sanskritdocuments carries only one of the two.
tel: బాలచంద్రుని వంటి ఫాలం గలది, నితంబఫలకంపై కొద్దిగా అరుణమైన చేలం ధరించినది; తన కోలాహలంతో కాలుని క్షపించినది, అమరులకు అకుశలం కోరేవారి కీలాలాన్ని (రక్తాన్ని) శోషించే సూర్యుని వంటిది; స్థూలమైన కుచాలు గలది, మేఘం వలె నల్లని కేశాలు గలది, కదంబవనంలో విహరించేది, శూలాయుధుడైన శివునికి ప్రణమిల్లడం అలవాటుగా గలది — ఆ శైలాధిరాజ తనయ నా హృదయంలో నిలుచుగాక. ఈ శ్లోకపు ఉత్తరార్ధం 12వ శ్లోకంలో దాదాపు అక్షరాలా పునరావృతమవుతుంది; సంస్కృతడాక్యుమెంట్స్ ఈ రెండింటిలో ఒకటే ఇస్తుంది.

Recension note: The title first. "Aśvadhāṭī" is the name of the METRE, not of the goddess and not of the author — dhāṭī is a charge or onrush, and aśva-dhāṭī is the gallop of a horse, so the title says only "the Goddess in horse-gait metre." A reader coming to the title cold would have no way to know that, which is why it is spelled out in the header and the Blurb. The metrical claim was verified by counting rather than taken on trust: every one of the fifty-two pādas has exactly twenty-two syllables, and every one of them places the same rhyming syllable at its 2nd, 9th and 16th position — a triple internal rhyme recurring at a fixed seven-syllable interval — with the rhyme held constant within a verse and changed between verses (v1 ṭī, v2 pā, v3 lī, v4 lā, v5 mbā, v6 sā, v7 ṅkā, v8 mbhā, v9 kṣā, v10 ndā, v11 trā, v12 lā, v13 ndhā). The identity is exact in eleven of the thirteen verses; in v9 the fourth pāda rhymes kṣe / kṣe / kṣā and in v10 the first pāda rhymes ndā / ndhā / ndā, so in those two the consonant cluster carries the rhyme while the vowel or aspiration shifts. The four-line pāda division printed by vignanam has therefore been preserved exactly and not regularized; where a compound runs across a pāda boundary (v3 pāda 1 to pāda 2) the break has been left where the source puts it, because the metre requires it there.

Base text: vignanam.org's śuddha-devanāgarī edition, collated against vignanam's own Telugu-script and IAST editions (which agree with it word for word), against stotranidhi.com's IAST edition, and against sanskritdocuments.org's doc_devii file ambAShTakam.itx. Cross-checking was fully achieved for all thirteen verses; no verse is unverified.

Attribution. The three sources do not agree and no attempt has been made here to pick a winner. vignanam.org prints "(कालिदास कृतम्)" under the title and files the text in its Kālidāsa category. stotranidhi.com closes with "iti mahākavi kālidāsa kṛta devī aśvadhāṭi stotram." sanskritdocuments.org closes its version with "iti śrīmacchaṅkarācāryaviracitam ambāṣṭakaṁ sampūrṇam," attributing it to Śaṅkarācārya — but its file header says "This is also attributed to Kalidasa or Vivekananda by some. It is difficult to prove the authorship," and its trailing note says explicitly that the Śaṅkara colophon was retained only "due to its reference as composed by Shankaracharya in some places," adding that both Kālidāsa and Śaṅkara "were known to have simpler construction in their compositions compared to this composition." A thread on the bhāratīya-vidvat-pariṣat scholarly list about this same stotra asks whether any reliable source supports the Kālidāsa attribution and gets no answer, and criticizes the circulating online versions as poorly edited, with compounds broken up so that the Sanskrit reads almost like Prakrit. So: the two Telugu-tradition sources say Kālidāsa, the pan-Indian archive says Śaṅkarācārya with an open disclaimer, and no verse carries an internal poet's signature.

Same-title-different-text warning, checked as instructed. "Ambā Stuti" and "Ambāṣṭakam" are generic enough to be shared by unrelated texts, and sanskritdocuments' file is in fact titled ambāṣṭakam athavā navaratnamañjarī, not aśvadhāṭī. It was confirmed to be this poem and not a different Ambā-stuti by opening line ("cheTIbhavannikhilakheTI...") and by metre (the same 22-syllable line with the 2/9/16 rhyme), and its own editorial note confirms the identification: it records that "this stotra is also known by different names… it is called aśvadhāṭīstutiḥ based on the metre used for these verses, translated as cadence of hooves of horses," and that it also circulates as devī praṇavaślokī stutiḥ or daśaślokī. Separately, vignanam.org and other sites carry an unrelated Ambā-stuti and an unrelated Devī Daśaślokī; those were not used.

Verse count and ordering. vignanam and stotranidhi both number 1–13 continuously with no repeat and no gap, in identical order. sanskritdocuments prints only eight of them as the numbered aṣṭaka, in a different order, and appends the other five unnumbered at the end of the file. Mapping its numbering to the one used here: sd 1 = v1, sd 2 = v12, sd 3 = v11, sd 4 = v2, sd 5 = v3, sd 6 = v7, sd 7 = v5, sd 8 = v13; appended unnumbered = v6, v8, v9, v10; and v4 is absent from sanskritdocuments altogether. This matters because v4 and v12 share their last two pādas almost verbatim (v4 "kalita vīlā… praṇati śīlā dadhātu hṛdi," v12 "kalita līlā… praṇati śīlā vibhātu hṛdi"), and their second pādas are variations on one another as well (v4 "kālāmarākuśala kīlāla śoṣaṇa raviḥ," v12 "kālāmarī kuśala kīlāla poṣaṇa ratā"). sanskritdocuments' own note flags this: "the following verses were added and number 2 and 12 have some commonality." Both verses have been kept and numbered as the Telugu-tradition sources print them; neither has been merged, dropped or renumbered.

Variant readings across the three sources, verse by verse. v3 pāda 1 is the least stable line in the poem: vignanam "yālībhi rātmatanutālīnakṛtpriyaka pālīṣu khelati bhavā"; stotranidhi the same but writing the double avagraha (yā''lībhir ātma-tanutā''līna-kṛt…); sanskritdocuments "yā''lībhir ātmatanutālīsakṛtpriyakapālīṣu khelati bhava-" with the compound continuing into pāda 2 as bhava-vyālī-nakulī, "a mongoose to the serpent of worldly existence," where the Telugu sources instead read "bhavā" as a name of the goddess and start pāda 2 at "vyālī." The English here follows the Telugu-tradition reading. v3 pāda 2 ends "lasanmaṇigaṇā" in vignanam, "lasanmuṇigaṇā" (an evident typo) in stotranidhi, "lasanmunivarā" in sanskritdocuments. v3 pāda 3 opens "yālī bhṛti" in both Telugu sources but "bālībhṛti" in sanskritdocuments. v4 "kalita vīlā" (vignanam) against "kalita līlā" (stotranidhi and, at v12, all three) — vīlā is not a word and līlā is almost certainly right, but vignanam's reading has been left standing at v4 as the base text prints it, and flagged here. v4 "praṇati śīlā" (vignanam) against "praṇata śīlā" (stotranidhi). v10 closes "aravindānanā," lotus-faced (vignanam), against "aravindāsanā," lotus-seated (stotranidhi and sanskritdocuments). v9 closes "jaya lakṣāvadhāna kalanā" (vignanam) against "jayalakṣmyā'vadhāna kalanā" (stotranidhi) and "jayalakṣmyāvadānakalanā" (sanskritdocuments); all three scan. v12 pāda 2 closes "poṣaṇa ratā" (vignanam) against "poṣaṇa nabhā" (stotranidhi) and "poṣaṇanabhaḥ" (sanskritdocuments, with a further variant "kalaśakīlāla" recorded in its own margin). v8 "gumbhā" (vignanam) against "gumphā" (stotranidhi). v11 "yatrāśayo lagati tatrāgajā bhavatu" (vignanam) against "…vasatu" (stotranidhi) and "tannāgajā vasatu" with "tatrāgajā" as a recorded variant (sanskritdocuments). sanskritdocuments records many further margin-variants of its own (vanavāṭīṣu, bhaya-, pakṣaviṣaye, bimbādharā, raha, surabhudhūpālakā, bhānumatisandhā) which are not repeated here individually.

Deliberate editorial corrections to the base text, all three metrically confirmed and all three supported by both other sources, listed so none of them is silent. (1) v2 pāda 3: vignanam prints "दुरितकूपादुदन्चयतुमाम्" with a dental न and mām glued on; corrected to "दुरितकूपादुदञ्चयतु माम्" (udañcayatu mām), which is what stotranidhi and sanskritdocuments both have and which gives the required 22 syllables. (2) v6 pāda 4: vignanam prints "तिमिर मासाये दुपरतिम्," which yields only 21 syllables and no sense; corrected to "तिमिरमासादयेदुपरतिम्" (timiram āsādayed uparatim, "may she bring the darkness to cessation"), the reading of both stotranidhi and sanskritdocuments, restoring 22 syllables. (3) v11 pāda 3: vignanam prints "पत्त्राभिभिराम" with भि dittographed, giving 23 syllables; corrected to "पत्त्राभिराम," the reading of both other sources, restoring 22. In addition, two of vignanam's word-splits that break a single word into meaningless pieces have been closed up without changing a syllable: v7 "पङ्कारि मे हि" to "पङ्कारिमेहि" (paṅkārim ehi) and v11 "कु त्रासहीन" to "कुत्रासहीन" (ku-trāsa-hīna). Everywhere else vignanam's own spacing has been kept as printed even where it leaves sandhi undone — notably v8 pāda 3 "शम्भा उदार" where stotranidhi and sanskritdocuments both print the joined "śambhāvudāra"; the syllable count is identical either way, so the base text stands.

Two further formatting notes. vignanam prints an abbreviation "शा ॥" after each verse number ("॥ 1 ॥ शा ॥"), on the Devanāgarī, Telugu and IAST pages alike; neither stotranidhi nor sanskritdocuments has it, and vignanam nowhere explains it. It is most likely a metre-label carried over from Telugu poetry typesetting, where a two-letter abbreviation before or after a verse names its chandas. Because it sits outside the verse text and is an editorial mark, it has been kept out of the deva and iast fields per the corpus rules and recorded here instead. And vignanam's IAST edition uses ē/ō for Sanskrit e/o and ḻ for ḷ (dhūḻi, yāḻī, tūḻāvaḻi) following Telugu-script habit; these have been normalized to plain e, o and l throughout, in line with the corpus convention and with vignanam's own Devanāgarī, which prints धूलि, याली, तूलावलि with plain ल. Anusvāra is written ṁ throughout.
