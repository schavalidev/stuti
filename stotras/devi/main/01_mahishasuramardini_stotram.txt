Title: Śrī Mahiṣāsuramardini Stotram (Ayigiri Nandini)
Devanāgarī: श्री महिषासुर मर्दिनी स्तोत्रम् (अयिगिरि नन्दिनि)
Telugu: శ్రీ మహిషాసుర మర్దినీ స్తోత్రం (అయిగిరి నందిని)

Author: traditionally attributed to Ādi Śaṅkarācārya; the text itself carries no author-signature and its colophon reads only "iti śrī mahiṣāsuramardini stotram." sanskritdocuments.org prints these same 21 verses as verses 7-27 of a longer thirty-verse Bhagavatī-padya-puṣpāñjali-stotra ascribed to Rāmakṛṣṇa Kavi, whose verse 29 carries the poet's signature "rāmakṛṣṇaḥ kavīnām," and its editorial note states plainly that the Śaṅkara attribution is incorrect since the poem is absent from Śaṅkara's collected works; that same note records a partial parallel in Śivarahasya part 1, adhyāya 53.9-21.

Source / recension: base text is vignanam.org's edition of Sri Mahishasura Mardini Stotram (Ayigiri Nandini), taken in all three of its parallel scripts — Devanāgarī, Telugu and IAST — which agree with one another word for word (the desktop pages at vignanam.org/devanagari/ and /telugu/ were serving mismatched body content when fetched, so the mobile mirrors at vignanam.org/mobile/<script>/sri-mahishasura-mardini-stotram-ayigiri-nandini.html were used instead; the English/IAST page served correctly). Cross-checked verse by verse against sanskritdocuments.org's mahisha.itx (doc_devii), which embeds the same 21 verses inside the Bhagavatī-padya-puṣpāñjali-stotra with its own double numbering and a substantial apparatus of variant readings; that collation is full and independent, and the differences it turns up are listed in the recension note. stotranidhi.com carries the text as well and its title-line and opening verse were confirmed, but its body could not be extracted through automated fetching, so it corroborates rather than adds a third full collation. The IAST here is transliterated directly from the Devanāgarī rather than copied from vignanam's English page, because that page uses the Telugu-tradition ē/ō convention and ch for च, both of which the corpus spec excludes for Sanskrit.

Verse count: 21, numbered 1-21 in vignanam.org with no irregularities. sanskritdocuments.org prints the identical 21 verses but double-numbers them, 1-21 within the stotra and simultaneously 7-27 within the enclosing thirty-verse poem, so the same body of text is 21 verses on any reading. The commonly reported alternative counts of 19 or 22 do not appear in either source consulted here.

Sections:
స్తోత్రం — Stotram (the 21 verses proper; the text has no viniyoga, nyāsa, dhyāna or phalaśruti)
ముగింపు వాక్యం — Colophon (the single closing line)

Blurb: This is a battle hymn in a fast mātrā metre. It has twenty-one verses. Each verse is made almost entirely of internal rhyme, with three or four rhyming compounds joined across each of its first three pādas. Every verse then closes with the same fourth pāda: 'jaya jaya he mahiṣāsuramardini ramyakapardini śailasute'. Through this fixed pattern of sound the poem carries the whole war story of the Devī Māhātmya. It names Madhu and Kaiṭabha, Dhūmralocana, Raktabīja, and Śumbha and Niśumbha, and at the close of the frame story Suratha and Samādhi. Only in its last four verses does it turn from the story of the war to personal prayer.

--- verse 1 | section: Stotram ---
deva:
अयि गिरिनन्दिनि नन्दितमेदिनि विश्वविनोदिनि नन्दिनुते
गिरिवरविन्ध्यशिरोधिनिवासिनि विष्णुविलासिनि जिष्णुनुते ।
भगवति हे शितिकण्ठकुटुम्बिनि भूरिकुटुम्बिनि भूरिकृते
जय जय हे महिषासुरमर्दिनि रम्यकपर्दिनि शैलसुते ॥
iast:
ayi girinandini nanditamedini viśvavinodini nandinute
girivaravindhyaśirodhinivāsini viṣṇuvilāsini jiṣṇunute |
bhagavati he śitikaṇṭhakuṭumbini bhūrikuṭumbini bhūrikṛte
jaya jaya he mahiṣāsuramardini ramyakapardini śailasute ||
en: O daughter of the mountain, who gladdens the earth, who delights the universe, praised by Nandin; you who dwell on the summit of Vindhya, best of mountains, who sport with Viṣṇu as his sister, praised by Jiṣṇu; O Bhagavatī, household-consort of the blue-throated Śiva, whose household is vast, who has done much — victory, victory to you, crusher of Mahiṣāsura, of the lovely coiled hair, daughter of the mountain. This closing line is the refrain that will end every one of the twenty-one verses unchanged.
tel: ఓ పర్వతరాజపుత్రీ, భూమిని ఆనందింపజేసేదానా, విశ్వాన్ని ఆహ్లాదపరిచేదానా, నందిచేత స్తుతింపబడేదానా; పర్వతశ్రేష్ఠమైన వింధ్య శిఖరంపై నివసించేదానా, విష్ణువుకు సోదరిగా విలసిల్లేదానా, జిష్ణువుచేత కీర్తింపబడేదానా; ఓ భగవతీ, నీలకంఠుని ఇల్లాలా, విస్తారమైన కుటుంబం కలదానా, ఎంతో చేసినదానా — జయము జయము నీకు, మహిషాసురమర్దినీ, రమ్యమైన జడముడి కలదానా, శైలసుతా. ఈ చివరి పాదమే ఇరవై ఒక్క శ్లోకాలన్నింటినీ మార్పు లేకుండా ముగించే ధ్రువపదం.
hi: हे गिरिराजनंदिनी, धरती को आनंदित करने वाली, विश्व को विनोदित करने वाली, नंदी द्वारा स्तुत; पर्वतश्रेष्ठ विंध्य के शिखर पर निवास करने वाली, विष्णु की भगिनी रूप में विलास करने वाली, जिष्णु द्वारा नुत; हे भगवती, नीलकंठ की गृहिणी, विशाल कुटुंब वाली, बहुत कुछ करने वाली — जय हो, जय हो, महिषासुरमर्दिनी, रम्य कपर्दिनी, शैलसुते। यही अंतिम पाद इक्कीसों श्लोकों में अपरिवर्तित ध्रुवपद के रूप में लौटता है।

--- verse 2 | section: Stotram ---
deva:
सुरवरवर्षिणि दुर्धरधर्षिणि दुर्मुखमर्षिणि हर्षरते
त्रिभुवनपोषिणि शङ्करतोषिणि कल्मषमोषिणि घोररते ।
दनुजनिरोषिणि दितिसुतरोषिणि दुर्मदशोषिणि सिन्धुसुते
जय जय हे महिषासुरमर्दिनि रम्यकपर्दिनि शैलसुते ॥
iast:
suravaravarṣiṇi durdharadharṣiṇi durmukhamarṣiṇi harṣarate
tribhuvanapoṣiṇi śaṅkaratoṣiṇi kalmaṣamoṣiṇi ghorarate |
danujaniroṣiṇi ditisutaroṣiṇi durmadaśoṣiṇi sindhusute
jaya jaya he mahiṣāsuramardini ramyakapardini śailasute ||
en: O you who rain down blessings on the best of the gods, who assail the unassailable, who crush Durmukha, who delight in joy; nourisher of the three worlds, delighter of Śaṅkara, thief of sin, who take pleasure in the terrible; you who blaze with anger at the Dānavas and at the sons of Diti, who dry up wicked arrogance, daughter of the ocean — victory, victory to you, crusher of Mahiṣāsura, of the lovely coiled hair, daughter of the mountain.
tel: ఓ దేవశ్రేష్ఠులపై వరాలను వర్షించేదానా, ఎదిరించలేని వారిని ఎదిరించేదానా, దుర్ముఖుడిని అణచేదానా, ఆనందంలో రమించేదానా; ముల్లోకాలను పోషించేదానా, శంకరుని సంతోషపెట్టేదానా, పాపాన్ని అపహరించేదానా, ఘోరమైనదానిలో రమించేదానా; దానవులపై, దితిపుత్రులపై కోపించేదానా, దుర్మదాన్ని ఎండగట్టేదానా, సింధుసుతా — జయము జయము నీకు, మహిషాసురమర్దినీ, రమ్యకపర్దినీ, శైలసుతా.
hi: हे देवश्रेष्ठों पर वरदान बरसाने वाली, दुर्धर को धर्षित करने वाली, दुर्मुख का मर्दन करने वाली, हर्ष में रत; त्रिभुवन का पोषण करने वाली, शंकर को संतुष्ट करने वाली, कल्मष को हरने वाली, घोर में रत; दनुजों पर तथा दिति-पुत्रों पर रोष करने वाली, दुर्मद को सुखा देने वाली, सिंधुसुते — जय हो, जय हो, महिषासुरमर्दिनी, रम्य कपर्दिनी, शैलसुते।

--- verse 3 | section: Stotram ---
deva:
अयि जगदम्ब मदम्ब कदम्बवनप्रियवासिनि हासरते
शिखरि शिरोमणि तुङ्गहिमालय शृङ्गनिजालय मध्यगते ।
मधुमधुरे मधुकैटभगञ्जिनि कैटभभञ्जिनि रासरते
जय जय हे महिषासुरमर्दिनि रम्यकपर्दिनि शैलसुते ॥
iast:
ayi jagadamba madamba kadambavanapriyavāsini hāsarate
śikhari śiromaṇi tuṅgahimālaya śṛṅganijālaya madhyagate |
madhumadhure madhukaiṭabhagañjini kaiṭabhabhañjini rāsarate
jaya jaya he mahiṣāsuramardini ramyakapardini śailasute ||
en: O mother of the world, my own mother, who love to dwell in the kadamba grove, who delight in laughter; you who abide in the midst of your own home, the peak of the lofty Himālaya, crest-jewel of mountains; sweet as honey, subduer of Madhu and Kaiṭabha, breaker of Kaiṭabha, who delight in the rāsa dance — victory, victory to you, crusher of Mahiṣāsura, of the lovely coiled hair, daughter of the mountain. The Madhu-Kaiṭabha episode invoked here is the first of the three Devī Māhātmya battles.
tel: ఓ జగదంబా, నా తల్లీ, కదంబవనంలో ఇష్టంగా నివసించేదానా, హాసంలో రమించేదానా; పర్వతాలకు శిరోమణి అయిన ఎత్తైన హిమాలయ శిఖరమనే నీ స్వగృహం మధ్య ఉండేదానా; తేనెవలె తీయనిదానా, మధుకైటభులను అణచినదానా, కైటభుని భంజించినదానా, రాసక్రీడలో రమించేదానా — జయము జయము నీకు, మహిషాసురమర్దినీ, రమ్యకపర్దినీ, శైలసుతా. ఇక్కడ స్మరించిన మధుకైటభ వృత్తాంతం దేవీమాహాత్మ్యంలోని మూడు యుద్ధాలలో మొదటిది.
hi: हे जगदंबा, मेरी माता, कदंबवन में प्रेमपूर्वक निवास करने वाली, हास में रत; पर्वतों के शिरोमणि उच्च हिमालय के शृंग रूपी अपने ही निवास के मध्य विराजने वाली; मधु-सी मधुर, मधु-कैटभ का मर्दन करने वाली, कैटभ का भंजन करने वाली, रास में रत — जय हो, जय हो, महिषासुरमर्दिनी, रम्य कपर्दिनी, शैलसुते। यहाँ स्मरण किया गया मधु-कैटभ प्रसंग देवीमाहात्म्य के तीन युद्धों में पहला है।

--- verse 4 | section: Stotram ---
deva:
अयि शतखण्ड विखण्डितरुण्ड वितुण्डितशुण्ड गजाधिपते
रिपुगजगण्ड विदारणचण्ड पराक्रमशुण्ड मृगाधिपते ।
निजभुजदण्ड निपातितखण्ड विपातितमुण्ड भटाधिपते
जय जय हे महिषासुरमर्दिनि रम्यकपर्दिनि शैलसुते ॥
iast:
ayi śatakhaṇḍa vikhaṇḍitaruṇḍa vituṇḍitaśuṇḍa gajādhipate
ripugajagaṇḍa vidāraṇacaṇḍa parākramaśuṇḍa mṛgādhipate |
nijabhujadaṇḍa nipātitakhaṇḍa vipātitamuṇḍa bhaṭādhipate
jaya jaya he mahiṣāsuramardini ramyakapardini śailasute ||
en: O mistress of elephants, who hacked headless trunks into a hundred pieces and severed the trunks of the war-elephants; O mistress of the lion, whose lion is fierce in tearing open the temples of the enemy's elephants and expert in valour; O mistress of the warrior-chiefs, who with your own staff-like arms felled them in pieces and struck their heads down — victory, victory to you, crusher of Mahiṣāsura, of the lovely coiled hair, daughter of the mountain.
tel: ఓ గజాధిపతీ, మొండేలను నూరు ముక్కలుగా చీల్చి, యుద్ధగజాల తొండాలను ఖండించినదానా; ఓ మృగాధిపతీ, శత్రుగజాల గండస్థలాలను చీల్చడంలో ఉగ్రమైన, పరాక్రమంలో నేర్పరి అయిన సింహాన్ని కలదానా; ఓ భటాధిపతీ, నీ దండువంటి భుజాలతోనే వారిని ముక్కలుగా కూల్చి, తలలను నేలగూల్చినదానా — జయము జయము నీకు, మహిషాసురమర్దినీ, రమ్యకపర్దినీ, శైలసుతా.
hi: हे गजाधिपते, जिसने कबंधों को सौ टुकड़ों में विखंडित किया और युद्ध-हाथियों की सूँड़ें काट डालीं; हे मृगाधिपते, जिसका सिंह शत्रु-गजों के गंडस्थलों को विदीर्ण करने में प्रचंड और पराक्रम में निपुण है; हे भटाधिपते, जिसने अपनी दंड-सदृश भुजाओं से उन्हें खंड-खंड गिराया और उनके मुंड धराशायी किए — जय हो, जय हो, महिषासुरमर्दिनी, रम्य कपर्दिनी, शैलसुते।

--- verse 5 | section: Stotram ---
deva:
अयि रणदुर्मद शत्रुवधोदित दुर्धरनिर्जर शक्तिभृते
चतुरविचारधुरीण महाशिव दूतकृत प्रमथाधिपते ।
दुरितदुरीह दुराशय दुर्मति दानवदूत कृतान्तमते
जय जय हे महिषासुरमर्दिनि रम्यकपर्दिनि शैलसुते ॥
iast:
ayi raṇadurmada śatruvadhodita durdharanirjara śaktibhṛte
caturavicāradhurīṇa mahāśiva dūtakṛta pramathādhipate |
duritadurīha durāśaya durmati dānavadūta kṛtāntamate
jaya jaya he mahiṣāsuramardini ramyakapardini śailasute ||
en: O bearer of the śakti-spear, irresistible and undecaying, raised up to slay the enemies drunk with the frenzy of battle; O mistress of the Pramatha hosts, foremost in clever deliberation, who made great Śiva himself your messenger; O you whose mind is death itself to the Dānavas' envoys, sinful, ill-intentioned, malicious and wicked-minded — victory, victory to you, crusher of Mahiṣāsura, of the lovely coiled hair, daughter of the mountain. The line about making Śiva her messenger refers to the Devī Māhātmya episode from which the Goddess takes the name Śivadūtī.
tel: ఓ శక్తిధారిణీ, యుద్ధమదంతో ఉన్మత్తులైన శత్రువులను సంహరించడానికి ఎత్తిన, ఎదిరించలేని, క్షయం లేని శక్త్యాయుధాన్ని ధరించినదానా; ఓ ప్రమథాధిపతీ, చతురమైన విచారణలో అగ్రగామివి, మహాశివుడినే దూతగా చేసినదానా; పాపిష్ఠులు, దురుద్దేశులు, దుష్టమతులు అయిన దానవదూతలకు కృతాంతము (యముడు) వంటి బుద్ధి కలదానా — జయము జయము నీకు, మహిషాసురమర్దినీ, రమ్యకపర్దినీ, శైలసుతా. శివుడినే దూతగా పంపిన ప్రస్తావన దేవీమాహాత్మ్యంలోని ఆ ఘట్టానిదే, దాని వలననే దేవికి శివదూతి అనే పేరు వచ్చింది.

en: O abode of compassion, O auspicious one — the man who serves your lotus foot day after day, O Kamalā, dwelling in the lotus, how should he not himself become a dwelling of Lakṣmī? Recension note: The base text is vignanam.org's Ayigiri Nandini edition, whose Devanāgarī, Telugu and IAST pages agree with one another word for word across all 21 verses and the colophon. One practical note on sourcing: vignanam.org's desktop pages for /devanagari/ and /telugu/ returned correct metadata but the body of an entirely different stotra (Khaḍgamālā) when fetched, and the page <title> tags on all three scripts named yet another text, so the mobile mirrors (vignanam.org/mobile/<script>/sri-mahishasura-mardini-stotram-ayigiri-nandini.html) were used for the Devanāgarī and Telugu; the desktop English page served correctly and was checked against the mobile one. The IAST field here was transliterated directly from the Devanāgarī rather than lifted from vignanam's English page, since that page uses the Telugu-tradition ē/ō and writes च as "ch", both of which the corpus spec excludes for Sanskrit; word division follows the Devanāgarī exactly, including vignanam's habit of splitting some long compounds across spaces (e.g. v.4 "ayi śatakhaṇḍa vikhaṇḍitaruṇḍa"), where sanskritdocuments.org runs them together.

Verse count: both sources print exactly these 21 verses and there is no disagreement between them on the count. The 19-verse and 22-verse figures sometimes reported for this poem did not appear in either source consulted. What does differ is the framing: sanskritdocuments.org's mahisha.itx (doc_devii) prints the 21 verses not as an independent stotra but as verses 7-27 of a thirty-verse Bhagavatī-padya-puṣpāñjali-stotra by Rāmakṛṣṇa Kavi, giving each verse a double number (its 1-21 within the stotra alongside its 7-27 within the longer poem) and prefixing the sequence with the word "chandaḥ," marking the metre-change at which the gallop begins. Verses 1-6 of that longer poem are anuṣṭubh/śārdūlavikrīḍita invocations of Gaṇeśa, Narasiṁha and the guru, and verses 28-30 are a phalaśruti and the poet's signature ("rāmakṛṣṇaḥ kavīnām," and the closing colophon "iti śrībhagavatīpadyapuṣpāñjalistotraṁ sampūrṇam"). Its editorial note states that the attribution to Śaṅkarācārya is incorrect, the poem being absent from his collected works, and records a partial parallel in Śivarahasya part 1, adhyāya 53.9-21. That framing is reported here rather than adopted: the file follows the Telugu-tradition circulation, in which the 21 verses stand alone under the title Mahiṣāsuramardini Stotram, and neither the six preceding nor the three following verses are included.

Variants. vignanam.org itself prints five bracketed variant readings inline, which are excluded from the deva/iast fields per the spec and recorded here: v.2 [kilbiṣa-, ghoṣa-] for kalmaṣa- and ghora-; v.4 [-caṇḍa] for nipātitakhaṇḍa; v.10 [jha-, jhiṁ-] for bhaṇa- and bhiñjimi-; v.12 [jhillika bhillika] for bhillika bhillika; v.21 [me] for te in apākuru te. Every one of vignanam's bracketed alternatives is in fact the primary reading of sanskritdocuments.org, and vice versa, so the two editions are mirror images of each other at these points. sanskritdocuments.org carries further variants not noted by vignanam: v.4 kramaśauṇḍa for parākramaśuṇḍa; v.5 mahāśaya for mahāśiva; v.6 dāyikare for dāyakare, and dhimidhimitāmara / muhurmukharīkṛtadiṅnikare for dumidumitāmara / maho mukharīkṛta tigmakare; v.7 bījata te for bījalate; v.9 an entirely different second pāda, "hāsavilāsahulāsamayi praṇatārtajane'mitapremabhare," with vignanam's kukuthaḥ line given only parenthetically, and a different first and third pāda as well (tathābhinayottaranṛtyarate; dhimikiṭadhikkaṭadhikaṭadhimidhvani-) — verse 9 is the single place where the two editions diverge substantively rather than by a syllable; v.14 bhālatale for bhālalate and kuntalamaṇḍala for kuvalayamaṇḍala; v.15 militamilinda for milita pulinda, plus a whole recorded variant third pāda "nijagaṇabhūtamahāśabarīgaṇaraṅgaṇasambhṛtakelirate"; v.16 caṇḍaruce for candraruce and madorjita for maulipadorjita, and nirjara for vignanam's nirbhara in nirbharakuñjara; v.18 suśive for sa śive and parampadamevanuśīlayato for parampadamityanuśīlayato; v.19 śiñcati te for anusiñcinute, plus a recorded variant third pāda "mṛḍāni sadā mayi dehi śivaṁ"; v.20 kalayannanu for nanu kūlayate and kimu na kriyate for kimuta kriyate; v.21 rame for rate. Where the two disagree, vignanam's reading is the one printed in the deva/iast fields, since it is the Telugu-tradition text this corpus follows.

The refrain "jaya jaya he mahiṣāsuramardini ramyakapardini śailasute" is identical in both sources in all 21 verses without a single variant, and it is written out in full in every verse block here rather than abbreviated, as instructed. The verses are not in a classical akṣara-vṛtta but in a mātrā metre built of four-mora gaṇas, which is what produces the galloping effect; sanskritdocuments.org marks the shift into it with a bare "chandaḥ" line, and this file follows both sources in dividing each verse into four lines with the daṇḍa after the second pāda. stotranidhi.com's page for this stotra was located and its title-line and opening verse confirmed, but the body text could not be extracted (the site serves the verses behind copy-protection scripting that defeats automated fetching), so it corroborates the identification without adding a third full collation. No verse in this file is unverified; every one was read in three parallel vignanam scripts and collated against sanskritdocuments.org.
