Blurb: This is a prayer to Śītalā, spoken by Śiva. Śītalā is worshipped against fever and against the diseases that break out on the skin, and the word that returns through the whole stotra is visphoṭaka, the pox. She is described in the first verse and the description does not change afterwards: she rides an ass, she wears no clothes, she carries a broom and a water pot, and a winnowing fan is set upon her head. Every one of these is a thing used to sweep, to cool and to clean. The verses ask for one thing in many ways. Whoever is burning with fever and can say no more than her name twice over is heard. Whoever meditates on her standing in the water is kept safe. To the man scorched by fever, foul-smelling and gone blind, she is called the medicine that is life itself. Two verses stand apart from the rest. The thirteenth gives six names of the ass she rides, and the fourteenth says that whoever recites those names before her will have no child in his house struck by her disease. The last verse says that this stotra is not to be given to anyone at all, but only to one who has faith and devotion.

Recension note: The two witnesses agree to the character, and that agreement is reported above as a fact about the witnesses rather than as a proof of the text. Nothing in this file was reconstructed, and no word was altered from what both witnesses print. Three points are recorded so that a later hand does not read them as slips of this transcription. First, `त्वमेकाऽमृतवर्षिणी` in verse 6 carries the avagraha exactly as both witnesses print it. Second, `यस्यकस्यचित्` in verse 15 is printed as one word in both, and is left as one word. Third, `शीतलारुङ्` in verse 14 ends in the guttural nasal before the following `न`, and is left so. The verse numerals differ between the two sites only in script — stotranidhi prints Devanāgarī digits and vignanam Arabic ones — and this file follows the Devanāgarī, which is what the rest of the corpus uses.
