Title: Śrī Indrākṣī Stotram
Devanāgarī: श्री इन्द्राक्षी स्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ ఇంద్రాక్షీ స్తోత్రం

Author: traditional; framed as a dialogue in which Nārada asks Nārāyaṇa for the hymn that Śiva once spoke to Pārvatī, and Nārāyaṇa replies that the stotra was first composed by Indra himself ("indreṇādau kṛtaṁ stotram"). The viniyoga accordingly names Śacī and Purandara (Indra and his consort) jointly as the ṛṣi, anuṣṭubh as the chandas, and Indrākṣī-Durgā as the devatā, with Lakṣmī as bīja, Bhuvaneśvarī as śakti and Bhavānī as kīlaka. The closing verse states that the Goddess granted Mahendra the boon of everlasting sovereignty over the gods, which is why it is called "Indra's stotra."

Source / recension: Base text is vignanam.org's Indrākṣī Stotram, taken from its parallel Devanāgarī, Telugu-script and IAST editions (https://vignanam.org/devanagari/indrakshi-stotram.html, /telugu/, /english/), which agree with one another line for line. This was collated in full, verse by verse, against stotranidhi.com's Telugu-script edition (https://stotranidhi.com/sri-indrakshi-stotram-in-telugu/), fetched with a browser User-Agent since the site blocks plain automated requests. The two are independent Telugu-tradition witnesses and agree almost completely, including on the section headings, the numbering, and the three long prose mantras; the handful of divergences are listed in the recension note. sanskritdocuments.org does NOT carry this text: its doc_devii catalog has only indrākṣīkavacam (a different, Bhairava-Devī dialogue kavaca) and indrākṣīsahasranāmastotram (from the Rudrayāmala), neither of which is this hymn, so no third witness was available. Cross-checking is therefore two-source but genuine and complete, not reduced.

Verse count: 47 numbered units, but the source does not run them in one sequence — it restarts the count three times. The dhyāna verses are numbered 1-4; the digdevatā-rakṣa verses restart at 1-3; the main sequence then runs 1-40, covering the name-litany (1-21), the four mantras (22-25), the samarpaṇa verse (26), and the phalaśruti (27-40). Two further verse-units — Nārada's question and Nārāyaṇa's reply — are printed unnumbered before the viniyoga, and Nārāyaṇa's reply is a verse and a half, its second half-line ("tadevāhaṁ bravīmyadya pṛcchatastava nārada") left without a matching pāda-pair. The prose blocks (viniyoga, karanyāsa/aṅganyāsa, pañcapūjā, uttaranyāsa, colophon) carry no numbers at all.

Blurb: This is a hymn for fever and disease, and it rests on the claim that Indra himself composed it. It recites a list of the Goddess's names under the title Indrākṣī, which means 'she of Indra's eyes', that is, the thousand-eyed one. It posts a different goddess in each of the ten directions as a guard. It then breaks into three long prose mantras. These name and command away, one by one, a detailed list of ailments. First come possessions by yakṣa, rākṣasa, skanda, vināyaka, bāla, kumāra, cora, bhūta, preta, piśāca and kūṣmāṇḍa. Then come fevers, classed as bhūta, preta and piśāca fevers, and as hot, bilious, windy, phlegmatic, sannipāta and artificially induced fevers, together with fevers recurring on the first, second, third, fourth or fifth day, and fortnightly, monthly, six-monthly and yearly. Last come consumption, epilepsy, leprosy and jaundice, and pains of the head, the waist, the side, the eye, the ear and the flank.

en: For this great mantra, the Śrī Indrākṣī Stotram, Śacīpurandara — Indra together with his consort Śacī — is the ṛṣi (seer), anuṣṭubh is the chandas (metre), Indrākṣī-Durgā is the devatā (deity); Lakṣmī is the bīja (seed), Bhuvaneśvarī is the śakti (power), Bhavānī is the kīlaka (the pin that locks the mantra); the viniyoga (ritual application) is for japa undertaken to win Indrākṣī's grace. Unusually, the bīja, śakti and kīlaka here are named as goddesses rather than given as syllables.

hi: इस महामंत्र, श्री इंद्राक्षी स्तोत्र के, शचीपुरंदर — इंद्र अपनी पत्नी शची सहित — ऋषि हैं, अनुष्टुप् छंद है, इंद्राक्षी दुर्गा देवता हैं; लक्ष्मी बीज हैं, भुवनेश्वरी शक्ति हैं, भवानी कीलक हैं; इंद्राक्षी की कृपा-सिद्धि हेतु किए जाने वाले जप में इसका विनियोग है। यहाँ बीज, शक्ति और कीलक को अक्षरों के रूप में नहीं, देवियों के नामों से बताया गया है — यह असामान्य है।

en: The karanyāsa places six names of the Goddess — Indrākṣī, Mahālakṣmī, Maheśvarī, Ambujākṣī, Kātyāyanī, Kaumārī — in turn on the thumbs, forefingers, middle fingers, ring fingers, little fingers, and both sides of the palms; the aṅganyāsa then places those same six names on the heart, head, tuft (śikhā), body-as-kavaca, three eyes and the weapon (astra), each with its own ritual exclamation (namaḥ, svāhā, vaṣaṭ, hum, vauṣaṭ, phaṭ). Note that this nyāsa uses names rather than bīja syllables, matching the viniyoga's practice. Reciting the three vyāhṛtis (bhūr bhuvaḥ suvaḥ) with oṁ then performs digbandha, binding the directions to seal the ritual space.

deva:
द्विभुजां सौम्यवदानां पाशाङ्कुशधरां पराम् ।

iast:
dvibhujāṁ saumyavadānāṁ pāśāṅkuśadharāṁ parām |
trailokyamohinīṁ devīṁ indrākṣī nāma kīrtitām ||
en: Two-armed, gentle of face, holding the noose (pāśa) and the goad (aṅkuśa), supreme, the Goddess who bewitches the three worlds — she is celebrated by the name Indrākṣī. Note that this dhyāna gives her the pāśa and aṅkuśa where the previous one gave her the vajra; the source preserves both iconographies side by side.
tel: రెండు చేతులు గల, సౌమ్యమైన ముఖం గల, పాశాంకుశాలను ధరించిన, పరాత్పరమైన, ముల్లోకాలను మోహింపజేసే దేవి — ఆమెను ఇంద్రాక్షి అనే పేరుతో కీర్తిస్తారు. ఇక్కడ ఆమెకు పాశాంకుశాలు ఇవ్వబడ్డాయి, ముందు శ్లోకంలో వజ్రం; మూలం ఈ రెండు రూపవర్ణనలనూ పక్కపక్కనే నిలుపుతుంది.
hi: द्विभुजा, सौम्य मुख वाली, पाश और अंकुश धारण किए, परा, त्रैलोक्य को मोहित करने वाली देवी — वे इंद्राक्षी नाम से कीर्तित हैं। ध्यान दें कि यहाँ उन्हें पाश-अंकुश दिए गए हैं जबकि पिछले श्लोक में वज्र; स्रोत दोनों रूप-वर्णनों को साथ-साथ रखता है।

--- verse 4 | section: Dhyānam ---
deva:

त्वामप्सरस्सेवितपादपद्मां

en: Clothed in yellow, one hand holding the vajra, adorned with ornaments of every kind, serene, your lotus-feet served by apsarases — you, Indrākṣī, the lawful consort of Śiva, I salute.

--- verse none | section: Pañcapūjā ---
deva:
लं पृथिव्यात्मिकायै गन्धं समर्पयामि ।
हं आकाशात्मिकायै पुष्पैः पूजयामि ।
यं वाय्वात्मिकायै धूपमाघ्रापयामि ।

en: That Goddess is called Indrākṣī by the gods; she is Gaurī, she is the Goddess Śākambharī, and she is renowned under the name Durgā. The name-litany begins here; the numbering starts again at 1.
tel: ఆ దేవిని దేవతలు ఇంద్రాక్షి అని పిలుస్తారు; ఆమె గౌరి, ఆమె శాకంభరీ దేవి, ఆమె దుర్గా అనే పేరుతో ప్రసిద్ధురాలు. నామావళి ఇక్కడ మొదలవుతుంది; సంఖ్యలు మళ్ళీ ౧ నుండి ప్రారంభమవుతాయి.
hi: उस देवी को देवता इंद्राक्षी कहते हैं; वे गौरी हैं, वे शाकंभरी देवी हैं, और वे दुर्गा नाम से विख्यात हैं। नाम-सूची यहाँ आरंभ होती है; गिनती पुनः १ से शुरू होती है।

Recension note: Base text is vignanam.org's Indrākṣī Stotram, whose Devanāgarī, Telugu-script and IAST editions agree line for line with one another; this was collated in full against stotranidhi.com's Telugu-script edition, fetched with a browser User-Agent because the site blocks plain automated requests. The two witnesses are independent Telugu-tradition editions and agree on the entire text, including all three long prose mantras and every section heading, so this item has genuine two-source verification and no verse is left unverified.

No third witness was available. sanskritdocuments.org does not carry this hymn: its doc_devii catalog holds only indrākṣīkavacam — a different text, a Devī–Bhairava dialogue kavaca of roughly fifty verses, whose opening ("bhagavān devadeveśa … indrākṣyāḥ kavacaṁ brūhi") does not match this one — and indrākṣīsahasranāmastotram from the Rudrayāmala, which is a thousand-name litany rather than this stotra. Both its .itx and .html files are also served behind mod_security, so they could not be pulled as raw text; the identification rests on the catalog listing and the kavaca's opening lines.

Variants between the two witnesses, all minor: verse 7, vignanam reads sthūlasūkṣmapravardhanī while stotranidhi reads pravardhinī (the sense is the same, "she who makes gross and subtle grow"); verse 26, vignanam prints japam and stotranidhi japaṁ. Both sources print the same in-line alternative reading at verse 4, vikaṭāṅgī with vikārāṅgī given as a variant — since the spec forbids editorial marks inside the deva and iast fields, the parenthetical variant has been dropped from the text (vikaṭāṅgī retained, as both sources' main reading) and recorded in the en/tel/hi fields instead.

Numbering: the source restarts its count three times and this has been reproduced exactly rather than renumbered. The dhyāna verses run 1-4, the digdevatā-rakṣa verses restart at 1-3, and the main sequence runs 1-40 across the name-litany (1-21), the mantras (22-25), the samarpaṇa (26) and the phalaśruti (27-40). Nārada's question and Nārāyaṇa's reply are printed unnumbered before the viniyoga, and Nārāyaṇa's reply is a verse and a half — its closing line "tadevāhaṁ bravīmyadya pṛcchatastava nārada" has no matching pāda-pair in either source, and has been left as printed. Dhyāna verse 2 is two complete anuṣṭubh ślokas printed under one number, and verse 39 is printed with six pādas across three lines rather than four; both are reproduced as printed. Verses 22 through 31 print with a single closing daṇḍa before the number in stotranidhi and a doubled one in vignanam — a typographic difference only, normalized here to the corpus convention.

Transliteration choices. vignanam's own IAST page uses ē/ō for e/o, ṃ for anusvāra, "cha" for च, and the Dravidian ḻ in words like kāḻarātrī, bhadrakāḻī, kaḻyāṇī, sarvamaṅgaḻā, niṣkaḻāyai and padapadmayugaḻāṁ. All four have been converted to the corpus convention: plain e and o, ṁ for anusvāra, c for च, and plain la wherever the Devanāgarī prints ल — which it does in every one of those words. The ḻ in vignanam's IAST and the ళ in both sites' Telugu script reflect Telugu orthographic practice for these Sanskrit words, not a different Sanskrit reading, so no variant is being suppressed. Bīja syllables (hrīṁ, śrīṁ, aiṁ, klīṁ, klūṁ, huṁ, phaṭ, svāhā, the hrāṁ–hraḥ ṣaḍaṅga series, the element-bījas laṁ haṁ yaṁ raṁ vaṁ saṁ, and the ya-ra-la-va-śa-ṣa-sa-ha string) are reproduced exactly as printed and identified rather than translated in the en, tel and hi fields. Daṇḍa placement in the four-line metrical verses (dhyāna 1 and 4, stotra 16) follows the corpus convention of । after the second line and ॥ at the end, where the source's own placement is inconsistent; no words were altered.
