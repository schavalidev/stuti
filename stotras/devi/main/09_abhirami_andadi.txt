Title: Abhirāmi Andādi (Abhirāmi Antāti)
Devanāgarī: अबिरामि अन्दादि
Telugu: అబిరామి అందాది (తమిళ్)

Author: Abhirāmi Bhaṭṭar, born Subrahmaṇya Ayyar (18th c.), priest of the Amṛtaghaṭeśvara–Abhirāmi temple at Tirukkaṭaiyūr (Thirukkadaiyur), Tanjore district. The poem carries no viniyoga, no colophon and no poet's signature — the poet never names himself in the hundred verses — so the attribution rests entirely on the temple's own tradition: that the Maratha king of Tanjore, finding the bhaṭṭar lost in the goddess and asked what lunar day it was, was told "full moon" on an amāvāsyā, and that the bhaṭṭar was set over a fire-pit to compose these hundred verses until the goddess flung her tāṭaṅka (ear-ornament) into the sky as a full moon. Tradition attaches the miracle to one of the verses in the seventies (usually the seventy-ninth), though the poem itself never mentions the incident; the moon-raising is named instead in the separate eleven-verse Sanskrit Abhirāmi Stotram ascribed to the same poet.

Source / recension: This is a TAMIL composition, not Sanskrit, and it is not in this corpus's usual Telugu-tradition sources at all. vignanam.org does not carry the Andādi: its "Abhirami Stotram" (slugs /devanagari/, /telugu/, /english/abhirami-stotram.html) is a different, eleven-verse SANSKRIT stotra beginning "namaste lalite devi" and closing "iti śrīabhirāmibhaṭṭār kṛta śrī abhirāmi stotram" — the same poet, a different work. stotranidhi.com and hariome.com carry that same Sanskrit stotra, not this one; sanskritdocuments.org's Devī catalogs do not carry a Tamil andādi. The base text used here is therefore the Tamil-script Unicode e-text published by Project Madurai (pm_etexts/utf8/pmuni0026_01.html, "apirAmi antAti of apirAmi paTTar with explanations from kavingar kaNNatAcan," prepared with Dr. Kumar Mallikarjunan, revised 4 August 2021), collated verse by verse against the popular Tamil lyrics page at pothunalam.com. Both are Tamil-script sources; no Telugu-tradition witness exists for collation, which is a real reduction in cross-checking relative to the rest of this corpus and is flagged again in the recension note. Kannadasan's Tamil prose commentary, which Project Madurai hosts by permission of his family, was consulted to settle the sense of several difficult verses but is nowhere reproduced.

Verse count: 100 verses numbered 1 to 100 in the base text with no irregularity, preceded by one unnumbered Gaṇapati kāppu and followed by one unnumbered closing verse — 102 units in all — printed under the heading நூற்பயன் (Nūṟpayan, "fruit of the work"). Popular Tamil sources count the nūṟpayan as a hundred-and-first song; the base text prints it unnumbered, and that is followed here. Each verse is four lines in the kaṭṭaḷaikkalitturai metre.

Sections:
కాప్పు — Kāppu, the unnumbered guardian verse invoking Gaṇapati and naming the work
అందాది — Antāti, the hundred chained verses proper
నూఱ్పయన్ — Nūṟpayan, the closing verse stating the fruit of reciting the work

Blurb: An andādi is a chain in which the last word of each verse becomes the first word of the next. In this poem the hundredth verse hands its closing word, udikkinṟa, meaning 'rising', back to the opening word of the first verse, so that the poem is a ring without an end. Through this form Abhirāmi Bhaṭṭar presents the whole Śrī Vidyā teaching for the goddess of Tirukkaṭaiyūr. Two verses are nothing but lists of names, namely 50 and 77, which give the benign and the fierce names. Verse 73 is a compact dhyāna, which states her garland, her weapon, her bow, the hour of her worship and the number of her eyes as precisely as a manual of iconography would. Verse 19 treats the nine interlocking koṇas of the Śrī Cakra. Verse 70 treats the Mātaṅgī of the kadamba forest. Threaded between these is a temple poet's running complaint to his goddess: that he is made to carry his Tamil verse to human patrons, and to speak half-truths there. That complaint appears in verse 57.

तार् अमर् कॊन्ऱैयुम् सण्बग मालैयुम् सात्तुम् तिल्लै ।
ऊरर्तम् पागत्तु उमै मैन्दने उलगु एऴुम् पॆऱ्ऱ ।
सीर् अबिरामि अन्दादि ऎप्पोदुम् ऎन्दन् सिन्दैयुळ्ळे ।
कार् अमर् मेनिक् कणबदिये निऱ्कक् कट्टुरैये ॥
iast:
tār amar konṟaiyum saṇbaga mālaiyum sāttum tillai |
ūrartam pāgattu umai maindanē ulagu ēḻum peṟṟa |
sīr abirāmi andādi eppōdum endan sindaiyuḷḷē |

en: O son of Umā, who is the half of the Lord of Tillai (Chidambaram), he who wears garlands of konṟai (Indian laburnum) and ceṇbagam (champak); O Gaṇapati whose body is dark as a raincloud: ordain by your word that this Abhirāmi Antāti, in praise of the glorious Abhirāmi who bore all seven worlds, may stand forever within my mind. This is the kāppu, the guardian verse addressed to Gaṇeśa that opens the work and names it.
tel: తిల్లై (చిదంబరం) నాథుని — కొన్ఱై (కొండగోగు) పూలమాలను, సంపెంగ మాలను ధరించే వాని — అర్ధభాగమైన ఉమాదేవి కుమారుడా; మేఘంవలె నల్లని దేహం గల గణపతీ! ఏడు లోకాలను కన్న కీర్తిమంతురాలైన అభిరామిని స్తుతించే ఈ అంతాది ఎల్లప్పుడూ నా మనస్సులో నిలిచి ఉండేలా నీ మాటతో ఆజ్ఞాపించు. ఇది గ్రంథాన్ని ప్రారంభించి దానికి పేరు పెట్టే గణపతి కాప్పు (రక్షణ శ్లోకం).
hi: तिल्लै (चिदंबरम्) के स्वामी — कोन्ऱै (अमलतास) और चंपक की मालाएँ धारण करने वाले — के अर्धांग उमा के पुत्र; मेघ-सम श्याम देह वाले गणपति! सातों लोकों को जन्म देने वाली कीर्तिमयी अभिरामी की स्तुति करने वाली यह अंतादि सदा मेरे चित्त में स्थिर रहे, ऐसा अपने वचन से नियत करो। यह ग्रंथ को आरंभ करने वाला और उसका नाम बताने वाला गणपति-काप्पु (रक्षा-श्लोक) है।

deva:
उदिक्किन्ऱ सॆङ्गदिर् उच्चित् तिलगम् उणर्वुडैयोर् ।
मदिक्किन्ऱ माणिक्कम् मादुळम्बोदु मलर्क्कमलै ।
तुदिक्किन्ऱ मिन् कॊडि मॆन् कडिक् कुङ्गुम तोयम् ऎन्न ।
विदिक्किन्ऱ मेनि अबिरामि ऎन्दन् विऴुत् तुणैये ॥
iast:
udikkinṟa seṅgadir uccit tilagam uṇarvuḍaiyōr |
madikkinṟa māṇikkam māduḷambōdu malarkkamalai |
tudikkinṟa min koḍi men kaḍik kuṅguma tōyam enna |
vidikkinṟa mēni abirāmi endan viḻut tuṇaiyē ||
en: The rising red sun; the tilaka set on the crown; the ruby that discerning men prize; the pomegranate bud; the streak of lightning that Kamalā (Lakṣmī, she of the lotus) praises; soft fragrant kuṅkuma paste — Abhirāmi, whose body is ordained to be like all these, is my one excellent help. This opening word udikkinṟa ("rising") is the word with which verse 100 closes: the andādi chain begins here and returns to this point.
tel: ఉదయిస్తున్న ఎర్రని సూర్యుడు; నడినెత్తిన పెట్టిన తిలకం; వివేకవంతులు మెచ్చే మాణిక్యం; దానిమ్మ మొగ్గ; కమల (లక్ష్మి) స్తుతించే మెరుపుతీగ; మృదువైన సుగంధ కుంకుమ లేపనం — వీటన్నిటి వలె ఉండేలా విధింపబడిన శరీరం గల అభిరామియే నాకు ఏకైక ఉత్తమ ఆధారం. ఈ మొదటి పదం "ఉదిక్కిన్ఱ" అనేదే నూరవ పద్యం ముగింపు పదం; అంతాది శృంఖల ఇక్కడ మొదలై ఇక్కడికే తిరిగి వస్తుంది.
hi: उदित होता लाल सूर्य; शिखर पर लगा तिलक; विवेकी जनों द्वारा सराहा गया माणिक्य; अनार की कली; कमला (लक्ष्मी) द्वारा स्तुत विद्युत्-रेखा; कोमल सुगंधित कुंकुम-लेप — इन सबके समान विधान की गई देह वाली अभिरामी ही मेरी एकमात्र श्रेष्ठ सहायिका है। यह प्रथम शब्द "उदिक्किन्ऱ" ही सौवें पद्य का अंतिम शब्द है; अंतादि की शृंखला यहीं से आरंभ होकर यहीं लौटती है।

Recension note: (1) This is a Tamil, not a Sanskrit, composition, and unlike every other item in this collection it has no Telugu-tradition witness at all. vignanam.org, stotranidhi.com and hariome.com all carry, under the name "Abhirami Stotram," an eleven-verse Sanskrit hymn ("namaste lalite devi... abhirāmi namo'stu te," colophon "iti śrīabhirāmibhaṭṭār kṛta śrī abhirāmi stotram") which is a different work by the same poet and is not this text; sanskritdocuments.org's Devī catalogs carry no Tamil andādi. The base recension here is therefore the Tamil-script Unicode e-text of Project Madurai (pmuni0026_01, revised 4 August 2021), collated line by line against the Tamil lyrics page at pothunalam.com. Two Tamil-script witnesses, no Telugu-script or Devanāgarī witness: cross-checking is genuinely reduced, and no reading below is supported by a Telugu-tradition edition. (2) Because the Tamil original was directly available, the Devanāgarī and IAST here are a rule-based mechanical transliteration of the Tamil script, not a re-transliteration of somebody else's Telugu-script rendering; the rules applied are stated in full below so that any of them can be reversed by hand later. (3) Tamil ன (alveolar n) and ந (dental n) are both rendered n / न. In file 06 of the Subrahmaṇya corpus that collapse was forced, because the Telugu-script source did not distinguish them; here the distinction WAS available in the Tamil and has been deliberately given up for consistency with that file and because Devanāgarī has no letter for the alveolar. This is a real loss of information, not a claim that the underlying Tamil is uniform. (4) Tamil ற is rendered ṟ / ऱ, kept distinct from ர (r / र); ழ is rendered ḻ / ऴ, kept distinct from ள (ḷ / ळ) and ல (l / ल). The clusters ற்ற and ன்ற are written ṟṟ and nṟ rather than respelt to their pronunciations ṭr and ndr, which is a departure from file 06's practice (it printed peṭra, pōṭri, neṭri from its Telugu source); the letters are kept, the pronunciation is not. (5) Short and long e and o are marked distinctly — e / ē and o / ō in the iast field, ऎ/ॆ against ए/े and ऒ/ॊ against ओ/ो in the deva field. This deliberately departs from the "plain e and o only" convention this collection uses for its Sanskrit texts, because in Tamil the contrast is phonemic and load-bearing (எ/ஏ, ஒ/ஓ are different letters distinguishing different words). (6) Tamil writes one letter for each stop series and leaves voicing to position, so a choice had to be made between a letter-for-letter transliteration (k, c, ṭ, t, p everywhere) and a pronunciation-based one. The pronunciation-based rendering is used, matching file 06: a stop is voiceless word-initially, when geminate, and after a non-nasal consonant; voiced after a nasal and between vowels. A geminate split across a printed space is treated as a geminate (கருப்புச் சிலையும் → karuppuc cilaiyum, not karuppuc silaiyum). Tamil ச is given as s word-initially and between vowels, c when geminate or after another consonant, j after ஞ். (7) The direct consequence of (6) is that Sanskrit loanwords appear in their Tamil phonetic shape and are NOT restored to Sanskrit spelling: சங்கரி is saṅgari not śaṅkarī, திரிபுர சுந்தரி is tiribura sundari, சகல கலா is sagalagalā, பக்தி is bakti, and — most visibly — அபிராமி is abirāmi, not abhirāmi. This is deliberate: the file transliterates a Tamil poem, not the Sanskrit etyma behind its loanwords. The header keeps the conventional Sanskritized form Abhirāmi in the Title line only, because that is how the work is catalogued everywhere. (8) The base e-text's commas, full stops, colons and editorial dashes are its modern editor's sense-marking, not part of the poem, and have been dropped; the four lines of each verse are closed with । / ॥ (| / ||) per this collection's format. No Grantha letters (ஜ ஶ ஷ ஸ ஹ) and no āytam (ஃ) occur anywhere in the base text, so no special handling was needed for them. (9) The two witnesses differ orthographically in about ninety of the hundred verses, but almost every difference is word-splitting and puṇarcci sandhi rather than a different word (அறிவு ஒன்று இலேன் against அறிவொன்றிலேன், வான் உலகம் against வானுலகம், and so on); these are not recorded individually. The places where the two actually disagree on a word are: v.3 நினது / உனது; v.7 மத்தின் / மத்திற்; v.14 தண்ணளியே / தண் ஒளியே; v.29 சக்தி / சத்தி; v.35 line 1 திங்கட் பகவின் / திங்கள் பசுவின்; v.62 உரி போர்த்த / புரி போர்த்த; v.83 குலிகமும் / குலிசமும்; v.87 திருமூர்த்தம் / திருமூர்த்தி; v.88 பத்தருக்குள் / பக்தருக்குள்; v.99 கடம்பாடவி / கடம்படாவி; and v.100 both கொன்றையந் தார் / ஒன்றை அம்தார் and the final word உதிக்கின்றவே / உதிக்கின்றனவே. The Project Madurai reading is adopted at every one of these. Several of the pothunalam variants are plainly typographic rather than genuine (v.90 புகுந்து printed as புதுந்து, v.99 கடம்படாவி, v.100 ஒன்றை for கொன்றை) and are noted here only for completeness; the v.100 pair உதிக்கின்றவே / உதிக்கின்றனவே is a real variant, and either form preserves the andādi link back to verse 1's உதிக்கின்ற. (10) Two passages are genuinely obscure and their English, Telugu and Hindi here are cautious rather than confident: v.35 line 1, திங்கட் பகவின் மணம் நாறும் சீறடி, where the second word is not securely explicable and the witnesses disagree (rendered here, with Kannadasan, as "feet fragrant as the crescent moon"); and v.93 line 2, முது கண் முடிவுயில், where both witnesses agree on the letters but the phrase resists a settled construal (rendered here, following Kannadasan's gloss that the verse mocks the conventional epithets devotees pile on the goddess, as "the aged eye and the final sleep"). (11) Numbering is regular: the base text numbers 1 to 100 with no repeated or skipped number, and prints the kāppu and the nūṟpayan unnumbered. pothunalam.com announces the nūṟpayan as a hundred-and-first song in its preamble but then omits the verse itself, ending at 100; the nūṟpayan given here rests on Project Madurai alone. (12) Popular Tamil sources head each verse with the specific boon its recitation is held to grant (verse 1 for wisdom and learning, verse 2 for the separated to be reunited, and so on through the hundred). That per-verse phalaśruti is a later devotional apparatus attached to the poem rather than anything the poem itself says, and it is not reproduced in the verse blocks. (13) The andādi linkage was checked at all hundred links, including the closure of the ring at 100 → 1, and holds throughout. Most links carry a whole word across (v.67 பார் எங்குமே → v.68 பாரும்; v.79 கூட்டு இனியே → v.80 கூட்டியவா; v.50 அரண் நமக்கே → v.51 அரணம்); four link on a single syllable rather than a word and were checked by hand — 41 பதித்திடவே → 42 இடங்கொண்டு, 54 சேர்மின்களே → 55 மின் ஆயிரம், 57 மெய்யருளே → 58 அருணாம்புயத்தும், 62 சிந்தையதே → 63 தேறும்படி. Since the link is the structural point of the form, it is noted in the en field of verses 1 and 100, where the ring closes, rather than repeated a hundred times.
