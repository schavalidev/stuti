Title: Śrī Jogulāmbāṣṭakam
Devanāgarī: श्री जोगुलाम्बाष्टकम्
Telugu: శ్రీ జోగులాంబాష్టకం

Author: unattributed. No viniyoga and no author-colophon; the text ends only with "iti śrī jogulāmbāṣṭakam." The hymn is anchored to one shrine — it names the river, the town and the presiding Śiva of Alampur in its first verse — so it is a local temple composition rather than a purāṇic excerpt.

Source / recension: https://stotranidhi.com/en/sri-jogulamba-ashtakam-in-english/ (IAST), https://stotranidhi.com/hi/sri-jogulamba-ashtakam-in-sanskrit/ (Devanāgarī), https://stotranidhi.com/sri-jogulamba-ashtakam-in-telugu/ (Telugu script). All three were fetched via curl with a browser user-agent (stotranidhi blocks direct WebFetch) and compared verse by verse; they agree throughout on all nine verses and the closing line. The Telugu page notes that these verses are also printed in Stotranidhi's own "Śrī Durgā Stotranidhi" recitation book. No independent second site carrying this text was located, so the cross-check rests on agreement among Stotranidhi's three language editions. Three readings are retained as printed and flagged where they occur: binduvaktrām (v. 3), mṛgendraiḥ and the active saṁstuvantīm where the sense wants a passive (v. 7), and the loose nominative cittaśuddhiḥ (v. 9).

Verse count: 9 — eight verses of praise, all sharing a refrain, followed by a phalaśruti.

Sections:
అష్టకం — Aṣṭakam (vv. 1–8)
ఫలశ్రుతి — Phalaśruti, the fruits of recitation (v. 9)

Blurb: Jogulāmbā is the goddess of Alampur in Telangana, which is one of the eighteen great śakti pīṭhas. This hymn is unusually exact about where it stands. Its first verse places her on the bank of the Tuṅgabhadrā, in the 'subtle Kāśī', which is Alampur's own title of Dakṣiṇa Kāśī. It places her on the left lap of the great yogin Brahmeśa, which is the name of the Śiva of that temple, Bāla Brahmeśvara. Everything after that is description, in the bhujaṅgaprayāta metre of twelve syllables to a pāda. Each verse ends with the same half-line: 'śaraccandrabimbāṁ bhaje jogulāmbām', meaning 'I worship Jogulāmbā, whose face is the disc of the autumn moon'. The eight verses build one figure, taken in from crown to feet. She has a gold crown set with cat's-eye and coral, a saffron line upon her brow, collyrium on her lotus eyes, a braid dark as a cloud falling to her feet, a parrot on her hand, yellow silk, and anklets that tinkle. Two verses, 6 and 8, break this sequence of description in order to say what she is: the boat across the ocean of becoming, the primal vidyā, the sister of Viṣṇu, and fire in its own form.

महायोगिब्रह्मेशवामाङ्कसंस्थां

mahāyogipīṭhasthale tuṅgabhadrā-
-taṭe sūkṣmakāśyāṁ sadāsaṁvasantīm |
mahāyogibrahmeśavāmāṅkasaṁsthāṁ
śaraccandrabimbāṁ bhaje jogulāmbām ||
en: Dwelling always at the seat of the great yogins, on the bank of the Tuṅgabhadrā, in the subtle Kāśī; seated on the left lap of Brahmeśa, the great yogin — I worship Jogulāmbā, whose face is the disc of the autumn moon. (Every place-name here is Alampur's own: the Tuṅgabhadrā that runs past it, its title Dakṣiṇa Kāśī, and Bāla Brahmeśvara, the Śiva of its temple.)

hi: महायोगियों के पीठस्थल में, तुंगभद्रा के तट पर, सूक्ष्म काशी में सदा निवास करने वाली; महायोगी ब्रह्मेश के वाम अंक पर स्थित — शरद्-चंद्र के बिंब जैसी जोगुलांबा का मैं भजन करता हूँ।

प्रभाते निशार्धेऽथवा चित्तशुद्धिः ।
पृथिव्यां परं सर्वभोगांश्च भुक्त्वा
श्रियं मुक्तिमाप्नोति दिव्यां प्रसिद्धः ॥
iast:
idaṁ jogulāmbāṣṭakaṁ yaḥ paṭhedvā
prabhāte niśārdhe'thavā cittaśuddhiḥ |
pṛthivyāṁ paraṁ sarvabhogāṁśca bhuktvā
śriyaṁ muktimāpnoti divyāṁ prasiddhaḥ ||
en: Whoever recites this Jogulāmbāṣṭaka, at dawn or else at midnight, gains purity of mind; and having enjoyed on earth every enjoyment to the full, he becomes renowned and attains divine fortune and liberation. (The source's cittaśuddhiḥ stands loosely in the nominative within the relative clause; retained as printed.)
tel: ఈ జోగులాంబాష్టకాన్ని ప్రభాతంలో గాని, అర్ధరాత్రి గాని ఎవడు పఠిస్తాడో అతనికి చిత్తశుద్ధి కలుగుతుంది; భూమిపై సర్వభోగాలనూ పూర్తిగా అనుభవించి, ప్రసిద్ధుడై దివ్యమైన సంపదను, ముక్తిని పొందుతాడు.
hi: जो इस जोगुलांबाष्टक को प्रभात में अथवा निशार्ध में पढ़ता है, उसे चित्तशुद्धि होती है; और पृथ्वी पर समस्त भोगों को भोगकर वह प्रसिद्ध होकर दिव्य श्री और मुक्ति प्राप्त करता है।

---
Recension note: Only Stotranidhi's tradition of this hymn could be located; its IAST, Devanāgarī and Telugu-script editions agree word for word across all nine verses and the closing line, so no variant readings are recorded. The metre throughout is bhujaṅgaprayāta (twelve syllables per pāda, four ya-gaṇas), which is why the compound in verse 1 has to break across the pāda boundary; the source's hyphenation there is reproduced. The three irregular readings flagged above (binduvaktrām v. 3; mṛgendraiḥ and the active saṁstuvantīm v. 7; the nominative cittaśuddhiḥ v. 9) stand identically in all three editions and so belong to the shared source; all are left as printed. The topographical detail of verse 1 is worth noting for anyone dating or placing the text: it identifies the shrine unambiguously as Alampur — Tuṅgabhadrā, Dakṣiṇa Kāśī, and Bāla Brahmeśvara — which makes this a hymn written for that pīṭha rather than a general Devī stotra later attached to it.
