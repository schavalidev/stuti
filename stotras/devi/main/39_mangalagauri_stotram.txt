Title: Śrī Maṅgalagaurī Stotram
Devanāgarī: श्री मङ्गलगौरी स्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ మంగళగౌరీ స్తోత్రం

Author: spoken by Ravi, the Sun (called Bhānu and Savitā in the closing verse). The colophon locates it precisely: "iti śrīskāndapurāṇe kāśīkhaṇḍe ravikṛta śrīmaṅgalagaurī stotram" — the Śrī Maṅgalagaurī Stotra made by Ravi, in the Kāśīkhaṇḍa of the Skanda Purāṇa.

Source / recension: https://stotranidhi.com/en/sri-mangala-gauri-stotram-in-english/ (IAST), https://stotranidhi.com/hi/sri-mangala-gauri-stotram-in-sanskrit/ (Devanāgarī), https://stotranidhi.com/sri-mangala-gauri-stotram-in-telugu/ (Telugu script). All three were fetched via curl with a browser user-agent (stotranidhi blocks direct WebFetch) and compared verse by verse; they agree throughout on all nine verses and the colophon. No independent second site carrying this text was located, so the cross-check rests on agreement among Stotranidhi's three language editions, backed by the colophon's precise purāṇic location. Three printing details are retained as printed and flagged where they occur: tavaśubhaḥ run together for tava śubhaḥ (v. 4), the feminine accusative tāṁ where the syntax wants the masculine plural tān (v. 5), and the loose śrīmaṅgalāṣṭaka mahāstavanena where the two words form one compound (v. 9). Where a word straddles a pāda break the source hyphenates it across the two lines; that hyphenation is reproduced.

Verse count: 9 — eight verses of praise (the hymn calls itself an aṣṭaka in v. 9), followed by one closing narrative verse describing the Sun falling silent before Śiva and Śivā.

Sections:
స్తోత్రం — Stotram, the eight verses of the aṣṭaka proper (vv. 1–8)
కథ — the narrative close, spoken about the Sun rather than by him (v. 9)

Blurb: This is a hymn of Kāśī from the Skanda Purāṇa. It is placed in the mouth of the Sun and addressed to the Maṅgalagaurī of Vārāṇasī, the goddess whose Tuesday worship gives the vrata its name. Its best writing is local and concerns the river. In verse 3 the singing of her name becomes a stream of clear merit, which undercuts and carries away the trees of sin standing upon its banks. In Kāśī that is not an image chosen at random. Verse 2 is built on pure repetition, with four pādas each opening with the word śrīmaṅgale. Verse 4 turns on a triple play of words that Sanskrit makes and English cannot: bhavāni, bhavatī and bhava, meaning mother Bhavānī, you yourself, and the existence whose fierce sorrows you carry away. The opening verse holds the neatest thought in the hymn. The devotee who bows until his forehead is whitened with the dust of her feet will find, in some later birth, that it is the crescent moon that whitens it instead. Verse 8 sets her in four places at once: as Gaurī beside the moon-crested Śiva, as Sāvitrī with Brahmā, as Lakṣmī with the bearer of the discus, and in Kāśī as the Lakṣmī of liberation itself.

deva:

भालस्थलीं वहति यः प्रणतिप्रवीणः ।
जन्मान्तरेऽपि रजनीकरचारुलेखा

tāṁ gaurayatyatitarāṁ kila tasya puṁsaḥ ||
en: Goddess, the man practised in prostration who carries a brow whitened by the dust of your lotus feet — in another birth too, so it is said, that same brow of his is whitened further still by the lovely crescent streak of the moon. (The devotee's forehead marked with her foot-dust becomes, later, a forehead bearing Śiva's own crescent.)

devīṁ ca devamasakṛtparitaḥ praṇamya
tūṣṇīṁ babhūva savitā śivayoḥ purastāt ||
en: Having praised her in this way — the splendour of the half-body of Smarahara — with this great hymn, the Śrī Maṅgalāṣṭaka, Bhānu the Sun bowed again and again on every side to the Goddess and to the God, and then Savitā fell silent before the two of them, Śiva and Śivā. (This closing verse speaks of the Sun in the third person and names the preceding eight verses as an aṣṭaka; the source prints śrīmaṅgalāṣṭaka mahāstavanena as two words where they form a single compound.)
tel: స్మరహరుని అర్ధశరీర శోభ అయిన ఆమెను ఈ శ్రీమంగళాష్టకమనే మహాస్తవంతో స్తుతించి, భానుడు దేవిని, దేవుని మరల మరల అన్ని వైపులా ప్రణమిల్లి, ఆ శివ-శివల ఎదుట సవిత మౌనం వహించాడు.
hi: इस प्रकार स्मरहर के अर्ध शरीर की शोभा रूप उन्हें इस श्रीमंगलाष्टक महास्तव से स्तुत करके, भानु ने देवी और देव को बार-बार चारों ओर से प्रणाम किया, और सविता उन शिव-शिवा के समक्ष मौन हो गए।

---
Recension note: Only Stotranidhi's tradition of this excerpt could be located; its IAST, Devanāgarī and Telugu-script editions agree word for word across all nine verses and the colophon, so no variant readings are recorded. The colophon's placement — Skanda Purāṇa, Kāśīkhaṇḍa — is precise enough to be collated against a printed edition of that purāṇa, which would be the natural next step for this text. The metre throughout is vasantatilakā (fourteen syllables per pāda), which accounts for the compounds running across the pāda break in verses 4 and 5; the source's hyphenation of those words is reproduced. The three irregular readings flagged above (tavaśubhaḥ v. 4, tāṁ for tān v. 5, and the loose śrīmaṅgalāṣṭaka mahāstavanena v. 9) stand identically in all three editions and so belong to the shared source; all are left as printed. Note the internal count: the hymn names itself an aṣṭaka and does consist of eight verses of praise, the ninth being a narrative frame verse spoken about the Sun rather than by him.
