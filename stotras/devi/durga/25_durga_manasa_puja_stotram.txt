Title: Śrī Durgā Mānasa Pūjā Stotram
Devanāgarī: श्री दुर्गा मानस पूजा स्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ దుర్గా మానస పూజా స్తోత్రం

Author: The verse-colophon itself ("iti durgātantre śrī durgā mānasa pūjā stotram") names no author, only its source text, the Durgā Tantra. stotranidhi.com's Telugu-language editorial introduction attributes it to Ādi Śaṅkarācārya; this is reported here as a traditional/popular attribution carried by that one source, not as something the stotra's own colophon claims.

Verse count: 19 numbered verses (verses 1–16 correspond one-to-one to the sixteen ritual upacāras of a formal pūjā, as verse 19 itself states; verses 17–18 are two further devotional verses on celestial music and on bhakti itself; verse 19 is the phalaśruti), followed by a one-line prose colophon

Sections:
షోడశోపచార పూజ — the sixteen mentally-offered upacāras (vv. 1–16): pādukā/feet (1), throne and anointing-oil (2), bath (3), perfume (4), garment (5), jewelry (6), neck-ornament, tilaka and collyrium (7), mirror (8), consecrated water (9), flowers (10), incense (11), lamp (12), food (13), betel (14), umbrella (15), fly-whisk and Vedic sound (16)
సంగీతోపహారం — an offering of celestial music and dance (v. 17)
భక్తిశ్లోకం — a verse on devotion itself as the truest offering (v. 18)
ఫలశ్రుతి — the fruit of reciting all sixteen verses (v. 19)
సమాప్తి వాక్యం — Colophon (the closing line attributing the text to the Durgā Tantra, unnumbered)

Blurb: This is a mānasa pūjā, that is, a worship performed in the mind. Each of the sixteen customary offerings of a formal Durgā pūjā is imagined and offered, instead of being gathered and placed physically. The offerings are sandals, a throne, bathing oil, perfume, garments, jewellery, a mirror, consecrated water, flowers, incense, a lamp, food, betel, an umbrella and a fly-whisk. The verses are composed in the Śārdūlavikrīḍita and Sragdharā metres. Because nothing physical is needed, the worship may be performed wherever the worshipper happens to be. The introduction on the source page at stotranidhi.com states this. The hymn does not end with a request. Verse 18 ends with a reflection: the simple longing for devotion is a rarer gift than anything that could be obtained even after ten million births. In this way the hymn is both an act of worship and a teaching on the nature of bhakti.

deva:
उद्यच्चन्दनकुङ्कुमारुणपयोधाराभिराप्लावितां
नानानर्घ्यमणिप्रवालघटितां दत्तां गृहाणाम्बिके ।
आमृष्टां सुरसुन्दरीभिरभितो हस्ताम्बुजैर्भक्तितो
मातः सुन्दरि भक्तकल्पलतिके श्रीपादुकामादरात् ॥
iast:
udyaccandanakuṅkumāruṇapayodhārābhirāplāvitāṁ
nānānarghyamaṇipravālaghaṭitāṁ dattāṁ gṛhāṇāmbike |
āmṛṣṭāṁ surasundarībhirabhito hastāmbujairbhaktito
mātaḥ sundari bhaktakalpalatike śrīpādukāmādarāt ||
en: Accept, O Ambikā, this pair of sacred sandals — bathed in streams of reddish water fragrant with rising sandal-paste and vermilion, set with countless priceless gems and coral, polished on every side by the devoted lotus-hands of heaven's beauties. O Mother, O beautiful one, O wish-fulfilling creeper to your devotees, receive this offering with love.
tel: ఓ అంబికా, చందనకుంకుమలతో ఎర్రబడిన జలధారలతో అభిషేకించబడి, అమూల్యమైన మణులు పగడములతో పొదగబడిన ఈ దివ్యపాదుకలను స్వీకరించుము — వీటిని సురసుందరీమణులు తమ హస్తపద్మములతో భక్తితో నలుదిక్కులా మెరుగుపరిచారు. ఓ మాతా, సుందరీ, భక్తుల కల్పలతికా, ఆదరముతో ఈ పాదుకలను స్వీకరించుము.
hi: हे अम्बिके, चंदन-कुंकुम से लालिमा लिए जलधाराओं से स्नात, अमूल्य मणियों और प्रवाल से जड़ी इन दिव्य पादुकाओं को स्वीकार करो — जिन्हें सुरसुंदरियों ने अपने करकमलों से भक्तिपूर्वक चारों ओर से निर्मल किया है। हे माता, सुंदरी, भक्तों की कल्पलतिका, आदरपूर्वक इस अर्पण को स्वीकार करो।
