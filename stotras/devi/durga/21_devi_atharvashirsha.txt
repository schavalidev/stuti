Title: Śrī Devyatharvaśīrṣam (Devyupaniṣat)
Devanāgarī: श्रीदेव्यथर्वशीर्षम् (देव्युपनिषत्)
Telugu: శ్రీదేవ్యథర్వశీర్షం (దేవ్యుపనిషత్)

Author: Traditional (attached to the Atharvaveda corpus; author unnamed, in the manner of an Upaniṣad)

Source / recension: sanskritdocuments.org, doc_devii/deviiatharva.html and deviiatharva.itx ("Shri Devyatharvashirsham evaM Devyupanishat" / "श्रीदेव्यथर्वशीर्षं अथवा देव्युपनिषत्", proofread by Ravin Bhalekar and PSA Easwaran). This is a minor Upaniṣad (Śākta Upaniṣad) attached to the Atharvaveda, not a Purāṇic stotra, so it was fetched from sanskritdocuments.org rather than stotranidhi.com. A handful of variant readings ("var") are marked in the source itself at mantras 3, 7, 14, 17, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24; these are noted inline below where they occur but the main running text (not the variant) is used throughout for consistency.

Verse count: 27 (the text's own numbered mantras 1–27; it is Upaniṣad-style prose interspersed with a few metrical verses, not a sequence of regular stotra ślokas, so segmentation follows the source's own mantra numbering rather than any artificial verse-division. The invocatory śānti-pāṭha "oṁ bhadraṁ karṇebhiḥ..." and the dedicatory "śrī gaṇeśāya namaḥ" that precede mantra 1 in the source are folded into verse 1 below; the closing repetition of the same śānti-pāṭha after mantra 27 is folded into verse 27.)

Blurb: A compact Śākta Upaniṣad in which the Goddess, questioned by "all the gods" as to her identity, answers in a series of first-person "aham" (I am) declarations identifying herself with Brahman, with every Vedic deity in turn, with the guṇas, and with the elements themselves — structurally very close to the Devī Sūktam of the Ṛgveda (10.125), on which its early mantras are clearly modeled. Because it is genuinely prose-and-verse Upaniṣadic material rather than a stotra of uniform ślokas, this file segments strictly by the text's own numbered mantras (1–27) rather than forcing artificial verse breaks; several mantras (e.g. 3, 7, 17, 23, 26–27) run to several sentences of prose in the source itself.

स्थिरैरङ्गैस्तुष्टुवांसस्तनूभिर्व्यशेम देवहितं यदायुः ।

स्वस्ति नस्तार्क्ष्यो अरिष्टनेमिः स्वस्ति नो बृहस्पतिर्दधातु ॥
ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः ।
श्री गणेशाय नमः ।
ॐ सर्वे वै देवा देवीमुपतस्थुः कासि त्वं महादेवीति ॥

sthirairaṅgaistuṣṭuvāṁsastanūbhirvyaśema devahitaṁ yadāyuḥ |
svasti na indro vṛddhaśravāḥ svasti naḥ pūṣā viśvavedāḥ |
svasti nastārkṣyo ariṣṭanemiḥ svasti no bṛhaspatirdadhātu ||
oṁ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ |
śrī gaṇeśāya namaḥ |
oṁ sarve vai devā devīmupatasthuḥ kāsi tvaṁ mahādevīti ||
en:
Om. May we hear what is auspicious with our ears, O gods worthy of worship; may we see what is auspicious with our eyes. Praising you with steady limbs and bodies, may we enjoy the life allotted by the gods. May Indra of great fame bring us well-being; may Pūṣan, who knows all, bring us well-being. May Tārkṣya of unharmed wheels bring us well-being; may Bṛhaspati grant us well-being. Om, peace, peace, peace. Salutation to Śrī Gaṇeśa. Om — all the gods once approached the Goddess and asked: "Who are you, O great Goddess?"

deva:
