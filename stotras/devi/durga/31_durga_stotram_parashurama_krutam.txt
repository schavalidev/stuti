Title: Śrī Durgā Stotram (Paraśurāma-kṛtam)
Devanāgarī: श्री दुर्गा स्तोत्रम् (परशुराम कृतम्)
Telugu: శ్రీ దుర్గా స్తోత్రం (పరశురామ కృతం)

Author: Ascribed within the text to Paraśurāma, who speaks it (the text opens "paraśurāma uvāca"). Drawn from the Brahmavaivarta Mahāpurāṇa, Gaṇapatikhaṇḍa, the Nārada–Nārāyaṇa dialogue, chapter 45. The narrative setting is Paraśurāma's confrontation at Kailāsa, where he praises Pārvatī — the wife of his own guru Śiva — and she answers him directly in vv. 42–48.

Blurb: This is one of the longest Durgā hymns in the purāṇic texts. It has four parts: an account of creation, a long list of her forms, a prayer, and then the Goddess's own reply. Paraśurāma begins with a story. He describes how the Goddess came forth from the body of Śrī Kṛṣṇa in Goloka as he turned towards creation, and how the Virāṭ-puruṣa was born, in each pore of whose body whole universes rest (verses 1 to 9). He then names her five chief forms: Rādhā, Sāvitrī, Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī and Sarvamaṅgalā (verses 10 to 18). From these he goes on to name her in every consort, every river, every faculty and every element. Verses 32 to 39 repeat the line 'tāṁ durgāṁ praṇamāmyaham', meaning 'I bow to that Durgā'. Each of these verses recalls an occasion when one far stronger than the speaker needed her help: Brahmā trembling before Madhu and Kaiṭabha, Viṣṇu in that same battle, Śiva fallen from his chariot at Tripura, and at last Śrī Kṛṣṇa himself, who cannot create without her. After all this, his prayer is a small and simple one. Verse 40 asks how a mother could be angry at the faults of her own children. Paraśurāma then weeps. The Goddess herself replies. She calls him her son and grants him freedom from death. In verses 47 and 48 she gives him two homely comparisons: if the moon is favourable, what harm can angry stars do; and if the king protects you, what harm can angry servants do.

ghaṭe sampūjya durgāṁ ca sā putraṁ labhate dhruvam ||
en: — she who listens to this king among hymns assuredly obtains a son. And the mother of daughters only, who has no son, if she listens for five months and worships Durgā in the sacred pot, she too assuredly obtains a son.
tel: — ఈ స్తోత్రరాజాన్ని వినేవారు తప్పక పుత్రుని పొందుతారు. కుమార్తెలు మాత్రమే కలిగి పుత్రుడు లేని తల్లి ఐదు నెలలు విని, ఘటంలో దుర్గను పూజిస్తే, ఆమె కూడా తప్పక పుత్రుని పొందుతుంది.
hi: — जो इस स्तोत्रराज को सुनती है वह निश्चय ही पुत्र प्राप्त करती है। और जो केवल कन्याओं की माता है, पुत्रहीन है, यदि वह पाँच मास सुने और घट में दुर्गा का पूजन करे, तो वह भी निश्चय ही पुत्र प्राप्त करती है।

Recension note: Single-source text (stotranidhi.com), verified across that site's Devanāgarī and IAST editions verse for verse across all fifty-nine verses with no divergence between the two scripts. The colophon reads: "iti śrībrahmavaivarte mahāpurāṇe gaṇapatikhaṇḍe nāradanārāyaṇasaṁvāde pañcacatvāriṁśo'dhyāye paraśurāmakṛta durgā stotram" — Brahmavaivarta Mahāpurāṇa, Gaṇapatikhaṇḍa, chapter 45. Two readings deserve flagging for anyone collating against printed Brahmavaivarta editions. First, v. 9's bhayadharmajalena is very likely a transmitted spelling of bhayagharmajalena, "the water of the sweat of awe" (gharma-jala being the standard compound for perspiration); the translation here follows that sense, since dharma-jala yields nothing coherent in context. Second, v. 11's vedāśāstraprasūr shows a metri causa lengthening of veda-śāstra-prasūḥ. Verse 59 carries three lines rather than two, which is how the chapter closes; editions that split it report sixty verses. Verses 1–9 presuppose the Brahmavaivarta's own distinctive cosmogony (Kṛṣṇa in Goloka, the Virāṭ born from the cosmic egg, universes in the pores) and read as compressed narrative rather than praise, which is unusual for a stotra and is the reason the hymn opens without any invocation.
