Title: Śrī Durgā Kavacam (Brahmāṇḍamohanam)
Devanāgarī: श्री ब्रह्माण्डमोहनाख्यं दुर्गा कवचम्
Telugu: శ్రీ బ్రహ్మాండమోహనాఖ్యం దుర్గా కవచం

Author: traditional; the text frames itself as a chain of transmission rather than naming a single human author. It opens with the sage Nārada asking "Nārāyaṇa" (here a teaching sage/deity-voice, addressed simply as "Nārāyaṇa" and not further identified within the verses themselves) to speak the kavaca "named Brahmāṇḍamohanam" ("that which enchants/bewilders the cosmic egg," i.e. the whole universe) belonging to Prakṛti (the Goddess as primordial Nature). Nārāyaṇa answers that it was told, out of grace, by Śrī Kṛṣṇa to Brahmā; by Brahmā to Dharma on the bank of the Jāhnavī (Gaṅgā); and by Dharma to "me" at Puṣkara — before listing those who wore it and prevailed: Śiva (as Tripurāri, destroying the triple city), Brahmā (escaping the danger of Madhu-Kaiṭabha), Bhadrakālī (slaying Raktabīja), Indra (regaining the "abode of the lotus"), the warrior Bāṇa, and the sage Durvāsas. The colophon of the fuller recension used here attributes the whole to the Brahmavaivarta Mahāpurāṇa's Prakṛtikhaṇḍa; a parallel, shorter recension's colophon (see below) further places it within a Nārada-Nārāyaṇa dialogue, in the "Durgopākhyāna" section, as the sixty-seventh chapter (saptaṣaṣṭitama), and is the source of the explicit phrase "brahmāṇḍamohanaṁ nāma śrī durgā kavacam" that gives this kavaca its distinguishing name.

Disambiguation from this folder's other Durgā-kavacas: this corpus already holds 05_devi_kavacam.txt (the standard 56-verse Devī Māhātmyam/Saptaśatī kavaca, opening "oṁ yad guhyaṁ paramaṁ loke," framed as Brahmā answering Mārkaṇḍeya, colophon attributing it to the Vārāha Purāṇa) and 09_durga_kavacam.txt (a short Kubjikātantra kavaca, opening "īśvara uvāca | śṛṇu devi pravakṣyāmi," colophon "iti kubjikātantroktaṁ śrī durgā kavacam"), and this batch also adds a "Dīpa Durgā Kavacam" at file 11. The present text is none of those three: its own opening line is "nārada uvāca | bhagavan sarvadharmajña sarvajñānaviśārada | brahmāṇḍamohanaṁ nāma prakṛte kavacaṁ vada" — Nārada explicitly naming "Brahmāṇḍamohanam" as the kavaca he is requesting — and its colophon (in the shorter recension) closes with the identical phrase "brahmāṇḍamohanaṁ nāma śrī durgā kavacam." sanskritdocuments.org's own kavacha index in fact catalogues four separate texts called "durgākavacham" (1: Kubjikātantra, "umādevī śiraḥ pātu" = this corpus's file 09; 2: this text, "brahmāṇḍamohanākhyam... durgeti caturthyantaḥ," from the Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa; 3 and 4: two more from the Muṇḍamālātantra, "siddhiṁ siddheśvarī pātu" and "pārvatī mastakaṁ pātu" respectively, neither used in this corpus), plus a separate "dīpadurgākavacham" (file 11) and a "vaṁśavṛddhikara durgākavacham" (not used here) — nine Durgā-kavaca-type texts in that one index alone, which is why the "Brahmāṇḍamohanam" epithet named in the task, rather than the generic title, was the operative search term and the confirming detail.

Source / recension: This kavaca circulates in two recensions of differing length, and both were used together to build this file. (1) The fuller, 19-verse recension — opening with Nārada's question and Nārāyaṇa's transmission-narrative (vv. 1-6) before the mantra-kavaca proper — is preserved at sanskritdocuments.org's doc_devii diplomatic transliteration, catalogued there specifically as "durgAkavacham 2 (brahmANDamohanAkhyam durgeti chaturthyantaH)" at durgaakavach2.itx (transliterated and proofread by Ravin Bhalekar, "Description: brahmavaivartapurANa," fetched via the browser tools). This was independently cross-checked, verse for verse, against a full Devanāgarī printing of the same 19 verses on gurukripa.org.in ("Brahmand Mohan Durga Kavach") — the two sources agree on the entire text (allowing for a few evident spelling slips on gurukripa's side, noted below), which is strong independent confirmation. (2) A shorter, 9-verse recension — omitting the transmission-narrative (vv. 1-6 above) and beginning directly at what is v. 7 in the fuller numbering ("oṁ durgeti caturthyantaḥ...") — is what vignanam.org (devanagari/durga-kavacham-brahmanda-puranam.html, with parallel english/ and telugu/ pages, titled there "Durga Kavacham (Brahmanda Puranam)") and stotranidhi.com ("Brahmanda Mohana Durga Kavacham," fetched via the browser tools since the site blocks direct WebFetch) both print; these two agree with each other word for word and were used to supply this shorter recension's own, fuller colophon (which names the Nārada-Nārāyaṇa dialogue, the "Durgopākhyāna," and chapter 67, and explicitly says "brahmāṇḍamohanaṁ nāma śrī durgā kavacam" — information the sanskritdocuments.org/gurukripa colophon omits). This file follows the fuller (19-verse) recension as its base text, since it is both more complete and is the one sanskritdocuments.org itself catalogues under this exact title; every verse where the shorter recension's wording differs is flagged at that verse and summarized below. A fourth source, an English-only translation by P. R. Ramachander posted at hindupedia.com and mirrored at celextel.org (Vedanta Spiritual Library) and shastras.com, was also checked: it follows the fuller recension's structure and numbering closely, but its translation includes one extra couplet, about the kavaca having been worn by "Mahakala" and "Nandi," between the Bhadrakālī/Indra couplet and the Bāṇa/Durvāsas couplet — this couplet has no Sanskrit counterpart in either of the two independent Sanskrit witnesses used here (sanskritdocuments.org and gurukripa.org.in both go straight from the Bhadrakālī/Indra couplet to the Bāṇa/Durvāsas couplet), so it is not reproduced as a verse in this file; it is flagged here as an unverified addition found only in translation, not in any Sanskrit text located.

Verse count: 19 numbered verses, following sanskritdocuments.org's durgaakavach2.itx numbering (independently confirmed by gurukripa.org.in's Devanāgarī): vv. 1-6 are the transmission-narrative (Nārada's question and Nārāyaṇa's reply), vv. 7-14 are the mantra-kavaca proper (the six-syllable mantra and bīja-strings protecting the body, then the ten directional guardian-forms), and vv. 15-19 are the injunction to secrecy and the phalaśruti. An unnumbered prose colophon follows. The parallel shorter recension (vignanam.org / stotranidhi.com) numbers only the material from v. 7 onward as its own 1-9, with several of those verses combined or worded differently — see the per-verse notes and the recension note at the end.

Sections:
పూర్వకథ — Pūrvakathā (vv. 1-6: Nārada's question and Nārāyaṇa's answer narrating the kavaca's transmission-lineage — Kṛṣṇa to Brahmā to Dharma to Nārāyaṇa — and the deities and sages said to have worn it: Śiva, Brahmā, Bhadrakālī, Indra, Bāṇa, Durvāsas)
కవచం — Kavacam proper (vv. 7-14: the six-syllable mantra and successive bīja-strings protecting the head, face, throat, shoulders, back, chest and whole body, then the ten directions each assigned to a named form of the Goddess)
ఫలశ్రుతి — Phalaśruti (vv. 15-19: the injunction against giving the kavaca to the unworthy, and the benefits — equality with Viṣṇu, the merit of every sacred bathing-place and of circumambulating the earth, siddhi through five hundred thousand japa, freedom from death by water, fire, poison or fever, and jīvanmukti)
సమాప్తి — Samāpti (the closing colophon attributing the text to the Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa's Prakṛtikhaṇḍa)

Blurb: This kavaca begins by telling how it was handed down. It names those who are said to have worn it before: Lord Śiva at the destruction of Tripura, Brahmā when escaping Madhu and Kaiṭabha, Bhadrakālī when slaying Raktabīja, Indra, the warrior Bāṇa, and the sage Durvāsas. It then protects the body part by part. For this it uses a mantra of six syllables, 'oṁ durgāyai svāhā'. The text does not state the mantra plainly, but describes it as the name Durgā in the fourth case, ending with svāhā. It also uses the bīja syllables hrīṁ, śrīṁ, klīṁ, aiṁ, haṁ and saṁ. The kavaca then gives each of the ten directions to a named form of the Goddess. These are Prakṛti, Caṇḍikā, Bhadrakālī, Maheśvarī, Vārāhī, Sarvamaṅgalā, Vaiṣṇavī, Śivapriyā and Jagadambikā, who guard the water, the land and the sky. The phalaśruti promises the reciter a state equal to that of Viṣṇu. It further promises liberation while still living to one who completes five hundred thousand repetitions.

deva:
नारद उवाच ।
भगवन्सर्वधर्मज्ञ सर्वज्ञानविशारद ।
ब्रह्माण्डमोहनं नाम प्रकृते कवचं वद ॥
iast:
nārada uvāca |
bhagavan sarvadharmajña sarvajñānaviśārada |
brahmāṇḍamohanaṁ nāma prakṛte kavacaṁ vada ||
en: Nārada said: "O Bhagavān, knower of all dharma, versed in all knowledge — tell me the kavaca called Brahmāṇḍamohanam ('that which enchants/bewilders the cosmic egg,' i.e. the whole universe), belonging to Prakṛti (the Goddess as primordial Nature)."
tel: నారదుడు పలికెను: "ఓ భగవానుడా, సర్వధర్మములను ఎరిగినవాడా, సర్వజ్ఞానమునందు నిపుణుడా — ప్రకృతికి సంబంధించిన 'బ్రహ్మాండమోహనం' అను కవచమును నాకు తెలుపుము."
hi: नारद ने कहा: "हे भगवन्, समस्त धर्मों के ज्ञाता, समस्त ज्ञान में निपुण — प्रकृति से संबंधित 'ब्रह्माण्डमोहनम्' नामक कवच मुझे बताइए।"

शृणु वक्ष्यामि हे वत्स कवचं च सुदुर्लभम् ।
श्रीकृष्णेनैव कथितं कृपया ब्रह्मणे पुरा ॥
iast:
nārāyaṇa uvāca |

en: Nārāyaṇa said: "Listen, dear child — I shall tell you this kavaca, which is very hard to obtain. It was told, out of grace, by Śrī Kṛṣṇa himself to Brahmā, long ago."
tel: నారాయణుడు పలికెను: "వినుము వత్సా, మిక్కిలి దుర్లభమైన ఈ కవచమును నీకు తెలిపెదను. దీనిని పూర్వం శ్రీకృష్ణుడే కృపతో బ్రహ్మకు బోధించెను."
hi: नारायण ने कहा: "हे वत्स, सुनो, मैं तुम्हें यह अत्यंत दुर्लभ कवच बताता हूँ। इसे पूर्वकाल में श्रीकृष्ण ने ही कृपापूर्वक ब्रह्मा को बताया था।"

deva:

धर्मेण दत्तं मह्यं च कृपया पुष्करे पुरा ॥
iast:

en: It was told before by Brahmā to Dharma on the bank of the Jāhnavī (the Gaṅgā); and Dharma, out of grace, gave it to me at Puṣkara, long ago.
tel: దీనిని పూర్వం బ్రహ్మ జాహ్నవీ (గంగా) తీరమున ధర్మునకు తెలిపెను; ధర్ముడు కృపతో పుష్కరమునందు పూర్వం నాకు దీనిని ఇచ్చెను.

--- verse 4 | section: Pūrvakathā ---
deva:
त्रिपुरारिश्च यद्धृत्वा जघान त्रिपुरं पुरा ।
ममोच ब्रह्मा यद्धृत्वा मधुकैटभयोर्भयात् ॥
iast:
tripurāriś ca yaddhṛtvā jaghāna tripuraṁ purā |
mamoca brahmā yaddhṛtvā madhukaiṭabhayor bhayāt ||
en: Wearing it, Tripurāri ("enemy of Tripura," Śiva) once destroyed the triple city; wearing it, Brahmā was freed from fear of the demons Madhu and Kaiṭabha. (Both sources checked read the verb as mamoca; this looks like a transmitted variant of mumoca, the expected perfect-tense form of √muc "to release" — see recension note.)
tel: దీనిని ధరించి త్రిపురారి (త్రిపురములను నశింపజేసిన శివుడు) పూర్వం త్రిపురమును నశింపజేసెను; దీనిని ధరించి బ్రహ్మ మధుకైటభుల భయము నుండి విముక్తుడాయెను.
hi: इसे धारण कर त्रिपुरारि (त्रिपुर को नष्ट करने वाले शिव) ने पूर्वकाल में त्रिपुर का नाश किया; इसे धारण कर ब्रह्मा मधु और कैटभ के भय से मुक्त हुए।

--- verse 5 | section: Pūrvakathā ---
deva:
सञ्जहार रक्तबीजं यद्धृत्वा भद्रकालिका ।
यद्धृत्वा हि महेन्द्रश्च सम्प्राप कमलालयाम् ॥

tel: దీనిని ధరించి భద్రకాళిక రక్తబీజుని సంహరించెను; దీనిని ధరించి మహేంద్రుడు కమలాలయను (లక్ష్మీదేవి కృపను లేదా తన స్వర్గపదవిని) పొందెను.
hi: इसे धारण कर भद्रकालिका ने रक्तबीज का संहार किया; इसे धारण कर महेन्द्र ने कमलालया (लक्ष्मी की कृपा अथवा अपने स्वर्गपद) को प्राप्त किया।

--- verse 7 | section: Kavacam ---
deva:
ॐ दुर्गेति चतुर्थ्यन्तः स्वाहान्तो मे शिरोऽवतु ।
मन्त्रः षडक्षरोऽयं च भक्तानां कल्पपादपः ॥
iast:

en: "Oṁ," followed by "Durgā" declined in the fourth (dative) case and ending in "svāhā" — that is, the six-syllable mantra "oṁ durgāyai svāhā" — let this protect my head; this six-syllable mantra is a wish-fulfilling tree (kalpapādapa) for its devotees.
tel: "ఓం" తరువాత "దుర్గా" అనునది చతుర్థీ విభక్తిలో (దుర్గాయై) చెప్పి "స్వాహా" తో ముగియు షడక్షర మంత్రము — అనగా "ఓం దుర్గాయై స్వాహా" — నా శిరస్సును రక్షించుగాక; ఈ షడక్షర మంత్రము భక్తులకు కల్పవృక్షము వంటిది.
hi: "ॐ" के बाद "दुर्गा" को चतुर्थी विभक्ति (दुर्गायै) में रखकर "स्वाहा" से समाप्त होने वाला षडक्षर मंत्र — अर्थात् "ॐ दुर्गायै स्वाहा" — मेरे सिर की रक्षा करे; यह षडक्षर मंत्र भक्तों के लिए कल्पवृक्ष के समान है।

en: Let this mantra "oṁ hrīṁ śrīṁ" continually protect my shoulders; let "hrīṁ śrīṁ klīṁ" always protect my back, on every side. hi: "ॐ ह्रीं श्रीं" यह मंत्र सदा मेरे कंधों की रक्षा करे; "ह्रीं श्रीं क्लीं" यह मंत्र सदा सब ओर से मेरी पीठ की रक्षा करे। (ह्रीं, श्रीं, क्लीं शाक्त मंत्रों के प्रमुख बीजाक्षर हैं।)

deva:

hrīṁ me vakṣasthale pātu haṁ saṁ śrīm iti santatam |

en: Let "hrīṁ" continually protect my chest, [together with] "haṁ saṁ śrīṁ"; let "aiṁ śrīṁ hrīṁ" protect my whole body always, in sleep and in waking. Aiṁ is the vāgbhava/Sarasvatī-bīja, here joined with śrīṁ and hrīṁ as a further three-syllable protective string. (The shorter recension on vignanam.org/stotranidhi.com reads this verse quite differently — "hrīṁ me vakṣaḥsthalaṁ pātu hastaṁ śrīm iti santatam | śrīṁ hrīṁ klīṁ pātu sarvāṅgaṁ svapne jāgaraṇe tathā," i.e. "hrīṁ" protects the chest, "śrīṁ" the hands, and the closing bīja-string is "śrīṁ hrīṁ klīṁ" rather than "aiṁ śrīṁ hrīṁ" — a genuine mantra-content variant between the two recensions, reproduced here as transmitted rather than harmonized.)
tel: "హ్రీం" నా వక్షస్థలమును, "హం సం శ్రీం"తో కలిపి నిరంతరము రక్షించుగాక; "ఐం శ్రీం హ్రీం" నా సర్వాంగమును నిద్రలోను మేల్కొనియు సదా రక్షించుగాక. (ఐం అనునది వాగ్భవ బీజము.)
hi: "ह्रीं", "हं सं श्रीं" के साथ, सदा मेरे वक्षस्थल की रक्षा करे; "ऐं श्रीं ह्रीं" सोते और जागते समय सदा मेरे सम्पूर्ण शरीर की रक्षा करे। (ऐं वाग्भव बीज है।)

The shorter, 9-verse recension of this text on vignanam.org and stotranidhi.com (which corresponds only to vv. 7-16 above) closes instead with a fuller colophon: "iti śrī brahmavaivarte mahāpurāṇe prakṛtikhaṇḍe nāradanārāyaṇasaṁvāde durgopākhyāne saptaṣaṣṭitamo'dhyāye brahmāṇḍamohanaṁ nāma śrī durgā kavacam" — "Thus, in the glorious Brahmavaivarta Mahāpurāṇa, in the Prakṛtikhaṇḍa, in the dialogue of Nārada and Nārāyaṇa, in the Durgopākhyāna [the section/episode of Durgā's story], in the sixty-seventh chapter: the Śrī Durgā Kavaca named Brahmāṇḍamohanam." That fuller colophon supplies both the chapter number and the explicit title-phrase "brahmāṇḍamohanaṁ nāma," which is what gives this kavaca its name and is the detail used to identify and disambiguate it in this file's header.

Recension note: This file follows the fuller, 19-verse recension of the Brahmāṇḍamohana Durgā Kavaca as catalogued at sanskritdocuments.org (doc_devii/durgaakavach2.itx, titled there "durgAkavacham 2 (brahmANDamohanAkhyam durgeti chaturthyantaH)," transliterated and proofread by Ravin Bhalekar, fetched via the browser tools), independently cross-checked verse for verse against a complete Devanāgarī printing of the same 19 verses on gurukripa.org.in — the two agree throughout, with only a few evident spelling slips on gurukripa's side silently normalized in the deva field here (श्रृणु → शृणु in v. 2; जान्हवीतटे → जाह्नवीतटे in v. 3, matching sanskritdocuments' jāhnavītaṭe, since jāhnavī, "daughter of Jahnu," is the standard form). A second, shorter (9-verse) recension of the same text — omitting the opening transmission-narrative (this file's vv. 1-6) and numbering only the material from this file's v. 7 onward — is what vignanam.org (devanagari/durga-kavacham-brahmanda-puranam.html and its parallel english/ and telugu/ pages) and stotranidhi.com (Brahmanda Mohana Durga Kavacham, fetched via the browser tools since the site blocks direct WebFetch) both print, agreeing with each other word for word; this recension's variant readings against the fuller text are flagged at vv. 8, 9, 11, 12 and 16 above, and its own fuller colophon (naming chapter 67, the Nārada-Nārāyaṇa dialogue and the Durgopākhyāna) is given at the end. A third source, P. R. Ramachander's English translation (hindupedia.com, mirrored at celextel.org and shastras.com), follows the fuller recension's structure but includes one couplet, about the kavaca having been worn by "Mahakala" and "Nandi," between vv. 5 and 6 above that has no Sanskrit counterpart in either of the two Sanskrit witnesses used for this file; that couplet is flagged at v. 6 above and is not reproduced as its own verse, since it could not be verified in any Sanskrit source located. The verb mamoca in v. 4 (agreed on by both Sanskrit witnesses) is preserved as transmitted despite reading like a corruption of the expected mumoca ("was freed," perfect of √muc); v. 9's namo'ntakaḥ (also agreed on by both witnesses, but grammatically opaque — antakaḥ ordinarily means "Death/Yama") is likewise preserved as transmitted, with the shorter recension's grammatically cleaner namo'ntataḥ ("ending in namaḥ") noted as the likely intended sense and used for the translation. No verse in this file is unverified in the sense of resting on a single source: every verse is attested identically (bar the noted spelling slips) in both sanskritdocuments.org and gurukripa.org.in, and the overlapping portion is further confirmed by vignanam.org and stotranidhi.com.
