Blurb: This is the last preparatory rite before the seven hundred verses begin. It is the nyāsa page of the Gītā Press edition, placed between the Navārṇa-vidhi and chapter 1 of the Saptaśatī. Its viniyoga is unusual, because it declares the materials of all three caritras together at one time. It names three ṛṣis, who are Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Rudra, together with three goddesses, three metres, three śaktis, three bījas, three tattvas, and the three Vedas as its three dhyānas. The six ślokas placed upon the fingers were not nyāsa mantras to begin with. They are the closing prayers that the gods offer to Nārāyaṇī in chapter 11 of the Durgā Saptaśatī, asking her to guard them in the east and in the west. Here they are applied to the reciter's own body as armour. After the dhyāna comes a plain Hindi handbook on how to recite. It gives six marks of a good reciter and six marks of a poor one, each quoted in verse below the rule. It forbids reading from a manuscript copied in one's own hand. It also instructs the reciter not to pronounce the words 'iti', 'vadha', 'adhyāya' or 'samāpta' when a chapter ends.
