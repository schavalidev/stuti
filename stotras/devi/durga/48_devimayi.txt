Title: Devīmayī
Devanāgarī: देवीमयी
Telugu: దేవీమయీ

Author: Mahāmāheśvara Ācārya Abhinavagupta (c. 950–1016 CE, Kashmir), named in the attribution line printed beneath the two verses on the page itself — the great Śaiva theologian of the Pratyabhijñā and Krama lineages, author of the Tantrāloka and the Īśvarapratyabhijñāvimarśinī. The Gītā Press volume prints the piece anonymously as to source-work, giving only the author's name after an em-dash; it supplies no title beyond the centred heading देवीमयी and no colophon.

Source / recension: The sole witness for this file is the printed Gītā Press, Gorakhpur edition of the Durgā Saptaśatī supplied as a PDF, read from the page images (the single unnumbered page at PDF page 210, following printed p. 207, whose last item is the श्रीदेवीजीकी आरती; PDF 211 is a blank leaf and PDF 212, the red-framed back cover, carries भगवतीस्तुतिः, a separate piece not belonging to this section). No independent collation was performed for this section. Both verses are, however, well known outside this volume as the closing benedictory verses attached to Abhinavagupta's own works — the second in particular circulates widely in Kashmir Śaiva literature — so the reading is not isolated in the tradition even though no second edition was actually consulted here; that general familiarity is a weak cross-check, not a collation, and it has not been used to alter a single syllable of the printed text. The page prints no verse numbers, no speaker tag and no इति colophon; the star ornament (=★=) used throughout the volume as an end-of-section rule follows the attribution line.

Verse count: 2 Sanskrit verses in drutavilambita metre (four pādas each, printed as four lines with the second and fourth pādas indented), followed by one unnumbered attribution line. The printed page numbers nothing; the count of two is the natural metrical division, not a printed enumeration.

Blurb: These are two verses of Abhinavagupta. They are printed on their own unnumbered page as the last item in the Gītā Press Durgā Saptaśatī. They form a closing benediction rather than a stotra for recitation. Their teaching is the one named in the heading. The body of the Goddess is the whole of speech, with nothing left outside it. Therefore no word that is spoken can fail to be her praise. In the same way, every form whatever, whether outward or only imagined, belongs to her. Therefore no moment of a life holds anything that is not worship. The second verse states the conclusion for practice: not one particle of time is mine that is empty of praise, of japa, of worship or of contemplation. This is why the volume ends with these verses. The recitation that has just been completed does not, in truth, come to an end.

--- verse 1 | section: Devīmayī ---
deva:
तव च का किल न स्तुतिरम्बिके !
सकलशब्दमयी किल ते तनुः।
निखिलमूर्तिषु मे भवदन्वयो
मनसिजासु बहिःप्रसरासु च॥
iast:
tava ca kā kila na stutirambike !
sakalaśabdamayī kila te tanuḥ |
nikhilamūrtiṣu me bhavadanvayo
manasijāsu bahiḥprasarāsu ca ||
en: And what utterance, O Ambikā, is not your praise? — for your very body, they say, is made of all speech without remainder (sakalaśabdamayī): every word that can be spoken is already a limb of you, so no sound a mouth makes falls outside your hymn. And in all forms whatsoever I perceive my connection with you (bhavadanvayaḥ, "descent from you" — the forms are of your lineage), whether they are born in the mind alone (manasijāsu, imagined) or spread out externally (bahiḥprasarāsu, the objects standing outside in the world).
tel: అంబికా! నీ స్తుతి కాని పలుకు ఏది ఉన్నది? — నీ శరీరమే సకల శబ్దమయమైనది; పలుకగలిగిన ప్రతి మాటా నీ అవయవమే కనుక నోటినుండి వెలువడే ఏ ధ్వనీ నీ స్తోత్రానికి బయటిది కాదు. మనసులోనే పుట్టిన (ఊహారూపమైన) రూపాలైనా, బయట విస్తరించి కనబడే (ప్రపంచ వస్తురూపమైన) రూపాలైనా — ఆ సమస్త రూపాలలోనూ నాకు నీతోడి అన్వయమే (నీ వంశసంబంధమే) కనబడుతున్నది.
hi: हे अम्बिके! ऐसी कौन-सी वाणी है जो तुम्हारी स्तुति न हो? — तुम्हारा शरीर ही समस्त शब्दमय है; जो भी शब्द बोला जा सकता है वह तुम्हारा ही अंग है, अतः मुख से निकली कोई भी ध्वनि तुम्हारे स्तवन से बाहर नहीं। और मन में ही उत्पन्न (कल्पित) रूप हों अथवा बाहर फैले हुए (जगत् के वस्तुरूप) — इन समस्त रूपों में मुझे तुम्हारा ही अन्वय (तुम्हीं से उद्भव) दिखाई देता है।

--- verse 2 | section: Devīmayī ---
deva:
इति विचिन्त्य शिवे ! शमिताशिवे !
जगति जातमयत्नवशादिदम्।
स्तुतिजपार्चनचिन्तनवर्जिता
न खलु काचन कालकलास्ति मे॥
iast:
iti vicintya śive ! śamitāśive !
jagati jātamayatnavaśādidam |
stutijapārcanacintanavarjitā
na khalu kācana kālakalāsti me ||
en: Having thought thus, O Śivā, O you who have stilled all that is inauspicious (śamitāśive) — this has come about for me in the world effortlessly, of its own accord (ayatnavaśāt, without any deliberate exertion): there is truly not one particle of time (kālakalā, the smallest measurable fragment of a moment) of mine that is empty of praise, of japa, of worship and of contemplation. Since all speech is her body and all form her lineage, the four standard limbs of upāsanā — stuti, japa, arcana, cintana — are being performed continuously and without effort, and the ritual can no longer be begun or finished; this is why the verse stands as the volume's last word after the Saptaśatī recitation is formally over.
tel: శివా! అశుభమునంతటినీ శమింపజేసిన తల్లీ! ఇలా భావించగా — ఈ లోకంలో నాకు ఎటువంటి ప్రయత్నమూ లేకుండానే ఇది సిద్ధించినది: స్తుతి, జపము, అర్చన, ధ్యానము లేని కాలకల (క్షణంలోని అతిసూక్ష్మమైన అంశము) ఒక్కటైనా నాకు నిజముగా లేదు. సర్వశబ్దమూ ఆమె శరీరమే, సర్వరూపమూ ఆమె అన్వయమే అయినందున ఉపాసనలోని ఆ నాలుగు అంగాలూ ప్రయత్నం లేకుండానే నిరంతరం జరుగుతూనే ఉంటాయి; అందుకే సప్తశతీ పారాయణం ముగిసిన తరువాత గ్రంథానికి చివరిమాటగా ఈ శ్లోకం నిలిచింది.
hi: हे शिवे! हे समस्त अशुभ को शांत कर देने वाली! ऐसा विचार कर लेने पर — इस जगत् में यह मुझे बिना किसी प्रयत्न के ही स्वतः सिद्ध हो गया: स्तुति, जप, अर्चन और चिंतन से रहित मेरी एक भी कालकला (क्षण का सूक्ष्मतम अंश) वस्तुतः है ही नहीं। जब समस्त शब्द उन्हीं का शरीर और समस्त रूप उन्हीं का अन्वय है, तब उपासना के ये चारों अंग बिना प्रयत्न निरंतर चलते ही रहते हैं; इसीलिए सप्तशती-पाठ समाप्त हो जाने के बाद भी यह श्लोक ग्रंथ के अंतिम वचन के रूप में रखा गया है।
