Title: Navārṇa Vidhiḥ
Devanāgarī: नवार्णविधिः
Telugu: నవార్ణవిధిః

Author: traditional ritual manual (prayoga), not a composed stotra. The nine-syllable mantra itself ("oṁ aiṁ hrīṁ klīṁ cāmuṇḍāyai vicce") is the navārṇa/caṇḍī mantra of the Śākta tantras; its viniyoga assigns the three ṛṣis Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Rudra, the three metres Gāyatrī, Uṣṇik and Anuṣṭubh, and the three goddesses Mahākālī, Mahālakṣmī and Mahāsarasvatī. The three dhyāna verses embedded here are the standard Durgā Saptaśatī dhyāna-ślokas of the three caritras, printed in this edition with the reference marks (दु॰ न॰ १) and (दु॰ न॰ २). The connecting Hindi instructions, the explanatory paragraph on the purpose of nyāsa, and the footnote listing extended nyāsa systems are the Gītā Press editors' own prose, not traditional Sanskrit text.

Blurb: This is the short ritual manual that Gītā Press prints between the Devyatharvaśīrṣa and the Saptaśatī-nyāsa. It tells the reciter how to prepare for japa of the nine-syllable navārṇa mantra, 'oṁ aiṁ hrīṁ klīṁ cāmuṇḍāyai vicce'. The viniyoga is recited while pouring water. Then six nyāsas place the syllables of the mantra in turn upon the limbs of the ṛṣi, the fingers, the aṅgas, the face and the ten directions. Next come the three dhyānas of the three caritras, for Mahākālī, Mahālakṣmī and Mahāsarasvatī. The rosary is then honoured, the mantra is recited one hundred and eight times, and the merit of the japa is offered into the left hand of the Goddess. The Gītā Press setting adds its own help for the reader. Each nyāsa mantra carries a short Hindi note in brackets describing the exact hand movement. A paragraph is inserted explaining why nyāsa is done at all: the reciter's body becomes the body of the mantra, and is therefore guarded by it. A footnote states plainly that the sequence given here is the shortened form in current use, and it lists eight longer nyāsa systems for those who want them.

en: Having in this way recited the Rātri Sūkta and the Devyatharvaśīrṣa, one should now perform, in the manner set out below, the viniyoga, the nyāsa and the dhyāna of the navārṇa mantra. "Victory to Śrī Gaṇapati. Oṁ. Of this Śrī Navārṇa mantra the ṛṣis are Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Rudra; the metres are Gāyatrī, Uṣṇik and Anuṣṭubh; the deities are Śrī Mahākālī, Mahālakṣmī and Mahāsarasvatī; aiṁ is the seed (bīja), hrīṁ the power (śakti), klīṁ the pin that fastens it (kīlaka); the application is in japa, for the pleasure of Śrī Mahākālī, Mahālakṣmī and Mahāsarasvatī." Having read this out, let the water held in the palm be poured away — the standard act by which a viniyoga is sealed. Then, pronouncing the nyāsa sentences given below one by one, let him touch with the fingers of the right hand, in order, the head, the mouth, the heart, the anus, both feet and the navel.

करन्यासः
करन्यासमें हाथकी विभिन्न अँगुलियों, हथेलियों और हाथके पृष्ठभागमें मन्त्रोंका न्यास (स्थापन) किया जाता है। इसी प्रकार अङ्गन्यासमें हृदयादि अङ्गोंमें मन्त्रोंकी स्थापना होती है। मन्त्रोंको चेतन और मूर्तिमान् मानकर उन-उन अङ्गोंका नाम लेकर उन मन्त्रमय देवताओंका ही स्पर्श और वन्दन किया जाता है, ऐसा करनेसे पाठ या जप करनेवाला स्वयं मन्त्रमय होकर मन्त्रदेवताओंद्वारा सर्वथा सुरक्षित हो जाता है। उसके बाहर-भीतरकी शुद्धि होती है, दिव्य बल प्राप्त होता है और साधना निर्विघ्नतापूर्वक पूर्ण तथा परम लाभदायक होती है।

In karanyāsa the mantras are set down (nyāsa, literally "placing") on the several fingers, on the palms and on the backs of the hands; in the same way, in aṅganyāsa they are set down on the heart and the other limbs. The mantras are treated as conscious and embodied beings: one names each limb and thereby touches and salutes those very deities who are the mantras. By doing so, the one who recites or does japa becomes himself mantra-bodied and is thus wholly guarded by the mantra-deities. His outer and inner purity is accomplished, divine strength is obtained, and the practice is carried through without obstruction to its completion and to its highest benefit.
