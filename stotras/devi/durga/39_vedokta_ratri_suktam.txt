Title: Vedokta Rātri Sūktam
Devanāgarī: वेदोक्तं रात्रिसूक्तम्
Telugu: వేదోక్త రాత్రి సూక్తం

Author: Vedic (apauruṣeya); Ṛgveda 10.127, the eight-ṛc hymn to the goddess Rātri (Night). The viniyoga line printed with it names the ṛṣi as Kuśika Saubhara — or, alternatively, Rātri herself, or Bhāradvāja — the devatā as Rātri and the chandas as Gāyatrī, with the viniyoga being recitation in the Devīmāhātmya pāṭha. The Gītā Press footnote to the heading gives the Vedic reference explicitly: "ऋग्वेद॰ मं॰ १० सू॰ १२७ मन्त्र १ से ८ तक" (Ṛgveda maṇḍala 10, sūkta 127, mantras 1 to 8).

Blurb: This is the Vedic one of the two Rātri Sūktas recited before a reading of the Durgā Saptaśatī. It is the whole of Ṛgveda 10.127, being eight ṛcs in the gāyatrī metre addressed to Rātri, who is Night herself. The hymn does not treat night as mere darkness. It praises her as a shining goddess who looks out with her many eyes, which are the stars, and who puts the whole world to rest: the villages, the four-footed animals, the birds in their roosts, and even the hawk that seeks its prey. The hymn is a prayer for safe passage through the night. It asks her to keep away the wolf and the she-wolf, to keep away the thief, and to be easy to cross. At the end it asks Rātri, the daughter of heaven, to accept the singer's praise, as a cowherd accepts cattle given into his care. Gītā Press prints this hymn immediately before the Tantrokta Rātri Sūkta, which is Devī Māhātmyam 1.68 to 1.83. Its introduction describes the two together as the vaidika and the tāntrika pair. Unlike the tāntrika hymn, this one has its own separate viniyoga, which is given here in full.

en: Oṁ. Of this eight-ṛc sūkta beginning with the word "rātrī," the seer (ṛṣi) is Kuśika Saubhara — or else Rātri herself, or Bhāradvāja; the deity (devatā) addressed is Rātri, Night; the metre (chandas) is gāyatrī; and the ritual application (viniyoga) is recitation within the Devīmāhātmya pāṭha. Such a viniyoga line is spoken before recitation while touching the head, heart and so on, and it is precisely what the Tantrokta Rātri Sūkta lacks — that hymn, being narrative verse from the Devī Māhātmyam itself, falls under the viniyoga stated once for the whole prathama caritra.
