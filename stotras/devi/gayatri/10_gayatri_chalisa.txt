Title: Śrī Gāyatrī Cālīsā
Devanāgarī: श्री गायत्री चालीसा
Telugu: శ్రీ గాయత్రీ చాలీసా

Author: Anonymous; a modern devotional composition of the North Indian cālīsā genre, widely circulated through the Gāyatrī Parivār movement, with no early manuscript attribution

Source / recension: stotranidhi.com — https://stotranidhi.com/hi/sri-gayatri-chalisa-in-hindi/ (Devanāgarī), https://stotranidhi.com/en/sri-gayatri-chalisa-in-english/ (IAST). The two pages were compared line by line across all forty-three units and agree throughout. The stotranidhi IAST page uses ē/ō and r̥; these are normalized here to plain e/o and precomposed ṛ per the conventions of this corpus.

LANGUAGE FLAG — this text is NOT Sanskrit. It is Hindi, in the Khaṛī Bolī of the cālīsā tradition with a heavy overlay of Braj and Avadhī forms (tumharī, huyi jāī, so soī, jāpara / tāpara, lahai, baḍabhāgī, nahi āve). Its metres are the dohā and the caupāī of Hindi prosody, not Sanskrit śloka. Sanskrit words appear throughout as loanwords (bhūrbhuvaḥ svaḥ, aṣṭa siddhi, kālarātri) but the grammar around them is Hindi, and the verb endings in -ai / -e / -āve are Hindi optatives, not Sanskrit forms. Readers should not parse these lines by Sanskrit rules. Note also that the sourced text preserves two nonstandard spellings — śukhadāī for sukhadāī (v. 33) and dūḥkha for duḥkha (v. 29) — which are reproduced here rather than silently corrected; see the recension note.

Verse count: 43

Sections:
दोहा — Dohā, the two opening couplets (vv. 1–2)
चौपाई — Caupāī, the forty verses of praise that give the poem its name (vv. 3–42; these carry the text's own numbering 1–40)
दोहा — Dohā, the closing couplet and phalaśruti (v. 43)

Blurb: This is the one text in this folder that is not in Sanskrit. It is a Hindi cālīsā of forty caupāīs, bracketed by dohās. It is built on the same pattern as the Hanumān Cālīsā, and it belongs to the modern popular devotional stream rather than to the purāṇic or tantric one that produced the kavacas and aṣṭakas here. What it lacks in age it gains in reach. This is the Gāyatrī as the twentieth century sang her, and its concerns are accordingly domestic and concrete. It makes the expected identifications with Sarasvatī, Lakṣmī and Kālī. Verse 22 gives a fine image: the seeker who knows her becomes her, as base metal is transformed at the touch of the philosopher's stone. The poem then works steadily through a list of ordinary troubles: poverty, illness, childlessness, affliction by the planets, and the fear of death. Unusually, it then addresses the married woman, the unmarried girl and the widow in three consecutive lines, each with her own particular petition.

--- verse 1 | section: Dohā ---
deva:
ह्रीं श्रीं क्लीं मेधा प्रभा
जीवन ज्योति प्रचंड ।
शांति कांति जागृति प्रगति
रचना शक्ति अखंड ॥
iast:
hrīṁ śrīṁ klīṁ medhā prabhā
jīvana jyoti pracaṁḍa |
śāṁti kāṁti jāgṛti pragati
racanā śakti akhaṁḍa ||
en: Hrīṁ, Śrīṁ, Klīṁ — you are intelligence and radiance, the blazing light of life; peace, lustre, awakening and progress; the unbroken power that creates.
tel: హ్రీం శ్రీం క్లీం — నీవే మేధ, నీవే ప్రభ, జీవనజ్యోతివి, ప్రచండమైనదానవు; శాంతి, కాంతి, జాగృతి, ప్రగతివి; సృష్టి చేయు అఖండ శక్తివి.
hi: ह्रीं श्रीं क्लीं — तुम्हीं मेधा हो, प्रभा हो, जीवन की प्रचंड ज्योति हो; शांति, कांति, जागृति और प्रगति हो; रचना करने वाली अखंड शक्ति हो।

--- verse 2 | section: Dohā ---
deva:

स्वाहा पूरन काम ॥
iast:
jagata jananī maṁgala karani
gāyatrī sukhadhāma |
praṇavo sāvitrī svadhā
svāhā pūrana kāma ||
en: Mother of the world, doer of all that is auspicious, Gāyatrī, abode of happiness; you are the Praṇava (Oṁ), you are Sāvitrī, Svadhā and Svāhā, the fulfiller of every desire.
tel: జగజ్జననీ, మంగళములను చేయునదానా, గాయత్రీ, సుఖధామా; నీవే ప్రణవము, నీవే సావిత్రి, స్వధా, స్వాహా, సమస్త కోరికలను పూర్తిచేయుదానవు.
hi: जगत की जननी, मंगल करने वाली, गायत्री, सुख का धाम; तुम्हीं प्रणव हो, तुम्हीं सावित्री, स्वधा और स्वाहा हो, समस्त कामनाओं को पूर्ण करने वाली हो।

--- verse 3 | section: Caupāī ---
deva:

Recension note: This is a modern printed text with no manuscript tradition behind it, and stotranidhi's Devanāgarī and IAST pages are two renderings of a single source; they were compared line by line and agree at every point, including the two nonstandard spellings preserved here. In verse 33 the sourced text reads śukhadāī (शुखदाई), an aspirated spelling of sukhadāī that appears to be a printing error carried through both pages; in verse 29 it reads dūḥkha (दूःख) with a long ū, likewise nonstandard for duḥkha. Both are reproduced as sourced rather than silently emended, since the aim here is to record what the recension actually prints. Copies of this Cālīsā circulating elsewhere differ chiefly in the opening dohās — some editions carry only one, others substitute a dohā beginning hrīṁ śrīṁ klīṁ with different second and third feet — and in the spelling of the Braj optatives, which are freely written -ai, -e or -ay across editions. The forty caupāīs themselves are stable in the copies consulted.
