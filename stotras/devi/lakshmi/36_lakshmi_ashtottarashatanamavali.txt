Author: Traditional; the source names no Purāṇa or composer. This is the widely recited list that opens `प्रकृत्यै विकृत्यै विद्यायै` and closes on `भुवनेश्वर्यै`, the nāmāvalī form of the Lakṣmī Aṣṭottaraśatanāma Stotram (`प्रकृतिं विकृतिं विद्यां …`).

Recension note: **The Smārtha nāmāvalī is followed; the sanskritdocuments witness (SD) is recorded, not adopted.** **THE LIST HAS 109 ENTRIES, NOT 108, and the print's tally is off by one.** The print marks every tenth name, and the mark "100" falls on `विष्णुपत्न्यै`, which is the 99th line; the list ends at the mark "110" on `भुवनेश्वर्यै`, which is the 109th. The extra entry is **no. 96 `मङ्गलायै` + no. 97 `देव्यै`**, which the print gives as two lines with two `నమః`, while SD has them as one name, `maṅgalā devyai` ("to the auspicious goddess"). Joined, they give SD's 108. The file keeps the print's two lines and reports 109. Other differences (Smārtha → SD): no. 30 `अनुग्रहपरायै` → `anugrahapradāyai`; no. 65 `पुष्ट्यै` → `puṣṭāyai` (SD's variant is `puṣṭyai`); no. 71 `तुष्ट्यै` → `tuṣṭāyai` (SD's variant is `tuṣṭyai`). SD's variant readings `nityapuṣṭyai` (no. 19) and `kāmāyai … kṣīrodasambhavāyai` (nos. 28–29) are not followed by the Smārtha print either. SD prefixes two dhyāna verses (`वन्दे पद्मकरां प्रसन्नवदनां …`, `सरसिजनिलये सरोजहस्ते …`); the Smārtha print has none, and none is added here. **Duplicates:** `दीप्तायै` (nos. 23 and 36) and `देव्यै` (nos. 50, 97 and 103). **Heading.** The print's title line reads `శ్రీ లక్ష్మ్యష్టోత్తర శతనామాశళిః నమః`, where `నామాశళిః` is a slip for `నామావళిః`; the title here is regularised, and nothing else is. **Prefix.** The source prints `ఓం` (ॐ) before the first name of each column; this corpus prints `श्री` for every nāmāvalī without exception (user, 2026-09-08). **Cross-reference:** `devi/lakshmi/34_lakshmi_sahasranama_stotram_brahmapurana.txt` and `35_…_skandapurana.txt` are Lakṣmī sahasranāmas, separate works; no Lakṣmī aṣṭottara stotram or nāmāvalī was found in the corpus.

Blurb: These are the one hundred and eight names that most households know by heart. They begin with nature itself, both primal and transformed. They go on to knowledge, faith and speech, and to the ritual calls svāhā and svadhā. Then comes a long bed of lotuses: she dwells in the lotus, holds the lotus, has lotus eyes, is born of the lotus, is scented like the lotus, and is garlanded with lotuses. The list ends by widening her out until she contains everything. She is the nine Durgās, she is Mahākālī, she is Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Śiva, and she is the queen of all the worlds.
