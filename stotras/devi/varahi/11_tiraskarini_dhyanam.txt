Title: Śrī Tiraskariṇī Dhyānam
Devanāgarī: श्री तिरस्करिणी ध्यानम्
Telugu: శ్రీ తిరస్కరిణీ ధ్యానం

Author: traditional; no ṛṣi/chandas/devatā viniyoga colophon is attached to this dhyānam in any of
the recensions consulted — it is transmitted as a bare set of six meditation verses, with no
framing ritual apparatus (no viniyoga, nyāsa, or phalaśruti) before or after them.

Source / recension: primary source stotranidhi.com, which publishes this text in five parallel
scripts (Telugu, Kannada, Tamil, Devanāgarī — served under its /hi/ path — and English/IAST) all
under the URL family stotranidhi.com/.../sri-tiraskarini-dhyanam-in-*. The Devanāgarī, Telugu-script,
and English/IAST editions were fetched directly (via curl with a browser user-agent, since the site
blocks plain WebFetch) and agree word-for-word with each other, so that three-script internal
agreement is the main cross-check achieved for this item. External cross-check: hariome.com carries
a page with the identical title, and its page metadata quotes the opening verse verbatim —
"muktakesim vivasanam sarvabharanabhusitam | svayonidarsanonmuhyatpasuvargam namamyaham || 1 ||" —
matching stotranidhi's text exactly. The text could NOT be located on vignanam.org (searched directly
and via its Vārāhī-stotra listing, which has Ādi Vārāhī Stotram, Vārāhī Sahasranāma, and Vārāhī
Aṣṭottaraśatanāmāvali but no Tiraskariṇī item) or in sanskritdocuments.org's doc_devii catalog (the
full alphabetical catalog was fetched and searched for "tiraskar"; no entry appears, though the same
catalog does carry the related kirātavārāhīstotram, already item 02 in this corpus) — so sourcing for
this item rests on the stotranidhi.com/hariome.com family alone. This is flagged as reduced
cross-checking beyond that one publisher lineage.
stotranidhi.com's Telugu-script page additionally carries a short unsigned Telugu editorial headnote
(not part of the verses themselves) identifying Tiraskariṇī as a powerful deity in the army (sainyam)
of Lalitā Parameśvarī, and glossing "tiraskariṇī" as "the power of concealment" that shields
sādhakas from hostile/uninitiated eyes. That headnote is drawn on below (Blurb, and this note) as
background, not keyed as a verse block. Note also, honestly: the six verses themselves never name
Vārāhī, and their iconography (sword, date-palm branch, pot, blue horse) is not the standard
boar-faced Vārāhī form — the tie to Vārāhī here is the publisher's own classification of the text
under "śrī vārāhī stotrāṇi" (Vārāhī-stotra group) plus the headnote's placement of Tiraskariṇī as a
guard-figure of the Goddess's retinue, not an explicit line inside the dhyānam naming Vārāhī.

Verse count: 6 numbered dhyāna verses (source numbers all six continuously, 1 through 6; no
unnumbered ritual material precedes or follows them in any recension consulted).

Sections:
ధ్యానం — Dhyānam (all six meditation verses; the sixth, in a different, longer meter than the
first five, functions as a closing benedictory/paripālana verse — "may she protect us")

Blurb: Six dhyāna verses that build Tiraskariṇī's form — loose hair, unclothed, four arms holding a
sword, a date-palm branch, and a water-pot, mounted on a dark-blue horse, three eyes rolling with
divine intoxication — around the single act that gives her her name: under the pretext of sleep
(nidrāmiṣeṇa) she makes the world tirobhava, veiled/concealed (v.4), so that the unawakened paśu is
kept from seeing what he is not yet fit to see; the source publishes the text within its Vārāhī
stotra group and an accompanying Telugu headnote places her in the Goddess's guard-retinue, matching
her role elsewhere in this corpus as the veiling gatekeeper-power at the threshold of Devī's assembly.

--- verse 1 | section: Dhyānam ---
deva:
मुक्तकेशीं विवसनां सर्वाभरणभूषिताम् ।
स्वयोनिदर्शनोन्मुह्यत्पशुवर्गां नमाम्यहम् ॥
iast:
muktakeśīṁ vivasanāṁ sarvābharaṇabhūṣitām |
svayonidarśanonmuhyatpaśuvargāṁ namāmyaham ||
en: I bow to her whose hair hangs loose and unbound (muktakeśī), who is unclothed (vivasanā), and
who is adorned with every ornament — who, by the very sight of her own yoni (a Kaula/Vāmācāra
iconographic emblem of the source of creation), throws the paśu-vargā, the "herd of paśus" (the
technical Śākta term for bound, unawakened souls, as opposed to the vīra and divya adept), into a
bewildered stupor.
tel: విరబోసిన కేశములు (ముక్తకేశీం) కలిగినట్టి, దిగంబరియైనట్టి (వివసనాం), సమస్త ఆభరణములతో అలంకరించబడినట్టి, తన యొక్క యోని ముద్రను దర్శింపజేసి పశుతుల్యులైన అజ్ఞానులను మోహింపజేసే (మురిపించే) దేవికి నేను నమస్కరిస్తున్నాను.
hi: जिनके केश खुले और बिखरे हुए हैं (मुक्तकेशी), जो वस्त्ररहित हैं (विवसना), जो समस्त आभूषणों से
अलंकृत हैं, तथा जो अपने स्वयं के योनि-चिह्न के दर्शनमात्र से पशु-तुल्य अज्ञानी जनों (पशुवर्ग) को
मोहित एवं स्तब्ध कर देती हैं — ऐसी देवी को मैं नमस्कार करता हूँ।

--- verse 2 | section: Dhyānam ---
deva:
श्यामवर्णां मदाघूर्णनेत्रत्रयां शिवाम् ।
कृष्णाम्बरां तथा खड्गं दधतीं च भुजद्वये ॥
iast:
śyāmavarṇāṁ madāghūrṇanetratrayāṁ śivām |
kṛṣṇāmbarāṁ tathā khaḍgaṁ dadhatīṁ ca bhujadvaye ||
en: [I meditate on her] who is dark-hued (śyāma), whose three eyes roll with mada (divine
intoxication), who is auspicious (śivā), who wears black garments, and who bears a sword in her
[first] pair of arms.
tel: నల్లని వర్ణము (శ్యామవర్ణాం) కలిగినట్టి, మదముచే (దైవీ ఆవేశం) తిరుగుతున్న మూడు కన్నులు కలిగినట్టి, మంగళప్రదమైనట్టి (శివామ్), నల్లని వస్త్రమును మరియు రెండు భుజములలో ఖడ్గమును ధరించిన దేవిని ధ్యానిస్తున్నాను.
hi: जो श्याम वर्ण की हैं, जिनके तीन नेत्र मद (दिव्य उन्माद) से घूर्णायमान हैं, जो कल्याणकारी (शिवा)
हैं, जो काले वस्त्र धारण करती हैं, तथा जो अपनी [प्रथम] दो भुजाओं में खड्ग धारण करती हैं — ऐसी देवी
का मैं ध्यान करता हूँ।

--- verse 3 | section: Dhyānam ---
deva:
द्वाभ्यां मनोहराभ्यां तु खर्जूरकुम्भधारिणीम् ।
नीलाश्वस्थां पुरोयन्तीं नीलाभरणभूषिताम् ॥
iast:
dvābhyāṁ manoharābhyāṁ tu kharjūrakumbhadhāriṇīm |
nīlāśvasthāṁ puroyantīṁ nīlābharaṇabhūṣitām ||
en: who, with her other two lovely arms, holds a date-palm branch (kharjūra) and a water-pot
(kumbha); who is seated upon a dark-blue horse, advancing forward; and who is adorned with
dark-blue (nīla) ornaments.
tel: మనోహరమైన తన రెండు చేతులతో ఖర్జూరము మరియు కుంభమును ధరించినట్టి, నీలి రంగు గుర్రముపై (నీలాశ్వస్థాం) కూర్చుని ముందుకు వెళ్తున్నట్టి, నీలి రంగు ఆభరణములతో ప్రకాశిస్తున్న దేవికి నమస్కారము.
hi: जो अपनी अन्य दो मनोहर भुजाओं से खर्जूर (खजूर की शाखा) और कुंभ धारण करती हैं, जो नीले वर्ण के अश्व
पर आरूढ़ होकर आगे बढ़ रही हैं, तथा जो नीले आभूषणों से सुशोभित हैं — ऐसी देवी को नमस्कार।

--- verse 4 | section: Dhyānam ---
deva:
नीलमाल्यादि वसनां नीलगन्धमनोहराम् ।
निद्रामिषेण भुवनं तिरोभवं प्रकुर्वन्तीम् ॥
iast:
nīlamālyādi vasanāṁ nīlagandhamanoharām |
nidrāmiṣeṇa bhuvanaṁ tirobhavaṁ prakurvantīm ||
en: who wears dark-blue garlands and the like, who is lovely with dark-blue sandal-paste
(nīla-gandha), and who, under the pretext of sleep (nidrā-miṣeṇa), makes the [whole] world become
tirobhava — hidden, veiled from view. This is the verse that carries her own name: "tiraskaraṇa,"
concealment, is exactly what "tirobhavaṁ prakurvantīm" describes her doing here.
tel: నీలి రంగు మాలలు మరియు వస్త్రములు ధరించినట్టి, మనోహరమైన నీల గంధమును పూసుకున్నట్టి, నిద్ర అనే నెపముతో (నిద్రామిషేణ) ఈ భువనమంతటినీ తన మాయలో కప్పివేసే (తిరోభవం) దేవిని ప్రార్థిస్తున్నాను.
hi: जो नीली मालाएँ और वस्त्र धारण करती हैं, जो नीले चंदन (गंध) से मनोहर हैं, तथा जो निद्रा के बहाने
(निद्रामिषेण) समस्त भुवन को तिरोहित/आवृत कर देती हैं — यहीं वह श्लोक है जिसमें उनका अपना नाम
("तिरस्करण", आवरण करना) साकार होता है — उन्हें मैं प्रणाम करता हूँ।

--- verse 5 | section: Dhyānam ---
deva:
खड्गायुधां भगवतीं भक्तपालनतत्पराम् ।
पशुनिर्मूलनोद्युक्तां पशुतर्जनमुद्रिकाम् ॥
iast:
khaḍgāyudhāṁ bhagavatīṁ bhaktapālanatatparām |
paśunirmūlanodyuktāṁ paśutarjanamudrikām ||
en: who bears the sword as her weapon, the Blessed Goddess (bhagavatī) intent on protecting her
devotees, who is engaged in uprooting paśu-nature (bound, unawakened ignorance in the aspirant),
and who displays the paśu-tarjanī-mudrā — the threatening/warding gesture aimed at the paśu, the
one not yet fit for the higher teaching.
tel: ఖడ్గమును ఆయుధముగా కలిగినట్టి, భగవతియైనట్టి, భక్తులను పాలించుటలో నిమగ్నమైనట్టి (తత్పరామ్), అజ్ఞానమును (పశుత్వమును) నిర్మూలించుటకు పూనుకున్నట్టి మరియు పశుత్వమును బెదిరించే ముద్రను (పశుతర్జనముద్రికామ్) ధరించిన దేవిని తలుచుకుంటున్నాను.
hi: जो खड्ग को आयुध रूप में धारण करती हैं, जो भगवती हैं, जो भक्तों के पालन में तत्पर हैं, जो
पशु-भाव (अज्ञान) के निर्मूलन में उद्यत हैं, तथा जो पशु को तर्जित करने वाली (धमकाने वाली) मुद्रा
धारण करती हैं — ऐसी देवी का मैं स्मरण करता हूँ।

--- verse 6 | section: Dhyānam ---
deva:
नीलं तुरङ्गमधिरुह्य विराजमाना
नीलांशुकाभरणमाल्य विलेपनाढ्या ।
निद्रा पटेन भुवनानि तिरोदधाना
खड्गायुधा भगवति परिपातु सा नः ॥
iast:
nīlaṁ turaṅgamadhiruhya virājamānā
nīlāṁśukābharaṇamālya vilepanāḍhyā |
nidrā paṭena bhuvanāni tirodadhānā
khaḍgāyudhā bhagavati paripātu sā naḥ ||
en: Resplendent, mounted upon a dark-blue horse; richly furnished with dark-blue garments,
ornaments, garlands, and unguents; veiling the worlds with the cloak (paṭa) of sleep; bearing the
sword as her weapon — may that Blessed Goddess protect us always.
tel: నీలి రంగు గుర్రమును అధిరోహించి విరాజిల్లుతున్నట్టి, నీలి వస్త్రములు-ఆభరణములు-మాలలు మరియు గంధముతో అలంకరించబడినట్టి, నిద్ర అనే ముసుగుతో (నిద్రా పటేన) లోకములను తన మరుగున దాచేట్టి, ఖడ్గధారిణియైన ఆ భగవతి మమ్మల్ని సర్వదా రక్షించుగాక (సా నః పరిపాతు).
hi: नीले अश्व पर आरूढ़ होकर सुशोभित होने वाली, नीले वस्त्रों-आभूषणों-मालाओं तथा अंगराग से समृद्ध,
निद्रा रूपी वस्त्र (पट) से समस्त भुवनों को तिरोहित करने वाली, खड्गायुधा भगवती — वे हमारी सदैव रक्षा
करें।

Transliteration note: stotranidhi's English/IAST page uses its own "readable roman" house style with
e and o (e.g. "muktakeśīṁ," "stotranidhi"); per this corpus's convention that Sanskrit iast uses
plain e and o only, all instances of ē/ō have been normalized to e/o here, and the iast lines were
independently re-derived from the Devanāgarī rather than copied as-is from that page. Anusvāra is
given as ṁ throughout, matching both the source's own usage and this corpus's convention.
Doctrinal note: paśu (verses 1 and 5) is kept as the technical Śākta term for the bound, unawakened
soul/aspirant (as opposed to vīra and divya) rather than translated as a bare "animal" — see the en
field at each occurrence. tirobhava/tirobhavam (verse 4) and the closing tirodadhānā (verse 6) are
the verbs from which Tiraskariṇī's own name derives; this is flagged in the verse 4 en field rather
than left implicit. As noted in the header, the explicit identification of Tiraskariṇī as an aspect
of or attendant upon Vārāhī specifically (rather than the Goddess's retinue generally) is not made
within these six verses themselves — it rests on stotranidhi's own classification of the text under
its Vārāhī-stotra group and on the site's separate Telugu headnote (reproduced/drawn on above), which
places her in Lalitā Parameśvarī's guard-army without naming Vārāhī by name either. That headnote
itself was treated as background/paratext, not as part of the transmitted verses, and so was not
given its own verse block.
