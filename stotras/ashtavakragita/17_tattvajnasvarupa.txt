Blurb: Twenty verses on the man who knows, and the whole chapter is description rather than instruction — the fullest portrait of him in the work. He is content, his senses clear, and he takes pleasure in being alone. In the whole of this world he is never once wearied, because this one circle of the universe is filled by him alone. Objects do not gladden him, as neem leaves do not gladden an elephant that has had its fill of sallakī shoots. Two verses in the middle list his activity in bare participles — seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, taking, speaking, walking — and say that through all of it he is free. The last section sets him against the two things that move anyone: he looks at a woman who desires him, and at his own death standing before him, and his mind is shaken by neither. The verse most often quoted is the fifth: in this world one finds men who want to enjoy and men who want to be free, but the great-hearted one who wants neither is rare.

--- verse 1 | section: What he has already got ---
deva:
तेन ज्ञानफलं प्राप्तं योगाभ्यासफलं तथा ।
तृप्तः स्वच्छेन्द्रियो नित्यमेकाकी रमते तु यः ॥
iast:
tena jñānaphalaṁ prāptaṁ yogābhyāsaphalaṁ tathā |
tṛptaḥ svacchendriyo nityamekākī ramate tu yaḥ ||
en: He has gained the fruit of knowledge and the fruit of the practice of yoga who is content, his senses clear, and who takes constant pleasure in being alone.
tel: తృప్తుడై, ఇంద్రియములు నిర్మలముగా ఉన్నవాడై, ఎల్లప్పుడును ఒంటిగా ఉండుటయందు ఆనందించువాడు — అతడే జ్ఞానఫలమును, యోగాభ్యాసఫలమును పొందినవాడు.
hi: जो तृप्त है, जिसकी इन्द्रियाँ स्वच्छ हैं, और जो नित्य एकाकी रहने में ही रमता है, उसी ने ज्ञान का फल और योगाभ्यास का फल पा लिया है।
