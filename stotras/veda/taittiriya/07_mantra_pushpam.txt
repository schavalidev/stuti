Title: Mantrapuṣpam (Taittirīya, Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda)
Devanāgarī: मन्त्रपुष्पम् (तैत्तिरीयम्)
Telugu: మంత్రపుష్పం (తైత్తిరీయం)

Author: No human author for the principal portion. Vedic — Taittirīya Āraṇyaka, prapāṭhaka 1, anuvāka 22, which the accented pāṭha edition numbers `०। १। २२। ७८` onward. The devatā is the Waters. The principal portion, Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 1.22, is units 1 to 8; the Āraṇyaka itself divides that passage into eight numbered sections, and the division here follows the sense, one for the flower of the waters and one for each of the seven abodes. The remaining ten units are the passages recited after it.

प्रश्नोत्तरम् — The question and the answer (verse 9)
राजाधिराजमन्त्रः — To the king of kings (verse 10)
ब्रह्मतत्त्वम् — That is Brahman (verses 11–13)
विष्णोः परमं पदम् — The highest step of Viṣṇu (verses 14–15)
गायत्र्यौ — The two Gāyatrīs (verses 16–17)
समर्पणम् — The offering given over (verse 18)

Blurb: Mantrapuṣpam is the flower of mantras. It is recited at the end of worship, when the flowers are offered, and it is the last thing said before the offering is given over. The principal passage teaches that the moon is the flower of the waters, and then that fire, wind, the sun, the moon, the stars, the rain cloud and the year are each in turn the abode of the waters, and the waters the abode of each of them. He who knows this, the passage says, gains flowers, children and cattle, and gains a place to stand. It closes by saying that one who knows the ship set firm in the waters stands firm himself. After it come the salutation to Kubera, the verses that name Brahman as the wind, the Self, truth and all that is, the two ṛcs on the highest step of Viṣṇu, and the Gāyatrīs of Viṣṇu and of Lakṣmī. The last verse says that as water fallen from the sky reaches the sea, so worship offered to any god reaches the one Lord.

--- verse 17 | section: गायत्र्यौ — The two Gāyatrīs ---
