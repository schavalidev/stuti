Title: Devī Sūktam (Ṛgveda 10.125, Śākala) — with Vedic accent
Devanāgarī: देवीसूक्तम् (ऋग्वेदः १०.१२५, शाकलम्)
Telugu: దేవీ సూక్తం (ఋగ్వేదం 10.125, శాకలం)

Author: No human author. Vedic — Ṛgveda 10.125, eight ṛcs, the Vāk Sūkta. The ṛṣikā is Vāgāmbhṛṇī, Vāk the daughter of the ṛṣi Ambhṛṇa, and she speaks the hymn in her own voice. The devatā is the ātman, the self that speaks as Vāk. The metre is triṣṭubh, with ṛc 2 in jagatī. The viniyoga recited before the hymn says the same, in the words `द्वितीयाया ऋच्चो जगती, शिष्टानां त्रिष्टुप् छन्दः`.

Recension / śākhā: **Ṛgveda, Śākala saṁhitā.** The accent printed below is Śākala accent, taken from Śākala sources only. **This matters more for this hymn than for most**, because the hymn is also printed, accented, in another tradition's form — see the note below on stotranidhi.

Source / recension: **This file exists to carry the accent, which the corpus's existing Devī Sūktam does not have.** See `devi/durga/42_rgvedokta_devi_suktam.txt`, which is the same eight ṛcs as the Gītā Press *Durgā Saptaśatī* prints them — without svara, because that volume prints the whole Saptaśatī unaccented. **Two witnesses were used here, both accented and both Śākala.** **(1) Base text: sa.wikisource.org, `ऋग्वेदः सूक्तं १०.१२५`** — the accented saṁhitā-pāṭha printed with Sāyaṇa's bhāṣya, with the accented pada-pāṭha of all eight ṛcs on the same page. **(2) sanskritdocuments.org, `doc_veda/r10.itx`**, category "veda, rigveda, svara" — the Aufrecht edition of 1877 as digitised by van Nooten and Holland, a European critical lineage entirely separate from the Indian printed edition above. **Result of the collation: the two agree letter for letter, and they agree on every accent mark**, allowing only for the two editions ordering a mark and a following anusvāra differently, which puts the accent on the same syllable either way. **No Gītā Press witness to the accent exists**, and archive.org holds no Gītā Press edition of the Ṛgveda saṁhitā.

Recension note: **stotranidhi.com's Devī Sūktam page was examined and deliberately not used, because the text it prints is not the Śākala text.** It differs from both Ṛgvedic witnesses in wording and in sandhi orthography at points that are easy to check: it writes `मया॒ सोऽअन्न॑मत्ति`, `वात॑ऽइव` and `अ॒प्स्वऽ॒१॒॑न्तः` with the `ोऽअ` sandhi spelling used in Śukla-Yajurveda printing, where both Ṛgvedic witnesses write `मया॒ सो अन्न॑मत्ति`, `वात॑ इव` and `अ॒प्स्व१॒॑न्तः`; it reads `श्रु॒णोति` where they read `शृ॒णोति`; it prints the kampa as `सुप्रा॒व्ये॒ ए॒ ३॒॑` where they print `सुप्रा॒व्ये॒३॒॑`; it opens with `ओं` and closes with a śānti; and it marks the double svarita `᳚` throughout, which neither Ṛgvedic witness uses anywhere in this hymn. That is a different recension, and under the svara rule in the repo-root `CLAUDE.md` its accents must not be laid on the Ṛgvedic text. **Its accents were not used, and not one mark in this file comes from it.** The same site's Rātri Sūktam page, by contrast, is the plain Śākala text and was used as a third witness in `veda/rigveda/02_ratri_suktam.txt`. **Compared with the existing file, letter for letter.** The two texts agree in every letter of all eight ṛcs, with three differences, none of them a difference of sense. **(1)** The Saptaśatī prefixes `ॐ` to ṛc 1. **(2)** The kampa numerals `३` in ṛc 2 and `१` in ṛc 7 — `सुप्रा॒व्ये॒३॒॑`, `अ॒प्स्व१॒॑न्तः` — are part of Ṛgvedic accent notation and mark an independent svarita, so a text printed without accent has no place for them. They are kept here in `deva:` as the base text prints them and omitted from `iast:`, following `pitr/04_pitr_suktam_rigveda.txt`. **(3)** In ṛc 3 the Saptaśatī prints `भूर्य्यावेशयन्तीम्` with the doubled `य्य` after `र्`, a Devanāgarī printing convention; both Ṛgvedic witnesses print `भूर्या॑वे॒शय॑न्तीम्`. **The hymn proper is printed here, and nothing else.** The Saptaśatī prints it inside a liturgical frame — a prose viniyoga and a dhyāna śloka of Durgā that is Purāṇic and not Vedic. Sections:
देवीसूक्तम् — Devī Sūktam (the eight ṛcs, ṚV 10.125.1–8)

Blurb: This is the hymn in which the Goddess speaks of herself. The ṛṣikā Vāgāmbhṛṇī utters it in the first person, as Vāk, speech itself, identified with the one self that holds up everything. She says that she moves with the Rudras, the Vasus, the Ādityas and all the gods, and that she carries Mitra and Varuṇa, Indra and Agni, and the two Aśvins. She gives wealth to the worshipper who offers the oblation. Whoever sees, breathes or hears, eats his food through her, and most of those who live beside her do not know her. Whomever she loves, she makes strong, and makes him a ṛṣi and a man of wisdom. She bends the bow of Rudra. Her birthplace is in the waters, and she spreads over all the worlds and touches the sky with her height. It is recited at the reading of the Devī Māhātmya, and it is the reason that text is read as the speech of the Goddess herself.

en: ṚV 10.125.8. I blow forth like the wind, taking hold of all the worlds. Beyond heaven, beyond this earth — so great have I become in my greatness.
tel: ఋగ్వేదం 10.125.8. వాయువువలె నేను వీచుచున్నాను, సమస్త భువనములను చేపట్టుచున్నాను. ద్యులోకమునకు ఆవల, ఈ భూమికి ఆవల — నా మహిమచేత నేను అంత గొప్పదానను అయితిని.
hi: ऋग्वेद 10.125.8. मैं वायु के समान बहती हूँ और सब भुवनों को धारण करती हूँ। द्युलोक से परे, इस पृथिवी से परे — अपनी महिमा से मैं इतनी बड़ी हो गयी हूँ।
