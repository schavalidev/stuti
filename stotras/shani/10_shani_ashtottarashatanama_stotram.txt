Title: Śrī Śani Aṣṭottaraśatanāma Stotram (Śanaiścara-stavarāja)
Devanāgarī: श्रीशनि-अष्टोत्तरशतनामस्तोत्रम् (शनैश्चरस्तवराजः)
Telugu: శ్రీ శని అష్టోత్తరశతనామ స్తోత్రం (శనైశ్చర స్తవరాజః)

Author: The colophon places it in the **Bhaviṣya Mahāpurāṇa**, as the **Śanaiścara-stavarāja spoken by Nārada**: Nārada tells how **Yudhiṣṭhira**, after meditating on Gaṇapati, composed the hymn, and how Śani appeared and granted him a boon (v. 26). The composer within the frame is therefore Yudhiṣṭhira; the narrator Nārada. The Purāṇa passage itself was not checked in any edition.

Source / recension: Primary text: **Gītā Press, Gorakhpur — Śatanāma Stotra Saṅgraha**, code 1850, pp. 95–99 (stotra pp. 95–97, nāmāvalī pp. 98–99; PDF pp. 96–100), transcribed from the page images of the printed edition. Entry 17 of the volume's forty. **Collated against sanskritdocuments.org `shanaishcharastava.itx`** ("Śanaiścarastavarājaḥ", with its own variant notes) — an independent digital witness of the whole text, same 26 verses in the same numbering, same Bhaviṣya Purāṇa colophon. Also compared against this corpus's own `shani/06_shanaischara_raksha_stavah.txt` (from stotranidhi), which is **the first five and a half verses of this same text** — see the cross-reference below. sanskritdocuments' `shani108nAmastotra` and `shani108` were checked and are **a different work** (a `namo namaḥ` hymn beginning `śanaiścarāya śāntāya`), not witnesses here. Stotra and nāmāvalī are paired in one file as the publisher prints them.

Recension note: **Gītā Press followed; the sanskritdocuments witness (SD) is recorded, not adopted.** Differences (Gītā Press → SD): v. 2 `भास्करः` → `bhAskariH` ("son of the Sun", which `shani/06` also reads); v. 3 `वलिमुखो` → `balimukho`; v. 9 `खञ्जस्तप्तः` → `kha.njo tR^iptaH` (SD's variant: `atR^iptaH`); v. 11 `कुशी` → `shashI`; v. 12 `राज्यहा` → `rAjyabhug` (SD's variant: `rAjyesho`); v. 22 `सौरेस्तदा` → `saurau tadA`; v. 23 `बाधा चान्यग्रहाणां` → `bAdhA yA.anyagrahANAM`; v. 26 `प्रत्यक्षोऽभूच्छनैश्चरः … वरं` → `pratyakSho.abhUt shanaishcharaH … varaH`. SD agrees with Gītā Press that **v. 25 is a single line** (`पुत्रवान्धनवान् श्रीमाञ्जायते नात्र संशयः`), so the monostich is not a transcription slip. **Structure.** The text is a kavaca and a nāma-stotra fused: vv. 1–5 are a limb-by-limb *rakṣā* (head, brow, eyes, ears, nose, mouth, shoulders, arms, heart, navel, waist, feet), v. 6 closes the rakṣā's phala in its first line and opens the names with `ॐ` in its second, and the names run to v. 19. The names inside the rakṣā (`भास्कर`, `भीषण`, etc.) are **not** in the nāmāvalī, which starts at v. 6 `शौरि`. **THE NĀMĀVALĪ RUNS TO 111, NOT 108**, numbered 1–111 against the text's `अष्टोत्तरशतं` (v. 19). **`कर्त्रे` appears twice** (nos. 61 and 99, from `कर्ता` in vv. 12 and 17), so 110 are distinct. **One name is split where the verse has a compound:** v. 16 `शतभिद्रुक्षदयिता` ("beloved of the asterism Śatabhiṣaj") becomes no. 92 `शतभिदे` and no. 93 `रुक्षदयितायै` — the second a feminine dative; the verse is translated by the compound's sense, the list is kept as printed. **Stotra and nāmāvalī diverge once:** v. 13 `सर्वकर्मावरोधकः`, no. 71 `सर्वकर्मविरोधकाय`; both kept. v. 12 `राज्यहा` appears in the list as no. 64 `राज्यघ्ने`, the regular dative. **The planets and gods are named as Śani** — Ravi, Śaśī, Kuja, Budha, Guru, Kāvya, Siṁhikāsuta (Rāhu), Ketu, Viṣṇu, Hara, Gaṇapati, Kumāra, Kubera — in the manner of the Sūrya list in entry 15. **Prefix.** Gītā Press prints `ॐ` before each name; this corpus prints `श्री` for every nāmāvalī without exception (user, 2026-09-08). **Cross-reference: `shani/06_shanaischara_raksha_stavah.txt` is vv. 1–6a of this text** (the rakṣā, ending at `नात्र संशयः`), written from stotranidhi by another session. It reads `भास्करिः`, `फालं` and `बलिमुखो` where Gītā Press has `भास्करः`, `भालं` and `वलिमुखो`. Neither file supersedes the other; both are kept, and the overlap is logged in `DEDUP_AUDIT.md`.

Blurb: Yudhiṣṭhira in exile, remembering Gaṇapati first, makes a hymn to the planet everyone fears. It opens as armour — the Sun's son over the head, the hollow-eyed one over the eyes, the slow walker over the feet — and then gives Śani's portrait without flattery: withered belly, fleshless limbs, long beard, lame, hard to look at, the time of Time. Then it claims every other planet and god for him, and ends with instructions for the bad years: Saturdays, the daśā, śamī flowers, an image made of iron.
