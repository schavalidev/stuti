Blurb: A Madurai hymn, spoken by Patañjali within the Hālāsya Māhātmya, to Śiva as Sundareśvara — the lord of the Silver Hall beside the Golden Lotus tank, husband of the fish-eyed Mīnākṣī. Eight long verses in pañcacāmara close on the same line, "sadā namaḥ śivāya te sadāśivāya śambhave," and each packs in a whole episode by compound alone rather than narration: Viṣṇu one lotus short in his worship and making up the count from his own thousand eyes, and receiving the sun-bright discus in return (v. 5); the Tripura chariot with the earth for its car, Meru for the bow, a serpent for the bowstring, Brahmā for the charioteer and the Vedas for horses (v. 6); Vīrabhadra's lion-roar scattering the guests at Dakṣa's sacrifice (v. 7); Yama's staff flung aside to save the boy Mārkaṇḍeya (v. 8). Two closing verses in a shorter meter name the place plainly — Hālāsya, the Silver Hall, Mīnākṣī's husband, the lord of the beautiful dance.

tel: మృకండు కుమారుని (మార్కండేయుని) రక్షించుటకై దండపాణిని (యముని) త్రోసివేసినవానికి; సుందరమైన చెక్కిళ్ళనుండి వెలువడు కాంతిచే అమృతకిరణుని (చంద్రుని) జయించినవానికి; అఖండమైన భోగసంపదల కొరకు లోకముచే ధ్యానింపబడు ఆత్మ కలవానికి — ఓ సదాశివా, శంభూ, నీకు ఎల్లప్పుడు నమః శివాయ.
hi: मृकंडु के पुत्र (मार्कंडेय) की रक्षा के लिए दंडपाणि (यम) को दूर हटा देने वाले को; सुंदर कपोलों से फूटती प्रभा से अमृतांशु (चंद्र) को जीत लेने वाले को; अखंड भोग-संपदा के लिए लोक द्वारा जिनके आत्मस्वरूप का ध्यान किया जाता है — हे सदाशिव, शंभो, तुम्हें सदा नमः शिवाय।

Recension note: The stotranidhi IAST and Devanāgarī editions were compared verse-by-verse and are identical throughout, including the speaker-line "patañjaliruvāca" and the colophon. Verses 4, 5, 7 and 9 carry word-splits across line breaks in the printed layout; those splits have been preserved in the line arrangement above. Verses 1–8 are in pañcacāmara and share the refrain line; verses 9 and 10 shift to a shorter meter and drop the refrain, a shift present identically in both sources. Verse 2's three eyes are read here as sun, moon and fire, taking "paṅkajāsuhṛt" as paṅkaja-asuhṛt ("foe of the day-lotus," the moon) and "kṛpīṭayoni" as a standard epithet of Agni. Verse 6 compresses the whole Tripura-dahana chariot into compounds; the readings given follow the standard purāṇic assignment of earth, Meru, serpent, Viṣṇu-as-arrow, Brahmā-as-charioteer and Vedas-as-horses. Hālāsya is the Sanskrit name of Madurai, and the Silver Hall (rajatasabhā) of verse 9 is its Veḷḷiyambalam.
