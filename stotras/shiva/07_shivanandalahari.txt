Blurb: Traditionally the most intimate of Sankara's Siva hymns, Sivanandalahari reads less like formal praise-poetry and more like a running, confessional dialogue between a restless devotee and his lord -- self-scolding one moment (the fickle "monkey-mind" of v.20, the "thief-mind" of v.22), tenderly playful the next (the meditations on Siva's soft dancing feet in vv.79-80, 95). Its closing third turns to the four states of liberation-through-devotion (sarupya, samipya, salokya, sayujya, v.28) and to vivid images of bhakti itself as a mother, a cow, a creeper, a swan on a lotus lake -- making it as much a meditation on the nature of devotion as a hymn to Siva.
