Blurb: Two short stotras built on the six syllables of the Śiva Pañcākṣara mantra prefixed with Oṁ -- oṁ, na, ma, śi, vā, ya -- each verse praising Śiva through one syllable and closing "namo namaḥ" to that syllable. The Rudrayāmala recension (7 verses) is the simpler, older-feeling praise; the Śaṅkarācārya recension (10 verses) elaborates the same six syllables with richer epithets, adds a vivid verse recalling Śiva swallowing the Halāhala poison at the churning of the ocean, and closes with two famous verses -- one on Śiva's name as expiation for all deeds done and undone, the other the well-loved refrain "śivaśiveti śiveti śiveti vā... bhajamanaḥ śivameva nirantaram" ("say Śiva, Śiva -- just Śiva -- O mind, worship only Śiva, unceasingly").

Yogis ever meditate on Oṁkāra joined with the bindu (the dot of nasal resonance), the giver of desires and of liberation both -- salutations again and again to that syllable Oṁ.

Whoever recites this six-syllable hymn in Śiva's presence has no fear of death -- how then could there be any fear of untimely death?

iast:

en:
Whatever ought to have been done and was left undone, whatever ought not to have been done and yet was done as if it should be -- for both, O Śiva, the atonement is simply the utterance of the two syllables of your name.
