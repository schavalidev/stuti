Title: Pitṛ Stotram (Ruci-kṛtam)
Devanāgarī: पितृस्तोत्रम् (रुचिकृतम्)
Telugu: పితృ స్తోత్రం (రుచి కృతం)

Author: Prajāpati Ruci, in the narrative of Garuḍa Purāṇa, Ācāra-kāṇḍa, adhyāya 89 (GRETIL numbering 1.89.13–48), told by Mārkaṇḍeya to Krauñcuki. Having been told by Brahmā to marry and to win his forefathers' favour, Ruci does pitṛ-tarpaṇa on a secluded river bank (89.11) and praises them "with these hymns" (`स्तवैरेभिः`, 89.12).

Source / recension: **No Gītā Press witness was found for this text.** The Gītā Press *Antya-karma Śrāddha-prakāśa*, which prints the second half of the same passage as the Rucistava (`pitr/01_rucistava.txt`), does not print this half; its OCR was searched and the matching page image checked. So this file stands on three witnesses, none of them Gītā Press: (1) **GRETIL, Garuḍa Purāṇa 1.89.13–48** — a scholarly digital edition and the highest-ranking witness available, so it governs where the witnesses disagree; (2) **Ṛṣipīṭham** (Telugu monthly), September 2014, pp. 12–13, Telugu script, supplied by the user as a PDF and read from the page images — an independent printed witness; (3) **stotranidhi.com** ("Pitṛ Stotram 1 (Ruci kṛtam)"), used as the Devanāgarī base text because it is well formed and GRETIL is in IAST. The base was then collated verse by verse against GRETIL by machine (both reduced to one transliteration) and against Ṛṣipīṭham by eye at the disputed places.

Recension note: **Where the witnesses disagree in sense, GRETIL + Ṛṣipīṭham outrank stotranidhi, and every such change to the stotranidhi base is listed here.** **(1) v. 1** `अधिदेवतम्` (GRETIL; Ṛṣipīṭham `అధిదైవతమ్`, the same accusative) for stotranidhi's nominative `अधिदेवताः`. **(2) v. 4** — three witnesses, three readings: stotranidhi `वाञ्छद्भिरृद्धिर्यात्यन्तिकीं`, GRETIL `vādhadbhiḥ ṛddhimātyantikīṁ`, Ṛṣipīṭham `వాంఛద్భి బుద్ధిమాత్యంతికీం`. The printed text here, `वाञ्छद्भिरृद्धिमात्यन्तिकीं`, takes `वाञ्छद्भिः` from stotranidhi and Ṛṣipīṭham (GRETIL's `vādhadbhiḥ` gives no sense) and `-म् आत्यन्तिकीं` from GRETIL and Ṛṣipīṭham (the accusative object of "longing for"); **it is a composite, each element attested by two of the three, and is flagged as such.** Ṛṣipīṭham's `बुद्धिम्` for `ऋद्धिम्` is recorded, not adopted. **(3) v. 8** `संयतात्मभिः` (GRETIL, Ṛṣipīṭham) for stotranidhi's `सम्यतात्मभिः`, a spelling slip. **(4) v. 15** `ते वै प्रतीच्छन्तु मयोपनीतम्` (GRETIL; Ṛṣipīṭham `స్తే వై ప్రతీచ్ఛన్తు మయోప[నీ]తమ్`) for stotranidhi's `ते मे प्रतीच्छन्तु मनोपनीतम्`. **(5) vv. 28–29** `अग्निष्वात्ताः` with retroflex ṣ (GRETIL, Ṛṣipīṭham; the standard Vedic name) for stotranidhi's `अग्निस्वात्ता`. **(6) v. 36** `मदाहितात्` (GRETIL, Ṛṣipīṭham) for stotranidhi's `मदाहितम्`. **Kept against GRETIL, as keying slips in GRETIL** confirmed by the other two: `अशेषैः` (v. 9; GRETIL `aśeṣai`), `सन्तर्प्यन्ते` (v. 11; GRETIL `santarpyate`), `अश्नन्ति` (v. 26; GRETIL `aśranti`), `तथोग्रान्निर्नाशयन्तु` (v. 27; GRETIL `tathogrātrirṇāśayantu`), `अनघः` (v. 32; GRETIL `anghaḥ`), `क्षत्रियाणां` (v. 24; GRETIL `kṣattriyāṇāṁ`). **Ṛṣipīṭham lacks v. 6** (`नमस्येऽहं पितॄन् विप्रैः… प्राजापत्यप्रदायिनः`), which GRETIL and stotranidhi both carry; it is kept. **v. 21 `खड्गमांसेन`** is rhinoceros flesh, one of the classical śrāddha offerings of the smṛtis, and **`कालेन शाकेन`** is taken as the kāla-śāka herb, also a named śrāddha offering, rather than "in time, with vegetables"; both are translated literally rather than softened. **The thirty-one companies.** vv. 31–35 name them in groups of 9 + 6 + 7 + 5 + 4 = 31, and v. 36 gives that total — the arithmetic of the text checks. **Relation to the Rucistava.** This hymn is 89.13–48; Mārkaṇḍeya's narration then fills two verses (89.49–50: the column of light, Ruci on his knees), and the Rucistava `अर्चितानाममूर्त्तानां` follows at 89.51–60. Ṛṣipīṭham prints the whole run continuously under one title; stotranidhi and Gītā Press treat the second half as a separate stotra. **Both files are kept and cross-referenced** — see `pitr/01_rucistava.txt`. The Mārkaṇḍeya narration between them is not reproduced in either. **Verse numbering** is this corpus's, 1–36 = GRETIL 89.13–48.

Verse count: 36

Sections:
नमस्येऽहम् — Namasye'ham, the forefathers as worshipped by each order of beings (vv. 1–14)
पितॄन्नमस्ये — Pitṝnnamasye, the forefathers where they dwell and what they receive, in upajāti (vv. 15–27)
पितृगणाः — Pitṛgaṇāḥ, the four guardian classes and the thirty-one companies (vv. 28–36)

Blurb: Before the Rucistava there stands this longer and stranger hymn. It works its way through creation, asking who feeds the forefathers. It asks of the gods and the great ṛṣis in heaven, of the siddhas and the guhyakas, of brāhmaṇas and forest ascetics, of kings, of vaiśyas and of śūdras, of the great asuras in Pātāla, and of the nāgas and serpents below them. It then turns to the forefathers themselves: in the rays of the moon, in the disc of the sun, eating through the bodies of the brāhmaṇas at the meal, and coloured differently for each varṇa. It ends by naming all thirty-one of their companies, and by sending the four great classes to guard the four directions.

hi: रुचि बोले — जो अधिदेवता रूप में निवास करते हैं और जिन्हें श्राद्धों में स्वधायुक्त अर्पणों से देवता भी तृप्त करते हैं, उन पितरों को मैं भक्तिपूर्वक नमस्कार करता हूँ।
