Title: Śrī Nṛsiṁhāṣṭakam – 2
Devanāgarī: श्री नृसिंहाष्टकम् – २
Telugu: శ్రీ నృసింహాష్టకం – 2

Author: Unattributed in the source; traditional.

Source / recension: stotranidhi.com, Devanāgarī page (hi/sri-narasimha-ashtakam-2-in-sanskrit) and IAST page (en/sri-narasimha-ashtakam-2-in-english), fetched and cross-checked verse by verse against each other; the two scripts agree exactly, with no variant readings. The site's r̥ has been normalised to precomposed ṛ, and its ē/ō to plain e/o, per this corpus's conventions. This is the second aṣṭakam of that name on the site; the differently-worded Nṛsiṁhāṣṭakam "1" (śrīmadakalaṅka…) is already in this collection as file 07.

Verse count: 10

Sections:
ధ్యానం — Dhyāna, the meditation verse (v. 1)
స్తోత్రం — Stotram, the eight aṣṭakam verses (vv. 2–9)
నమస్కారం — Namaskāra, the closing salutation (v. 10)

Blurb: This aṣṭakam has eight stanzas, and they all close with the same line: 'śrīnārasiṁha paripālaya māṁ ca bhaktam', meaning 'O Śrīnārasiṁha, protect me too, your devotee'. The whole hymn therefore reads as one petition repeated eight times, from eight different angles. What is unusual is where it places the god's power. It places it not in the claws, but in the compassion that set Lakṣmī herself aside, in verse 2, in order to hide inside a pillar. Its last stanza, verse 9, quietly concedes that knowledge, good works and devotion have all served as roads to liberation, and that all of them work only by grace.

--- verse 1 | section: Dhyāna ---
deva:
ध्यायामि नारसिंहाख्यं ब्रह्मवेदान्तगोचरम् ।
भवाब्धितरणोपायं शङ्खचक्रधरं पदम् ॥
iast:
dhyāyāmi nārasiṁhākhyaṁ brahmavedāntagocaram |
bhavābdhitaraṇopāyaṁ śaṅkhacakradharaṁ padam ||
en: I meditate on that supreme state which bears the name Nārasiṁha — the one reached through knowledge of Brahman and the Vedānta, the very means of crossing the ocean of worldly existence, the bearer of conch and discus.
tel: నారసింహుడనే పేరుగల ఆ పరమపదాన్ని నేను ధ్యానిస్తున్నాను — బ్రహ్మవేదాంత జ్ఞానం ద్వారా తెలియబడేవాడు, భవసాగరాన్ని దాటించే ఉపాయమైనవాడు, శంఖచక్రాలను ధరించినవాడు.
hi: नारसिंह नामक उस परमपद का मैं ध्यान करता हूँ — जो ब्रह्म और वेदांत के ज्ञान से जाना जाता है, जो भवसागर से पार उतरने का उपाय है, जो शंख और चक्र धारण करता है।

--- verse 2 | section: Stotram ---
deva:
नीलां रमां च परिभूय कृपारसेन
स्तम्भे स्वशक्तिमनघां विनिधाय देवीम् ।
प्रह्लादरक्षणविधायवती कृपा ते
श्रीनारसिंह परिपालय मां च भक्तम् ॥
iast:
nīlāṁ ramāṁ ca paribhūya kṛpārasena
stambhe svaśaktimanaghāṁ vinidhāya devīm |
prahlādarakṣaṇavidhāyavatī kṛpā te
śrīnārasiṁha paripālaya māṁ ca bhaktam ||
en: Setting aside even Nīlā and Ramā out of the sheer flood of your compassion, and placing your own stainless power, the Goddess, within the pillar — such was your grace, which accomplished the rescue of Prahlāda. O Śrīnārasiṁha, protect me too, your devotee.
tel: కృపారసంతో నీలాదేవిని, రమాదేవిని కూడా పక్కకు నెట్టి, నిర్మలమైన నీ స్వశక్తిని దేవిగా స్తంభంలో ఉంచి — ప్రహ్లాదరక్షణను సాధించిన నీ కృప అటువంటిది. శ్రీనారసింహా, నీ భక్తుడనైన నన్ను కూడా పరిపాలించుము.
hi: कृपा के प्रवाह से नीला और रमा को भी परे रखकर, अपनी निर्मल शक्ति देवी को स्तंभ में स्थापित करके — प्रह्लाद की रक्षा करने वाली तुम्हारी वह कृपा ऐसी थी। हे श्रीनारसिंह, मुझ भक्त की भी रक्षा करो।

namaste padmanetrāya namaste duḥkhahāriṇe ||
en: Salutation to you, Nārasiṁha; salutation to you, foe of Madhu; salutation to you, the lotus-eyed; salutation to you, remover of sorrow.
tel: నారసింహుడవైన నీకు నమస్కారం; మధువైరివైన నీకు నమస్కారం; పద్మనేత్రుడవైన నీకు నమస్కారం; దుఃఖాన్ని హరించే నీకు నమస్కారం.
hi: नारसिंह तुम्हें नमस्कार; मधु के शत्रु तुम्हें नमस्कार; कमलनयन तुम्हें नमस्कार; दुःख हरने वाले तुम्हें नमस्कार।

Recension note: Both stotranidhi.com pages (Devanāgarī and IAST) were fetched directly and compared line by line; they are the same recension with no divergences. Verse 3 is printed on the site with a hyphenated line break inside the compound kirīṭakoṭi-pratyupta-, retained here as a single hyphen at the line end. Verse 5's avagraha in vidrāvitā'khila- appears on the IAST page in the site's own ":'" notation and has been rendered as a plain avagraha here. Verse numbering in this file is sequential across the whole text; the source itself numbers only the eight stotram stanzas 1–8, leaving the opening dhyāna and the closing namaskāra unnumbered.
