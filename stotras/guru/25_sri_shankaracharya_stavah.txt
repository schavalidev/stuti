Title: Śrī Śaṅkarācārya Stavaḥ (Śrīśaṅkarācāryavaryaṁ)
Devanāgarī: श्री शङ्कराचार्य स्तवः (श्रीशङ्कराचार्यवर्यं)
Telugu: శ్రీ శంకరాచార్య స్తవః (శ్రీశంకరాచార్యవర్యం)

Author: Śrī Bhāratī Tīrtha Mahāsvāmi, Jagadguru Śaṅkarācārya of the Śṛṅgeri Śāradā Pīṭha, named both in the colophon and, in the traditional way, inside the closing verse itself ("śrībhāratītīrthagītaṁ śaṅkarāryastavaṁ"). A contemporary composition by a reigning pontiff in praise of the founder of his own lineage.

Source / recension: stotranidhi.com — Devanāgarī at https://stotranidhi.com/hi/sri-shankaracharya-varyam-in-sanskrit/, IAST at https://stotranidhi.com/en/sri-shankaracharya-varyam-in-english/ (direct WebFetch returned HTTP 403/redirect; pages retrieved via curl with a browser User-Agent per corpus convention). The two script versions were compared verse-for-verse and match exactly, with no variant readings. The English, Telugu and Hindi renderings below are original for this corpus.

Verse count: 6

Sections:
స్తవం — Stavaḥ, the praise verses (vv. 1–5)
ఫలశ్రుతి — Phalaśruti, the fruits of recitation, containing the composer's signature (v. 6)

Blurb: Short, formally elegant, and grammatically continuous — the entire hymn is a single sentence, since only the opening line carries a finite verb ("bhaje deśikendram," I worship that lord among teachers) and every verse that follows piles on further accusative epithets qualifying him. What distinguishes it from older Śaṅkara hymns is that it is written from inside the institution Śaṅkara founded: the fifth verse dwells on his residence at Śṛṅgeri, and the third on the worship of Candramaulīśvara, the crystal liṅga that the Śṛṅgeri Jagadgurus have venerated in unbroken succession. The composer, the present Jagadguru of that seat, signs it in the final verse.

--- verse 1 | section: Stavaḥ ---
deva:
श्रीशङ्कराचार्यवर्यं
सर्वलोकैकवन्द्यं भजे देशिकेन्द्रम् ।
धर्मप्रचारेऽतिदक्षं
योगिगोविन्दपादाप्तसन्यासदीक्षम् ।
दुर्वादिगर्वापनोदं
पद्मपादादिशिष्यालिसंसेव्यपादम् ॥
iast:
śrīśaṅkarācāryavaryaṁ
sarvalokaikavandyaṁ bhaje deśikendram |
dharmapracāre'tidakṣaṁ
yogigovindapādāptasanyāsadīkṣam |
durvādigarvāpanodaṁ
padmapādādiśiṣyālisaṁsevyapādam ||
en:
I worship that lord among teachers, the eminent Śrī Śaṅkarācārya, the one being whom all the worlds must revere — supremely skilled in the spreading of dharma; who received initiation into sannyāsa at the feet of the yogī Govinda; who scattered the pride of ill-arguing disputants; whose feet are served by the swarm of disciples beginning with Padmapāda.
tel:
సర్వలోకములచేత ఏకైకముగా వందనీయుడైన, దేశికేంద్రుడైన శ్రీశంకరాచార్యవర్యుని నేను భజించుచున్నాను — ధర్మప్రచారమునందు అతిదక్షుడు; యోగియైన గోవిందపాదులవలన సన్యాసదీక్షను పొందినవాడు; దుర్వాదుల గర్వమును పోగొట్టినవాడు; పద్మపాదుడు మొదలైన శిష్యసమూహముచేత సేవింపబడిన పాదములు కలవాడు.
hi:
समस्त लोकों के एकमात्र वंदनीय, देशिकेन्द्र श्रीशंकराचार्यवर्य का मैं भजन करता हूँ — जो धर्म-प्रचार में अत्यंत दक्ष हैं; जिन्होंने योगी गोविंदपाद से संन्यास-दीक्षा प्राप्त की; जिन्होंने दुर्वादियों का गर्व दूर किया; जिनके चरण पद्मपाद आदि शिष्य-समूह द्वारा सेवित हैं।

Colophon (unnumbered): iti śrīśrī bhāratītīrtha mahāsvāmi kṛta śrī śaṅkarācārya stavaḥ | — "Thus the Śrī Śaṅkarācārya Stavaḥ composed by Śrī Śrī Bhāratī Tīrtha Mahāsvāmi."

Recension note: Each verse is printed on the source pages as six short lines forming three couplets, each couplet running eight syllables and then thirteen. The whole hymn is syntactically one sentence: the only finite verb is bhaje in verse 1, and everything from "dharmapracāre'tidakṣaṁ" to the end of verse 5 consists of accusative epithets governed by it. Candramaulīśvara in verse 3 is the crystal liṅga traditionally held to have been given by Śiva to Ādi Śaṅkara and worshipped daily by the Jagadgurus of Śṛṅgeri. The compound ratnagarbhebhavaktrāṅghri in verse 5 is read here as ratnagarbha-ibhavaktra-aṅghri, "the feet of the elephant-faced Ratnagarbha [Gaṇapati]"; the reading is identical in both source scripts, but the epithet is uncommon and other splits of the compound are possible. The text prints सन्यास / sanyāsa in verse 1 rather than the usual sannyāsa; the source spelling has been kept.
