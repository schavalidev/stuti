Title: Śrī Rāghavendra Stotram
Devanāgarī: श्री राघवेन्द्र स्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ రాఘవేంద్ర స్తోత్రం

Author: Śrī Appaṇṇācārya (Appaṇācārya), named in the colophon and in the first of the supplementary verses. Tradition holds that he composed it extempore while hurrying toward Mantrālaya on learning that Rāghavendra Tīrtha was entering the vṛndāvana alive, and that he faltered at the last line until the voice from within the vṛndāvana completed it with "sākṣī hayāsyo'tra hi" — Hayagrīva himself is witness here.

Blurb: The central text of the Mantrālaya tradition, recited daily wherever Rāghavendra is worshipped. It opens with two verses of sustained allegory in which the guru's speech is a river — flowing from the ocean of Madhva's teaching, touching Śiva's head like the Gaṅgā, carrying paramahaṁsa swans on the waves of the two Mīmāṁsās, and infested with crocodiles that turn out to be the five Mādhva doctrines devouring the flocks of false disputants. From there it changes register repeatedly: metrically restless (four different metres in the first ten verses), it moves through portrait, petition, the eight-syllable mantra, a drowning man's cry in verse 22, and then ten verses of unusually specific promises — water charged with the hymn, one hundred and eight repetitions at an eclipse, a lamp lit beside the vṛndāvana. Verse 32 ends with the line tradition says the guru supplied himself.

Colophon (unnumbered): iti śrī appaṇṇācāryaviracitaṁ śrīrāghavendra stotraṁ sampūrṇam || — "Thus is completed the Śrī Rāghavendra Stotram composed by Śrī Appaṇṇācārya."

Recension note: The hymn changes metre repeatedly — vasantatilakā in verses 1, 2 and 9; upajāti in 3 through 7; śārdūlavikrīḍita in 8, 22 and 32; anuṣṭubh from verse 10 onward. Verses 15 and 21 are printed with three lines rather than two, and verses 20–21 and 26–27 each run as one sentence across the verse break; they have been kept as the source numbers them. The source omits the single daṇḍa at the end of the first line in verses 10 and 11, and closes the last supplementary verse with a single daṇḍa; these have been normalized. Verse 16 prints इत्यऽष्टाक्षर / itya'ṣṭākṣara with an anomalous avagraha, which is retained. The five supplementary verses are enclosed in the source within brackets under the label अधिकाश्लोकाः / adhikāślokāḥ, marking them as additions found in some traditions but not part of the numbered hymn; they are numbered 33–37 here for reference and their bracketed status is recorded by the section heading. The difficult compound कामारिमाक्षविषमाक्ष / kāmārimākṣaviṣamākṣa in verse 1 is read here as referring throughout to Śiva — kāmāri, the foe of Kāma, and viṣamākṣa, the god of the uneven number of eyes — following the standard gloss, though the intervening syllables resist a settled analysis. Verses 1–2 sustain a double reading in which the river of Rāghavendra's speech is simultaneously the Gaṅgā: its crocodiles are the five characteristic Mādhva doctrines, and its swans are the paramahaṁsa ascetics who ride the waves of Pūrva and Uttara Mīmāṁsā.
