Verse count: 9

Sections:
అష్టకం — Aṣṭakam, the eight verses of praise in drutavilambita metre (vv. 1–8)
ఫలశ్రుతి — Phalaśruti, the fruits of recitation, in anuṣṭubh (v. 9)

Blurb: Vyāsa is worshipped in this section as the ādi-guru, the first teacher of the lineage. This hymn takes the unusual course of praising him almost entirely through what he did, rather than through what he is. Each of the eight verses fixes on one concrete act. He divided the Veda, because he foresaw how little strength of mind people would have in the Kali age. He composed the Purāṇas. He strung together the Brahma Sūtras as a mirror, to reconcile the apparent contradictions of the Śruti. He granted Sañjaya the sight by which the war was narrated. In the quietest verse of the set, he showed the blind old king and his queen their fallen sons once more in the forest. The refrain never varies: 'guruvyāsam ahaṁ bhaje', meaning 'Guru Vyāsa I worship'.

Colophon (unnumbered): iti śrīparamahaṁsasvāmi brahmānandaviracitaṁ śrīvedavyāsāṣṭakam | — "Thus the Śrī Vedavyāsāṣṭakam composed by Śrī Paramahaṁsa Svāmi Brahmānanda."

Recension note: Verses 1–8 are in drutavilambita (twelve syllables to the pāda, na-bha-bha-ra), the ninth in anuṣṭubh, which is the usual pattern for an aṣṭaka with an appended phalaśruti. The episodes alluded to in verses 6, 7 and 8 are all from the Mahābhārata: Arjuna's ascent to Indra's heaven and acquisition of the divine weapons; Vyāsa granting Sañjaya divine sight so that he could narrate the war to Dhṛtarāṣṭra (Bhīṣma Parva); and the raising of the slain warriors from the Gaṅgā for Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Gāndhārī during their forest retirement (Āśramavāsika Parva). "Kuruvara" in verse 7 and "kurudampatī" in verse 8 are both Dhṛtarāṣṭra, with Gāndhārī in the latter. No variant readings were found between the two source pages.
