Title: Śrī Rāghavendra Aṣṭakam
Devanāgarī: श्री राघवेन्द्र अष्टकम्
Telugu: శ్రీ రాఘవేంద్ర అష్టకం

Author: Śrī Suvidyendra Tīrtha, named in the colophon (iti śrī suvidyendratīrtha viracita). The closing verse describes the hymn as "composed with devotion to the Guru by Vidyendra, [disciple] of the noble Suśamīndra," placing it within the recent succession of the Śrī Rāghavendra Svāmī Maṭha at Mantrālaya, where Śrī Suśamīndra Tīrtha was followed by Śrī Suvidyendra Tīrtha. It is therefore a modern composition, not a medieval one.

Verse count: 9

Sections:
అష్టకం — Aṣṭakam, the eight verses of praise (vv. 1–8)
కర్తృవాక్యం — Kartṛvākyam, the composer's signature verse (v. 9)

Blurb: This is a short hymn, deliberately song-like, in a light lilting metre rather than the heavy śārdūlavikrīḍita of the older Mantrālaya hymns. Nearly every line ends on the same open -e sound, so that rhyme carries the whole piece. It divides cleanly in two. The first three verses address Rāghavendra directly and ask for something: show me compassion, grant me divine sight, take up residence in my mind. Verses four to six drop the address and simply place image after image before the reciter: the moon of Timmaṇṇa's line, molten gold, the staff and the water-pot, the ochre cloth, the tulasī beads. Each of these closes with the same wish, that the reciter's heart may find its joy there. The signature verse at the end is unusual for this kind of hymn, because it names both the composer and the composer's own guru.

Colophon (unnumbered): iti śrī suvidyendratīrtha viracita śrī rāghavendra aṣṭakam | — "Thus the Śrī Rāghavendra Aṣṭakam composed by Śrī Suvidyendra Tīrtha."

Recension note: Two small typographic matters in the source were normalized here. In verse 2 the printed text omits the single daṇḍa at the end of the first line in both script versions; it has been supplied, since the verse structure is otherwise identical to the rest of the hymn. In verse 9 both pages print a hyphen (सुशमीन्द्रार्यकुमारै-र्विद्येन्द्रैर्) purely as a line-break device; the word has been joined here. No substantive variant readings were found between the Devanāgarī and IAST pages. The hymn is not in a classical akṣara metre but in a loose mātrā-based devotional metre of the kind common in Kannada- and Marathi-region Mādhva song literature, which is why the -e rhyme runs almost unbroken through the first six verses. "Vaiṇika" in verse 8 refers to the hereditary vīṇā-playing family into which Rāghavendra Tīrtha was born; "Timmaṇṇa" in verse 4 is his father, Timmaṇṇa Bhaṭṭa.
