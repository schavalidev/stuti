Title: Śrī Guru Stotram (Guru Vandanam)
Devanāgarī: श्री गुरु स्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ గురు స్తోత్రం

Author: Anonymous / traditional. The verses are drawn from the Guru Gītā of the Skanda Purāṇa (Uttara Khaṇḍa, Umā-Maheśvara saṁvāda) and circulate as a compact independent stotra; no single named author is attached to the compilation. Verse 3 ("gururbrahmā gururviṣṇuḥ") and verse 2 ("ajñānatimirāndhasya") are among the most widely recited ślokas in the whole Hindu tradition, used as the opening invocation of countless texts and classes.

Blurb: This is the most recited of all hymns to the guru, and the one that people know without knowing that they know it. It has thirteen verses in the anuṣṭubh metre, each ending with the same three words: 'tasmai śrīgurave namaḥ', meaning 'salutation to that revered Guru'. What holds them together is not praise of a person but one sustained argument. The Guru is identified in turn with the pervading whole, with the eye-salve that opens sight, with Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Śiva, and at the last with Brahman itself. Verse 11 states the conclusion plainly: there is no principle higher than the Guru, no austerity higher, and nothing beyond the knowledge of that truth. The two closing verses drop the refrain and change register entirely. The first is a long, floating meditation on the Sadguru as the seer beyond the three guṇas. The second is the plainest possible surrender: you alone are mother and father to me.

en:

tel:

hi:

bhāvātītaṁ triguṇa-rahitaṁ sad-guruṁ taṁ namāmi ||
en:
I bow to that Sadguru who is the bliss of Brahman, the giver of the highest happiness, the absolute, whose very form is knowledge; who is beyond all pairs of opposites, like the sky, the one indicated by "That thou art" and the other great sayings; who is one, eternal, stainless, unmoving, the witness of every intellect, beyond all mental states, and free of the three guṇas.
tel:
బ్రహ్మానందస్వరూపుడు, పరమసుఖమును ఇచ్చువాడు, కేవలుడు, జ్ఞానమే మూర్తియైనవాడు, ద్వంద్వములకు అతీతుడు, ఆకాశమువంటివాడు, "తత్త్వమసి" మొదలైన మహావాక్యములకు లక్ష్యమైనవాడు, ఏకుడు, నిత్యుడు, విమలుడు, అచలుడు, సమస్త బుద్ధులకు సాక్షిభూతుడు, భావములకు అతీతుడు, త్రిగుణరహితుడు — ఆ సద్గురువునకు నేను నమస్కరించుచున్నాను.
hi:
जो ब्रह्मानंदस्वरूप, परम सुख के दाता, केवल, ज्ञानमूर्ति हैं; जो द्वंद्वों से अतीत, आकाश के समान, "तत्त्वमसि" आदि महावाक्यों के लक्ष्य हैं; जो एक, नित्य, विमल, अचल, समस्त बुद्धियों के साक्षी, भावों से परे और त्रिगुणरहित हैं — उन सद्गुरु को मैं नमस्कार करता हूँ।

--- verse 15 | section: Samarpaṇam ---
deva:
त्वमेव माता च पिता त्वमेव
त्वमेव बन्धुश्च सखा त्वमेव ।
त्वमेव विद्या द्रविणं त्वमेव
त्वमेव सर्वं मम देव-देव ॥
iast:
tvam-eva mātā ca pitā tvam-eva
tvam-eva bandhuś-ca sakhā tvam-eva |
tvam-eva vidyā draviṇaṁ tvam-eva
tvam-eva sarvaṁ mama deva-deva ||
en:
You alone are my mother, and you alone my father; you alone are my kinsman, and you alone my friend; you alone are my learning, and you alone my wealth; you alone are everything to me, O God of gods.
tel:
నీవే నాకు తల్లివి, నీవే తండ్రివి; నీవే బంధువువు, నీవే సఖుడవు; నీవే విద్యవు, నీవే ధనమువు; ఓ దేవదేవా, నీవే నాకు సర్వమూను.
hi:
तुम ही मेरी माता हो, तुम ही पिता; तुम ही बंधु हो, तुम ही सखा; तुम ही विद्या हो, तुम ही धन; हे देवदेव, तुम ही मेरे लिए सब कुछ हो।

---
Recension note: The verses are an anthology drawn from the Guru Gītā (Skanda Purāṇa, Uttara Khaṇḍa), and the number of verses printed under the title "Guru Stotram" varies widely between editions — some circulate a shorter set of eight or nine, others extend to twenty or more by pulling in further Guru Gītā ślokas. The fifteen verses given here follow the stotranidhi printed recension exactly, including the order. The Devanāgarī and IAST pages of that source were compared line by line and no variant readings were found. The closing verse "tvameva mātā ca pitā tvameva" also circulates independently as a universal prayer of surrender addressed to Viṣṇu or to the iṣṭadevatā; in this stotra it is directed to the Guru as Devadeva.
