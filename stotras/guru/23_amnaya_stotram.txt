Blurb: This is less a hymn of praise than the constitution of the Advaita order set to verse. Ādi Śaṅkara is said to have founded four maṭhas at the four quarters. For each one he fixed a sacred site, a presiding deity, a Śakti, a tīrtha, a name for the sampradāya, a Veda, a mahāvākya, an initiatory title for its brahmacārins, and a founding ācārya from among his own four chief disciples. This text simply lists all of it, quarter by quarter, in plain anuṣṭubh. That is why its sentences run straight across the verse boundaries without pausing. To recite it is to recite the map of the tradition. Padmapāda is in the west, with tattvamasi and the Sāmaveda. Hastāmalaka is in the east, with prajñānaṁ brahma and the Ṛgveda. Toṭaka is in the north, with ayamātmā brahma and the Atharvaveda. Sureśvara is in the south, with ahaṁ brahmāsmi and the Yajurveda.

Colophon (unnumbered): ityāmnāyastotram | — "Thus the Āmnāya Stotram."

Recension note: The whole text is in anuṣṭubh, and its sentences run straight across the verse boundaries — the eastern seat begins in the second line of verse 7, the northern in the second line of verse 11, the southern in the second line of verse 14, and the summary in the second line of verse 20. The section headings above therefore mark the dominant subject of each verse rather than a clean break. Several readings in this recension diverge from the more familiar Maṭhāmnāya lists: the Dvārakā maṭha is called Śāradā Maṭha here (elsewhere Kālikā Maṭha, with Śāradā Pīṭha as an alternative name), the northern one Śrī Maṭha (elsewhere Jyotir Maṭha), and the goddess of the eastern seat is printed as Vṛṣalā Devī, where the standard listing has Vimalā Devī of Purī. All of these appear identically in both stotranidhi script versions and have been kept as printed. Verse 22 also prints अम्नाय / amnāya without the long ā, against the title itself; that too is retained. The ten daśanāmī titles are distributed across verses 4, 8, 13, 18 and 19 (Tīrtha, Āśrama; Araṇya, Vana; Giri, Parvata, Sāgara; Sarasvatī, Purī, Bhāratī), with verses 18–19 gathering them into a single summary list.
