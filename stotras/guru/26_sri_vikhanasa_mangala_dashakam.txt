Blurb: A maṅgala is a hymn of blessing rather than of petition. This one is addressed to Vikhanas, the founder of the Vaikhānasa system, in ten plain verses in the anuṣṭubh metre, each ending on the single word maṅgalam. The Vikhanasāṣṭakam paints his figure. This hymn instead states his credentials. He was born of Viṣṇu's own will, with Lakṣmī for mother and her consort for father. Bhṛgu and the rest are his sons. He is the author of the Sūtra that his followers still keep. He instituted image-worship, after first establishing the supremacy of Viṣṇu. Verse 5 carries the greatest institutional weight, quietly: he is the ground into which the compassion of Veṅkaṭeśa first enters. That is a claim staked by the community that has served Tirumala since before anyone kept records.

Colophon (unnumbered): iti śrī vikhanasa maṅgala daśakam | — "Thus the Śrī Vikhanasa Maṅgala Daśakam."

Recension note: The whole text is in anuṣṭubh. Despite the title "Daśakam," eleven verses are printed: ten benediction verses and a phalaśruti, which verse 11 itself acknowledges by calling the preceding portion a padyadaśaka. This recension carries a number of irregular readings, all of which appear identically on both the Devanāgarī and the IAST page and have therefore been preserved in the text above rather than emended, with the intended sense supplied in the translations. They are: verse 3 विहीत / vihīta for vihita; verse 4 मुक्त्यापायं / muktyāpāyaṁ where the sense requires muktyupāyaṁ, "the means to liberation," and स्वयं दर्शय ते / svayaṁ darśaya te, read here as svayaṁ darśayate, "he himself shows"; verse 6 विखससे / vikhasase, an obvious slip for vikhanase, and परत्वकधन / paratvakadhana, read as paratva-kathana, "the declaration of supremacy"; verse 7 नारायाण / nārāyāṇa for nārāyaṇa, श्रुतिवाक्त्येक / śrutivāktyeka for śrutivākyaika, and दृषते / dṛṣate, a form of uncertain derivation rendered here as "who is beheld"; verse 8 a bracketed (च) / (ca) evidently inserted by an editor to fill out the pāda; and verse 9 जन्मन्येन / janmanyena, read as janmany eva, "from birth itself." The four-armed iconography of verse 9 — conch and discus above, varada and abhaya below — is the standard Vaikhānasa depiction of Vikhanas, and the viṣṇubali of verse 3 is the in-womb saṁskāra on which the school's claim to hereditary Vaiṣṇava status rests. The reference to Veṅkaṭeśa in verse 5 reflects the Vaikhānasa community's role as the hereditary priesthood of Tirumala.
