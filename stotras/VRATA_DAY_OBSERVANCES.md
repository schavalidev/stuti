# What is done on the vrata days that have no pūjā of their own — 2026-09-17

**Status: research only. No pūjā file was written, and nothing in the corpus was changed.**

The design's vrata list carries a number of days that fall back on some other deity's pūjā, or on
the nitya pūjā, because no vidhāna of their own is printed anywhere. `AUTHORING_QUEUE.md` records
those fallbacks and the searches behind them. What it does not record is the other half of the
question: on such a day the reader still does something, and still says something. This file
gathers, for each such day, the observance itself, the verses that belong to it, and the pārāyaṇa —
each with the print it was taken from.

Every verse below is quoted exactly as its source prints it. Nothing here was composed,
reconstructed, or tidied. Where a verse survives only in broken OCR, that is said plainly, and the
verse is not fit to be written into a file until a clean witness is found.

**Sources used.** The two that carried most of this:

- **Vratarāja** of Viśvanātha, Khemrāj Śrīkṛṣṇadās edition, Sanskrit with Mādhavācārya's Hindi
  bhāṣā-ṭīkā — archive `vrataraj-of-vishvanath-sharma-with-bhasha-tika-by-madhavacharya-khemraj`.
  Its OCR is clean and its printed page numbers survive in the text layer, so it can be cited by
  page. Line numbers below are lines of that item's `_djvu.txt`.
- **Dharmasindhu** of Kāśīnātha Upādhyāya, 1888 Nirṇayasāgara — archive
  `dharmasindhubykashinathupadhyaya1888nirnaysagarpress_498_a`. This is the opposite case: the
  text is complete but the OCR has lost its word-spaces and mangles many akṣaras, so it reads as
  evidence of what a passage says and not as a text witness. Its page numbers are mostly eaten;
  citations are by line, with the nearest legible printed page given.

Also searched and reported on below where they bear: Hemādri's Caturvargacintāmaṇi vrata-khaṇḍa,
Vratha Ratnākaramu 1–2, Āru Vratālu (1999), the dli Telugu scans, stotranidhi and vignanam.

## 1. Rakṣā Bandhan / Śrāvaṇī Pūrṇimā

**This day does have a printed vidhāna, and a mantra.** `AUTHORING_QUEUE.md` line 3662 sets it
aside as "thread-tying is a custom", and that judgment can now be corrected: the Vratarāja prints
the whole rite as the **Rakṣābandhana-paurṇamāsī-vrata of the Bhaviṣyottara-purāṇa**, quoted
through Hemādri, in twenty-seven verses with a colophon.

Source: Vratarāja, printed **pp. 803–804**, lines 36954–37005 (archive
`vrataraj-of-vishvanath-sharma-with-bhasha-tika-by-madhavacharya-khemraj`). The Hindi ṭīkā of the
same passage follows on p. 804. The section opens `अथ श्रावणपौर्णमास्यां रक्षा बन्धनं तच्चोक्तं हेमाद्रौ
भविष्ये` and closes `इति भविष्योत्तरपुराणे रक्षाबन्धनपौर्णमासीव्रतम्`.

### What is done

The order of the day, from verses 16 to 24 of that passage:

1. **Bathing at sunrise**, by the rule of śruti and smṛti — `संप्राप्ते श्रावणे मासि पौणमास्यां दिनोदये ॥
   स्नानं कुर्वीत मतिमाञ्च्छतिस्मृतिविधानतः` (v. 16, line 36989).
2. **Tarpaṇa** of the devas and the pitṛs, and of the ṛṣis — `ततो देवान्पितैइचेव तर्पयेत्परमाम्भसा ॥`
   (v. 17, line 36990; `पितैइचेव` is the OCR's slip for पितॄंश्चैव).
3. **Upākarma**, named in the same breath as the ṛṣi-tarpaṇa — `उपाकर्मादि चेवोक्तमुषीणां चेव तर्पणम्`
   (v. 17; `चेव` for चैव, `मुषीणां` for मृषीणां). Done with brāhmaṇas, with the Veda in view, as one is able (v. 18). For śūdras the
   verse allows the bath and the giving without mantras.
4. **In the aparāhṇa, the rakṣā-poṭalikā is made** — a small auspicious bundle with akṣata, white
   mustard and gold, in cotton or silk cloth, unstained, strung with many-coloured thread, set on a
   vessel (vv. 19–20, lines 36992 onward).
5. **The house is marked** with well-drawn cow-dung circles with dūrvā and colour, to quiet
   misfortune (v. 21); a kalaśa is set on a prepared square (v. 22).
6. **The deity and the brāhmaṇas are worshipped first** with cloths and with the rakṣās (v. 24),
   and only then does the purohita tie the rakṣā with the mantra.
7. It is not for one class only — `ब्राह्मण: क्षत्रियेवॅव्येः शाद्रैरन्येहच सानवेः ॥ कतंव्यो रक्षिकाबन्धो
   द्विजान्सस्पुज्य भक्तितः` (v. 26, OCR verbatim; the first pāda is damaged — it is
   ब्राह्मणैः क्षत्रियैर्वैश्यैः शूद्रैरन्यैश्च मानवैः — so this verse needs the page image before use).

**Prohibition.** The tying is not done in bhadrā — `अयं रक्षाबन्धो भद्रायां न कार्यः`, on the authority
of `भद्रायां द्वे न कतंव्ये श्रावणी फाल्गुनी तथा ॥ श्रावणो नूर्पात हन्ति ग्रामं दहति फाल्गुनी` (line
37003, OCR verbatim; `कतंव्ये` and `श्रावणो नूर्पात` are damaged for कर्तव्ये and श्रावणी नृपतिं).

### The mantra

Verse 25, at line 36999, printed p. 803:

> येन बद्धो बली राजा दानवेन्द्रो महाबलः ॥
> तेन त्वामभिबध्नामि रक्षे माचल माचल ॥ २५ ॥

This is the verse asked after. It is printed, in a purāṇic vrata with a colophon, and it is clean
in the OCR.

### The pārāyaṇa

The passage carries its own reading: verses 3 to 13 are the **Indrāṇī story** — Śacī's tying of the
rakṣā on Indra's right hand on the paurṇamāsī of a twelve-year deva-asura war, and Indra's victory
— told by Kṛṣṇa to Yudhiṣṭhira, and closing `एष प्रभावो रक्षायाः कथितस्ते युधिष्ठिर ॥ जयदः सुखदश्चैव
पुत्रारोग्यधनप्रदः` (v. 13). That is the day's kathā, in the same 803–804 spread.

### Upākarma

The task took the upākarma to be already in the corpus. **It is not.** `stotras/vidhi/` has no
upākarma or utsarjana file in any śākhā, and a search of the whole of `stotras/` for the word in
Devanāgarī, Telugu and transliteration turns up only passing mentions — in
`vidhi/taittiriya/03_yajnopavita_dharana_vidhi.txt`, in
`puja/vrata/06_satyanarayana_vrata_kalpam.txt`, and in the queue entry itself. The Dharmasindhu
treats upākarma and utsarjana as a nirṇaya of their own (lines 3748–3757), a Vedic karma with its
own prāyaścitta, which is why it sits outside the vrata literature. If the day is to be complete,
the upākarma is a separate piece of work and a śākhā-specific one.

### Also on this day

The Dharmasindhu (lines 3620–3626, Śrāvaṇa section) makes Śrāvaṇa paurṇamāsī the **catch-all day
for pavitrāropaṇa**: Viṣṇu's on the pāraṇā dvādaśī, Śiva's on the caturdaśī, aṣṭamī or the
paurṇamāsī, and for Devī, Gaṇeśa, Durgā and the rest on their own tithis — `तत्तत्तिथिष्वसंभवे
सर्वदेवानां श्रावणपौर्णमास्यां कार्यम्`, failing which Kārtikī is the outer limit. The same page prints
the pavitra-making in detail (the number of threads and knots for the uttama, madhyama and
kaniṣṭha pavitra) and the adhivāsana verse `क्रियालोपविधानार्थं यत्त्वया विहितं प्रभो ...`, which in that
OCR is broken and not usable as text.

## 2. Ugādi — the saṁvatsarādi

### What is done

The clearest printed statement of the day is the **Dharmasindhu, dvitīya-paricchheda, the Caitra
month**, printed **pp. 31–32** (the heading `द्वितीयपरिच्छेदः ३१` stands at line 3041), in the
marginal rubric `वत्सरारम्भनिर्णयः` at lines 3056–3062. The year begins on Caitra śukla pratipat,
and the udayikī pratipad is the one taken. The list of what belongs to the day is given twice over,
because the text is deciding what happens when Caitra is a mala-māsa:

- **तैलाभ्यङ्ग** — the oil bath, done for the sake of the year's beginning. The text makes it
  obligatory, not optional: `अस्यां तैलाभ्यङ्गो नित्यः, अकरणे प्रत्यवायोक्तेः` (line 3062) — it is nitya
  on this day, because failing to do it is said to bring harm. When Caitra is intercalary the
  tailābhyaṅga goes to the śuddha pratipat, on the authority of the Madhukośa.
- **the saṅkalpa naming the new year** — `नूतनवत्सरनामकीर्तन`, spoken in the saṅkalpa (line 3059).
  The Dharmasindhu names the act but prints no saṅkalpa formula for it.
- **ध्वजारोपण** at every house — `प्रतिगृहं ध्वजारोपण` (line 3060).
- **निम्बपत्राशन** — the eating of the neem leaf (line 3060).
- **वत्सरादिफलश्रवण** — the hearing of the year's phala, that is the pañcāṅga-śravaṇa (line 3060).
- **नवरात्रारम्भ** — the Vasanta Navarātri begins on the same pratipat, and its pāraṇā and the rest
  follow the Śāradā Navarātra.
- **प्रपादान** — the setting up of the water-booth for the four hot months, with the verse printed
  at lines 3066–3068 and the giving-mantra `एष धर्मघटो दत्तो ब्रह्मविष्णुशिवात्मकः । अस्य प्रदानात्सकला
  मम सन्तु मनोरथाः`. The OCR of both is broken; the sense is legible, the text is not.

The same pratipat is also a **kalpādi**, and the text goes on to the Caitra Gaurī-vrata (a couple
worshipped together for the month, with curds, milk, ghee and honey given up), the Candra-vrata on
the dvitīyā, and Rāma's dolotsava from the tṛtīyā for a month.

### The nimba mantra

The verse asked after is **not in any printed nibandha searched.** It is not in the Dharmasindhu,
which prescribes the nimba-patra-āśana but prints no mantra for it. It is not in the Vratarāja,
whose own `संवत्सरारम्भविधिः` (`vrataraj-of-vishvanath-sharma-with-bhasha-tika-by-madhavacharya-khemraj`,
pp. 65–67, lines 4612–4712 of the text layer) was read through for this: that section takes its whole
vidhi from the Brahmapurāṇa — the udayikī pratipad, the śānti, the worship of Brahmā, and the long
`नमो ब्रह्मणे तुभ्यम्` prayer to the divisions of time — and it names no nimba-eating and carries no
verse for one. It is not in the Nirṇayasindhu (`xqwn_nirnaya-sindhu-…`), whose `वत्सरारम्भः` passage
likewise prints none; that book's one occurrence of `निम्बपत्राणि` is Yājñavalkya on the chewing of
nīm leaves at the house door after a cremation, a different act altogether. It is not in Hemādri,
nor in Vratha Ratnākaramu 1–2 or Āru Vratālu — the `నింబ` hits in those Telugu prints are all
tree-lists in pūjā material, not this. Full-text searches on archive.org for both incipits return
nothing.

**No printed witness was found.** What follows is the verse as it circulates, recorded because the
day is kept with it, and marked unverified.

The text is stotranidhi's, from `stotranidhi.com/ugadi-slokas-in-telugu/`, where it stands as the
"Ugādi prasāda-prāśana śloka" and cites no book:

- **deva** — शतायुर्वज्रदेहाय सर्वसम्पत्कराय च ।
  सर्वारिष्टविनाशाय निम्बकन्दलभक्षणम् ॥
- **iast** — śatāyurvajradehāya sarvasampatkarāya ca |
  sarvāriṣṭavināśāya nimbakandalabhakṣaṇam ||
- **tel** — శతాయుర్వజ్రదేహాయ సర్వసంప(సమ్ప)త్కరాయ చ ।
  సర్వారిష్టవినాశాయ నింబ(నిమ్బ)కంద(కన్ద)లభక్షణమ్ ॥
- **en** — For a hundred years of life, for a body hard as the vajra, for every kind of wealth, and
  for the destruction of every affliction, this eating of the nīm shoot.
- **hi** — सौ वर्ष की आयु के लिये, वज्र के समान दृढ़ शरीर के लिये, सब प्रकार की सम्पत्ति के लिये और सब
  अरिष्टों के नाश के लिये यह नीम के कोंपल का भक्षण है।

Two readings differ, and both should be known.

- **The incipit.** It is `शतायुर्वज्रदेहाय`, not `निम्बकुसुमसम्भूतं`. The second is a separate
  circulating opening, kept on hindupad under the title "Nimba Kusuma Bakshanam" and repeated on the
  recipe and festival sites; it belongs to the same act but is not this verse's first pāda.
- **What is eaten.** stotranidhi's fourth pāda reads `निम्बकन्दल` — the kandaḷa, the new shoot.
  Elsewhere the same pāda is printed `निम्बकदल` (nimbakadala), and elsewhere again
  `निम्बकुसुम` (nimbakusuma), the flower. The three are not a spelling matter: the shoot and the
  flower are different offerings, and the Telugu practice of the day, which puts both the vēpa
  flower and the tender leaf into the pacaḍi, will support either. Since no print decides it, none
  is preferred here.

The circulated text was found on stotranidhi, hindupad, great-indian-knowledge and the festival and
recipe sites, and in that last group in Roman transliteration only. Not one of them names a source.
The Dharmasindhu, which is the authority for the day, prescribes only `निम्बपत्राशन`, the eating of
the leaf, and gives it no mantra at all. **Unverified. Do not write it into a file as a sourced
text.**

### The pañcāṅga-śravaṇa and its phala

The Dharmasindhu prescribes the hearing (`वत्सरादिफलश्रवण`) and prints **no phala verse**. Neither
does the Vratarāja's saṁvatsarārambha section, nor the Nirṇayasindhu's; archive.org's full text
returns nothing for the incipit. **No printed witness was found for this verse either.** It is
recorded here on the same terms as the nimba mantra.

The text as it circulates, taken from the vedvikas pañcāṅga post (`vedvikas.blogspot.com`, Vijaya
saṁvatsara, 2013), which cites no book:

- **deva** — तिथेश्च श्रियमाप्नोति वारादायुष्यवर्धनम् ।
  नक्षत्राद्धरते पापं योगाद्रोगनिवारणम् ॥
  करणात्कार्यसिद्धिश्च पञ्चाङ्गफलमुत्तमम् ॥
- **iast** — titheśca śriyamāpnoti vārādāyuṣyavardhanam |
  nakṣatrāddharate pāpaṁ yogādroganivāraṇam ||
  karaṇātkāryasiddhiśca pañcāṅgaphalamuttamam ||
- **tel** — తిథేశ్చ శ్రియమాప్నోతి వారాదాయుష్యవర్ధనమ్ ।
  నక్షత్రాద్ధరతే పాపం యోగాద్రోగనివారణమ్ ॥
  కరణాత్కార్యసిద్ధిశ్చ పంచాంగ(పఞ్చాఙ్గ)ఫలముత్తమమ్ ॥
- **en** — From the tithi one gains prosperity, from the vāra the lengthening of life; the nakṣatra
  takes away sin, the yoga wards off disease, and from the karaṇa comes the accomplishment of one's
  work — this is the highest fruit of the pañcāṅga.
- **hi** — तिथि से सम्पत्ति मिलती है, वार से आयु बढ़ती है, नक्षत्र पाप हरता है, योग रोग दूर करता है और
  करण से कार्य सिद्ध होता है — यही पञ्चाङ्ग का उत्तम फल है।

The verse is unstable in the sources: the last line is printed sometimes as a third half-verse, as
here, and sometimes with `करणं कार्यसिद्धिं च`. Search results also attach a further line giving the
fruit of the hearing as a bath in the Gaṅgā, which the pages that print the verse itself do not
carry; that line is not recorded here. It was found on the vedvikas post, on religionworld and on
the Ugādi festival pages, and in Roman transliteration on the rest. None of them names a source.
**Unverified. Do not write it into a file as a sourced text.**

### The nimba mantra

which prescribes the nimba-patra-āśana but prints no mantra for it; not in the Vratarāja; not in

> సర్వారిష్టవినాశాయ నింబ కందళ భక్షణం |

Two things follow. The incipit is not `निम्बकुसुमसम्भूतं` as the task supposed, but

## 3. Ekādaśī

The queue skipped this day on 17 Sep because no pūjā vidhāna is printed for it, which is right.
But the day is not empty of prescription: the **Vratarāja prints a full Ekādaśī-nirṇaya
prakaraṇa**, pp. 452–485 (it opens at line 22784, `अथैकादशीव्रतानि` / `एकादशीनिर्णयः`), before the
month-by-month kathā chapters begin. Almost everything the day needs is there, each item under its
own sub-heading. The Dharmasindhu confirms the central verse independently.

### The vow

Printed at **p. 474, line 22889**, under the sub-heading `उपवासग्रहणविधिः`, with its frame: it is
taken facing north, holding a copper vessel full of water, and the mantra is Viṣṇu's own
(`मंत्रस्तु विष्णूक्तः`):

> एकादश्यां निराहारः स्थित्वाहमपरेऽहनि ॥
> भोक्ष्यामि पुण्डरीकाक्ष शरणं मे भवाच्युत ॥

A puṣpāñjali follows, and then the water in the vessel is drunk, having been charged three times
with the aṣṭākṣara `ॐ नमो नारायणाय` — `उपवासफलं प्रेप्सुः पिबेत्पात्रगतं जलम्`.

The same vow appears in the morning form in the Indirā Ekādaśī chapter (Brahmavaivarta),
**p. 592, lines 27974–27976**, after rinsing the mouth at clear dawn:

> अद्य स्थित्वा निराहारः सर्व भोगविवर्जित: ।। २६ ॥। इवो भोक्ष्ये पुण्डरीकाक्ष
> शरणं से भवाच्युत ॥।

That is the OCR verbatim, and it has three slips — `इवो` for श्वो, `से` for मे, and a broken
compound in `सर्व भोगविवर्जित:`. Take the vow from line 22889 above, which is clean, and use this
occurrence only as the witness that the `अद्य स्थित्वा` form is printed.

The vow stands again at **p. 497, line 23857** in the Kamalā chapter. On when it is spoken, the nirṇaya prints
both: the Brahmavaivarta has it made at night on the daśamī, and Mādhava's rule
`मध्यरात्रे उदये वा दशमीवेधे रात्रौ सङ्कल्पः` puts it at night when the ekādaśī is vedha-struck.

### The night vigil

**p. 474, lines 22897–22898**, under the heading `जागरणम्`, citing Devala — `देवस्य पुरतः कुर्याज्जागरं
नियतो व्रती`. What fills the night is given at **p. 497, line 23855**:

> गीतेर्वाद्यैच नृत्येच पुराणपठनादिभिः ॥ ३५ ॥

— quoted as the OCR has it; `गीतेर्वाद्यैच नृत्येच` is a slip for गीतैर्वाद्यैश्च नृत्यैश्च, so this line
needs a second witness before it is written into a file. The sense is not in doubt, and it is
repeated at greater length in the udyāpana section, **p. 476, lines 22987–22988** —
`गीतैर्वाद्यैस्तथा नृत्यैरितिहासैर्मनोरमैः ॥ पुराणैः सत्कथाभिश्च रात्रिशेषं नयेत्सुधीः` — song,
instruments, dance, purāṇa-reading, itihāsa and good kathā, before the image, all night. Beyond the
general `हरिकीर्तन`, **no nāma-saṅkīrtana list is prescribed for the vigil.**

### The verse on dvādaśī

The verse asked after as the "pāraṇa verse" is **not a pāraṇa verse.** It is the dvādaśī-morning
**nivedana, the handing over of the vrata**, said after the bath and the Viṣṇu-pūjā and before the
brāhmaṇas are fed. The Vratarāja labels it as such and names its authority — **pp. 474–475, lines
22898 and 22907**, `द्वादश्यां निवेदनमन्त्र उक्तः कात्यायनेन`:

> अज्ञानतिमिरान्धस्य व्रतेनानेन केशव ॥
> प्रसीद सुमुखो नाथ ज्ञानदृष्टिप्रदो भव ॥

It stands again, clean and numbered, at **p. 497, line 23858**, in the Kamalā chapter, at exactly
the same point in the day. The Dharmasindhu prints the same verse at the same place — printed
**p. 98**, lines 2541–2542, `द्वादश्यां प्रातर्नित्यपूजां विधाय भगवते व्रतमर्पयेत् — [verse] — इति तत्र
मन्त्रः`. That Dharmasindhu OCR is broken and should not be used as the text; the Vratarāja reading
is the clean one.

**No verse is printed for the moment of eating.** The sequence ends: this samarpaṇa, then feed the
brāhmaṇas and give dakṣiṇā, then the five mahāyajñas with one's kin, then eat, silently.

### The reading

The Vratarāja prints **one māhātmya chapter per ekādaśī — twenty-four for the ordinary months,
kṛṣṇa and śukla, plus Kamalā and Paramā for the adhika-māsa, twenty-six in all** — each from a
named Purāṇa and each with its own colophon (`इति श्रीकूर्मपुराणे मोहिन्याख्यवैशाखशुक्लैकादशीमाहात्म्यं
समाप्तम्`, `इति श्रीभविष्यपुराणे विष्णोः शयन्येकादशीमाहात्म्यं संपूर्णम्`, and so on). The day's own
māhātmya is the day's reading, and the chapters say so — `एकादश्याश्च माहात्म्यं पादमेकं शृणोति यः`
(p. 485, line 24741): even a quarter of it heard is fruit.

### The daśamī and the pāraṇa

- **Daśamī** — `दशम्यां विधिः`, p. 474, lines 22865–22868, from the Kūrma and, through the
  Madanaratna, the Nāradīya: give up bell-metal, meat, masūra, chickpeas, koradūṣaka, greens,
  honey, another's food and woman; also māṣa, eating twice, gaming and excess water; take unsalted
  haviṣya, sleep on the ground. **One meal** — `एकभक्तं ततः कृत्वा रात्रौ भूमौ शयीत च` (p. 592,
  line 27973).
- **What breaks the fast** — p. 474, line 22872, Devala through Hemādri: repeated drinking of
  water, a single chewing of tāmbūla, daytime sleep, sex. Water taken in distress does not break
  it (line 22874). Tooth-cleaning is forbidden on the fast day itself, which is why the daśamī and
  dvādaśī routines are the ones that carry `दन्तधावनपूर्वकम्`.
- **Dvādaśī** — Bṛhaspati's eight, p. 475, lines 22908–22909: `दिवा निद्रां परान्नं च पुनर्भोजनमैथुने ॥
  क्षौद्रं कांस्यं माषतैलं द्वादश्यामष्टवर्जयेत्`.
- **Pāraṇa timing** — `पारणासमयः`, p. 475, lines 22924–22927. The first quarter of dvādaśī is
  Harivāsara and must be let pass: `द्वादश्याः प्रथमः पादो हरिवासरसंज्ञितः ॥ तमतिक्रम्य कुर्वीत पारणं
  विष्णुतत्परः`. Even when the dvādaśī is long, pāraṇa is in the first three muhūrtas of the
  morning, `सर्वेषामुपवासानां प्रातरेव हि पारणम्`. Where the dvādaśī is deficient, pāraṇa is on
  trayodaśī (p. 452, line 22811).
- **Who fasts when both days are full** — p. 497, line 23850: `गृहस्थानां च पूर्वा तु यतीनामुत्तरा
  स्मृता`, the householder the first, the ascetic the second. And the bracket, p. 474, line 22855,
  from Kātyāyana: over eight years old and under eighty, in both fortnights.
- **Vedha** — the whole of p. 452: a daśamī still standing at aruṇodaya (the last half-yāma of
  night, two muhūrtas) makes the ekādaśī viddhā, and for a Vaiṣṇava fasting on it is sin.

### A caution on the Telugu retellings

`in.ernet.dli.2015.333460`, a Telugu-script Sanskrit Ekādaśī māhātmya, carries the same vidhi
throughout, but its OCR is broken at the akṣara level (`భోమ్యూమి` for భోక్ష్యామి) and it is no use
as a text witness. Yamijala Padmanābha Swami's Telugu *Ekādaśī Māhātmyam*

pass** — the opposite of the nirṇaya's Harivāsara rule. That is popular retelling, and the
Vratarāja should be followed against it.

## 4. The jayantīs that fall back on a deity's pūjā

Five days were grouped together in the task. They do not behave alike. Two of them have nothing
printed at all; two have a tithi rule and an arghya and no more; **one of them — Vāmana — has a

### Sītā Navamī (Vaiśākha śukla navamī)

`जानकी` in it is inside the Rāma pūjā mantras. The Dharmasindhu's daśāvatāra-jayantī list (line

has no jayantī in it. Hemādri has nothing. The Telugu prints have nothing; their `సీతా` hits are

So there is no fast, no arghya and no prescribed pārāyaṇa. The fallback on

### Rādhā Aṣṭamī (Bhādrapada śukla aṣṭamī)

18923), on the authority of the Vṛddhayama and of the Purāṇasamuccaya quoted by Hemādri, `शुक्लाष्टमी
तिथिर्या तु मासि भाद्रपदे भवेत् ॥ दूर्वाष्टमीति विज्ञेया नोत्तरा सा विधीयते`. The one Rādhā vrata the
Vratarāja does print is the **Rādhā-Dāmodara vrata** (lines 44880–44943), kept on a Saturday under
the pippala, not on any aṣṭamī. The Dharmasindhu's only Rādhā entry (line 4874, garbled) is the
Rādhākuṇḍa bath, enjoined on residents of the Mathurā maṇḍala, on the **dark**-fortnight aṣṭamī —
a different day.

The fallback on `puja/smarta/36_krishna_shodashopachara_puja.txt` stands, and the reading can be
drawn from `krishna/11_radha_stotram_uddhava_krtam.txt`, `krishna/17_radha_krupa_katakshastotram.txt`
or `krishna/36_radha_ashtottarashatanama_stotram.txt` — again as a choice.

### Hayagrīva Jayantī (Śrāvaṇa paurṇamāsī)

The Dharmasindhu records **only the utpatti and the tithi rule**, printed **p. 49** (the heading
`द्वितीयपरिच्छेदः ४९` stands at line 3763), at line 3771: Hayagrīva's origin is on this paurṇamāsī,
and the tithi taken is the three-muhūrta sāyāhna-vyāpinī, pūrva-viddhā; where it falls short of
three muhūrtas, the latter day. That is all. **No fast, no pūjā, no arghya, no dāna and no stotra
is prescribed.** The Vratarāja and Hemādri have no Hayagrīva at all — zero hits — and the Telugu
prints have none.

The same Dharmasindhu page, immediately before, carries the rakṣābandhana rule and the same
`येन बद्धो बली राजा` mantra in garbled form, and immediately after, the **śrāvaṇā-karma and
sarpabali** by śākhā: Āśvalāyanas do both the śravaṇā-karma and the sarpabali on this paurṇamāsī,
Taittirīyas the sarpabali only, Kāṭhyāyanas and Sāmagas both. This is the same day as entry 1, and
those two entries should be read together.

**The stotra proper to the day** is Vedānta Deśika's **Hayagrīva Stotra**, thirty-three verses,
whose phalaśruti names its purpose as vāg-artha-siddhi — `वागर्थसिद्धिहेतोः पठत हयग्रीवसंस्तुतिं भक्त्या ॥
कवितार्किककेसरिणा वेङ्कटनाथेन विरचितामेताम् ॥ ३३ ॥` (stotranidhi,

arghya mantra follows at line 3312. **Its OCR is broken** — it reads

### Vāmana Jayantī / Vāmana Dvādaśī (Bhādrapada śukla dvādaśī)

**This day should not be in this list.** `AUTHORING_QUEUE.md` line 3668 records that the Vratha
Ratnākaramu `వామన` hits are "nāmāvaḷi entries, not a vrata". That is wrong, and so is the premise

**The Vratarāja prints a complete Vāmana pūjā**, printed pp. 629–632, lines 29552–29676. The vrata
sits inside the Śravaṇa-dvādaśī, framed `इति हेमाद्रौ भविष्योत्तरे श्रवणद्वादशीकथा ॥ अस्यामेव
वामनजयन्तीव्रतम्`, and a footnote on the same page argues from the Bhāgavata, against the

The observance, from the Bhaviṣyottara kathā: **sopavāsa**, with the worship at night; a vessel of

`वामनः प्रतिगृह्णाति वामनो वै ददाति च ॥ वामनस्तारकोभाभ्यां वामनाय नमो नमः ॥ ४६ ॥`.

Then, under its own heading **अथ वामनपूजा** (lines 29648–29675), a ṣoḍaśopacāra with a verse to
each upacāra. Its saṅkalpa: `मम इह जन्मनि जन्मान्तरे च कृतदोषप्रायश्चित्तार्थं पुत्रपौत्राद्यभिवृद्ध्यर्थं
वामनजयन्तीव्रतमहं करिष्ये, तदङ्गतया विहितं षोडशोपचारैर्वामनपूजनं करिष्ये`. **Its arghya mantra** is

> सहस्रशीर्षा त्वं देव श्रवणर्क्षसमन्वितः ॥
> अर्घ्यं गृहाण देवेश रसया सहितो हरे ॥

— and this is the only one of the day's three arghyas that survives in sound OCR. The aṅga-pūjā
assigns the ten avatāras to the limbs (`मत्स्याय नमः पादौ पूजयामि ॥ कूर्माय० जानुनी० ॥ …
कल्किने० शिरः पूज०`), and the prārthanā closes `नमो वामनरूपाय नमस्तेऽस्तु त्रिविक्रम ॥ नमस्ते
बलिबन्धाय वासुदेव नमोऽस्तु ते ॥`. The dāna is the **śikya**, the swing-basket, with curd-rice, a
water-pot, an umbrella and sandals, given with `दध्योदनयुतं शिक्यं वारिधानीयुतं विभो ॥ छत्रोपानहसंयुक्तं
ब्राह्मणाय ददाम्यहम् ॥`.

**The Dharmasindhu**, printed **p. 58** (heading at line 4119), lines 4122–4136, gives the day
differently: at **midday**, bathe at the confluence of rivers, worship a golden Vāmana and give
arghya in a golden vessel. It prints a pūjā mantra (`देवेश्वराय देवाय देवसम्भूतिकारिणे ॥ प्रभवे
सर्वदेवानां वामनाय नमो नमः`), **two arghya mantras** beginning `नमस्ते पद्मनाभाय नमस्ते जलदायिने ॥
तुभ्यमर्घ्यं प्रयच्छामि वामनरूपिणे`, and a dāna mantra for giving Vāmana with retinue to a brāhmaṇa
the next day. **All of these are in garbled OCR only** and must be read off the page image before
use. The same passage adds the payo-vrata and dadhi-vrata saṅkalpas made on this dvādaśī night.

**The Telugu witness** is Vratha Ratnākaramu 2, lines 14277–14380 — a complete
**వామనజయంతి** vidhāna with its saṅkalpa, the midday bath at the saṅgama with a saṅgama-prārthanā,
the karaka-pūraṇa, the śikya-sthāpana with its dadhi-bhakta, and a **Puruṣasūkta-based
ṣoḍaśopacāra** whose arghya is `त्रिपादूर्ध्व…`. Its OCR is poor and it should be treated as a
second witness to the shape of the rite, not as a text to lift.

**Three distinct arghyas, not three readings of one verse.** The Vratarāja's
`सहस्रशीर्षा त्वं देव`, the Dharmasindhu's `नमस्ते पद्मनाभाय`, and the Telugu print's Puruṣasūkta
`त्रिपादूर्ध्व` are different verses belonging to different forms of the rite. They must not be
collated into one. If the day is authored, the Vratarāja's form is the one that can be written now;
the other two need their page images read first.

**The pārāyaṇa** is printed and unambiguous: the Bhaviṣyottara **Śravaṇa-dvādaśī-kathā** (ending at
line 29552), and then the **Vāmana-dvādaśī-vrata-kathā**, the Bali story in fifty-one verses, lines
29556–29646.

## 5. Guru Pūrṇimā

The pādukā pūjā now at `puja/smarta/52_guru_paduka_puja.txt`, with
`puja/smarta/44_shankaracharya_shodashopachara_puja.txt` as the day's Guru pūjā, is what the
corpus holds. What the printed sources add is mostly a negative, and it is worth writing down
because it keeps the question from being asked a fourth time.

### The Vyāsa pūjā is the sannyāsin's rite, not the householder's

The Dharmasindhu, **āṣāḍha-māsa-nirṇaya** (the section runs to its colophon at line 3593; the
nearest legible printed page is ६९ at line 3454, so this falls about pp. 71–72), says at line 3563:

`अस्यां पौर्णमास्यां सन्न्यासिनां चातुर्मास्यावासं सङ्कल्पाङ्गत्वेन क्षौरव्यासपूजादिकं विहितम्`

— on this paurṇamāsī the tonsure and the Vyāsa-pūjā are prescribed **for sannyāsins, as a limb of
the saṅkalpa of the cāturmāsya residence.** The passage then gives the rite in brief (lines
3565–3580, in badly broken OCR): the kṣaura, twelve baths with earth, prāṇāyāma and the rest, then
the saṅkalpa `चातुर्मास्यवाससङ्कल्पं कर्तुं श्रीकृष्णव्यासभाष्यकाराणां सपरिवाराणां पूजनं करिष्ये`, and then
the maṇḍala — Kṛṣṇa in the middle; Vyāsa before him and, clockwise from Vyāsa, Sumantu, Jaimini,
Vaiśampāyana and Paila; to Kṛṣṇa's left the bhāṣyakāra Śaṅkara and, clockwise, Padmapāda,
Viśvarūpa, Troṭaka and Hastāmalaka; before the five, the guru-paramparā Brahmā, Vasiṣṭha, Śakti,
Parāśara, Vyāsa, Śuka, Gauḍapāda, Govindapāda, Śaṅkarācārya; Gaṇeśa in the south-east, Kṣetrapāla
in the north-east, Durgā in the north-west, Sarasvatī in the south-west, and the lokapālas in the
eight directions. Worship is with the eight-syllable mantra for Kṛṣṇa and with `ओं ... नमः` on each
name for the rest.

This is a monastic rite, and it is also exactly the paramparā the Śaṅkarācārya pūjā at
`44_shankaracharya_shodashopachara_puja.txt` addresses — which is why that file serves the day
well. **No householder Guru-pūjā or Vyāsa-pūjā vidhāna was found in any print.** The Vratarāja has
no Vyāsa-pūjā at all; its forty-two `व्यास` hits are all citations of Vyāsa as an authority on
other matters.

### What the householder's day otherwise holds

The same Dharmasindhu page (lines 3556–3560) puts three other things on Āṣāḍha paurṇamāsī, and
these are the householder's:

- **Śiva's śayanotsava**, on the paurṇamāsī that is joined to pradoṣa.
- **Śiva's pavitrāropaṇa**, on the paurṇamāsī, the caturdaśī or the aṣṭamī, of Āṣāḍha or Śrāvaṇa.
- **The Kokilā-vrata**, begun on this day by women and kept for a month, with the saṅkalpa verse
  printed there — `स्नानं करिष्ये नियता ब्रह्मचर्यस्थिता सती । भोक्ष्यामि नक्तं भूशय्यां करिष्ये प्राणिनां
  दयाम्` — followed by daily worship of Gaurī in the kokilā form and a nakta meal. The Dharmasindhu
  OCR of this verse is broken. **The Vratarāja prints the whole Kokilā-vrata cleanly**, at printed
  **p. 891**, lines 36487–36553, with its saṅkalpa (`... अवैधव्यसपत्नीनाशाय कोकिलारूपगौरी प्रीत्यर्थं
  कोकिलाव्रतं करिष्ये`), the dhyāna `... चम्पकवृक्षस्थां पुष्टस्वरसमन्विताम् ॥ चिन्तयेत्पार्वतीं देवीं
  कोकिलारूपधारिणीम्`, the nīrājana, the giving of a kokilā image to a brāhmaṇa, and the visarjana
  `वसन्ते देवि गच्छ त्वं देवानां नन्दने वने`. That is a complete, printed, citable vrata for Āṣāḍha
  paurṇamāsī which the corpus does not have, and it is a better candidate for authoring than
  anything further on the Guru side.

### The pārāyaṇa

No nibandha assigns one. The corpus holds the natural readings for the day already in `guru/` —
`03_gurugita_chapter1.txt`, `01_guru_paduka_stotram.txt`, `04_sri_guruparampara_stotram.txt`,
`06_veda_vyasa_stuti.txt`, `22_sri_vedavyasashtakam.txt`, `16_shukashtakam_vyasaputrashtakam.txt`
— and the last three are the Vyāsa texts that match the Dharmasindhu's paramparā.

## What this changes, and what is still missing

Three of the queue's earlier judgments should be revisited in the light of the above. None of them
was careless; each was made before the Vratarāja's text layer was searched.

1. **Rakṣā Bandhan is not only a custom** (queue line 3662). The Vratarāja prints a
   Bhaviṣyottara vrata with twenty-seven verses, a mantra, a kathā and a colophon, on pp. 803–804.
2. **Vāmana Jayantī has a printed vidhāna** (queue line 3668). The Vratarāja prints a complete
   ṣoḍaśopacāra Vāmana pūjā with its own arghya at pp. 629–632, and Vratha Ratnākaramu 2 carries a
   Telugu vidhāna at lines 14277–14380.
3. **Ekādaśī has a full printed nirṇaya** (queue line 3633), even though it has no pūjā: the
   Vratarāja's Ekādaśī-nirṇaya at pp. 452–485, with the vow, the vigil, the dvādaśī nivedana, the
   daśamī rules and the pāraṇa timing, and twenty-six māhātmya chapters as the day's reading.

Still missing, and each a separate piece of work:

- **The upākarma.** Absent from `stotras/vidhi/` in every śākhā. It is Vedic and śākhā-specific, so
  it needs a print of its own śākhā, not a general one.
- **The Deśika Hayagrīva Stotra.** The corpus has two other Hayagrīva texts but not this one, and
  it is the only reading that fits the day by its own phalaśruti.
- **The Kokilā-vrata**, printed whole and clean in the Vratarāja at p. 891 — a complete Āṣāḍha
  paurṇamāsī vrata the corpus does not have.
- **Four verses that need their page images read** before they can be written anywhere: the
  Paraśurāma arghya (Dharmasindhu p. 27), the two Vāmana arghyas and the Vāmana pūjā and dāna
  mantras (Dharmasindhu p. 58), and the rakṣābandhana v. 26 (Vratarāja p. 803, one damaged pāda).
- **Two verses with no printed witness at all**: the Ugādi nimba-prāśana śloka, and the
  pañcāṅga-phala verse. Both are in current use, and both are now recorded in the Ugādi section
  above in the form in which they circulate, marked unverified. Neither should be given a source it
  does not have.
