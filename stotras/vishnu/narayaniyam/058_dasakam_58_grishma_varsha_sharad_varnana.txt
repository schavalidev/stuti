Title: Nārāyaṇīyam — Daśaka 58: the forest fire, and the seasons of summer, rain and autumn

Metre: mālinī throughout — four pādas of fifteen syllables, six light syllables followed by a heavy close, with the break after the eighth.

Blurb: This daśaka carries the reciter through three seasons in ten verses. It opens in the heat of summer. While the Lord is delayed by the killing of Pralamba, the cows stray far out of Vṛndāvana in search of fresh grass and are caught in a thicket called Aiṣīka, where a forest fire closes around them. The rescue is the turning point of the daśaka, and it is done without any weapon: the Lord tells the boys and the cattle only to shut their eyes, and when they open them the fire and the thicket are gone and they are standing at Bhāṇḍīra. The praise they offer afterwards names what has happened as his māyā. The seventh verse is the most carefully made in the daśaka. It describes the summer season itself through three comparisons with the Lord — it bears a weight of heat like a man turned away from him, it dries the mud as devotion to him dries the mire within, and the brilliance rising from it is like the brilliance of his arms. The rains follow, with the clouds taking the colour of his body and the lightning serving as his yellow garment, and Govardhana appears here for the first time, praising him with the cries of its peacocks and offering kuṭaja and kadamba flowers. Autumn closes the daśaka with its clear water, compared to the mind of a devotee, and with the poet's own prayer for health of body.

en: While you were absorbed in play with the crowd of cowherd boys, and were kept long at the work of killing Pralamba, the cows wandered as they pleased. Eager for fresh grass, they grazed further and further away and came at last into a certain forest called Aiṣīka. — The daśaka begins by fixing the cause. Nothing is wrong yet, and the Lord is doing nothing careless; the delay is the killing of Pralamba, told in the daśaka before this one. The danger of the next verses grows out of that delay alone.

--- verse 2 ---
deva:
अनधिगतनिदाघक्रौर्यवृन्दावनान्ताद्
बहिरिदमुपयाताः काननं धेनवस्ताः।
तव विरहविषण्णा ऊष्मलग्रीष्मताप-

iast:
anadhigatanidāghakrauryavṛndāvanāntād
bahiridamupayātāḥ kānanaṁ dhenavastāḥ|
tava virahaviṣaṇṇā ūṣmalagrīṣmatāpa-

en: Those cows had come out from the border of Vṛndāvana, where the cruelty of the hot season is never felt, and had entered this forest. They were already sorrowing at separation from you, and the spreading heat of the burning summer made the sweat run over their bodies until they were distressed. So they stood there unable to move. — Two causes of their suffering are named, and the first is separation from the Lord. The heat is the second. The verse also makes a point about Vṛndāvana itself: within its bounds the harshness of summer is not known, and it is only outside them that the season has its full force.
tel: ఆ ఆవులు నిదాఘముయొక్క క్రౌర్యము తెలియని బృందావన సీమ నుండి బయటికి వచ్చి ఈ వనమును చేరినవి. అవి ఒకవైపు మీ విరహముచేత దుఃఖించుచుండెను, మరొకవైపు వేడిమితో నిండిన గ్రీష్మతాపము వ్యాపించుటచే వాటి శరీరములన్నియు చెమటతో తడిసి వ్యాకులములైనవి. అందుచేత అవి అచటనే కదలలేక నిలిచిపోయినవి. — వాటి బాధకు రెండు కారణములు చెప్పబడినవి; మొదటిది మీ విరహము, రెండవది తాపము. బృందావనము గూర్చి కూడ ఒక మాట ఇచట ఉన్నది — దాని సరిహద్దులలో గ్రీష్మముయొక్క ఉగ్రత తెలియదు, బయట మాత్రమే ఆ ఋతువు తన పూర్ణబలముతో ఉండును.

en: After that, O Śauri, you searched far with your companions and saw the herd of cattle, which had lost the path and come to grief in the muñja forest. Just as you went near to bring them back quickly — alas, alas — fire blazed up on every side. — The address Śauri, son of Vasudeva, is the poet's way of reminding the reciter who this child is. The repeated cry hī hī is the poet speaking in his own voice, as he does at the sharpest moments of the story.

"హీ హీ" అను పలుకు కవి తన స్వరముననే పలికినది; కథలోని అత్యంత తీవ్రమైన ఘట్టములలో ఆయన ఇట్లే పలుకును.

deva:
सकलहरिति दीप्ते घोरभाङ्कारभीमे
शिखिनि विहतमार्गा अर्धदग्धा इवार्ताः।
अहह भुवनबन्धो पाहि पाहीति सर्वे

sakalahariti dīpte ghorabhāṅkārabhīme

en: The fire blazed in every direction and was terrible with its dreadful roar. Their way was cut off, and they suffered as though half burnt already. "Ahaha, friend of all the worlds, save us, save us" — so crying, all of them took refuge in you alone, the one who removes affliction. — The cowherd boys and the cattle are described together, and their cry is given in their own words. The verse calls the Lord the remover of tāpa, which means both the heat of this fire and the burning of worldly sorrow. That double sense is the reason the daśaka ends with a prayer for the poet's own relief.
tel: అగ్ని అన్ని దిక్కులయందు మండుచు, భయంకరమైన భాంకారధ్వనితో భీకరముగా ఉండెను. వారి మార్గము అడ్డగింపబడెను; సగము కాలినవారివలె వారు ఆర్తులైరి. --- verse 5 ---
deva:
अलमलमतिभीत्या सर्वतो मीलयध्वं
दृशमिति तव वाचा मीलिताक्षेषु तेषु।
क्व नु दवदहनोऽसौ कुत्र मुञ्जाटवी सा
सपदि ववृतिरे ते हन्त भाण्डीरदेशे॥
iast:
alamalamatibhītyā sarvato mīlayadhvaṁ
dṛśamiti tava vācā mīlitākṣeṣu teṣu|
kva nu davadahano'sau kutra muñjāṭavī sā
sapadi vavṛtire te hanta bhāṇḍīradeśe||
en: "Enough, enough of this great fear. Close your eyes on every side." At this word of yours they closed their eyes. Where then was that forest fire, and where was that muñja thicket? At once they found themselves in the region of Bhāṇḍīra. What a wonder this is. — This is the turn of the daśaka. Nothing is fought and nothing is struck down. The Lord asks only for the eyes to be shut, which is to say he asks for trust, and the danger is simply not there when they are opened. The word hanta at the end is the poet's own astonishment.
