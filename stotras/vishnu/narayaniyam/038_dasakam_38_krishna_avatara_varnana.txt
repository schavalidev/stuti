Metre: vasantatilakā throughout — four lines of fourteen syllables.

Blurb: This daśaka is the birth itself. It opens not with the child but with the weather: the rainy season is shining, and the poet says the clouds that cover the sky are the streams of light pouring out of the Lord's own body as he descends. The second verse fixes the hour — the middle of the night, the directions cooled by the rain, good men full of expectation — and says that the Lord appeared then as the moon rises. The third and fourth verses look at what was born in the prison chamber: a body that touched childhood and yet wore crown, bracelets, armlets and garland, dark as a rain cloud, bearing conch, discus, mace and lotus, and lighting the house of Kaṁsa as though wiping away the ill fortune that wicked man had brought on it. The middle of the daśaka gives the parents their part. Vasudeva, seeing with his own two eyes a form that does not come within the reach even of the minds of steady sages, praises him with tears and a broken voice; Devakī, her body wet with weeping, praises him too. To both of them the Lord recalls their two earlier births, and then, at his mother's word, puts on the appearance of an ordinary human child. The last three verses are the going out: the exchange with Nanda's daughter, the sleep that falls on the whole city, the bolted doors opening of themselves, and Śeṣa holding back the rain with his hoods and lighting the road with the jewels on them. The daśaka closes, as these do, with a request — that the one who was carried out that night destroy the force of the poet's disease.

en: Lord whose nature is bliss, when the time of your descent had come, the rainy season shone, covering the sky with masses of heavy clouds that were as if the streams of light issuing from your blazing body. — The birth is announced through the season rather than through the child. The comparison is exact and reversed: the dark rain clouds are the Lord's own radiance, so the darkness of the sky is itself a sign of his presence.

deva:

नैशाकरोदयविधौ निशि मध्यमायां

en: When the directions had been made very cool by the water of the clouds, and good men were overcome with the gaining of what they had hoped for, at the middle of the night, in the manner of the rising of the moon, you appeared here as the remover of the affliction of the three worlds. — The hour is stated plainly: midnight. The comparison with moonrise answers the darkness of the previous verse, and it is the standard way this birth is described.
tel: మేఘజలముచేత దిక్కులు మిక్కిలి చల్లబడినప్పుడు, సత్పురుషులు తాము కోరినది లభించుటచేత పరవశులైనప్పుడు, అర్ధరాత్రివేళ చంద్రోదయమువలె, ముల్లోకముల క్లేశమును తొలగించువాడవై నీవు ఇచట ఆవిర్భవించితివి. — సమయము స్పష్టముగా చెప్పబడినది: అర్ధరాత్రి. చంద్రోదయముతో పోలిక క్రిందటి శ్లోకములోని చీకటికి సమాధానము; ఈ జన్మమును వర్ణించు సామాన్య రీతి కూడ ఇదియే.
hi: जब मेघ-जल से सब दिशाएँ अत्यन्त शीतल हो गयी थीं और सत्पुरुष अभीष्ट की प्राप्ति से विभोर थे, तब अर्धरात्रि के समय चन्द्रोदय की भाँति, त्रिलोकी के क्लेश को हरने वाले आप यहाँ प्रकट हुए। — समय स्पष्ट कहा गया है: अर्धरात्रि। चन्द्रोदय की उपमा पिछले श्लोक के अन्धकार का उत्तर है, और इसी जन्म के वर्णन की यह परम्परागत रीति है।

en: With a body that touched childhood and yet bore the marks of lordship, shining with the radiance of crown, bracelets, armlets and necklace, made splendid by the conch, the discus, the lotus and the mace, and dark as a rain cloud, you shone in the chamber of birth. — The verse holds two things together that do not ordinarily go together: a newborn child and the full four-armed form with its ornaments and weapons. The contrast is the point of the descent.

en: Vāsudeva, you shone there as though wiping away, by the various sidelong glances cast with modesty by the graceful Lakṣmī who rests happily on your chest, the ill fortune that the wicked Kaṁsa had brought upon that house. — Lakṣmī is present because she never leaves that chest, and her glance is enough to remove alakṣmī. The pun on lakṣmī and alakṣmī is the whole argument of the verse.

--- verse 6 ---
deva:
देव प्रसीद परपूरुष तापवल्ली-
निर्लूनदात्र समनेत्र कलाविलासिन्।
खेदानपाकुरु कृपागुरुभिः कटाक्षै-
रित्यादि तेन मुदितेन चिरं नुतोऽभूः॥
iast:
deva prasīda parapūruṣa tāpavallī-
nirlūnadātra samanetra kalāvilāsin|
khedānapākuru kṛpāgurubhiḥ kaṭākṣai-
rityādi tena muditena ciraṁ nuto'bhūḥ||
en: Lord, be gracious. Supreme Person, sickle that cuts down the creeper of affliction, you whose eye is equal towards all, you who sport with your own portions, remove my sorrows by glances heavy with compassion. In these words and in others like them you were praised for a long time by that glad man. — Samanetra, the equal eye, is the one word that is not praise of power but of impartiality, and it is what makes the request possible. Kalāvilāsin refers to his sporting through his own portions, which is how the creation is carried on.
