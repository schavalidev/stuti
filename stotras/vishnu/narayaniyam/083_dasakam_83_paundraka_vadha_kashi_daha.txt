Title: Nārāyaṇīyam — Daśaka 83: the killing of Pauṇḍraka, the burning of Kāśī, and the strength of Balarāma
Devanāgarī: श्रीनारायणीयम् – दशमस्कन्धपरिच्छेदः – त्र्यशीतितमदशकम् – पौण्ड्रकवधकाशीपुरीदाहबलभद्रप्रतापवर्णनम्

Metre: Mixed. Verses 1 to 8 are vasantatilakā, four lines of fourteen syllables. Verse 9 is hariṇī, four lines of seventeen. Verse 10 is sragdharā, four lines of twenty-one. The change of metre marks the change of subject: the Pauṇḍraka and Kāśī story runs in vasantatilakā, and the two Balarāma episodes that close the daśaka take the longer measures.

Blurb: This daśaka tells of a man who dressed himself as the Lord and was destroyed by the imitation. Pauṇḍraka, king of Karūṣa, was flattered by his servants into believing that he himself was Vāsudeva. He wore a Śrīvatsa branded on his chest with hot iron, a Kaustubha, makara earrings and yellow silk, and he sent a messenger ordering the Lord to give up those marks and take refuge in him. The court laughed at the messenger. The Lord went to his city, cut off his head with the discus, and cut off the head of his friend the king of Kāśī as well. Then comes the turn of the daśaka: this fool, who had thought of himself as Vāsudeva day after day out of sheer stupidity, obtained union with the Lord by that very thought, and the poet stops to say that no one can measure whose merit is of what kind. The rest of the daśaka follows the consequences. Sudakṣiṇa, son of the king of Kāśī, worshipped Śiva and sent a fire-demoness against Dvārakā; the Lord, who was at play and did not rise, simply sent the discus at his side, and it burned both the sender and his city. The last two verses turn to Balarāma — the killing of the monkey Dvivida, and the dragging of Hastināpura when Sāmba was held captive. The daśaka closes by calling this conduct hard to understand, and asking for relief all the same.

deva:
रामेऽथ गोकुलगते प्रमदाप्रसक्ते
हूतानुपेतयमुनादमने मदान्धे।
स्वैरं समारमति सेवकवादमूढो
दूतं न्ययुङ्क्त तव पौण्ड्रकवासुदेवः॥
iast:
rāme'tha gokulagate pramadāprasakte
hūtānupetayamunādamane madāndhe|
svairaṁ samāramati sevakavādamūḍho
dūtaṁ nyayuṅkta tava pauṇḍrakavāsudevaḥ||
en: Then Balarāma had gone to Gokula. He was fond of the company of the women there, blind with intoxication, and when the Yamunā did not come though she was called he subdued her. While he was sporting there at his pleasure, Pauṇḍraka, who called himself Vāsudeva and whose head had been turned by the talk of his servants, sent a messenger to you. — The verse sets two things side by side. Balarāma's absence from Dvārakā is the occasion, and Pauṇḍraka's absence of judgement is the cause. The word sevakavādamūḍha, made foolish by what his servants said, states the whole disease of the man in one compound.

--- verse 2 ---
deva:
नारायणोऽहमवतीर्ण इहास्मि भूमौ
धत्से किल त्वमपि मामकलक्षणानि।
उत्सृज्य तानि शरणं व्रज मामिति त्वां
दूतो जगाद सकलैर्हसितः सभायाम्॥
iast:
nārāyaṇo'hamavatīrṇa ihāsmi bhūmau
dhatse kila tvamapi māmakalakṣaṇāni|
utsṛjya tāni śaraṇaṁ vraja māmiti tvāṁ
dūto jagāda sakalairhasitaḥ sabhāyām||
en: I am Nārāyaṇa, and I have come down here upon the earth. You too, it seems, are wearing marks that belong to me. Give them up and take refuge in me. This is what the messenger said to you in the assembly hall, and everyone there laughed at him. — The message is quoted whole, in the speaker's own words, so that the reader hears the absurdity without being told of it. The last half-line answers it without argument: the court simply laughed, and the poet lets that be the reply.
tel: "నేనే నారాయణుడను; ఈ భూమిపై అవతరించియున్నాను. నీవు కూడ నా చిహ్నములను ధరించుచున్నావు. వాటిని విడిచిపెట్టి నన్ను శరణు పొందుము" — ఇట్లు ఆ దూత సభయందు నీతో చెప్పెను; అచట ఉన్నవారందరు అతనిని చూచి నవ్విరి. — సందేశమును కవి ఆ వక్త మాటలలోనే పూర్తిగా ఉదహరించుచున్నాడు; దానివలన దాని అసంగతత్వము చెప్పకనే తెలియును. చివరి అర్ధపాదము వాదము లేకుండ సమాధానము ఇచ్చుచున్నది — సభ నవ్వెను, అంతే.
hi: 'मैं ही नारायण हूँ और इस पृथ्वी पर अवतीर्ण हुआ हूँ। तुम भी मेरे चिह्नों को धारण करते हो। उन्हें छोड़कर मेरी शरण में आ जाओ' — इस प्रकार उस दूत ने सभा में आपसे कहा, और वहाँ उपस्थित सब लोग उस पर हँस पड़े। — सन्देश पूरा का पूरा वक्ता के ही शब्दों में उद्धृत है, जिससे उसकी असंगति कहे बिना समझ में आ जाती है। अन्तिम अर्धपाद बिना तर्क के उत्तर देता है — सभा हँस दी, और कवि उसी को उत्तर रहने देता है।

--- verse 3 ---
deva:
दूतेऽथ यातवति यादवसैनिकस्त्वं
यातो ददर्शिथ वपुः किल पौण्ड्रकीयम्।
तापेन वक्षसि कृताङ्कमनल्पमूल्य-
श्रीकौस्तुभं मकरकुण्डलपीतचेलम्॥
iast:
dūte'tha yātavati yādavasainikastvaṁ
yāto dadarśitha vapuḥ kila pauṇḍrakīyam|
tāpena vakṣasi kṛtāṅkamanalpamūlya-
śrīkaustubhaṁ makarakuṇḍalapītacelam||
en: When the messenger had gone, you went with the Yādava army and saw the person of Pauṇḍraka: a mark made on his chest by burning, a Kaustubha of great price, makara earrings and yellow silk. — Tāpena, by heat, is the word on which the verse turns. The Śrīvatsa is a natural mark on the Lord's chest; on this man it was branded with hot iron. Everything else in the list could be bought, and the poet says so by calling the Kaustubha costly rather than divine.
tel: దూత వెళ్ళిన పిమ్మట నీవు యాదవసేనతో వెళ్ళి పౌండ్రకుని రూపమును చూచితివి: వక్షస్థలమున వేడిచేత చేయబడిన చిహ్నము, మిక్కిలి విలువైన కౌస్తుభము, మకరకుండలములు, పీతాంబరము. — "తాపేన" — వేడిచేత — అను పదమే ఈ శ్లోకమునకు ఆధారము. శ్రీవత్సము భగవంతుని వక్షమున సహజమైన చిహ్నము; ఈ మనుష్యునికి అది కాల్చిన ఇనుముతో వేయబడినది. మిగిలినవన్నియు కొనదగినవే; అందుకే కవి కౌస్తుభమును దివ్యమనక, విలువైనదని మాత్రమే అనుచున్నాడు.
hi: दूत के चले जाने पर आप यादव सेना के साथ गये और पौण्ड्रक का रूप देखा — छाती पर ताप से बनाया हुआ चिह्न, बहुमूल्य कौस्तुभ, मकर के आकार के कुण्डल और पीताम्बर। — 'तापेन', अर्थात् ताप से, इस श्लोक का मुख्य शब्द है। श्रीवत्स भगवान् के वक्ष का स्वाभाविक चिह्न है; इस मनुष्य पर वह तपे हुए लोहे से बनाया गया था। शेष सब वस्तुएँ मोल ली जा सकती हैं, और इसीलिये कवि कौस्तुभ को दिव्य नहीं, बहुमूल्य कहता है।

--- verse 4 ---
deva:
कालायसं निजसुदर्शनमस्यतोऽस्य
कालानलोत्करकिरेण सुदर्शनेन।
शीर्षं चकर्तिथ ममर्दिथ चास्य सेनां
तन्मित्रकाशिपशिरोऽपि चकर्थ काश्याम्॥
iast:
kālāyasaṁ nijasudarśanamasyato'sya
kālānalotkarakireṇa sudarśanena|
śīrṣaṁ cakartitha mamarditha cāsya senāṁ
tanmitrakāśipaśiro'pi cakartha kāśyām||
en: He hurled at you his own Sudarśana, made of black iron. With your Sudarśana, which scatters showers of the fire of destruction, you cut off his head and crushed his army, and you cut off in Kāśī the head of his friend the king of that city as well. — The two discs are named by the same word in the same line, and the difference between them is left to the two adjectives: one is black iron, the other rains the fire that ends the age. This is the whole argument of the daśaka in a single verse.

--- verse 5 ---
deva:
जाड्येन बालकगिरापि किलाहमेव
श्रीवासुदेव इति रूढमतिश्चिरं सः।
सायुज्यमेव भवदैक्यधिया गतोऽभूत्
को नाम कस्य सुकृतं कथमित्यवेयात्॥
iast:
jāḍyena bālakagirāpi kilāhameva
śrīvāsudeva iti rūḍhamatiściraṁ saḥ|
sāyujyameva bhavadaikyadhiyā gato'bhūt
ko nāma kasya sukṛtaṁ kathamityaveyāt||
en: Out of dullness, and on nothing better than children's talk, he had held the settled conviction for a long time that he himself was Śrī Vāsudeva. By that very thought of oneness with you he attained union with you. Who indeed can know whose merit is of what kind? — This is the turn of the daśaka. The man was not a devotee and his thought was not worship, yet it was constant, and constancy in thinking of the Lord bore its own fruit.
