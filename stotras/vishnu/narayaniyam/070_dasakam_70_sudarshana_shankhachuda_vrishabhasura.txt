Title: Nārāyaṇīyam — Daśaka 70: Sudarśana released from his curse, and the killing of Śaṅkhacūḍa and Vṛṣabhāsura

Blurb: After the long night of the rāsa the poem returns to plain narrative, and this daśaka gathers three short episodes that all turn on the same point. A serpent swallows Nanda on the night of a festival in the forest of Ambikā. The cowherds beat it with firebrands and it will not let go, so they fall at the Lord's feet; he touches it with one foot, and the serpent stands up as the Vidyādhara Sudarśana, who had once laughed at sages and been cursed to that shape. A touch of the foot undoes what years of the curse had fixed. Next Śaṅkhacūḍa, a follower of Kubera, carries off the women of the settlement, and the Lord runs him down, takes his crest jewel and gives it to Balarāma. Then Ariṣṭa comes in the form of a bull, harsh in look and harsh in voice, and is crushed. The daśaka closes with the gods making a play on the word vṛṣa, which means both a bull and dharma: by killing the bull the Lord has made dharma stand firm on the earth. The last verse leaves the Lord walking home with the cowherds, who are laughing and calling out to the cattle to run, and the poet asks for protection in the middle of that laughter.

en: Lord, holder of the discus Sudarśana, I too am named Sudarśana. Once I laughed at some sages, and they made me a serpent here. Today, by the touch of your feet, I have become clean again. Praising you in these words, he went to his own world, and the cowherds went back to Vraja in gladness. — The name is the same as the Lord's discus, and the poet makes the meeting of the two names carry the verse. The fault was mockery of sages, and the punishment fitted the fault by taking away the fair form his name claimed.

en: Day after day you played in the woods with your friends. Your beauty took the mind even of the god of love. You poured out the nectar of the sound of your flute. To the eyes of the women of heaven you gave the breaking of their long fast with nectar.
