Blurb: This daśaka opens the fourth skandha-pariccheda, and it begins with a family. Dakṣa, the son of Brahmā, marries Prasūti and has sixteen daughters; from one of them, Mūrti, the Lord is born as Nārāyaṇa with Nara for his companion, and it is from this birth that the poem takes its name. The two brothers destroy the demon Sahasrakavaca by performing austerity and fighting by turns, and then live at Badarikāśrama, practising the way of liberation and teaching it to others. Indra cannot bear the strength that such penance stores up, and sends Kāma with the women of heaven to break it, but the Lord is not moved at all. He tells Kāma and his company not to be afraid, and shows them the women who wait upon him, at whose very fragrance Kāma and the rest lose their senses; they go away with Urvaśī, and Indra is left troubled because he did not understand whom he had challenged. Here Bhaṭṭatiri stops the story and says plainly that of all the descents this one is the most peaceful and the loveliest, and that only the body of Kṛṣṇa stands above it. The daśaka then returns to Dakṣa, who lost his peace of mind, set himself against Śiva and slighted his own daughter, and whose sacrifice was destroyed and head struck off before the Lord completed the rite and restored him. Twice in these ten verses Śiva is called the Lord's own portion and the Lord's own body, and the daśaka ends by addressing the Lord as the maker of peace and asking for protection.

en: Then Dakṣa, the son of Brahmā, obtained as his wife Prasūti, the daughter of Manu, and by her he had sixteen daughters. The last word of the verse is the one that carries weight: Śiva is called your portion, and the daśaka will say the same thing again at verse 9.

paryāyanirmitatapassamarau bhavantau

en: The demon Sahasrakavaca was covered about with armours, and each of them could be reached only by a thousand years of austerity and a thousand years of battle. You two performed the austerity and the battle by turns, and when a single armour was left to him, you struck him down in play. — The taking of turns is what the verse rests on. While one brother sat in penance the other fought, and in that way the thousand armours came off one after another. The last word, in play, sets the ease of the killing against the length of the labour that made it possible.

en: Practising the way of liberation yourself and teaching it to others as well, you dwelt with your brother in the middle of Badarikāśrama. Indra's fear is of the power that penance stores up, and the attack that follows is his answer to that fear.

hi: क्रुद्ध शंकर के द्वारा उनका यज्ञ नष्ट कर दिया गया और उनका सिर काट लिया गया; फिर देवताओं के द्वारा प्रसन्न किए गए हर से ही उन्हें पुनः जीवन मिला। उनके उस श्रेष्ठ यज्ञ को आपने पूर्ण किया और वे फिर शान्ति को प्राप्त हुए। हे प्रशान्ति करने वाले, हे मरुत्पुरेश, मेरी रक्षा कीजिए। — दशक वहीं समाप्त होता है जहाँ उपासक खड़ा है। 'प्रशान्तिकर' यह नाम अभी कही गई कथा से ही लिया गया है — दक्ष ने शान्ति खोई और फिर पाई, और यहाँ जो माँगा जा रहा है वह भी वही है।
