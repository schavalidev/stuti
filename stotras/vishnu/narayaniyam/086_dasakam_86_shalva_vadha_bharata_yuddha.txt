Blurb: This daśaka closes the account of the enemies who had to be removed and then runs the whole Mahābhārata war past in a few verses. It opens with Śālva, who had been beaten by the Yādava army at Rukmiṇī's wedding, obtained the flying fortress Saubha from Śiva, and attacked Dvārakā while the Lord was away at Indraprastha. Pradyumna held him off for twenty-seven days; the Lord returned, broke the fortress, and killed Śālva and then Dantavaktra, who fell upon him at once. A single line then states the doctrine that governs the whole stretch: this descent was for the release of all those who had fixed their minds on him, enemies included. From the fifth verse the daśaka becomes the war. Arjuna chooses the Lord himself while Duryodhana takes the army; the embassy to Hastināpura fails and the universal form is shown in the assembly; it is shown a second time on the field, to a charioteer's friend who has lost heart at the thought of killing his own people. Then the vow to take up no weapon is broken: the Lord seizes the discus and runs at Bhīṣma, who receives him with folded hands, having sworn to make him do exactly that. The next verse counts what was done without weapons at all — the Vaiṣṇava missile received on the chest, the sun hidden by the discus, the earth pressed down so that Arjuna lost only his crown. Balarāma keeps his own line through it all, on pilgrimage while the war is fought, and leaves when the mace duel will not be stopped. At the end Bhīṣma, who chose his own hour of death, teaches Yudhiṣṭhira the whole of dharma and passes into the undivided Brahman by devotion alone, and the poet, having brought the war to its close, asks for protection from all disease.

en: Śālva, defeated by the Yādava army at the wedding of Bhīṣmaka's daughter, obtained from the moon-crested Śiva the flying fortress called Saubha. That master of illusion attacked your city while you were staying among the Kurus. Pradyumna held him back, and with all the Yādava warriors struck down his minister Dyumān, a man of fierce strength. The battle lasted twenty-seven days. — The daśaka opens with an old grudge. Śālva had come to Rukmiṇī's wedding on Śiśupāla's side, and the defeat he suffered there is what sends him to Śiva for a weapon. The Lord is absent by design: the poet has him at Indraprastha so that the son may hold the city.

en: Meanwhile you came back quickly with Balarāma and confronted the lord of Saubha, whose army was by then almost destroyed. He struck you with his mace and made the Śārṅga bow fall from your hand. He then killed an illusory Vasudeva before your eyes. Some say that for half a moment even you did not see through that illusion. Vyāsa himself rejected this as improper. — The verse records an objection and its answer, which is unusual in a hymn. The Bhāgavata itself sets aside the opinion that the Lord was deceived, and the poet keeps both the opinion and the rejection rather than passing over the difficulty.

iast:
kṣiptvā saubhaṁ gadācūrṇitamudakanidhau maṅkṣu śālve'pi cakre-
ṇotkṛtte dantavaktraḥ prasabhamabhipatannabhyamuñcadgadāṁ te|
kaumodakyā hato'sāvapi sukṛtanidhiścaidyavat prāpadaikyaṁ
sarveṣāmeṣa pūrvaṁ tvayi dhṛtamanasāṁ mokṣaṇārtho'vatāraḥ||
en: You ground the Saubha to powder with your mace and threw it into the sea, and at once cut off Śālva's head with the discus. Just then Dantavaktra rushed upon you with force and hurled his mace. He too was killed by the Kaumodakī, and that store of merit attained oneness with you, as Śiśupāla had. This descent of yours was for the release of all those who had earlier fixed their minds upon you. — The last line is the doctrine of the whole episode. Dantavaktra is called a store of merit, and the enmity is counted as a fixing of the mind on the Lord. Hatred that never lets go of its object is treated here as a form of holding to him.

क्रन्दन्त्या याज्ञसेन्याः सकरुणमकृथाश्चेलमालामनन्ताम्।

iast:
tvayyāyāte'tha jāte kila kurusadasi dyūtake saṁyatāyāḥ
krandantyā yājñasenyāḥ sakaruṇamakṛthāścelamālāmanantām|
annāntaprāptaśarvāṁśajamunicakitadraupadīcintito'tha
prāptaḥ śākānnamaśnan munigaṇamakṛthāstṛptimantaṁ vanānte||
en: After you had returned, the game of dice took place in the Kuru assembly. Draupadī was seized and wept, and out of compassion you made her garment endless. Later, in the forest, Durvāsā, born of a portion of Śiva, arrived when the meal was over; Draupadī was frightened and thought of you.
