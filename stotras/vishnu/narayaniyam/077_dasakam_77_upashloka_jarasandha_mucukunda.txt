Title: Nārāyaṇīyam — Daśaka 77: the birth of Upaśloka, the wars with Jarāsandha, and grace to Mucukunda
Devanāgarī: श्रीनारायणीयम् – दशमस्कन्धपरिच्छेदः – सप्तसप्ततितमदशकम् – उपश्लोकोत्पत्तिवर्णनं जरासंधादियुद्धवर्णनं मुचुकुन्दानुग्रहवर्णनं च

Blurb: This daśaka carries the Lord out of Mathurā and into Dvārakā. It opens quietly, in the house of the serving woman whose crooked back he had made straight, who had waited for him every day with her chamber prepared. He goes to her with Uddhava, and the poet stops to remark on what she asked for when a boon was offered: she asked only for his company again, and not for union with him, which is what a wise person would have asked. From there the daśaka turns outward and does not turn back. Akrūra is honoured and sent to Hastināpura for news of the Pāṇḍavas. Jarāsandha, blind with rage at the killing of his son-in-law Kaṁsa, besieges Mathurā, and is beaten seventeen times over; he is once taken prisoner by Balarāma and let go again, and the verse that records it pauses to say that no king of that time had greater strength than the one so released. At the eighteenth assault Kālayavana arrives with three crores of soldiers, and the Lord has Dvārakā built in the sea and carries his people there. The last three verses are the episode of Mucukunda — the flight on foot, the sleeping king, the Yavana burnt to ash by a glance, and a king who, offered anything, wanted nothing but grace. The daśaka ends with the Lord leaving Jarāsandha a victory to be proud of and withdrawing to the city in the water.

āvāsaṁ tvadupagamotsavaṁ sadaiva
dhyāyantyāḥ pratidinavāsasajjikāyāḥ||
en: After that you went, gracefully adorned and with Uddhava beside you, to the house of the serving woman. She had long been afflicted by love, and every day she made her chamber ready and thought of nothing but the festival of your coming. — The serving woman is Trivakrā, whose bent body the Lord had made straight when he first entered Mathurā. The word used of her, vāsasajjikā, is the name given in poetry to a woman who keeps her room prepared for one who has not yet come. The daśaka begins with this small private scene before it turns to armies.

en: When you came, her every wish was fulfilled. In the hurry of her delight her body trembled, and with joy she offered you honour of many kinds. Nothing is said of what he wanted; what is recorded is that a wish long held was granted.

--- verse 3 ---
deva:
पृष्टा वरं पुनरसाववृणोद्वराकी
भूयस्त्वया सुरतमेव निशान्तरेषु।
सायुज्यमस्त्विति वदेद् बुध एव कामं
सामीप्यमस्त्वनिशमित्यपि नाब्रवीत् किम्॥
iast:
pṛṣṭā varaṁ punarasāvavṛṇodvarākī
bhūyastvayā suratameva niśāntareṣu|
sāyujyamastviti vaded budha eva kāmaṁ
sāmīpyamastvaniśamityapi nābravīt kim||
en: Asked to name a boon, that poor woman chose only to be with you again on some further nights. A wise person would indeed have said, let there be union with you. But why did she not say even this much — let there be nearness to you always? — The poet grades three possible requests: union, which only a wise person would think of; unbroken nearness, which anyone might have asked for; and a few more nights, which is what she asked. He calls her varākī, poor thing, and the pity is in the word, not in a judgement.

en: Then, Lord, on some nights you gave that doe-eyed woman quiet delight, and you gave her a son known by the name Upaśloka. He learned the Sātvata Tantra from Nārada and shone in the world. — The boon is granted exactly as it was asked, and then more is added than was asked. The birth of a son to her is not found in the older accounts. It is understood as something shown to the poet himself.

en: From there you went with Balarāma and Uddhava to the house of Akrūra, and he, full of joy, worshipped you and praised you greatly. bhūyo balodyamarasena mumocithainam|
niśśeṣadigjayasamāhṛtaviśvasainyāt

en: Then Balarāma seized by force that man who was strong beyond others, and you released him again, out of interest in seeing him raise his strength once more. For who at that time had greater strength and valour than he, who by conquering every direction had gathered the armies of the whole world? — The word bala is repeated four times in two lines: Balarāma's name, Jarāsandha's strength, force, and the vigour he would gather again. The second half is not praise of the enemy for its own sake. It measures the one who released him.

en: Broken though he was, his heart still fixed on it, and urged on by other kings, he made war with you in this way sixteen times over. deva:

aikṣvāko'haṁ virakto'smyakhilanṛpasukhe tvatprasādaikakāṅkṣī

en: He praised you saying, Lord, I am of the line of Ikṣvāku, I have turned away from every royal pleasure, and I desire your grace alone. Seeing him without longing even amid the boons spread before him, you were pleased. You gave him at once devotion equal to liberation, which washes away every impurity, and liberation as well, and you then told him to perform austerity for the cleansing of the violence he had done, so that the world might understand. — The last instruction is not for Mucukunda's sake, since he had already been given liberation. It is for the world's, so that no one may take the killing of living beings lightly. Mucukunda is the son of Māndhātṛ.
tel: "ఓ దేవా, నేను ఇక్ష్వాకువంశమువాడను; రాజభోగములన్నిటియందు విరక్తుడనైతిని; నీ అనుగ్రహమొక్కటే నాకు కావలెను" అని అతడు స్తుతించెను. ఎదుట పరచిన వరముల మధ్యను కూడ నిస్పృహుడైన అతనిని చూచి మీరు సంతోషించితిరి. సకల మలములను కడుగు, ముక్తితో సమానమైన భక్తిని, మోక్షమును కూడ వెంటనే ఇచ్చితిరి. పిదప లోకప్రతీతి కొరకు, చేసిన హింస శుద్ధియగుటకు తపస్సు చేయుమని ఆదేశించితిరి. — ఈ చివరి ఆదేశము ముచుకుందుని కొరకు కాదు; అతనికి మోక్షము ఇదివరకే ఇవ్వబడినది. ప్రాణిహింసను ఎవరును తేలికగా తీసికొనరాదని లోకమునకు తెలియుటకే అది. ముచుకుందుడు మాంధాత కుమారుడు.
