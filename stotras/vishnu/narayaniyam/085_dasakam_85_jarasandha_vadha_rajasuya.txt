Blurb: This daśaka carries two events that the tradition keeps together, because the second could not happen until the first was done. Twenty thousand eight hundred kings had been held in prison by Jarāsandha of Magadha for a long time, and they send a messenger asking for one thing only, that he be destroyed. At the same moment Nārada brings word that Yudhiṣṭhira intends the Rājasūya sacrifice, and the Lord is caught between two duties. Uddhava settles it in a sentence: a sacrifice performed after the enemy is subdued accomplishes both. The killing itself is done by Bhīma, at a silent sign given by tearing a twig in two, and the Lord stands by with Arjuna and watches. The freed kings are given devotion and then sent back to rule, which is the point of the release. The sacrifice follows, with the Lord washing the feet of the brāhmaṇas with his own hands and accepting the first honour at Sahadeva's word. Śiśupāla's abuse and his death by the discus close that scene, and the daśaka ends with Duryodhana stumbling in Maya's hall and the laughter of Draupadī and Bhīma — a small thing which the Lord allows to grow, because the seed of the war that will unburden the earth is sown there.

en: After that, O Lord, the body of kings — twenty thousand and eight hundred of them — who had suffered long imprisonment at the hands of the king of Magadha, sent some man as a messenger to you, the refuge of those who have no protector. The kings ask for nothing for themselves, not even release; they ask only that Jarāsandha be ended.

Uddhava's counsel is one line long and joins the two halves of the daśaka.

--- verse 5 ---
deva:
अशान्तसमरोद्धतं विटपपाटनासंज्ञया
निपात्य जरसः सुतं पवनजेन निष्पाटितम्।
विमुच्य नृपतीन् मुदा समनुगृह्य भक्तिं परां
दिदेशिथ गतस्पृहानपि च धर्मगुप्त्यै भुवः॥
iast:
aśāntasamaroddhataṁ viṭapapāṭanāsaṁjñayā
nipātya jarasaḥ sutaṁ pavanajena niṣpāṭitam|
vimucya nṛpatīn mudā samanugṛhya bhaktiṁ parāṁ
dideśitha gataspṛhānapi ca dharmaguptyai bhuvaḥ||
en: The combat would not end, and the son of Jarā grew more insolent. By the sign of tearing a twig in two you had him thrown down and split apart by the son of the Wind. Then you released the imprisoned kings, gladly gave them the highest devotion, and, although desire had left them, ordered them back for the protection of the earth according to dharma. — The sign is the whole of the killing: Jarāsandha had been joined from two halves, and a twig torn lengthwise tells Bhīma how to undo him. What follows is the reason for the war — the kings are freed, given bhakti, and then sent back to rule.

en: At that moment laughter broke out from the daughter of Drupada and from Bhīma, and with their sidelong looks it was allowed to grow a little further, O all-pervading one. In that way you at once sowed the seed of the removal of the earth's burden. Janārdana, dweller in Marutpurī, protect me from this illness. — The whole war is traced back to a glance that allowed a laugh to continue. The poet does not excuse it or explain it away; he calls it the sowing of a seed, and the harvest is the eighteen days at Kurukṣetra.
tel: ఆ సమయమున ద్రౌపదియు భీమసేనుడును నవ్విరి; ఓ విభూ, వారి కడకంటి చూపులచేత ఆ నవ్వు కొంత పెరిగినది. ఆ విధముగా భూభారమును తొలగించు కార్యమునకు వెంటనే బీజమును నాటితివి. ఓ జనార్దనా, మరుత్పురీనిలయా, ఈ రోగమునుండి నన్ను రక్షింపుము. — యుద్ధమంతయు, ఒక నవ్వును కొనసాగనిచ్చిన ఒక చూపునుండియే వచ్చినది. కవి దానిని సమర్థింపడు, దాటవేయడు; బీజము నాటుట అని చెప్పును, ఆ పంటయే కురుక్షేత్రములోని పదునెనిమిది దినములు.
