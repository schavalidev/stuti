Title: Sudarśanāṣṭakam
Devanāgarī: श्री सुदर्शनाष्टकम्

Author: Śrī Vedānta Deśika (Veṅkaṭanātha), named in the closing verse as Veṅkaṭanāyaka and in the colophon as Vedāntācārya

Verse count: 9 (8 of the aṣṭaka + 1 closing phalaśruti verse in a different metre)

Sections:
Stotram — the eight verses of the aṣṭaka (vv. 1–8)
Phalaśruti — the fruit of recitation, in a changed metre (v. 9)

Blurb: Deśika's hymn to the Sudarśana cakra is one of the most formally extreme pieces in the Śrīvaiṣṇava stotra corpus. Each verse is six feet of nine syllables, made of solid compound, locked in rhyming pairs, and closed by the double shout 'jaya jaya śrī sudarśana'. It is a hymn built to be chanted at speed, so that the metre itself imitates the spinning of the discus. Almost nothing in it is a sentence; it is eight verses of direct address. Its content runs from the discus as a cage of lightning-shot flame, in verse 3, through its purāṇic exploits in verses 4 and 5, namely the burning of Hara's city, the routing of Indra's foes and the cutting away of asura sorcery. It goes on to its ritual identity as the six-syllabled mantra seated in the six-spoked yantra, in verse 7. It ends in verse 8 by identifying it with the three Vedas, the three pressings, and consciousness itself. The last verse changes metre altogether and turns on a play of words: the reciter's manoratha, his chariot of desire, cannot be wrecked even on rough ground, since the foremost of wheels is guarding it.

Recension note: In verse 3 both source pages print a bracketed alternative "[parimita]" immediately after the second line — a known variant for parigata in parigatapratnavigraha, giving "whose ancient form is measured/defined" instead of "whose ancient form encompasses all". The bracket is not reproduced inside the verse text here, but the variant is recorded in the translation. Verse 5's janitamisrāvikartana is read as jani-tamisrā-vikartana, "the sun to the dark night of birth", which matches the hymn's own use of jani in verse 1 (janibhayasthānatāraṇa); some printed glosses instead split it as janita-misrā-, which yields no satisfactory sense. Verses 1–8 are in the nine-syllable rhyming feet Deśika uses nowhere else in quite this way; verse 9 changes to a different, longer metre, which is why some editions count the hymn as eight verses and print the ninth separately as a phalaśruti.
