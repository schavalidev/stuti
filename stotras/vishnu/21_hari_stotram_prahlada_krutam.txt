Title: Hari Stotram (Prahlāda-kṛtam)
Devanāgarī: श्री हरि स्तोत्रम् (प्रह्लाद कृतम्)

Blurb: Prahlāda's hymn from the Upaśama-prakaraṇa of the Yoga-vāsiṣṭha, eight verses in the swaying puṣpitāgrā metre and ending each one on the same word, prapadye — "I take refuge." What distinguishes it from the ordinary refuge-hymn is that it is built almost entirely of colour and optics: Hari is blue as a water-lily petal, black as bee, darkness and collyrium (v. 2), then in verse 4 the whole figure is resolved into an autumn night sky, his pale nails scattered as stars, his smiling face the full moon, the rays from his breast-jewel the streaming Gaṅgā overhead. Verses 7 and 8 shift to a single sustained image of ponds — Hari a killing frost on the demons' lotus-pond, an unsetting sun over the gods', the flood that fills Brahmā's, and finally the one white lotus in the pond of the three worlds.

en: He is the treasure-chamber that is the beauty of the mansion of the three worlds; he takes away every stain; he is the supreme light; he is the refuge of the refugeless, the Lord to be resorted to. In Hari, the unborn, the unfallen, the Master, I take refuge.

hi: वे त्रिभुवन-भवन की अभिराम कोशस्थली हैं, समस्त कलंकों को हरने वाले, परम प्रकाश, अशरणों के शरण, शरण्य ईश — उन अज, अच्युत, ईश्वर हरि की मैं शरण लेता हूँ।

deva:

en: The one white lotus in the lotus-pond of the three worlds; the foremost lamp against a delusion that is no less than darkness; most manifest of all, never inert, the very principle of consciousness — in Hari, who takes away all the affliction of the world, I take refuge. Thus ends the Śrī Hari Stotram made by Prahlāda, in the thirty-third sarga of the Upaśama-prakaraṇa of the Mokṣopāya, in the Śrī Vāsiṣṭha Mahārāmāyaṇa.

Recension note: This is the Yoga-vāsiṣṭha Prahlāda-stuti and is quite distinct from the better-known Hari Stotram beginning "jagajjālapālaṁ", which circulates separately (and appears on the same site under its own slug). The whole hymn is in puṣpitāgrā, with every verse closing on prapadye. Verse 4 depends on a single sustained rūpaka in which the deity and the autumn night sky are identified term for term; a translation that renders nakha, ānana and maṇi-marīci literally without marking the identification loses the verse entirely, so the correspondences are made explicit above. Both source pages carry the full Purāṇic colophon locating the hymn at Upaśama-prakaraṇa sarga 33.
