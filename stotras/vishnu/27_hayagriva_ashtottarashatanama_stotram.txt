Title: Śrī Hayagrīva Aṣṭottaraśatanāma Stotram
Devanāgarī: श्रीहयग्रीव-अष्टोत्तरशतनामस्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ హయగ్రీవ అష్టోత్తరశతనామ స్తోత్రం

Author: Anonymous; Śrī Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, per the colophon of both stotra and nāmāvalī. The viniyoga names **Saṁkarṣaṇa** as the ṛṣi of the stotra-mantra.

Source / recension: Primary text: **Gītā Press, Gorakhpur — Śatanāma Stotra Saṅgraha**, code 1850, pp. 52–56 (stotra pp. 52–54, nāmāvalī pp. 55–56), transcribed from the page images of the printed edition rather than from OCR. Entry 8 of the volume's forty. Both colophons read `इति श्रीब्रह्माण्डपुराणे`. Stotra and nāmāvalī are paired in one file as the publisher prints them, and were checked against each other name by name: the stotra lists the names in the nominative, the nāmāvalī re-inflects them into the caturthī, and **the two run in exactly the same order with no divergence and no gap — including through all six names past the hundred and eight** (see the recension note). The viniyoga bīja `ऋं`, the nāmāvalī's numbering at 109–114, and the dhyāna's `मुद्रापुस्तकशङ्खचक्रविलसच्छ्रीमद्भुजामण्डितं` were re-rendered at 400–450 dpi. Hayagrīva is filed under `vishnu/` in this corpus; that folder was checked and holds only `vishnu/11_hayagriva_ashtakam.txt`, a different work, so no collation partner was available on disk. **No second edition was collated**, and no web witness has been sought.

Recension note: **Prefix.** Gītā Press prints `ॐ` before each name; this corpus prints `श्री` for every nāmāvalī without exception (user, 2026-09-08). **THE COUNT IS 114, NOT 108, AND BOTH LISTS AGREE ON IT.** The nāmāvalī is numbered continuously 1–114 with no gap; its last entry is `११४ ॐ वेदाहरणकर्मकृते नमः`, and its colophon still calls it `अष्टोत्तरशतनामावलिः`. Counting the nominatives in the stotra itself gives the same 114, distributed 9-8-8-8-10-5-9-9-7-8-6-9-7-7-4 across vv. 2–16, in exactly the nāmāvalī's order. So this is **not** the Veṅkaṭeśa case (entry 6), where a single surplus entry sat at the very end of the list and looked like a compositor setting a closing salutation as a numbered name. Here the excess is six, it is spread through the hymn rather than appended to it, and it is attested twice over by two separately typeset lists. **The count is left at 114 because that is what the volume prints, twice**; `Name count: 114` is deliberate. The text's own claim of 108 is made in v. 17 (`नाम्नामष्टोत्तरशतं हयग्रीवस्य`) and again in v. 18 (`इदमष्टोत्तरशतं`) — the title is traditional and does not describe the list. Do not trim six rows to reach the famous number. **111 distinct names in 114 slots.** Three repeat: `शरण्य` (nos. 24, 51), `विश्वगोप्ता` (nos. 69, 106) and `पुण्यकीर्ति` (nos. 37, 95). All three repetitions are present in the stotra too, at vv. 4/8, 10/15 and 6/13 — so they are the text's, not the list-maker's, and the second occurrence is kept as printed rather than collapsed. **The viniyoga bīja is `ऋं`, and this is what the page shows.** Verified at 450 dpi: `श्रीहयग्रीवो देवता ऋं बीजं नमः शक्तिः`. The bījas usually associated with Hayagrīva elsewhere are `ह्सौं` and `ह्रीं`; `ऋं` is unusual, and is recorded here as a printed reading rather than emended — this is a hypothesis about its oddity, not a finding against the edition. **`तमोपहा` / `तमोपघ्ने` and `अमित्रहा` / `अमित्रघ्ने` are not divergences.** Both are `-हन्` stems: `तमोपहन्` and `अमित्रहन्` give nominative `-हा` in the verse and dative `-घ्ने` in the nāmāvalī, exactly as expected. **`परस्मै ज्योतिषे` (no. 40)** is printed as two words, the neuter `परं ज्योतिः` of v. 6 properly declined, and is kept that way; likewise `श्रेयसां पतये` (76) and `श्रेयसां निधये` (103). **Structure.** This is the first entry of the eight to open with a **prose viniyoga**, and the first to carry a **separate mantra** standing between the dhyāna and the stotra — `ॐ ऋग्यजुःसामरूपाय … महाश्वशिरसे नमः`, already in the dative, and two of its four epithets recur in altered form in v. 16 and as nos. 113–114 (`प्रणवोद्गीथवचसे` against `प्रणवोद्गीथरूपाय`, `वेदाहरणकर्मणे` against `वेदाहरणकर्मकृते`), while `महाश्वशिरसे` occurs nowhere in the list. Both are given their own blocks and their own section labels.

Blurb: The hymn of the horse-headed Viṣṇu who went down after the stolen Vedas and brought them back, and who is asked for one thing above all: learning. Its viniyoga says so plainly — recite it `विद्यार्थे`, for the sake of knowledge — and its promise is that even a dullard who keeps at it daily ends up the equal of Vācaspati. The names crowd around speech and scripture: lord of all the lords of speech, the treasury of the Veda, the form of the praṇava and the udgītha.
