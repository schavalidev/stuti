en: He said: Those who take the body to be the self are given many bodies, one after another, made by the good and bad acts that are mixed together under the particular guṇas beginning with sattva. Through these bodies, and through the group of six senses, which is the door to the beginningless experience of worldly life with its partings and its meetings, this world of living beings has fallen upon a road as hard to travel as a road through a fortress. It is set there by the māyā that obeys the Lord, the blessed Viṣṇu. Like a band of traders bent upon gain, it reaps the fruit of the acts done by its own body. It has gone into the forest of worldly life, which is most unholy, like a cremation ground. Its many efforts have borne no fruit, and to this day it has not found the path of those bees that hover at the lotus feet of Hari and of the guru, the one path that puts out that heat. In that forest these six, which bear the names of the senses, are robbers indeed by what they do.

en: Now, whatever part of a man's wealth serves as a means to dharma is gained with very great hardship. That dharma whose mark is the direct worship of the Supreme Person is declared by the wise to be the means of reaching the world beyond. They plunder it from the man whose leader is bad and who has not conquered himself, just as robbers plunder a band of traders.
