Blurb: This chapter begins the story of Gajendra, the king of elephants. Śuka tells King Parīkṣit of the mountain Trikūṭa in the ocean of milk, with its three peaks of silver, iron and gold, and of the garden of Varuṇa in one of its valleys, with a great lake full of lotuses. The long list of trees and birds is part of the story: it shows a world of plenty and ease. Into this garden comes the leader of an elephant herd. He is strong, proud and full of care for his family, and he plays in the water with them without a thought of danger. A crocodile seizes his foot. His kin cannot help him. The struggle lasts a thousand years, and his strength fails while the crocodile's grows. At the end the elephant understands that no one in the world can save him. He resolves to take refuge in the Lord whom even Death obeys. The hymn he then recites is the next chapter.
