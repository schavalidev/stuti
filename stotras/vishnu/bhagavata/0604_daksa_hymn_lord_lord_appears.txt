Title: Śrīmad Bhāgavata 6.4 — Dakṣa's hymn to the Lord, and the Lord appears
Devanāgarī: श्रीमद्भागवतम् – षष्ठस्कन्धः – चतुर्थोऽध्यायः
Telugu: శ్రీమద్భాగవతమ్ – షష్ఠస్కంధః – చతుర్థోఽధ్యాయః

Author: Vedavyāsa, by tradition. The Bhāgavata is spoken by the sage Śuka to King Parīkṣit on the bank of the Gaṅgā, and retold by Sūta to the sages gathered at Naimiṣāraṇya.

Recension note: Meanings written 22 Oct 2026 (session S78). The differences between the witnesses listed below were read as the verses were glossed; except where this note says otherwise, each is a spelling, a sandhi or a word order of the two editions, none of them changes the sense, and the print's reading therefore stands unbracketed. The print is followed throughout. The print numbers 54 verses and GRETIL numbers 54. Of the 54 verses GRETIL has under the same number, 42 agree with the print letter for letter once spacing and the spelling of nasals are set aside; the rest differ: 7, 11, 22, 24, 25, 27, 29, 32, 34, 35, 42, 49. The edition's footnotes give these older printed readings (प्रा० पा०), which are not bracketed into the line: at 3, यथायोगी; at 5, वृक्षेभ्यः; at 10, त्वादिष्टा; at 12, लोकानां पितरौ; at 13, भूतानां शास्तास्ते; at 26, स्वसंज्ञया; at 30, वा०. Spacing is the print's, as its text layer gives it. At 46 the print sets the verse number straight after the text with no daṇḍas; the closing ॥ is supplied. The speaker lines (श्रीशुक उवाच and the others) are printed in italic above the verse they introduce, and are given as its first line. A footnote's numeral keys it to one word, and which word that is cannot be told from the note alone, so those readings stay unbracketed until the page itself is read. Three lost spaces were restored as the verses were read, each confirmed against GRETIL. At verse 35 the space between स्तुतः and संस्तुवतः had gone, at verse 37 the space between वनमालानिवीताङ्गो and लसच्छ्रीवत्सकौस्तुभः, and at verse 51 the space between प्रजेश and प्रतिगृह्यताम्. In each the first word carries its own ending — a visarga nominative, a nominative in °ो, and a vocative — so it cannot be the first member of a compound, and this print breaks almost without exception after a visarga or a vowel; GRETIL reads all three as two words. At verses 36 and 38 GRETIL also puts a space inside one long compound (चर्मेषु धनुःपाश°, वलय नूपुर°), and there the print's joined reading stands, since the compound closes in °धरः and °भूषितः and has no word break within it. Two readings the verse alone does not settle are taken from the commentarial tradition and should be known: at verse 22 कस्य is the name Ka, which the commentators take for Dakṣa the Prajāpati, and the meaning names him so; at verse 27 पाञ्चदश्य is the fire won by the fifteen kindling mantras, and the meaning says that, since the word standing alone cannot be read. At verse 15 खिलानां is taken of the trees left unburnt.

Verse count: 54, plus the closing colophon printed unnumbered.

en: The king said: You have told me in brief of the creation of the gods, the asuras and men, of the nāgas, of beasts and birds, which took place in the manvantara of Svāyambhuva.
tel: రాజు పలికెను: స్వాయంభువ మన్వంతరమునందు జరిగిన దేవతల, అసురుల, మనుష్యుల, నాగుల, మృగముల, పక్షుల సృష్టిని నీవు సంక్షేపముగా చెప్పితివి.

--- verse 54 ---
deva:
श्री-शुक उवाच

स्वप्नोपलब्धार्थ इव तत्रैवान्तर्दधे हरिः॥
iast:
śrī-śuka uvāca

en: Śrī Śuka said: Having said this, the blessed Hari, who brings the universe into being, vanished there itself, while Dakṣa was looking on, like an object gained in a dream.
tel: శ్రీశుకుడు పలికెను: ఇట్లు పలికి, విశ్వభావనుడైన భగవంతుడు హరి, దక్షుడు చూచుచుండగనే, కలలో లభించిన వస్తువువలె అచటనే అంతర్ధానమయ్యెను.
hi: श्रीशुकदेवजी ने कहा: ऐसा कहकर विश्व के भावन भगवान् हरि, दक्ष के देखते-देखते, स्वप्न में प्राप्त वस्तु के समान वहीं अन्तर्धान हो गये।
