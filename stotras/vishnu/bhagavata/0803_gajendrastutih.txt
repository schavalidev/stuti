Blurb: This is the hymn of Gajendra, the heart of the story and one of the best-loved prayers of the Bhāgavata. Held by the crocodile and near the end of his strength, the elephant remembers a prayer he had learned in an earlier life as a king. It names no god and asks for no particular form. It praises the Lord as the origin of consciousness in all beings, the ground in which the world appears and disappears, the One who sees all, and the One who remains when every name and quality has been set aside. Its central verses are salutations, and its turning point comes at verse 25, where Gajendra says that he no longer wishes to live on in this body; he asks to be freed from ignorance, not only from the crocodile. Because the hymn was addressed to no single god, none of the gods came. The Lord who is all the gods came himself on Garuḍa, drew the elephant out of the lake, and cut open the crocodile's mouth with his discus. The hymn is recited daily by many, often at dawn, and the next chapter gives the Lord's own promise to those who do so.
