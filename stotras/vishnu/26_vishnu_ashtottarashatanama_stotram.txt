Title: Śrī Viṣṇu Aṣṭottaraśatanāma Stotram
Devanāgarī: श्रीविष्णु-अष्टोत्तरशतनामस्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ విష్ణు అష్టోత్తరశతనామ స్తోత్రం

Author: Anonymous; Padma Mahāpurāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa. The closing verses address a `विप्रेन्द्र`, "best of brahmins", so the hymn is framed as instruction given to a sage.

Source / recension: Primary text: **Gītā Press, Gorakhpur — Śatanāma Stotra Saṅgraha**, code 1850, pp. 36–39 (stotra pp. 36–37, nāmāvalī pp. 38–39), transcribed from the page images of the printed edition rather than from OCR. Entry 5 of the volume's forty. The colophon of both stotra and nāmāvalī reads `इति श्रीपद्मपुराणे उत्तरखण्डे`. Stotra and nāmāvalī are paired in one file as the publisher prints them. Cross-checked internally: the 108 names of the nāmāvalī correspond in order to the name-sequence running from v. 2 to v. 15a, and that internal check is the collation this file rests on. Checked against `vishnu/25_vishnu_sahasranama_stotram.txt` for overlap: the two are unrelated compositions and share only the common stock of Vaiṣṇava epithets — this is not an extract from the thousand, unlike `ganesha/30`. **No second edition was collated**, and no web witness has been sought; the readings rest on Gītā Press alone.

Recension note: **Prefix.** Gītā Press prints `ॐ` before each name; this corpus prints `श्री` for every nāmāvalī without exception (user, 2026-09-08). The divergence is recorded here rather than in the rows. **Case.** Every name is in the caturthī (dative), the form `नमः` governs; thirty-four of the 108 re-inflect irregularly. Only the printed dative is recorded. **`सहस्रशीर्षे` (48) — a conclusion revised.** The recension note of `ganesha/30` called the identical form at its no. 92 "anomalous and possibly a compositor's error", since the stem `सहस्रशीर्षन्` (cf. v. 7 here, `सहस्रशीर्षा`) would give the dative `सहस्रशीर्ष्णे`. Its recurrence here, in a second and independently typeset nāmāvalī of a different text, settles the matter: **it is the volume's consistent practice, not a slip.** Verified at 500 dpi against the page image — there is no `ण्`. `ganesha/30` has been corrected accordingly. **`सहस्रपादे` (51)** is likewise the volume's regular dative of the root-noun `सहस्रपाद्` (cf. v. 7, `सहस्रपात्`), now attested in four files. **Three names repeat.** `धरणीधर` at nos. 30 and 43 (from vv. 5 and 6), `सनातन` at nos. 34 and 96 (from vv. 5 and 13), and `मधुसूदन` at nos. 79 and 108 (from vv. 11 and 15), so the list holds 105 distinct names in 108 slots, as printed — the highest count of repetitions in the volume so far. **`रुद्रमूर्ती` (v. 15)** is printed with a long final `ī`, as if from an in-stem, while the nāmāvalī inflects the name as an i-stem (`रुद्रमूर्तये`, no. 106); both retained as printed, the same verse/list divergence recorded at `shiva/30` and `shiva/31`. **Numbering.** v. 17 is a **tristich** — three lines under one number — where every other verse is a distich; the volume does not renumber. **`अदितेः सुताय` (9)** keeps the genitive of the verse (`अदितेः सुतः`) intact inside the row, as the volume prints it.

Blurb: A short, plain hundred and eight, and the most Rāma-inflected of the Vaiṣṇava name-hymns in the corpus — Kausalyā's joy, Jānakī's beloved, the enemy of Rāvaṇa and destroyer of the rākṣasa line stand beside Kṛṣṇa's Mathurā and Yaśodā, and Śiva's names are folded in too (haraḥ, rudrātmakaḥ, rudramūrtī). It promises what its length suggests: recite it at dawn with a steady mind and the heaps of calamity fall away.
