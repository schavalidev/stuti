Sections:
Stotram — invocation, the sixteen weapons one by one, and the summary (vv. 1–18)
Samāpti — the author's signature verse (v. 19)

Blurb: This is Deśika's benediction upon the sixteen weapons carried by the sixteen-armed Sudarśana. An opening verse calls them fashioned out of portions of his own will. The hymn then gives one anuṣṭubh to each: the discus, the axe, the spear, the staff, the goad, fire, the sword, the śakti, the conch, the bow, the noose, the plough, the thunderbolt, the mace, the pestle and the trident. Every one of them ends in a blessing built from a play upon the weapon's own name. May the daṇḍa punish, daṇḍayet, your foes. May the gadā quiet your diseases, gada. May the triśūla leave you without a thorn, viśūlatā. Beneath the wordplay runs a serious Sāṁkhya and Pāñcarātra scheme, in which the weapons are cosmic principles. The śakti is the power of action. The Śārṅga bow is sāttvika ahaṅkāra, with the senses for its arrows. The mace is buddhi, and the power of universal dissolution. One deft aside in verse 14 quotes the Gītā's line 'among weapons I am the vajra', only to add that this thunderbolt, unlike Indra's, was never made from Dadhīci's bones.

en: It is the one sung of in the words "among weapons I am the vajra" — and this thunderbolt, unlike Indra's, did not arise from the bones of Dadhīci: may that vajra of the Lord of Weapons protect you.

---
Recension note: The sixteen weapons occupy vv. 2–17 in order — cakra, paraśu, kunta, daṇḍa, aṅkuśa, agni, nistriṁśa (sword), śakti, śaṅkha, dhanus, pāśa, sīra (plough), vajra, gadā, musala, triśūla — with v. 1 as invocation, v. 18 as summary and v. 19 as the author's signature; hence 19 verses for sixteen weapons. Verse 14 quotes Bhagavad-gītā 10.28 ("āyudhānām ahaṁ vajram") and then distinguishes this vajra from Indra's by the negative compound adadhīcyasthisambhavaḥ. Two printing details are preserved from the source pages rather than normalized: the space in yatprasūti śatairāsan (v. 3) and the unsandhied viśvaṁ āyudhaiḥ in v. 18. This hymn is distinct from the five-weapon Pañcāyudha Stotram already in this collection: there the weapons are Viṣṇu's five, here they are the sixteen of the Sudarśana mūrti.
