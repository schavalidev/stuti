Title: Navagraha Gāyatrī Mantrāḥ — the Gāyatrī mantras of the nine planets
Devanāgarī: नवग्रह गायत्री मन्त्राः
Telugu: నవగ్రహ గాయత్రీ మంత్రాః

Author: No single author. These are purāṇic and tāntric mantras. The independent witness used here names the Liṅga Purāṇa, the Mantra-mahārṇava and the Gāyatrī-mahātantra as their sources. They are not Vedic and they carry no accent.

Source / recension: Base text: **stotranidhi.com**, `https://stotranidhi.com/hi/navagraha-gayatri-mantra-in-sanskrit/` (thirty-two mantras, unaccented). Independent collation: **sanskritdocuments.org**, `doc_z_misc_navagraha/navagrahagAyatrI.itx`, encoded by Sunder Hattangadi, whose header names the Liṅga Purāṇa, the Mantra-mahārṇava and the Gāyatrī-mahātantra. That is a different editorial lineage, drawn from printed purāṇic and tāntric sources, and it carries forty-three mantras. **Result of the collation: fifteen of the base text's thirty-two agree with it word for word; nine more agree in substance with a difference of one word; eight are carried by the base text alone.** Every one of the three groups is itemised in the note below, so a reader can see which mantra rests on how many witnesses. Gītā Press was checked on archive.org and publishes no collection of navagraha gāyatrīs; its gāyatrī titles are on the Sāvitrī Gāyatrī and on sandhyā.

Recension note: **These mantras are not Vedic and are printed without accent in both witnesses.** No accent mark has been added. **The fifteen that agree exactly** are units 2, 4, 7, 8, 10, 12, 17, 19, 20, 24, 27, 28, 30, 31 and 32. **The nine that differ by one word.** Unit 3: the witness prints `आदित्याय च विद्महे`, with `च`. Unit 5: the witness ends `तन्न आदित्यः` where the base text ends `तन्नः सूर्यः`; both name the sun. Unit 14: the witness reads `वाणेशाय` for `बाणेशाय`, and both readings are printed in the line. Unit 15: the witness reads `सुखहस्ताय` for `शुकहस्ताय`, and both are printed in the line. Unit 18: the witness reads `क्रुनिहस्ताय`, which is not a word, against the base text's `घृणिहस्ताय`; the base reading stands and the other is not printed in the line, because a corrupt reading is not a variant. Unit 21: the witness reads `भार्गवाय च विद्महे दानवार्चिताय`. Unit 22: the witness reads `सूर्यपुत्राय` for `छायापुत्राय`; Śani is the son of Sūrya and of Chāyā, so both are true of him, and the base reading stands. Unit 25: the witness ends `तन्नः शनैश्चरः` for `तन्नः शनिः`. Unit 29: the witness reads `धूम्रवर्णाय विद्महे विकृताननाय`. **The eight carried by the base text alone** are units 1, 6, 9, 11, 13, 16, 23 and 26. They are printed because the base text is a published collection and the rule of this corpus is to report what the source yields, but a reader should know that no second edition was found for them. Unit 26 deserves a word: it addresses Rāhu as `भृगुपुत्र`, and `भृगुपुत्र` is elsewhere a name of Śukra. The reading is given as the base text prints it and is not corrected. **The witness carries eleven mantras that the base text does not**, among them the Sāvitrī Gāyatrī itself under Sūrya, and further alternatives for Candra, Bhauma, Budha, Guru, Śukra, Śani and Ketu. They are not printed here, since this file follows its base text. **Cross-references:** the tāntric chapter of fifty-four deity gāyatrīs from the Mantra-mahārṇava stands at `devi/gayatri/17_gayatri_patala_mantra_maharnava.txt`; its Sūrya, Candra and Bhauma mantras overlap with this file's, and neither file supersedes the other. The Sūrya gāyatrī of unit 5 also stands in the Vedic garland at `veda/taittiriya/06_gayatri_mala_mantrah.txt` (unit 10) and in `puja/smarta/11_surya_nitya_puja.txt`, each in its own wording. Neither file is altered by this one.

Verse count: 32 — one for the nine planets together, then four for Sūrya, four for Candra, three for Aṅgāraka, three for Budha, three for Bṛhaspati, three for Śukra, four for Śani, three for Rāhu and four for Ketu. The planets are nine but the mantras are more, because several are given for each planet and a worshipper recites the one their teacher has given them.

Blurb: These are the Gāyatrī mantras of the nine planets. Each keeps the shape of the Sāvitrī Gāyatrī — first "may we know", then "may we meditate", then "may that deity impel us" — and fills it with the name, the weapon and the banner of one planet. They are used in navagraha pūjā, in graha-śānti and in the japa prescribed for a planet whose period is running. More than one mantra is given for most of the planets.

--- verse 1 | section: नवग्रहाः ---
deva:
विश्वमण्डलाय विद्महे नवस्थानाय धीमहि ।
तन्नो ग्रहाः प्रचोदयात् ॥
iast:
viśvamaṇḍalāya vidmahe navasthānāya dhīmahi |
tanno grahāḥ pracodayāt ||
en: The nine together. May we know him whose circle is the whole universe. May we meditate on him who stands in nine places. May the planets impel us.
tel: నవగ్రహములు. విశ్వమండలము కలవానిని ఎరుగుదుము. తొమ్మిది స్థానములలో ఉండువానిని ధ్యానించుదుము. ఆ గ్రహములు మమ్ము ప్రేరేపించుగాక.
hi: नवग्रह। विश्वमण्डल वाले को हम जानें। नौ स्थानों में स्थित का हम ध्यान करें। वे ग्रह हमें प्रेरित करें।
