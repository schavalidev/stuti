Title: Śrī Sūrya Aṣṭottaraśatanāma Stotram (Mahābhārata)
Devanāgarī: श्रीसूर्य-अष्टोत्तरशतनामस्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ సూర్య అష్టోత్తరశతనామ స్తోత్రం

Author: The **Mahābhārata, Vana-parva (Āraṇyaka-parvan) adhyāya 3** — taught by the purohita **Dhaumya** to Yudhiṣṭhira at the start of the forest exile, so that he could feed the brāhmaṇas who followed him; Sūrya, pleased, gives him the akṣaya-pātra in the next chapter. The Gītā Press colophon: `इति श्रीमहाभारते वनपर्वणि धौम्ययुधिष्ठिरसंवादे`.

Source / recension: Primary text: **Gītā Press, Gorakhpur — Śatanāma Stotra Saṅgraha**, code 1850, pp. 87–90 (stotra pp. 87–88, nāmāvalī pp. 89–90; PDF pp. 88–91), transcribed from the page images of the printed edition. Entry 15 of the volume's forty. **Collated against the Mahābhārata critical edition (Poona, BORI), Āraṇyaka-parvan 3.3.18–33, as input on GRETIL** (`gretil/1_sanskr/2_epic/mbh/mbh_03_u.htm`) — a genuinely independent editorial lineage, and the one the CLAUDE.md epic rule names. The two texts are **the vulgate and the critical edition of the same passage**, and they differ substantially; every difference is listed below. sanskritdocuments.org's `sUrya108nAmastotra` was checked and is **a different work** (an Atharvaṇa-rahasya stotra beginning `aruṇāya śaraṇyāya`), so it is not a witness here. Stotra and nāmāvalī are paired in one file as the publisher prints them.

Recension note: **Gītā Press followed throughout; the critical edition (CE) is recorded, not adopted.** The Gītā Press text is the vulgate, and several of its lines are ones the CE relegates to its apparatus. **Differences (Gītā Press → CE 3.3):** v. 3 `वरुणो` → `vaiśravaṇo` (so the CE has Kubera where Gītā Press has Varuṇa — and Gītā Press then has Varuṇa twice, in vv. 3 and 7, which is why the nāmāvalī lists `वरुणाय` at both no. 36 and no. 65); v. 5 `सर्वमलाश्रयः` → `sarvāmarāśrayaḥ` ("refuge of every impurity" against "refuge of all the immortals"); v. 5 `क्षपा यामस्तथा क्षणः` → `pakṣā māsā ṛtus tathā`; v. 7 `कालाध्यक्षः` → `lokādhyakṣaḥ`; v. 8 `सर्वलोकनमस्कृतः` → `sarvabhūtaniṣevitaḥ`; **v. 8b `स्रष्टा संवर्तको वह्निः सर्वस्यादिरलोलुपः` and v. 9a `अनन्तः कपिलो भानुः` are not in the CE text** — they are its starred passage 3.*19, so the CE count of names is lower; v. 9 `सर्वधातुनिषेचिता` → CE apparatus *20 `sarvadhātuniṣevitaḥ` ("served by all elements"); v. 10 `मनःसुपर्णो` → `maṇiḥ suvarṇo` ("jewel, gold"), and CE `prāṇadhāraṇaḥ` for `प्राणधारकः`; v. 12 `मैत्रेयः करुणान्वितः` → `maitreṇa vapuṣānvitaḥ`; v. 13 `सूर्यस्यामिततेजसः … प्रोक्तमेतत् स्वयम्भुवा` → `sūryasyaiva mahātmanaḥ … śakreṇoktaṃ mahātmanā` (Śakra, not Brahmā, speaks the names), and **the CE's next verse, 3.3.30 — the transmission Śakra → Nārada → Dhaumya → Yudhiṣṭhira — is absent from Gītā Press**; v. 14 `प्रणिपतितोऽस्मि हिताय भास्करम्` → `tvam api manasy abhidhehi bhāskaram` (a first-person bow against an instruction to the hearer); v. 15 `पुत्रदारान्` → `putralābhaṃ`, `धृतिं` → `smṛtiṃ`, `स विन्दते पुमान्` → `sa vindate parām`; v. 16 `विमुच्यते` → `sa mucyate`. **The dhyāna and its Hindi footnote are the volume's, not the Mahābhārata's.** **THE NĀMĀVALĪ RUNS TO 110, NOT 108**, numbered 1–110, and the stotra says `नामाष्टशतकं` (108). It is not all distinct: **`वरुणाय` appears twice** (nos. 36, 65). Several list entries fuse two names into one number — no. 49 `सर्वमलाश्रयाय कलये`, no. 56 `कालचक्राय विभावसवे`, no. 57 `शाश्वताय पुरुषाय`, no. 50 `कलाकाष्ठामुहूर्तेभ्यो` (three units of time, plural), no. 98 `पितृमातृपितामहेभ्यो` (three, plural) — while no. 88 keeps `मनःसुपर्णाय` as one. All are kept exactly as numbered. `Name count:` reports 110, as the list yields. **Prefix.** Gītā Press prints `ॐ` before each name; this corpus prints `श्री` for every nāmāvalī without exception (user, 2026-09-08). **Cross-reference:** `navagraha/13_aditya_dvadashanama_stotram.txt` and `navagraha/29_surya_sahasranama_stotram.txt` are separate Sūrya name-hymns; no overlap in text was found.

Blurb: This is the oldest hymn of its kind in the book: the Mahābhārata's list of the Sun's hundred and eight names, which Dhaumya taught Yudhiṣṭhira in the forest so that he could feed the brāhmaṇas who had followed him into exile. It is less a portrait than an inventory of the world — the five elements, the planets, the three fires, the four ages, the units of time from the year down to the instant, father, mother and grandfather, and the doors of heaven, of offspring and of release — all of it named as the Sun.
