Title: Sūrya-grahaṇa Śānti Ślokāḥ — the verses said at a solar eclipse
Devanāgarī: सूर्यग्रहण शान्ति श्लोकाः
Telugu: సూర్యగ్రహణ శాంతి శ్లోకాలు

Author: No single author. The eight verses are Purāṇic. They stand as **Matsya Purāṇa, adhyāya 67, verses 9 to 16**, within that chapter's rite for the quieting of an eclipse, and they are quoted from there into the dharmaśāstra and jyotiṣa nibandha literature — the Śānti-Kusumākara and the Bṛhaddaivajñarañjana both carry them. The opening verse is a separate prayer in the Indravajrā metre and is not part of that chapter.

Recension / paddhati: not śākhā-specific. This is a naimittika rite — one performed on an occasion, not daily — and its authority is a Purāṇa and the nibandhas that quote it, so it belongs to no one branch of the Veda. It carries no accent, and none is to be supplied: these are Purāṇic ślokas, not Vedic mantras.

Source / recension: base text `https://stotranidhi.com/hi/surya-grahana-shanti-parihara-shloka-in-sanskrit/` (fetched 2026-09-12; Devanāgarī, nine verses, unaccented).

Independent collation: **four witnesses in four separate lineages — the Purāṇa itself, and three dharmaśāstra nibandhas that quote it, one of them in a dated printed edition. Between them they settle the text, and they explain why this set exists in two forms.**

**(1) The Matsya Purāṇa itself, adhyāya 67**, in the GRETIL electronic text (`gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de`, `sa_matsyapurANa1-176.txt`, 1,147,983 bytes, fetched 2026-09-12; the file's header names its base printing as Calcutta, Caukhamba Vidyabhavan, 1954, entered by Oliver Hellwig). This is the scriptural source, in a scholarly lineage entirely separate from any devotional website. **It carries all eight verses, at 67.9 to 67.16, and one more that the base text does not have.** It gives them in the **lunar** form throughout — `candroparāga`, the eclipse of the moon.

**(2) The Bṛhaddaivajñarañjana**, a jyotiṣa nibandha, in the Wikimedia transcription at `sa.wikisource.org/wiki/बृहद्दैवज्ञरञ्जनम्/ग्रहणप्रकरणम्` (fetched 2026-09-12 through the MediaWiki API; 14,982 Devanāgarī characters). It carries all eight verses and the ninth, in the lunar form. **It is the witness that most often agrees with the base text where the Purāṇa does not** — `शक्रः` at the first verse, `लोकानां` at Yama, `प्रलयानलसन्निभः` at Nirṛti, and `सदा मकरवाहनः` at Varuṇa, all as the base text prints them.

**(3) The Śānti-Kusumākara**, a dharmaśāstra nibandha, as printed at `aupasana.com/prayoga/prayoga/kusumakara/uparaga_shanti.html` under the heading `अथ उपरागशान्तिः (शान्तिकुसुमाकरे)` (fetched 2026-09-12). This is a ritual manual rather than a scripture, and **it is much the closer of the two to the base text** — it agrees with it word for word in several places where the Purāṇa does not. It gives the verses in the **solar** form, `sūryoparāga`, as the base text does.

**(4) The Nirṇayasindhu of Kamalākara Bhaṭṭa, printed by the Nirṇaya Sāgara Press, Mumbai, 1901**, in the eclipse section — read from the page-image OCR on archive.org (item `NZxT_nirnaya-sindhu-by-shri-kamalakara-bhatta-mumbai-1901-nirnaya-sagara-press`, the `_djvu.txt` fetched 2026-09-12 through the metadata `server` and `dir` fields, 3,195,669 bytes; running head `निर्णयसिन्धौ [ग्रहणनिर्णये`, printed side-heading `अथ ग्रहण-शान्तिः`, closing `इति ग्रहणशान्तिः`). **This is the only witness here that is a dated print from a named press**, and it is the most important of the four for that reason. The same work in the 1892 Gyan Sagar Press printing (`SUdd_nirnaya-sindhu-of-kamlakar-bhatt-ed-babu-shastri-gokhale-mumbai-1892-gyan-sagar-press`) carries the same passage; the two printings are one work and count as one witness.

**What the Nirṇayasindhu settles, and where it stands apart.** It prints all eight verses in order, in the **lunar** form, and adds the same ninth verse the Purāṇa has. It introduces them by naming their source — `मात्स्ये`, "in the Matsya" — which is **an independent printed confirmation of the Purāṇic attribution this file already rests on**. Two of its readings are not variants but a different line. At Nirṛti it prints `रक्षोगणाधिपः साक्षान्नीलाञ्जनः समप्रभः`, "black as collyrium", where the base text prints `प्रलयानलसन्निभः`, "like the fire of dissolution"; and it continues `खड्गहस्तोऽतिभीमश्च`, which the base text does not have. At Śiva it prints `योऽसाविन्दुधरो देवः पिनाकी वृषवाहनः`, where the base text prints `योऽसौ शूलधरो रुद्रः शङ्करो वृषवाहनः`. It also lacks the opening prayer `इन्द्रोऽनलो दण्डधरश्च रक्षः` entirely — searched for under three separate keys across the whole volume, with no hit — and puts in its place two verses calling the waters into the pots. **None of these was adopted. The base text's readings stand and the differences are recorded.** The page is nineteenth-century Devanāgarī print read through uncorrected OCR, so single letters in it are decoder noise rather than readings, and no reading was taken from it on that basis alone.

**Why the same verses appear in two forms — the Kusumākara says so itself.** After the first verse it prints a footnote: `अत्र शान्तिश्चन्द्रग्रहार्थं यदि तदा सूर्यपदस्थाने चन्द्रपदं निवेशनीयम्। एवमुत्तरत्रपि॥` — "here, if the śānti is for an eclipse of the moon, then in place of the word *sūrya* the word *candra* is to be put; and so in what follows." **The solar and the lunar sets are one text with a substitution the tradition itself directs**, which is why the Purāṇa reads *candra* throughout and the manuals read *sūrya*. Nothing was harmonised: the base text's solar readings stand, and the Purāṇa's lunar ones are recorded below.

**A caution about two other pages.** `vignanam.org/devanagari/surya-grahana-shanti-parihara-slokas.html` carries this text and was **rejected as a witness**: set beside the base text and normalised for digits, the two are **character-identical**, including, at the same word in verse 5, a zero-width joiner standing between the virāma of `निर्` and the `ऋ` of `ऋतिः`, which both share. That is one text published twice, not two lineages, and it is recorded here so that no later session mistakes it for a collation. **Gītā Press was checked and does not apply**: its *Vrata Paricaya* has an eclipse-śānti entry, but it prescribes a different mantra — `तमोमय महाभीम सोमसूर्यविमर्दन। हेमनागप्रदानेन मम शान्तिप्रदो भव॥` — so it witnesses the observance and not these verses.

Corrections applied to the base text: **one.** A zero-width joiner (U+200D) standing between the virāma of `निर्` and the `ऋ` of `ऋतिः` at verse 5 was removed. It is a rendering hack that prevents a ligature and spells no sound, and the corpus holds no zero-width joiners in its text fields. No reading was changed.

Recension note: **every difference found between the three texts is listed here, and none has been reconciled away. The base text's readings stand throughout.**

**(1) Verse 2, the first of the eight.** The base text reads `सहस्रनयनः शक्रः`, and **the Bṛhaddaivajñarañjana reads the same**, `सहस्रनयनः शक्रो`. The Kusumākara reads `सहस्रनयनः सूर्यः`; the Purāṇa reads `सहस्रनयनश्चेन्द्रः`. Four witnesses, three readings, all naming the same god.

**(2) Verse 3, Agni — a whole half-line.** The base text, the Kusumākara and the Purāṇa all read `मुखं यः सर्वदेवानां सप्तार्चिरमितद्युतिः`. **The Bṛhaddaivajñarañjana reads something else entirely** — `चतुःशृंगः सप्तहस्तः त्रिपादो मेषवाहनः`, "four-horned, seven-handed, three-footed, riding the ram", which is the Vedic description of Agni. That is a real difference of recension, not a variant word, and it is recorded and not adopted.

**(3) Verse 4, Yama.** The base text, the Kusumākara and the Bṛhaddaivajñarañjana agree on `लोकानां`. The Purāṇa reads `भूतानां`. On the next word the base text and the Kusumākara read `यमो` where the Purāṇa and the Bṛhaddaivajñarañjana read `धर्मो`, naming Yama in the following line instead. Not adopted.

**(4) Verse 5, Nirṛti — four readings of one line.** The base text reads `उग्रः करालो निर्ऋतिः ग्रहपीडां व्यपोहतु`; the Kusumākara reads `कराळो निरृतिः सूर्यग्रहपीडां व्यपोहतु`, without `उग्रः`; the Purāṇa reads `खड्गव्यग्रोऽतिभीमश्च रक्षःपीडां व्यपोहतु` and the Bṛhaddaivajñarañjana `खड्गचर्मातिकायश्च रक्षःपीडां व्यपोहतु`, neither of which names Nirṛti at all. **The base text is also alone against both in reading `प्रलयानलसन्निभः`** — like the *fire* at the end of an age — where the Kusumākara reads `प्रळयानिलसन्निभः`, like the *wind*. **Both the Purāṇa and the Bṛhaddaivajñarañjana read the fire**, `प्रलयानलसन्निभः`, and so agree with the base text; the Kusumākara stands alone. The base reading therefore has three witnesses, and the Kusumākara's is recorded rather than corrected.

**(5) Verse 6, Varuṇa.** The base text reads `नागपाशधरो देवः सदा मकरवाहनः । वरुणो जललोकेशो`, and **the Bṛhaddaivajñarañjana reads the first line identically**, `सदा` included, then `वरुणोऽम्बुपतिः साक्षात्`. The Kusumākara reads `नित्यं मकरवाहनः । सलिलाधिपतिः`; the Purāṇa reads `साक्षान्मकरवाहनः । स जलाधिपतिः`. The base text and the Bṛhaddaivajñarañjana are the two that name Varuṇa outright.

**(6) Verse 7, Vāyu.** The base text reads `यः प्राणरूपो लोकानां`; the Kusumākara and the Bṛhaddaivajñarañjana both read `प्राणरूपो हि लोकानां`; the Purāṇa reads `प्राणरूपेण यो लोकान् पाति`.

**(7) Verse 8, Kubera.** The base text and the Kusumākara agree exactly on `खड्गशूलधरो वरः`. The Purāṇa and the Bṛhaddaivajñarañjana both read `खड्गशूलगदाधरः`, adding the mace. Not adopted.

**(8) Verse 9, Śiva.** The base text and the Kusumākara agree exactly on `योऽसौ शूलधरो रुद्रः शङ्करो वृषवाहनः`. The Purāṇa reads `योऽसाविन्दुधरो देवः पिनाकी वृषवाहनः` and the Bṛhaddaivajñarañjana `योऽसौ बिन्दुधरो देवः पिनाकी वृषवाहनः` — the bearer of the moon, holder of the Pināka. The closing half-lines differ in all four: the base text `दोषं नाशयतु द्रुतम्`, the Kusumākara `विनाशयतु सर्वदा`, the Purāṇa `विनाशयतु शंकरः`, the Bṛhaddaivajñarañjana `स नाशयतु शंकरः`.

**(9) All three witnesses carry a ninth verse that the base text does not.** The Purāṇa's 67.17 reads `त्रैलोक्ये यानि भूतानि स्थावराणि चराणि च । ब्रह्मविष्ण्वर्कयुक्तानि तानि पापं दहन्तु वै`; the Kusumākara prints it with `ब्रह्मेशविष्णुयुक्तानि तान्यायान्तु हरन्त्वघम्`; the Bṛhaddaivajñarañjana with `ब्रह्मार्कविष्णुयुक्तानि तानि पापं दहन्तु मे`. **It was not imported.** This file prints what its base text prints.

**(10) The opening verse of this file has no second witness.** `इन्द्रोऽनलो दण्डधरश्च रक्षः प्राचेतसो वायु कुबेर शर्वाः । मज्जन्म ऋक्षे मम राशि संस्थे सूर्योपरागं शमयन्तु सर्वे` is in a different metre from the eight and is absent from both the Purāṇa chapter and the Kusumākara page, though the Kusumākara opens its rite with the same thought in other words — `यस्य राशिं समासाद्य ग्रहणं चन्द्रसूर्ययोः` — and the Purāṇa does the same at 67.2. It rests on the base text alone. It rests on the base text alone and that is said here plainly rather than passed over.

**(11) All three witnesses set these verses inside a much larger rite** — the setting up of four pots of water, earth gathered from eight places, the invocation of the guardians of the quarters into the pots, the writing of the mantras on a plate of gold, silver or copper to be bound on the head, a homa of sesame with the vyāhṛtis, and the bathing of the worshipper with the water of the pots. The Bṛhaddaivajñarañjana adds the making of a golden image of the serpent. **None of that was imported.** The base text gives the verses alone, for a worshipper who says them at home, and that is what this file is.

Not collated: verse 1, for the reason given at (9) above.

Verse count: 10 — one opening prayer, eight verses to the guardians of the eight quarters, and the closing peace.

Sections:
शान्तिश्लोकः — The verse for peace (verse 1)
ग्रहपीडापरिहारश्लोकाः — The verses that remove the affliction (verses 2–10)

Blurb: These verses are said at the time of a solar eclipse. An eclipse was held to trouble those in whose birth-star or zodiacal sign it falls, and these verses ask that the trouble be lifted. The first verse calls on the eight guardians of the quarters together. The eight that follow call on them one at a time — Indra who holds the thunderbolt, Agni who is the mouth of the gods, Yama who rides the buffalo and witnesses every deed, Nirṛti who is like the fire at the end of an age, Varuṇa who holds the serpent-noose and rides the sea-creature, Vāyu who is the breath of the worlds, Kubera the lord of treasures, and Śiva who rides the bull. Each is asked to remove the affliction that the eclipse has raised. The set closes with the threefold prayer for peace. The same verses are said at an eclipse of the moon, with the word for the moon put in place of the word for the sun.

rakṣo gaṇādhipaḥ sākṣāt pralayānalasannibhaḥ |
ugraḥ karālo nirṛtiḥ grahapīḍāṁ vyapohatu || 4
en: The lord of the host of the rākṣasas himself, who is like the fire at the end of an age, fierce and terrible — may Nirṛti remove the affliction of the planet.
tel: రాక్షస గణములకు అధిపతి, ప్రళయాగ్నితో సమానుడు, ఉగ్రుడు, కరాళుడు అయిన నిరృతి — గ్రహపీడను తొలగించును గాక.
hi: राक्षसों के गणाधिप, प्रलयाग्नि के समान, उग्र और कराल निर्ऋति — वे ग्रहपीड़ा दूर करें।

--- verse 6 | section: ग्रहपीडापरिहारश्लोकाः ---
deva:
नागपाशधरो देवः सदा मकरवाहनः ।
वरुणो जललोकेशो ग्रहपीडां व्यपोहतु ॥ ५
iast:
nāgapāśadharo devaḥ sadā makaravāhanaḥ |
varuṇo jalalokeśo grahapīḍāṁ vyapohatu || 5
en: The god who holds the noose of the serpent, who always rides the sea-creature, Varuṇa the lord of the world of waters — may he remove the affliction of the planet.
tel: నాగపాశము ధరించినవాడు, ఎల్లప్పుడు మకర వాహనుడు, జల లోకమునకు ఈశుడైన వరుణుడు — గ్రహపీడను తొలగించును గాక.
hi: जो नागपाश धारण करते हैं, सदा मकर पर सवार रहते हैं, जल लोक के ईश वरुण हैं — वे ग्रहपीड़ा दूर करें।

--- verse 7 | section: ग्रहपीडापरिहारश्लोकाः ---
deva:
यः प्राणरूपो लोकानां वायुः कृष्णमृगप्रियः ।
चन्द्रसूर्योपरागोत्थां ग्रहपीडां व्यपोहतु ॥ ६
iast:
yaḥ prāṇarūpo lokānāṁ vāyuḥ kṛṣṇamṛgapriyaḥ |
candrasūryoparāgotthāṁ grahapīḍāṁ vyapohatu || 6
en: He who is the very breath of the worlds, Vāyu to whom the black antelope is dear — may he remove the affliction that has risen from the eclipse of the moon or the sun.
tel: లోకములకు ప్రాణ స్వరూపుడు, కృష్ణ మృగము ప్రియమైన వాయువు — చంద్ర సూర్య గ్రహణము వలన కలిగిన గ్రహపీడను తొలగించును గాక.
hi: जो लोकों के प्राणरूप हैं, जिन्हें कृष्णमृग प्रिय है, वे वायु — चंद्र और सूर्य के ग्रहण से उत्पन्न ग्रहपीड़ा दूर करें।

--- verse 8 | section: ग्रहपीडापरिहारश्लोकाः ---
deva:
योऽसौ निधिपतिर्देवः खड्गशूलधरो वरः ।
चन्द्रसूर्योपरागोत्थां कलुषं मे व्यपोहतु ॥ ७
iast:
yo'sau nidhipatirdevaḥ khaḍgaśūladharo varaḥ |
candrasūryoparāgotthāṁ kaluṣaṁ me vyapohatu || 7
en: The god who is the lord of treasures, the excellent one who holds the sword and the spear — may he remove from me the stain that has risen from the eclipse of the moon or the sun.
tel: నిధులకు అధిపతి, ఖడ్గము శూలము ధరించిన శ్రేష్ఠుడైన దేవుడు — చంద్ర సూర్య గ్రహణము వలన కలిగిన కలుషమును నా నుండి తొలగించును గాక.
hi: जो निधियों के स्वामी हैं, खड्ग और शूल धारण करने वाले श्रेष्ठ देव हैं — वे चंद्र और सूर्य के ग्रहण से उत्पन्न कलुष को मुझसे दूर करें।

--- verse 9 | section: ग्रहपीडापरिहारश्लोकाः ---
deva:
योऽसौ शूलधरो रुद्रः शङ्करो वृषवाहनः ।
चन्द्रसूर्योपरागोत्थां दोषं नाशयतु द्रुतम् ॥ ८
iast:
yo'sau śūladharo rudraḥ śaṅkaro vṛṣavāhanaḥ |
candrasūryoparāgotthāṁ doṣaṁ nāśayatu drutam || 8
en: Rudra who holds the spear, Śaṅkara who rides the bull — may he quickly destroy the fault that has risen from the eclipse of the moon or the sun.
tel: శూలధరుడైన రుద్రుడు, వృష వాహనుడైన శంకరుడు — చంద్ర సూర్య గ్రహణము వలన కలిగిన దోషమును వెంటనే నశింపజేయును గాక.
hi: शूलधारी रुद्र, वृषभ पर सवार शंकर — वे चंद्र और सूर्य के ग्रहण से उत्पन्न दोष को शीघ्र नष्ट करें।

--- verse 10 | section: ग्रहपीडापरिहारश्लोकाः ---
deva:
ओं शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः ।
iast:
oṁ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ |
en: Om, peace, peace, peace.
tel: ఓం, శాంతి, శాంతి, శాంతి.
hi: ओम्, शांति, शांति, शांति।
