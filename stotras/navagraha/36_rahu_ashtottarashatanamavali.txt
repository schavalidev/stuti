Source / recension: Primary text: **a Telugu-script nāmāvalī collection, Smārtha e-books (smarthamitra.in), "108–1008"** (scanned PDF, page images only, PDF metadata author "kprss", 2016), printed pp. 245–247 (PDF pp. 247–249; the scan omits printed pp. 242–243, so the offset here is +2). **Not a Gītā Press edition; Gītā Press's *Śatanāma Stotra Saṅgraha* (code 1850) does not contain this text.** Transcribed from the Telugu page images into Devanāgarī; doubtful readings were checked on 300-dpi crops. **Collated against sanskritdocuments.org `rAhu108nAmAvalI.itx`** (transliterated by S. Recension note: **The Smārtha nāmāvalī is followed; the sanskritdocuments witness (SD) is recorded, not adopted.** The two run in the same order, and the list is largely **jyotiṣa**: from no. 66 on, the names mostly state Rāhu's effect in a given house (`चतुर्थे मातृनाशकाय`, "in the fourth, destroyer of the mother"). Those names are printed run together here, as the source prints them. **Numbering.** The print marks a tally every ten names and reaches 107. The file has 108, because it (a) joins nos. 89 `धनेककुत्` and 90 `प्रदायकाय`, which the print numbers as two lines but which make up one name (SD `dhane kakutpradāya`), and (b) splits the two comma-joined pairs `దీర్ఘకృష్ణాయ, అశిరసే` and `విష్ణవే, నేత్రారయే`. The result matches the title's 108. **Readings kept as printed, all checked at 300 dpi (Smārtha → SD):** no. 6 `प्रणये` → `phaṇine`; no. 7 `गार्ग्यानाय` → `gārgyāyanāya`; no. 8 `सुरागवे` → `surāpine` (the print also puts `ॐ` before this name, mid-column); no. 22 `उल्कापातमित्राय` → `ulkāpātayitre`; no. 39 `नीलचन्दनरोहिताय` → no counterpart in SD; no. 68 `सन्महिम्ने` → `janmasiṁhe`; no. 72 `मत्तकाज्ञानप्रदायिने` → `mattako jñānadāya`. The English glosses for these are marked "reading as printed", because their sense is not secure. Other differences: no. 15 `कृष्णध्वजपताकिने` → `-patākāvate`; no. 16 `दक्षिणाभिमुखरथाय` → `dakṣiṇāśāmukharatāya`; no. 21 `काश्यपर्षिनन्दनाय` → same; no. 30 `विष्णुचक्रच्छेदिताय` → SD `dviṣaccakracchedakāya`; no. 43 `अश्विनीतारकोद्भवाय` → `aśvyarkṣabhavāya`; no. 56 `श्रीराहुमूर्तये` and no. 100 `राहुमूर्तये` both appear; nos. 95–96 `दीर्घकृष्णाय अशिरसे` → SD `dīrghāya kṛṣṇāya atanave`. **Prefix.** The source prints `ఓం` (ॐ) before the first name of each column; this corpus prints `श्री` for every nāmāvalī without exception (user, 2026-09-08). **Cross-reference:** `navagraha/18_rahu_kavacham.txt` and `navagraha/26_rahu_stotram.txt` are separate Rāhu texts, not this list.

Blurb: The severed head that swallows Sun and Moon: Siṁhikā's son, half-bodied, cut off by Viṣṇu's discus, cloud-dark, sharp-fanged, seated on a winnowing-basket, fond of hessonite and black gram. Halfway through, the list turns into an astrologer's handbook, naming Rāhu by what he does in each house of the birth chart — destroying the mother from the fourth, bringing wealth from the sixth, fame from the first and enmity from the last.
