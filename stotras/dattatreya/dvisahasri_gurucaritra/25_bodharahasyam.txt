Blurb: This is the second of the two secret teachings that stand at the end of the work, and it is read with the first on the seventh day of the saptāha. It sets out the Advaita of Śaṅkara in plain verse. The teacher addresses the disciple by four names at once and tells him what he must learn: to tell apart what is inert and what is conscious. He then takes the body apart part by part and traces each part to one of the five elements — hair, skin, flesh and bone to earth, the fluids of the body to water, hunger and thirst to fire, and all movement to air. Desire, anger, greed, delusion and fear are counted with the body as well, and the disciple is told that he himself stands beyond caste, beyond stage of life, and beyond the pairs of opposites. The teaching turns next to the inner organ, which takes four names according to the work it does: mind when it wills and doubts, intellect when it decides, citta when it dwells upon a thing again and again, and ego when it says I. Each of these four is joined to one of the vital breaths, to one organ of knowledge and to one organ of action, so that every act of knowing and of doing is shown to belong to the body and not to the Self. The mistake, the teaching says, is identification with the subtle body, and the remedy is knowledge alone and not any further action. The disciple is then told his own true station: he is the witness, beyond the gross, the subtle, the causal and the great causal body, unmoving, without form, the Lord within. The second half of the piece is marked in the print with the heading of liberation, and sets out how release comes about through the dissolving of the gross body and the rest, one step at a time, until the teaching closes.

--- verse 1 ---
deva:
सन्तप्त धन्य संन्यासिन् गुरुभक्त गतागतात् । मुक्त्या आत्माप्तये तत्त्व-जडचैतन्यविद्भव
iast:
santapta dhanya saṁnyāsin gurubhakta gatāgatāt | muktyā ātmāptaye tattva-jaḍacaitanyavidbhava
en: O afflicted one, O fortunate one, O sannyāsin, O devotee of the guru — for release from coming and going, and for the attaining of the Self, learn the truth, the knowledge of what is inert and what is conscious. — This is the opening address of the teacher to the disciple, and it names him four times over before it asks anything of him. Coming and going means birth and death repeated. The whole teaching that follows is placed under one heading: to tell apart the inert, which is the body and all that belongs to it, and the conscious, which is the Self.
tel: ఓ సంతప్తుడా(సన్తప్తుడా), ఓ ధన్యుడా, ఓ సన్యాసీ, ఓ గురుభక్తుడా — రాకపోకల నుండి విడుదల పొందుటకును, ఆత్మను పొందుటకును, జడము ఏది చైతన్యము ఏది అను తత్త్వజ్ఞానమును తెలిసికొనుము. — ఇది గురువు శిష్యుని ఉద్దేశించి చేయు మొదటి సంబోధన(సమ్బోధన). రాకపోకలు అనగా మరల మరల కలుగు జననమరణములు. ముందు చెప్పబోవు బోధ అంతయు ఒకే శీర్షిక క్రింద ఉంచబడినది — శరీరము మొదలైన జడమును, ఆత్మ అను చైతన్యమును వేరు చేసి తెలిసికొనుట.
hi: हे संतप्त, हे धन्य, हे संन्यासी, हे गुरुभक्त — आवागमन से छूटने के लिए और आत्मा को प्राप्त करने के लिए तत्त्व को जानो, अर्थात् जड़ और चेतन का ज्ञान प्राप्त करो। — यह गुरु का शिष्य को पहला सम्बोधन है। आवागमन का अर्थ है बार-बार होने वाला जन्म और मरण। आगे का सारा उपदेश एक ही शीर्षक के नीचे रखा गया है — शरीर आदि जड़ को और आत्मा रूप चेतन को अलग-अलग पहचानना।

deva:
स्वान्याज्ञं जडमन्या चिन्नामरूपात्मकं जडम् । रोमत्वङ्मांसनाड्यस्थि भुवोऽब्धो मूत्ररेतसी

svānyājñaṁ jaḍamanyā cinnāmarūpātmakaṁ jaḍam | romatvaṅmāṁsanāḍyasthi bhuvo'bdho mūtraretasī
en: What is one's own is consciousness, and what is other and unknowing is inert; whatever has name and form is inert. Hair, skin, flesh, sinews and bone come from earth; urine and semen come from water. — The teaching now begins its list. hi: जो अपना है वह चेतन है, और जो अन्य तथा अज्ञ है वह जड़ है; जिसका नाम और रूप है वह सब जड़ है। रोम, त्वचा, मांस, नाड़ियाँ और अस्थि पृथ्वी से बने हैं; मूत्र और रेतस् जल से बने हैं। — यहाँ से उपदेश अपनी सूची आरम्भ करता है। शरीर को भाग-भाग करके खोला जाता है और हर भाग को किसी एक भूत से जोड़ा जाता है। यह शरीर का वर्णन नहीं, यह दिखाना है कि इनमें से कोई भी आत्मा नहीं है।

deva:
कामक्रुड्लोभमोहाभीस्-तदाढ्येयं जडा तनुः । वर्णाश्रमादिद्वन्द्वादि-संबन्धादिपरो भवान्

kāmakruḍlobhamohābhīs-tadāḍhyeyaṁ jaḍā tanuḥ | varṇāśramādidvandvādi-saṁbandhādiparo bhavān
en: Desire, anger, greed, delusion and fear arise; filled with these, this body is inert. --- verse 6 ---
deva:
अनुसन्धानकं चित्तमहङ्कारस्त्वहङ्कृतिः । व्यानाधारायाद्यं श्रुतेस्तु शब्दभुग्वक्ति वाक्सखम्
iast:
anusandhānakaṁ cittamahaṅkārastvahaṅkṛtiḥ | vyānādhārāyādyaṁ śrutestu śabdabhugvakti vāksakham
en: That which dwells upon a thing again and again is the citta, and the sense of I is the ego. Resting on the vyāna breath, the first of these, through hearing, enjoys sound and speaks, being the companion of speech. — The four names of the inner organ are completed here. Each of the four is then joined to one of the five vital breaths and to one pair of organs. The first, the mind, works with the vyāna breath, takes in sound through the ear and puts out speech through the mouth.

en: A garland is absent in the rope both before it is imagined and after the mistake is removed, and in the same way this world is absent in Brahman. The teacher uses the old example of the rope seen as a garland or a snake: the garland was never there, before or after, yet the rope was always there. iast:
adehaprāṇahṛddharmaḥ prārabdhāśīḥ sukhaṁ vasa | idaṁ rahasyaṁ bodhākhyaṁ tajjño brahmamayo bhavet
en: You are not the body, not the breath, not the qualities of the heart. Let prārabdha alone be your expectation, and live happily. This is the secret named bodha, and the one who knows it becomes Brahman itself. — This closes the teaching. The disciple is told to stop identifying with the body, the life breath and the states of the mind. Prārabdha is the portion of past action that has already begun to bear fruit; the body will spend it out on its own, and the knower need neither hasten it nor resist it. Bodha means awakening, and the verse gives the chapter its name.
