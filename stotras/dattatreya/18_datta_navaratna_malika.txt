Title: Śrī Datta Navaratnamālikā
Devanāgarī: श्री दत्त नवरत्नमालिका
Telugu: శ్రీ దత్త నవరత్నమాలికా

Author: Jagadguru Śrī Candraśekhara Bhāratī Svāmī of Śṛṅgeri, per the text's own colophon; the poet describes himself inside the poem only as "some devotee-renunciant" (kenacid yatinā, v. 10)

Source / recension: stotranidhi.com — Devanāgarī: https://stotranidhi.com/hi/sri-datta-navaratna-malika-in-sanskrit/ ; English/IAST cross-check: https://stotranidhi.com/en/sri-datta-navaratna-malika-in-english/ . The two script pages were compared verse-by-verse and agree exactly, including the compressed compounds in vv. 3 and 6 discussed in the recension note; no variant readings were found between them.

Verse count: 10

Sections:
నవరత్నములు — The nine gems (vv. 1–9)
సమర్పణ — Samarpaṇa, the offering of the garland (v. 10)

Blurb: Nine gems on a thread, exactly as the title promises — nine verses in the eleven-syllable Svāgatā meter, each closing on the same line, dattadevam aniśaṁ kalayāmi, "ceaselessly I hold the god Datta in mind." The craftsmanship is deliberate: the opening syllables of the three pādas in each verse chime against one another (nāka / pāka / koka in v. 3; tārakaṁ / pūrakaṁ / vārakaṁ in v. 7), and the whole set is bound by the unvarying fourth line. What is asked for is stated once, in the first verse, and never repeated: purity of mind, and quickly. Verse 6 remembers Dattātreya as the teacher who expounded the Jñāna Khaṇḍa of the Tripurā Rahasya to Paraśurāma, and the tenth verse steps outside the sequence to lay the finished garland at the guru's feet, its author signing himself only as "some renunciant."

--- verse 1 | section: The nine gems ---
deva:
वित्ततर्षरहितैर्मनुजानां
सत्तमैरनिशसेव्यपदाब्जम् ।
चित्तशुद्धिमभिलिप्सुरहं द्राक्
दत्तदेवमनिशं कलयामि ॥
iast:
vittatarṣarahitairmanujānāṁ
sattamairaniśasevyapadābjam |
cittaśuddhimabhilipsurahaṁ drāk
dattadevamaniśaṁ kalayāmi ||
en: His lotus feet are served without pause by the very best of men, those free of any thirst for wealth. I, longing to gain purity of mind — and quickly — ceaselessly hold the god Datta in mind.
tel: ధనతృష్ణ లేని ఉత్తమ మనుష్యులచే ఆయన పాదపద్మములు నిరంతరము సేవింపబడుచున్నవి. చిత్తశుద్ధిని వెంటనే పొందగోరుచు, నేను దత్తదేవుని నిరంతరము మనసున నిలుపుకొనుచున్నాను.
hi: धन की तृष्णा से रहित श्रेष्ठ मनुष्यों द्वारा उनके चरणकमल निरंतर सेवित हैं। चित्त की शुद्धि शीघ्र पाने की इच्छा से मैं दत्तदेव का निरंतर चिंतन करता हूँ।

कोकबन्धुसमवेक्ष्यमहस्कं

en: His feet are worshipped by the lord of heaven; his descent is borne upon the crowns of Indra and of Śiva who wears the moon; his splendour bears comparison only with the sun, the friend of the cakravāka bird — ceaselessly I hold the god Datta in mind.

hi: उनके चरण स्वर्ग के अधिपति द्वारा सम्यक् अर्चित हैं; उनका अवतार इंद्र और चंद्रधर शिव के मुकुटों पर धारित है; उनका तेज कोकबंधु सूर्य से ही तुलनीय है — दत्तदेव का मैं निरंतर चिंतन करता हूँ।

hi: जमदग्नि के पुत्र मुनि को उन्होंने त्रिपुरा [रहस्य] का ज्ञानखंड समझाया; और नमन करने वालों की पंक्ति की आंतरिक न्यूनता को वे विदीर्ण करते हैं। दत्तदेव का मैं निरंतर चिंतन करता हूँ।

Recension note: Neither stotranidhi script page carries prose apparatus — no viniyoga, nyāsa or dhyāna — so the ten numbered verses are the whole text as sourced. Verses 1–9 are in Svāgatā (ra-na-bha-ga-ga, eleven syllables to the pāda); verse 10 shifts to Āryā, which is why its lines are set shorter. Two compounds are compressed enough to admit more than one parsing and are printed identically in both scripts, so they have been left exactly as sourced and translated for the most natural sense: pākacandradhara maulyavatāram in v. 3, taken here as "an incarnation borne on the crowns of Indra (Pākaśāsana) and of the moon-bearing Śiva," and jāmitāvidalanaṁ in v. 6, taken as the shattering of an inner deficiency in those who bow. The Tripurā Rahasya referred to in v. 6 is the work whose Jñāna Khaṇḍa is traditionally Dattātreya's instruction to Paraśurāma, the son of Jamadagni.
