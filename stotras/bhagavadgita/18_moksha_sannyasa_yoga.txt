Blurb: The closing chapter opens with a question of definition. Arjuna asks how saṁnyāsa differs from tyāga. Kṛṣṇa answers by taking the whole Gītā apart into lists. Sacrifice, gift and austerity are not to be abandoned. Abandonment itself is of three kinds. He then gives the five factors behind any act, followed by a long classification, guṇa by guṇa, of knowledge, action, agent, intellect, firmness and happiness, from sattva down to tamas. From this survey the chapter turns to one's own dharma, which is proper even when imperfectly performed, and to the discipline that ends in brahman. It then turns to bhakti, and to the last and most secret instruction: abandon all dharmas and take refuge in me alone. Arjuna says that his delusion is gone. Sañjaya closes the chapter by recalling the wonder of what he had heard.
