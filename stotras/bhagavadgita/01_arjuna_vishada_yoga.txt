Blurb: The Gītā does not open with teaching. It opens with a roll-call on the battlefield. Dhṛtarāṣṭra asks what happened at Kurukṣetra. Sañjaya replies by describing Duryodhana walking to Droṇa and naming the warriors on both sides. Then the conches are sounded, among them Pāñcajanya, Devadatta and Pauṇḍra, and their sound fills the sky. Arjuna asks to be driven into the space between the two armies. There the movement of the epic halts. He sees fathers, teachers and cousins drawn up to kill one another. His limbs give way, and the bow Gāṇḍīva slips from his hand. The rest of the chapter is his own argument against fighting. He speaks of families ruined, of rites that will cease, of women corrupted, and of forefathers fallen. The chapter ends with him sinking down upon the chariot seat, having laid aside his bow and arrows.
