Blurb: Kṛṣṇa collapses the distinction between renouncing action and doing it: the saṁnyāsī and the yogī are the same person, known by what he has given up inwardly. The chapter then turns practical — the Self is its own friend or its own enemy; a seat neither too high nor too low, kuśa grass and cloth and skin, the gaze steady, the body erect, eating and sleeping and working in measure. The lamp in a windless place fixes what a still mind is, and yoga is defined as severance from union with pain. Arjuna objects that the mind is as hard to hold as the wind; the answer is practice and dispassion. It closes on the seeker who dies mid-way, reborn into a yogī's household to resume where he stopped.
