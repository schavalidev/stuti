Blurb: Kṛṣṇa opens with a plain list of twenty-six qualities that belong to the divine endowment, called daivī. Among them are fearlessness, purity, giving, restraint, study, austerity, non-injury, truth, freedom from anger, compassion and modesty. He then names the six qualities of the demonic endowment, called āsurī, and tells Arjuna which of the two he was born to. The rest of the chapter describes those of the second kind. They know neither what should be done nor what should be avoided. They hold that the world has no cause and is driven by desire alone. They are bound by hundreds of cords of hope. They count their gains and their enemies aloud, saying 'this I have got today, and this wish I shall obtain'. They sacrifice in name only. The chapter closes tightly. Desire, anger and greed are the three gates of hell, and the śāstra is the standard for what should and should not be done.
