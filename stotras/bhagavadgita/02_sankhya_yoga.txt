Blurb: The chapter opens with Arjuna still sunk in grief. Here he stops arguing and asks to be taught, and at this point the epic becomes a dialogue of instruction. Kṛṣṇa answers on two levels, one after the other. First he gives the Sāṅkhya argument: the Self within the body is unborn and cannot be slain, so grief for those who die is misplaced. Then he speaks of the kṣatriya's own duty, pressing it with plain appeals to honour and to disgrace. From verse 39 the ground changes to yoga proper. Kṛṣṇa teaches that one should act without any claim upon the fruit, and that a single steadied intelligence should take the place of the many branching desires of ritual ambition. The chapter closes with Arjuna's question about the sthitaprajña, the one of settled wisdom, and with Kṛṣṇa's long description of the senses withdrawn, as a tortoise withdraws its limbs.

tel: కోరికలన్నిటినీ విడిచి, ఏ కోరికా లేకుండా, మమకారం లేకుండా, అహంకారం లేకుండా సంచరించే పురుషుడు శాంతిని పొందుతాడు.

en: This is the brahman-state, O Pārtha — attaining it one is not deluded; standing in it even at the last hour, one reaches nirvāṇa in brahman.
