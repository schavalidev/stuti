Blurb: Arjuna asks about six terms: prakṛti and puruṣa, the field and the knower of the field, and knowledge and the knowable. Kṛṣṇa answers each in turn. The body is the field. Its contents are listed, running from the great elements down to desire, aversion and the will to hold together. Knowledge is then given not as doctrine but as a list of twenty dispositions, from the absence of conceit to steady solitude. The knowable is brahman, which is described only through statements that appear to contradict one another: it has hands and feet on every side; it is without senses, yet it lights them; it is far away and near at the same time. The chapter closes with prakṛti as the only agent, and the Self as a presence that takes no part. The Self is like space, which nothing stains, and like a sun that lights the whole field.

--- verse 27 | section: Paths by which the Self is seen ---

Recension note: Verse numbering follows the recension printed by Stotra Nidhi, in which this chapter has 35 verses because it opens with the Arjuna-uvāca question "prakṛtiṁ puruṣaṁ caiva…". Śaṅkara's recension omits that verse and numbers the chapter 34, which is how the familiar total of 700 verses for the Gītā is arrived at; counting it, as here, gives 701. Editions differ on this one verse only, and the rest of the chapter is numbered one higher here than in Śaṅkara-based printings. Collation against github.com/gita/gita (data/verse.json) and vedicscriptures.github.io, two independent digital editions flagged 6 verses in this chapter. 5 were left as printed, the divergence being orthographic or an artefact of the comparison text — v. 4: Only sandhi written separate vs. joined; the other source additionally corrupts शृणु to श्रृणु; v. 5: The alternative's निश्िचतैः is plain encoding corruption of विनिश्चितैः; v. 10: असक्ितर् is a misplaced vowel sign for असक्तिर्, an encoding error; v. 11: भक्ितर् is a misplaced vowel sign for भक्तिर्, an encoding error; and so on. 1 reading was corrected — v. 17: Both independent sources read तज्ज्ञेयं, the correct sandhi of तत् + ज्ञेयं and the vulgate text; stotranidhi's तत्ज्ञेयं is an error.
