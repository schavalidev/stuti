Blurb: Kṛṣṇa opens by placing the teaching in a line of transmission. It was given to Vivasvat, was lost in the course of time, and is now restored. Arjuna objects plainly that Kṛṣṇa was born later than Vivasvat. This objection draws out the central claim of the chapter: the Lord is born age after age, his birth and his action are divine, and one who knows them is not born again. From there the argument becomes technical. Action, inaction and wrong action are distinguished. The one who sees inaction within action is called wise. A long list then names the many things that are offered as sacrifice: the objects of sense, the senses themselves, the breath, wealth, austerity and knowledge. Knowledge is ranked above them all. The close is practical: approach those who see, ask them, serve them, and cut away doubt with the sword of knowledge.
