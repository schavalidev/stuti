Blurb: Arjuna opens by catching Kṛṣṇa in an apparent contradiction: if insight outranks action, why push him into a terrible deed? The answer refuses the escape route — no one stays actionless even a moment, and sitting still while the mind runs after objects is only hypocrisy. From there the chapter widens: work done as sacrifice keeps a reciprocal wheel of gods, rain, food and creatures turning, and the one who does not turn it lives in vain. Kṛṣṇa then makes himself the example, working though he needs nothing, lest people follow him into collapse. The close turns inward, tracing desire and anger to rajas and locating them in senses, mind and buddhi — smoke over fire, the enemy to be killed.

|| iti śrīmadbhagavadgītāsūpaniṣatsu brahmavidyāyāṁ yogaśāstre śrīkṛṣṇārjunasaṁvāde karmayogo nāma tṛtīyo'dhyāyaḥ ||
en: Thus ends the third chapter, named the Yoga of Action, in the Upaniṣads of the glorious Bhagavad Gītā — the knowledge of brahman, the scripture of yoga — in the dialogue of Śrī Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna.
tel: ఈ విధంగా బ్రహ్మవిద్యయు యోగశాస్త్రమునగు శ్రీమద్భగవద్గీతా ఉపనిషత్తులలో, శ్రీకృష్ణార్జున సంవాదంలో, కర్మయోగం అనే మూడవ అధ్యాయం సమాప్తం.
hi: इस प्रकार ब्रह्मविद्या तथा योगशास्त्र रूप श्रीमद्भगवद्गीता उपनिषद् में, श्रीकृष्ण और अर्जुन के संवाद में, कर्मयोग नामक तीसरा अध्याय समाप्त हुआ।
