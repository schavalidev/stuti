Blurb: This is the chapter in which the argument stops and something is shown. Arjuna, having heard Kṛṣṇa list his glories, asks to see them. Kṛṣṇa grants him a divine eye. Sañjaya, who is narrating from the palace, then describes a form with countless mouths, eyes and arms, blazing like a thousand suns. Arjuna's own speech shifts into long triṣṭubh verses as wonder turns to terror. He watches Bhīṣma, Droṇa and the assembled warriors rush into those jaws as moths rush into a fire. He hears the answer: I am Time. He begs pardon for the familiarity he had shown in the past, and asks for the four-armed form to be restored. The chapter closes quietly. This sight is reached by undivided devotion alone, and not by the Vedas, by austerity or by sacrifice.

--- verse 50 | section: The gentle form restored ---

--- verse 52 | section: The gentle form restored ---
