Blurb: Arjuna presses what seems to be a contradiction. Kṛṣṇa has praised both the renunciation of actions and the yoga of action, so which of the two is better? The answer is that they are not rivals, and that whoever sees Sāṅkhya and yoga as one sees truly. The chapter then works out what it means to act while claiming no agency. The body, the senses and the mind do the work. The Self neither creates agency nor gathers karma. The one who knows this, while eating, walking, breathing and sleeping, thinks 'I do nothing at all'. The argument then widens into the even vision that sees the same in a brāhmaṇa, a cow, an elephant and an outcaste. It closes with a short account of meditation, and with the peace of one who knows Kṛṣṇa as the friend of all beings.
