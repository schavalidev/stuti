Title: Śrī Viṭṭhala Aṣṭottaraśatanāma Stotram
Devanāgarī: श्रीविट्ठल-अष्टोत्तरशतनामस्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ విఠ్ఠల అష్టోత్తరశతనామ స్తోత్రం

Author: Anonymous; the colophon places it in the **Padma Purāṇa** (`पञ्चोनषष्टिसाहस्र्यां संहितायां`, "the saṁhitā of fifty-five thousand"), in a section called **Hara-rahasya**, as an **Umā–Maheśvara saṁvāda**. Sūta narrates the frame; Pārvatī asks, having just heard Viṭṭhala's thousand names (v. 1); Śiva answers and names himself the ṛṣi (v. 7). No such passage was checked in any edition of the Padma Purāṇa, so the attribution is the colophon's, not verified.

Source / recension: Primary text: **Gītā Press, Gorakhpur — Śatanāma Stotra Saṅgraha**, code 1850, pp. 80–86 (stotra pp. 80–84, nāmāvalī pp. 85–86; PDF pp. 81–87), transcribed from the page images of the printed edition — the local PDF has no text layer. Entry 14 of the volume's forty. Stotra and nāmāvalī are paired in one file as the publisher prints them, and were checked against each other name by name. Filed under `krishna/`, beside `krishna/13_pandurangashtakam.txt`, the only other Viṭṭhala text in the corpus; the two are separate works. **No second edition was collated**, and no web witness has been sought, as with entries 1–13 of this volume.

Recension note: **Prefix.** Gītā Press prints `ॐ` before each name; this corpus prints `श्री` for every nāmāvalī without exception (user, 2026-09-08). **THE NĀMĀVALĪ RUNS TO 111, NOT 108.** The stotra says 108 (v. 34: `अष्टोत्तरशतं नाम्नां विट्ठलस्य महात्मनः`), and so do the title, the running heads and both colophons; the nāmāvalī is numbered 1–111 and stops at `१११ ॐ कृष्णाय नमः`. `Name count:` reports 111, as the list yields. Where the extra three probably come from — recorded as observation, not resolved: v. 16 `नरो नारायणो महान्` is split into three names (nos. 30–32), v. 28 `दधिभुक्कामपूरकः` into two (nos. 88–89), and v. 25 `चानपेक्षोऽपेक्षितो जनैः` into two (nos. 72–73). This is the same kind of overrun as entries 6, 8 and 12. **All 111 are distinct.** **Stotra and nāmāvalī diverge in one place, and the print of each is kept:** v. 13 reads `कटिस्थितकरद्वन्द्वो` ("hands on the hips", as also in v. 5 and in the dhyāna's `कटिनिहितकरं`); nāmāvalī no. 18 prints `कटस्थितकरद्वन्द्वाय`, which looks like a dropped i-mātrā and is left as printed. **Two names keep syntactic phrases** exactly as the nāmāvalī prints them: no. 27 `वेणुनादेन देवानां मनःश्रवणमङ्गलाय` and no. 92 `सखिनेत्रे पिधायाशु कोऽहं पृच्छाविशारदाय`; no. 109 is `परस्मै ब्रह्मणे`, two words. **No. 72 `अनपेक्षाय नमः` lacks its daṇḍa** in the print; the name itself is unaffected. **Obscure readings kept as printed.** v. 14 / no. 21 `तितऊक्षेत्रपोश्वत्थकोटीश्वरवरप्रदः` — `तितऊ` is not a Sanskrit word and is probably a local place name; the English renders it literally. v. 17 / no. 34 `ससध्रीचीत्सवीबूचीरितिश्रुत्यर्थरूपधृक्` names itself as the meaning of a *śruti*; it appears to quote the Ṛgvedic phrase `स सध्रीचीः स विषूचीः` said of the cowherd (*gopā*) — that identification is ours, offered to explain the translation, and the printed spelling (`सवीबूची`) is **not** corrected to it. v. 36 `सर्वविद्यते` is printed as one word and rendered "becomes all-knowing". **The text is a Paṇḍharpūr sthala-stotra.** Most of its names are local: Puṇḍarīka (Puṇḍalīka) and his hermitage, the Candrabhāgā (the Bhīmā at Paṇḍharpūr), Padmā-tīrtha, the Nīrā confluence, Karavīra (Kolhapur), the Bodhinī and Śayanī ekādaśī festivals, the Karṇāṭaka woman (`कार्णाटकी`), and a run of boyhood games — hide-and-seek, "who am I?", odd-or-even, dice — that are Viṭṭhala's own. The English column renders these literally rather than guessing at the legends behind them. **Dhyāna.** The Hindi footnote to v. 8 opens `[इष्टिकापीठ (ईंट)-पर]`, "on the brick seat" — the brick Puṇḍalīka threw for him to stand on; the `hi:` field keeps that gloss. **The speaker-lines (`सूत उवाच`, `श्रीशङ्कर उवाच`, `पार्वत्युवाच`) fall mid-verse** in vv. 4, 5, 38 and 39, and are kept in the `deva:` field where the print sets them.

Blurb: Viṭṭhala stands on his brick with his hands on his hips, and this is his home ground: Puṇḍalīka's hermitage, the Candrabhāgā, the confluence of the Nīrā, the gates held by Śāradā, Nārada and Durgā. Then the names turn into a boy's games — hiding in a hole, covering a friend's eyes and asking "who am I?", holding out fists for odd-or-even, the dice board, carrying one another — before they end, as all his names do, on Pāṇḍuraṅga, the supreme Brahman, Kṛṣṇa.

॥ इति श्रीपद्मपुराणे पञ्चोनषष्टिसाहस्र्यां संहितायां हररहस्ये उमामहेश्वरसंवादे श्रीविट्ठलाष्टोत्तरशतनामस्तोत्रं सम्पूर्णम् ॥
iast:
|| iti śrīpadmapurāṇe pañconaṣaṣṭisāhasryāṁ saṁhitāyāṁ hararahasye umāmaheśvarasaṁvāde śrīviṭṭhalāṣṭottaraśatanāmastotraṁ sampūrṇam ||
en: Thus, in the Śrī Padma Purāṇa, in the saṁhitā of fifty-five thousand verses, in the Hara-rahasya, in the dialogue of Umā and Maheśvara, the Śrī Viṭṭhala Aṣṭottaraśatanāma Stotram is complete.
tel: ఇట్లు శ్రీపద్మపురాణమునందు, పంచపంచాశత్సహస్రసంహితయందు, హరరహస్యమునందు, ఉమామహేశ్వరసంవాదమునందలి శ్రీవిఠ్ఠలాష్టోత్తరశతనామస్తోత్రము సంపూర్ణము.
hi: इस प्रकार श्रीपद्मपुराणकी पचपन हजार श्लोकोंवाली संहिताके हररहस्यमें उमा-महेश्वर-संवादमें श्रीविट्ठलाष्टोत्तरशतनामस्तोत्र सम्पूर्ण हुआ।
