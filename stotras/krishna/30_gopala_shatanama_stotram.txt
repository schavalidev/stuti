Title: Śrī Gopāla Śatanāma Stotram
Devanāgarī: श्रीगोपाल-शतनामस्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ గోపాల శతనామ స్తోత్రం

Author: Anonymous; the colophon places it in a **Hara–Gaurī saṁvāda** — a dialogue of Śiva and Pārvatī, with Śiva speaking, no source text named. The viniyoga gives the ṛṣi as **Nārada**. Verse 16 addresses the hearer as `देवदेवेशि`, and v. 14 says the hundred names are disclosed `तव स्नेहात्`, out of affection for her; so the frame is consistent throughout.

Source / recension: Primary text: **Gītā Press, Gorakhpur — Śatanāma Stotra Saṅgraha**, code 1850, pp. 76–79 (stotra pp. 76–77, nāmāvalī pp. 78–79), transcribed from the page images of the printed edition rather than from OCR. Entry 13 of the volume's forty. Stotra and nāmāvalī are paired in one file as the publisher prints them, and were checked against each other name by name: they run in the same order, and the two places where they divide the names differently are recorded below. Filed under `krishna/`, which was checked and holds 29 files, including the Kṛṣṇa aṣṭottara written from entry 12 of this same volume (`krishna/29`); the two share only a handful of names and are separate works. **No second edition was collated**, and no web witness has been sought.

Recension note: **Prefix.** Gītā Press prints `ॐ` before each name; this corpus prints `श्री` for every nāmāvalī without exception (user, 2026-09-08). **THIS IS A ŚATANĀMA, NOT AN AṢṬOTTARAŚATANĀMA — 100 NAMES, AND THE TEXT SAYS SO THREE TIMES.** The nāmāvalī is numbered 1–100 and stops at `१०० ॐ प्रेमसागराय नमः`. The stotra's colophon reads `श्रीगोपालशतनामस्तोत्रं सम्पूर्णम्`; the viniyoga specifies `शतनामपाठे विनियोगः`; and v. 14 says `नाम्नां शतं समासेन तव स्नेहात्प्रकाशितम्`. Against that, the **running heads and the display titles of both stotra and nāmāvalī call it `अष्टोत्तरशतनाम`**, and so does the nāmāvalī's colophon. The volume is therefore inconsistent with itself on this one entry, and the inconsistency is between its **editorial furniture** (headings, running heads) and the **text's own three statements**. This file follows the text: `Title:` and `Verse count:` treat it as a śatanāma and `Name count:` is 100. This is the mirror image of entries 6, 8 and 12, where the count ran *over* the title; here it runs under, and here alone the text itself supplies the correct figure. **All 100 are distinct.** **Two names are divided differently by the verse and the list, and the list is followed for the index.** (a) V. 6 reads `कान्तकलानन्दी` as one compound; the nāmāvalī splits it into no. 32 `कान्तकलायै` and no. 33 `आनन्दिने`. Note that no. 32 is **feminine** (`कान्तकला`, dative `-कलायै`) — the only feminine dative in the list. (b) V. 11 reads `शमनस्त्राता`, plausibly one name ("saviour from Śamana", i.e. from Yama); the nāmāvalī splits it into no. 72 `शमनाय` and no. 73 `त्रात्रे`. **Without these two splits the verses yield 98, not 100** — so the split is what makes the round number, and it is the list-maker's arithmetic that is being followed. Recorded as a divergence rather than resolved: a second edition would settle whether the compounds or the splits are original. **The hymn declares itself secret** (v. 15: `अप्रकाश्यमिमं मन्त्रं गोपनीयं प्रयत्नतः`) and calls itself a *mantra* rather than a stotra. **Alliterative arrangement.** The names run in alphabetical blocks — `ग-`, then `न-`, then `प-`/`क-`, then a long `क-` run, `स-`, `व-`/`विश्व-`, `ध-`, `र-`/`रस-` — which is why the sense sometimes strains (no. 54 `सुन्दाय`, no. 40 `हराय`). It is a sound-pattern list, and the English column renders it literally rather than smoothing. **V. 17 is a tristich**, three lines under one number where the rest are distichs; the volume does not renumber. **The dhyāna is the well-known `कस्तूरीतिलकं` verse**, which circulates widely as an independent stanza; it is printed here without attribution, and no cross-reference in this corpus was found.

Blurb: Śiva tells Pārvatī a hundred names for the cowherd and tells her to keep them to herself. They are arranged by sound rather than by story — a run on *ga-*, a run on *na-*, a long run on *ka-*, then *dha-* and *ra-* — so the list turns on itself: the rasa, the knower of rasa, the giver of rasa, the lord of the rāsa, the maker of the rāsa's joy. It ends where it was always going: worshipped by Rādhikā, lord of Rādhā's life, an ocean of love.
