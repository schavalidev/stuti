Title: Śukla-Yajurveda Sandhyāvandanam (Kāṇva śākhā)
Devanāgarī: शुक्लयजुर्वेद सन्ध्यावन्दनम् (काण्वशाखा)
Telugu: శుక్ల యజుర్వేద సంధ్యావందనం (కాణ్వ శాఖ)

Author: Anonymous (traditional Kāṇva smārta rite). No compiler is named. The order of service is the received Kātyāyana-sūtra sequence, and the base text says so on its first line: `कात्यायन सूत्रानुसारम्`. The mantras are drawn from the Śukla-Yajurveda, from the Ṛgveda, and — for the two aghamarṣaṇa mantras, which the text itself cites — from the Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa.

Type: vidhi (ritual manual) — not a stotra. Uses the one-extra-field vidhi format documented in `../madhyandina/01_sandhyopasana_vidhi.txt`: a `vidhi:` field carrying the ritual instruction, then deva / iast / en / tel / hi.

Recension / śākhā: **Kāṇva (Śukla-Yajurveda), Kātyāyana Sūtra.** This is not inferred. The rite states it in its own abhivādana at unit 63: `शुक्ल यजुर्वेदान्तर्गत काण्व शाखाध्यायी कात्यायन सूत्रः` — "a student of the Kāṇva branch within the Śukla-Yajurveda, of the Kātyāyana Sūtra". The Śukla-Yajurveda has two living śākhās, and this is the southern one. **This file is therefore NOT the northern Śukla-Yajurveda rite, and it is not the form printed in Gītā Press book 210.** That book is Vājasaneyi **Mādhyandina**, whose own abhivādana reads `वाजसनेयि माध्यान्दिनीय शाखाध्यायी`, and it stands in this corpus at `../madhyandina/01_sandhyopasana_vidhi.txt`. The two are the daily rite of two different communities of the same Veda. **They must never be merged, harmonised or collated against each other, and no accent may pass between them.** A worshipper who follows the Mādhyandina rite should not use this file, and a worshipper who follows this one should not use that. The southern Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda rite is again a third thing and stands at `../taittiriya/01_krishna_yajurveda_sandhyavandanam.txt`; the Ṛgvedic rite is at `../rigveda/01_rigveda_sandhyavandanam.txt`.

Accent: **present throughout — 657 marks.** Udātta `॑` (U+0951) and anudātta `॒` (U+0952) as printed, and the double svarita as `᳚` (U+1CDA), which is how the base text itself writes it. The guttural nasal before a spirant is given as `ग्ं` (U+0917 + U+094D + U+0902), the spelling used in `../taittiriya/`, romanised `gṁ`. **The base text spells that one sound in seven different ways on a single page** — `ग्` + zero-width joiner + anusvāra (9 times), `ग्ग्` + joiner + anusvāra (5), plain `ग्ं` (5), `ग्ं` with an anudātta after it (1), `ग्` followed only by an accent mark (2), `ग्ग्` or `ग्` before a space (3), and `ग्ग्` + joiner before a sibilant (1). All 26 sites are written `ग्ं` here, and where an accent mark stood inside the cluster it is placed after it, which is the spelling the sibling Taittirīya files use. **No accent was added, inferred, moved between syllables or reconstructed at any point.** The accent count was measured rather than assumed: 293 udātta, 360 anudātta and 4 double svarita in the source, and 293, 360 and 4 in this file. There are no zero-width joiners and no zero-width non-joiners anywhere in this file; the source's 16 joiners were all inside the nasal clusters and one `र्ह` ligature, and carry no sound.

**How the Devanāgarī is written, and why it looks unusual.** This tradition prints the anusvāra as it is actually pronounced, assimilated to the consonant that follows it, and marks the join with a hyphen: `ओ-ङ्केशवाय` for `ओं केशवाय`, `ओ-म्भुवः` for `ओं भुवः`, `त्व-ञ्च` for `त्वं च`, and a candrabindu on a following semivowel, `ॐ-वँन्दे` for `ॐ वन्दे`. **That orthography is the base text's own and is transcribed exactly**, in `deva:` and in `iast:` alike, so that the two fields stay in step. It is not a defect and it has not been normalised away. The reader who wants the plain spelling will find it in the sibling witness named below, which prints the same rite with the anusvāra written plainly.

Corrections applied to the base text, with the count. **(1) One reading, settled by the text's own citation.** At unit 22 both websites print `दु॒र्मि॒त्रास्तस्मै॑ भुयासुः` with a short `u`. The word is the precative `भूयासुः` and the base text cites this mantra to Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa 2.6.6.3, where the accented pāṭha edition reads `भूयासुः`. The long vowel is restored. This is the only reading changed in the file. **(2) Sixteen zero-width joiners and thirteen zero-width spaces removed.** Fifteen of the joiners sat inside the guttural-nasal clusters described above and one inside a `र्ह` ligature; the zero-width spaces sat inside `र्ष` and at two word joins, where they do the work of the hyphen the text uses elsewhere, and the hyphen is written at those two places. None of them spells a sound. **(3) The base text's Telugu ritual glosses moved out of the mantra text and into the `vidhi:` field, where instructions belong** — `(చే.)` "do", printed as `(चे.)` five times after `आचम्य` and `प्राणानायम्य`, and the nine sprinkling directions `(पादमुल पै)`, `(शिरस्सु पै)` and `(हृदयमु पै)`, "on the feet", "on the head", "on the heart", at unit 15. They are Telugu words printed in Devanāgarī letters, and they are instructions, not mantra. **No other change was made to the source's readings.**

Recension note: **every difference found between the witnesses is listed here, and none has been reconciled away.**

**(1) The base text is a Devanāgarī transliteration of a Telugu original, and says so by accident.** Four of its section headings carry Telugu inflection — `मार्जनमु`, `अर्घ्यप्रदानमु`, `सूर्योपस्थानमु` for `मार्जनम्` and the rest, `गायत्री मुद्रलु` and `उत्तर मुद्रलु` with the Telugu plural, and `प्रवरलु चू.` for "see the pravaras". They are transcribed as printed in the text and the reader-facing section names are given in English. Its description of the midday goddess likewise reads `यव्वन रूपा`, using the Telugu word for youth in place of Sanskrit `यौवन`. Left as printed.

**(2) Printing slips in the base text that no witness settles, all left as printed and none corrected.** `सर्वर्थसिद्धिकरी` at the midday tarpaṇa against `सर्वार्थसिद्धिकरी` in the same list at the other two times; `भूर्भवस्स्वः` in one tarpaṇa line against `भूर्भुवस्स्वः` everywhere else; `आवहनीयाग्नि` for `आहवनीयाग्नि`; `हबोध्युरुशग्ं स` at unit 36, where the received text of that verse reads `इह बोध्युरुशꣳस`; `मृलय` for `मृळय` in the same unit; and `ब्रह्मादैव तमुच्यते` at unit 54. Each is a defect of one letter or one syllable, the Telugu witness carries every one of them too, and none of the three printed manuals covers those passages, so there is nothing to correct them against. They are recorded, not repaired.

**(3) The shuklayajurveda.org manual gives twelve ācamana names where this text gives twenty-four**, and it prints the body-touching for each one — right cheek, left cheek, right eye, left eye, and so on — where the base text prints only the numbered names. It also adds a Vināyaka verse, `śuklāmbaradharaṁ viṣṇum`, which the base text does not have at this point, and it directs that ācamana be done twice. **Nothing was imported from it.**

**(4) The same manual writes `oṁ suvaḥ` plainly** in the prāṇāyāma vyāhṛtis where the base text writes the guttural nasal `ओग्ं सुवः`. A real difference of recitation, recorded and not reconciled.

**(5) The srimatham manual gives a different meditation verse.** It reads `bālāṁ vidyāṁ tu gāyatrīṁ lohitāṁ caturānanaṁ` in the morning, with separate verses for midday and evening, where this text has the single verse `मुक्ताविद्रुमहेमनीलधवल-च्छायैर्मुखैस्त्रीक्षणैः` for all three times. Both stand.

**(6) The closing hand positions differ in three of eight.** This text reads `सुरभिः ज्ञान चक्रे च योनिः कूर्मो-ऽथ पङ्कजम् । लिङ्ग-न्निर्याण मुद्रा`; the srimatham manual reads `surabhi, jñānam, vairāgyaṁ, yoniḥ, śaṅkhaḥ, paṅkajam, liṅgam, nirvāṇam` — vairāgya, śaṅkha and nirvāṇa for cakra, kūrma and niryāṇa. The twenty-four positions of unit 49 agree in both, name for name. Not reconciled.

**(7) The valedictory verse differs in two places.** This text reads `ब्राह्मणे॑भ्यो-ऽभ्य॑नुज्ञा॒ता॒ ग॒च्छ दे॑वि य॒था सु॑खम्`; the srimatham manual reads `brāhmaṇebhyo hyanujñātā gaccha devī yathā sukham`. Both stand.

**(8) The srimatham manual has no sandhyā-tarpaṇa and no Gāyatrī tarpaṇa**, which are units 30 to 32 and 54 to 57 here, and it closes with `anena sandhyopāsanākhyena karmaṇā śrī parameśvaraḥ prīyatāṁ na mama`, which this text does not print. This is a difference in the extent of two manuals of one tradition, not a variant reading, and nothing was filled in from either side.

**(9) Where the same mantra stands in a sibling file of this corpus, the two are not the same text and were not compared for accent.** `आपो हिष्ठा`, the Gāyatrī, `उत्तमे शिखरे`, `उदुत्यं जातवेदसम्`, `चित्रं देवानाम्` and `आ कृष्णेन` all occur in `../madhyandina/`, `../taittiriya/` and `../rigveda/` as well. They belong to four different rites of four different śākhās. **No accent, reading or pāda division was carried between them in either direction.** The Ṛgvedic file reads `आकृ॒ष्णेन॒ रज॑सा` and the Taittirīya `आ स॒त्येन॒ रज॑सा`; this file reads `आकृ॒ष्णेन॒ रज॑सा` with the Ṛgveda, and that is what its own source prints, not an import.

Blurb: Sandhyāvandana is the daily worship performed at dawn, at midday and at dusk. This file gives the rite as it is followed by the Kāṇva branch of the Śukla-Yajurveda, which is the southern branch of that Veda. It is not the only form of the rite. The northern branch of the same Veda, the Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda and the Ṛgveda each have their own, and those are kept as separate files. The worshipper sips water, prays to the earth, controls the breath, states his intention, and sprinkles himself with water. He then drinks water with a mantra, washes away his wrongdoing, and offers water to the sun. He offers water to the deities of the hour, stands before the sun, and recites the Gāyatrī. At the close he offers water to the Gāyatrī, bows to the ten quarters, takes leave of the goddess, gives the recitation to the deity of that time, and announces his descent and his branch of the Veda. The Devanāgarī here is printed in the manner of this tradition, which writes the anusvāra as it is actually pronounced and joins it to the word that follows.
