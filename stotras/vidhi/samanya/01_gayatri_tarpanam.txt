Recension note: **every difference found between the three witnesses is listed here, and only one has been carried into the text, in brackets, as the corpus's variant rule directs.**

**(1) `स्वर्गलोक` and `स्वर्लोक`, at unit 3.** The base text reads `ओं स्वः स्वर्गलोक पुरुषं तर्पयामि`; Gītā Press reads `ॐ स्वः स्वर्लोकपुरुषं तर्पयामि`. Both are well attested and both are recited. The base text's reading stands in the line and **the Gītā Press reading is given after it in round brackets**, in `deva:` and in `iast:` alike. The Purāṇa writes the line as `स्वः स्वर्गलोकपुरुषं तर्पयामि`, agreeing with the base text.

**(2) The viniyoga is given in a different order.** The base text reads `गायत्र्या गायत्री छन्दः, विश्वामित्र ऋषिः, सविता देवता`; Gītā Press reads `गायत्र्या विश्वामित्र ऋषिः सविता देवता गायत्री छन्दः`; the Purāṇa's verse has the metre first, as the base text does. The base text's order stands. Nothing here differs in substance.

**(3) The case endings of the four measures differ.** The base text reads `एकपदा गायत्रीं`, `द्विपदा गायत्रीं`; Gītā Press reads `एकपदां गायत्री`, `द्विपदां गायत्री`; the Purāṇa reads `एकपदा नाम गायत्रीं` and `द्विपदां गायत्रीं`. The three do not agree with one another, the sense is the same in all of them, and **the base text is left exactly as it prints.** This is not a variant reading worth a bracket.

**(4) The three witnesses close differently, and this is the one difference of substance.** The base text closes with the peace invocation `तच्छं योरावृणीमहे` and `ओं शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः`, which is unit 6 here. **Gītā Press closes instead with `ॐ तत्सद्ब्रह्मार्पणमस्तु`** and prints no peace invocation at this place. The Purāṇa directs four mantras for the peace — `जातवेदसम्`, `मा नस्तोके`, `त्र्यम्बकम्` and `तच्छंयोः` — where the base text prints only the last of them. **Neither the Gītā Press closing nor the Purāṇa's three further mantras was imported.** The base text's close stands as it prints, and a reciter who follows either of the others will find what they use named here.

**(5) `साङ्कृतीं`, `सांकृति` and `साङ्कृतिं`** are the three witnesses' three spellings of the ninth name. The base text's spelling is kept. Nothing turns on it.

Not collated: nothing. Every line of this file stands in at least two of the three witnesses.

Sections:
गायत्रीतर्पणम् — The tarpaṇa of the Gāyatrī (units 1–6)

Blurb: Tarpaṇa is the offering of water. This one is made to the Gāyatrī at the end of the morning sandhyā, after the recitation of the mantra is finished. Water is released once at each name. The worshipper first satisfies the Person of each of the four Vedas, then of the histories and the Purāṇas, of the āgamas and of the world of truth. He then satisfies the Person of the earthly, the middle and the heavenly world. He then satisfies the goddess herself in her four measures, of one foot, of two, of three and of four. Ten names of the goddess follow, ending with Sārvajitī, she who conquers all. The offering closes with the prayer for peace, which asks for well-being for men, for the healing herbs to grow, and for peace upon the two-footed and the four-footed alike.

स्व॒स्तिर्मानु॑षेभ्यः । ऊ॒र्ध्वं जि॑गातु भेष॒जम् ।
शं नो॑ अस्तु द्वि॒पदे᳚ । शं चतु॑ष्पदे ॥
ओं शान्ति॒ः शान्ति॒ः शान्ति॑ः ॥
iast:
oṁ tacchaṁ yorāvṛṇīmahe | gātuṁ yajñāya |
gātuṁ yajñapataye | daivīḥ svastirastu naḥ |
svastirmānuṣebhyaḥ | ūrdhvaṁ jigātu bheṣajam |
śaṁ no astu dvipade | śaṁ catuṣpade ||
oṁ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ ||
en: Om. We choose that which brings us peace and health. Give us a straight path for the sacrifice, and a straight path for the lord of the sacrifice. Let the blessing of the gods be upon us. Let there be well-being for men. Let healing herbs grow upward. Let there be peace for the two-footed, and peace for the four-footed. Om, peace, peace, peace.
