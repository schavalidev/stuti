Recension note: **this file rests in part on a bad scan, and every place where that matters is named here.**

**The rule followed for the *Dharmasindhu* scan.** That scan runs whole sentences together without word-breaks and it drops and confuses conjuncts. The edition prints a Marathi translation immediately under each Sanskrit passage, and that Marathi came through the scan far more cleanly. **Where the Sanskrit line was illegible at a word and the edition's own Marathi names the thing plainly, the Marathi was used to settle it, and each such place is listed below.** The Marathi is the same edition and is therefore **not** an independent witness; it is the editor's own translation of the line above it, and it is used here only to read a damaged line of that same edition. Nothing was supplied from outside the two volumes named.

Beyond that, damage was repaired only where it is a failure of the glyph and never where a word-form is in question: a dropped repha (`पाथिवम्` for `पार्थिवम्`), a stray svara mark left by the scanner (`यत्त॒` for `यत्तु`), a long vowel for a short one in a word the same page spells correctly elsewhere (`वारूणं` for `वारुणं`). Where a whole word ending is missing, it was left as printed. That distinction is stated so that a reader can see exactly what was and was not touched.

**Unit 1 — two places where the scan is not fully trustworthy.** First, at the third line the scan prints the conjunct broken, so that the word may be read as either `मान्त्रं` or `मन्त्रं`. `मान्त्रं` is printed here, because the first line of the same passage gives that form clearly and the word stands in the same list. Second, at the sixth line the scan reads `अवगाह वारुणं स्यात्`, without the ending the sentence requires. **It is left exactly as printed.** The sense is not in doubt, but restoring a word ending is not reading a damaged glyph, and this corpus does not correct a reading that no witness settles.

**Unit 3 — three limb-names read off the Marathi.** The Sanskrit line of the *Dharmasindhu* is illegible at three words. It prints what reads as `शारासे`, `सुखे` and `शद्ये`. The edition's own Marathi translation of that same line names the three places as मस्तक (the head), तोंड (the mouth) and गुह्यस्थान (the private parts). `शिरसि`, `मुखे` and `गुह्ये` are therefore printed here, on that basis and on no other. The five mantras themselves — `ईशानाय नमः`, `तत्पुरुषाय नमः`, `अघोराय नमः`, `वामदेवाय नमः`, `सद्योजाताय नमः` — came through both the Sanskrit line and the Marathi identically and are firm. **The daṇḍas in this unit are this corpus's own**, added as the list rule requires; the scan carries no punctuation at all in that sentence.

**Unit 4 — the mantras are named, not printed, and one name is damaged.** The source gives only the opening words of each mantra. At the third of them the scan prints what reads as `हिरण्यगऊ` in the Sanskrit line and `हिरण्यश्श्डंग` in the Marathi. Neither is a word. The mantra meant is the one beginning `हिरण्यशृङ्गं वरुणं प्रपद्ये`, and `हिरण्यशृङ्ग` is printed here as its opening. **This is an identification and not a transcription, and it is marked as such here.** The source also says that two mantras are used at that point, not one, and that is recorded in the unit's instruction.

**Unit 5 — the two verses, and exactly which edition gives which half.** This is the one place in the file where the two editions meet, and the join is stated in full.

The *first* verse — `अश्वक्रान्ते रथक्रान्ते विष्णुक्रान्ते वसुन्धरे । शिरसा धारयिष्यामि रक्षस्व मां पदे पदे ॥` — is the *Dharmasindhu*'s, and it is printed here as that edition prints it. Gītā Press does not carry it in this form.

The *second* verse is a join, and both halves are Gītā Press readings. Its first half, `उद्धृतासि वराहेण कृष्णेन शतबाहुना`, is printed **by Gītā Press**, in its chapter on the worship of the clay liṅga, where it stands with a different second half of its own (`मृत्तिके त्वां च गृह्णामि प्रजया च धनेन च`). The *Dharmasindhu* scan prints the same half-verse but reads `दातबाहुना` at the last word, which is not a word; **Gītā Press settles it as `शतबाहुना`, and that is why the Gītā Press reading is taken.** Its second half, `मृत्तिके हर मे पापं यन्मया दुष्कृतं कृतम्`, is printed by **both** editions, word for word — by Gītā Press in its bath verse and by the *Dharmasindhu* here. The order in which the two halves are joined is the *Dharmasindhu*'s.

**So unit 5 does combine two editions inside one verse, and it is said plainly rather than passed over.** It was done because the alternative was to print a syllable that the scan has destroyed, and because the reading adopted is the authority's own and not a repair of ours.

**How unit 5 stands to what this corpus already holds.** `05_nadi_snana_sankalpa_ganga.txt` in this folder prints the Gītā Press single verse — `अश्वक्रान्ते रथक्रान्ते विष्णुक्रान्ते वसुन्धरे । मृत्तिके हर मे पापं यन्मया दुष्कृतं कृतम् ॥`, cited there to Dakṣasmṛti 2.46 and the Padma Purāṇa — and it records in full that the *Dharmasindhu* prints two verses in its place and assigns each to a separate act. **That difference was adjudicated there, and it is not re-opened here.** This file prints the two-verse form because it is transcribing the *Dharmasindhu*'s own order of service, in which the two verses belong to two different acts: the first is said over the clay where it lies, the second as the clay is taken into the hand. File 05 prints the one-verse form because it is transcribing Gītā Press. **Neither file supersedes the other, and file 05 was not altered in any way.**

**Unit 8 — the third witness, what it settles and where it differs.** The *Nirṇayasindhu* gives the same six assignments in the same order, and it agrees with the *Dharmasindhu* on five of the six: the Gāyatrī for the cow's urine, `गन्धद्वारां` for the cow-dung, `आप्यायस्व` for the milk, `दधिक्राव्ण` for the curd, and `देवस्य त्वा` for the kuśa water — which it gives as the reading of "some", its own first reading being `आपो हि ष्ठा`. **The two editions differ on the ghee alone.** The *Dharmasindhu*'s pañcagavya section reads `तेजोऽसि शुक्रमसि`; the *Nirṇayasindhu* reads `घृतं मिमिक्षे`. A third place, the *Dharmasindhu*'s own chapter on the disposal of the bones, reads `घृतमसि शुक्रमसि`, which is a third form again. **The reading of the section this unit transcribes is kept**, and the other two are recorded here and were not adopted and not put in brackets, because which mantra a manual assigns to a substance is an editorial decision of that manual and not a spelling of one word.

**One further difference, of context and not of reading.** In the ten-baths passage transcribed here, `मानस्तोके` is the mantra of the **cow-dung** bath and the five Brahma-mantras are the mantras of the **ash** bath. In the bone-disposal passages of both the *Dharmasindhu* and the *Nirṇayasindhu*, `मानस्तोके` is the mantra of the **ash**. These are two different rites in the same books, not two readings of one line, and nothing was changed on account of it.

**What was left out, and why.** The *Dharmasindhu* has a short section of its own on the **gauṇa-snāna**, the lesser baths that stand in for a bath when a full one cannot be taken. It names mantra-snāna (sprinkling the body with `आपो हि ष्ठा` and the mantras that follow it), gāyatra-snāna (water charged ten times with the Gāyatrī and sprinkled over all the limbs), āgneya-snāna (ash smeared on all the limbs), kāpila-snāna (wiping the body with a wet cloth), and the sprinkling of the water from the feet of Viṣṇu or of a brāhmaṇa, or meditation on Viṣṇu. It states that these suffice for japa and for the sandhyā but **not** for śrāddha or for the worship of the gods, and that for the brahmayajña the authorities are divided. **The scan of that passage is too poor to transcribe** — its Sanskrit reads in part `साथेय स आदेवखेणा छुमाजेनं कापिलम्`, which cannot be recovered — so it is recorded here in prose only, from the edition's Marathi, and **no unit was written for it.** It should be written from a printed copy or a better scan if one is ever to hand.

Also left out: Gītā Press prints `गन्धद्वारां`, `दधिक्राव्ण`, `पयः पृथिव्यां` and `घृतं मिमिक्षे` at length in its pūjā chapters, but there they belong to the **pañcāmṛta bathing of an image of the deity**, which is a different rite from pañcagavya taken by a person. They were not carried across.

Blurb: A bath is not only water. This file gives the seven kinds of bath that the śāstra counts, and then the order of service for four of them. The seven are the bath of mantras, the bath of earth, the bath of fire, the bath of air, the bath of heaven, the bath of water and the bath of the mind. Sacred ash is the bath of fire, clay is the bath of earth, the dust raised by the hooves of cattle is the bath of air, rain falling while the sun still shines is the bath of heaven, and going down into water is the bath of water. Thinking upon the Self is the bath of the mind. The file then gives what is said at the bath of ash, at the bath of cow-dung, at the bath of clay and at the bath of water, and last the making of pañcagavya, the five products of the cow, with the mantra and the measure for each. A person who cannot bathe at all is told what to do in its place.

Take sacred ash and smear it on the body, saying one of these with each place in turn. The last is the praṇava, oṁ, and with it the ash is put on the whole body. The source gives a second way as well: instead of the five separate mantras, all of them may be said together, each prefixed with īśāna and the rest, and the ash then smeared on.

4. वामदेवाय नमो गुह्ये ।
5. सद्योजाताय नमः पादयोः ।
6. प्रणवेन सर्वाङ्गे ।

--- unit 4 | section: The bath of cow-dung ---
vidhi:
Take cow-dung in the hand. With the praṇava, oṁ, throw the southern part of it to the directions and the northern part into the holy water. Charge what is left with the first of these mantras, and smear it on all the limbs with the second. Pray with the third — the source says two mantras are used at this point, not one. Touch the holy water with the fourth, bathe, and then sip water twice. The source names these mantras by their opening words only and does not print them in full. Only those opening words are given here.
deva:
1. मानस्तोके
2. गन्धद्वारां
3. हिरण्यशृङ्ग
4. याः प्रवत
iast:
1. mānastoke
2. gandhadvārāṁ
3. hiraṇyaśṛṅga
4. yāḥ pravata
en: These are the opening words of the four mantras used at this bath. The first charges the cow-dung. The second is said while it is smeared on all the limbs. The third is the prayer. The fourth is said while the holy water is touched. The full mantras are not printed in the source.
tel: ఈ స్నానమునందు ఉపయోగించు నాలుగు మంత్రముల ప్రారంభ(ప్రారమ్భ) పదములు ఇవి. మొదటిదానితో గోమయము అభిమంత్రించబడును. రెండవదానితో అది సర్వాంగ(సర్వాఙ్గ)ములకు పూయబడును. మూడవది ప్రార్థన. నాలుగవదానితో తీర్థము స్పృశించబడును. మంత్రములు పూర్తిగా మూలమునందు ముద్రించబడలేదు.
hi: ये इस स्नान में प्रयुक्त चार मन्त्रों के आरम्भिक शब्द हैं। पहले से गोबर अभिमन्त्रित किया जाता है। दूसरे से वह सारे अंगों पर लगाया जाता है। तीसरा प्रार्थना है। चौथे से तीर्थ का स्पर्श किया जाता है। पूरे मन्त्र मूल में छपे हुए नहीं हैं।

--- unit 5 | section: The bath of clay: the two verses ---
vidhi:
Say the first verse over the clay and charge it. Then take the clay into the hand with the second verse.
deva:
अश्वक्रान्ते रथक्रान्ते विष्णुक्रान्ते वसुन्धरे ।
शिरसा धारयिष्यामि रक्षस्व मां पदे पदे ॥
उद्धृतासि वराहेण कृष्णेन शतबाहुना ।
मृत्तिके हर मे पापं यन्मया दुष्कृतं कृतम् ॥
iast:
aśvakrānte rathakrānte viṣṇukrānte vasundhare |
śirasā dhārayiṣyāmi rakṣasva māṁ pade pade ||
uddhṛtāsi varāheṇa kṛṣṇena śatabāhunā |
mṛttike hara me pāpaṁ yanmayā duṣkṛtaṁ kṛtam ||
en: O earth, over whom horses have passed, over whom chariots have passed, whom Viṣṇu strode across — I shall bear you upon my head. Protect me at every step. You were lifted up by the dark boar of a hundred arms. O clay, take away my wrongdoing, whatever wrong I have done.
tel: అశ్వములు నడచిన, రథములు నడచిన, విష్ణువు కొలిచిన ఓ వసుంధరా — నిన్ను శిరస్సున ధరించెదను. అడుగడుగునా నన్ను రక్షించుము. వంద(వన్ద) బాహువులు గల నల్లని వరాహముచేత నీవు పైకి ఎత్తబడితివి. ఓ మృత్తికా, నేను చేసిన పాపమును, ఏ దుష్కృతము చేసితినో దానిని, హరించుము.
hi: हे वसुन्धरा, जिस पर घोड़े चले, जिस पर रथ चले, जिसे विष्णु ने नापा — मैं तुम्हें सिर पर धारण करूँगा। पद-पद पर मेरी रक्षा करो। सौ भुजाओं वाले कृष्ण वराह के द्वारा तुम ऊपर उठायी गयी थीं। हे मृत्तिके, मेरा पाप हर लो, जो कुछ बुरा मैंने किया है।

en: These are the opening words of the mantras used after the two verses. The first is said while the clay is shown to the sun. The second, the third or the fourth is said while the clay is smeared on the body from the head downward. The full mantras are not printed in the source.
tel: రెండు శ్లోకముల తరువాత ఉపయోగించు మంత్రముల ప్రారంభ(ప్రారమ్భ) పదములు ఇవి. మొదటిదానితో మృత్తికను సూర్యునకు చూపుదురు. రెండవది, మూడవది లేదా నాలుగవది చెప్పుచు శిరస్సు నుండి క్రిందికి శరీరమునకు మృత్తికను పూయుదురు. మంత్రములు పూర్తిగా మూలమునందు ముద్రించబడలేదు.

--- unit 8 | section: The making of pañcagavya ---
vidhi:
Take a copper vessel, or a cup made of palāśa leaves. Put into it, with each mantra in turn: the urine of a red cow, eight māṣas; the dung of a white cow, sixteen māṣas; the milk of a yellow cow, twelve māṣas; the curd of a blue cow, ten māṣas; the ghee of a black cow, eight māṣas; and last four māṣas of kuśa water. Then stir the whole with the praṇava, oṁ. A māṣa here is the weight of five guñjā seeds. The source names these mantras by their opening words only and does not print them in full.
deva:
1. गायत्र्या गोमूत्रम् ।
2. गन्धद्वारामिति गोमयम् ।
3. आप्यायस्वेति पयः ।
4. दधिक्राव्ण इति दधि ।
5. तेजोऽसि शुक्रमसीति घृतम् ।
6. देवस्य त्वेति कुशोदकम् ।
iast:
1. gāyatryā gomūtram |
2. gandhadvārāmiti gomayam |
3. āpyāyasveti payaḥ |
4. dadhikrāvṇa iti dadhi |
5. tejo'si śukramasīti ghṛtam |
6. devasya tveti kuśodakam |
en: With the Gāyatrī, the cow's urine. With the mantra beginning gandhadvārāṁ, the cow-dung. With the mantra beginning āpyāyasva, the milk. With the mantra beginning dadhikrāvṇa, the curd. With the mantra beginning tejo'si śukramasi, the ghee. With the mantra beginning devasya tvā, the kuśa water.
tel: గాయత్రీ మంత్రముతో గోమూత్రము. గంధద్వారాం అను మంత్రముతో గోమయము. ఆప్యాయస్వ అను మంత్రముతో పాలు. దధిక్రావ్ణ అను మంత్రముతో పెరుగు. తేజోఽసి శుక్రమసి అను మంత్రముతో నెయ్యి. దేవస్య త్వా అను మంత్రముతో కుశోదకము.
hi: गायत्री मन्त्र से गोमूत्र। गन्धद्वारां मन्त्र से गोबर। आप्यायस्व मन्त्र से दूध। दधिक्राव्ण मन्त्र से दही। तेजोऽसि शुक्रमसि मन्त्र से घी। देवस्य त्वा मन्त्र से कुशोदक।
