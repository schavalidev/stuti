Title: Tarpaṇa-vidhi (Āpastamba-smārta Amāvāsyā Tila-tarpaṇam)
Devanāgarī: तर्पण-विधिः (आपस्तम्बस्मार्त अमावास्या तिलतर्पणम्)
Telugu: తర్పణ-విధి (ఆపస్తంబ స్మార్త అమావాస్య తిలతర్పణము)

Author: Anonymous (the received rite of the Āpastamba sūtra). The base text of this file was compiled by Paiṅkānāḍu Veṅkaṭarāma Śāstrigaḷ, who is named on its page 8 as the compiler, and published by Patthaṅgi Aruṇācalam of the Bhavani Book Centre, Madras.

Type: vidhi (ritual manual) — not a stotra. Uses the one-extra-field vidhi format documented in `../madhyandina/01_sandhyopasana_vidhi.txt`: a `vidhi:` field carrying the ritual instruction, then deva / iast / en / tel / hi.

Recension / śākhā: **South Indian smārta, Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda, Taittirīya śākhā, Āpastamba sūtra.** The base text says so on its own title page: आपस्तम्ब स्मार्त अमावास्यै तर्प्पणम्. This is the naimittika rite, done on the new moon day and on the other occasions the base text lists — the śrāddha day, the Mahālaya fortnight, the month's saṅkramaṇa, and an eclipse of the sun or the moon. **It is not the daily tarpaṇa.** See the cross-references below.

**This file is not a version of `../madhyandina/03_tarpana_vidhi.txt` and was not derived from it.** That is the northern Vājasaneyi rite, and nothing in it was consulted while this file was written. The two are the practice of different communities and neither supersedes the other.

Accent: **present in six units and absent everywhere else, and the difference is the point.** 161 accent marks in the units — 66 udātta `॑` (U+0951), 91 anudātta `॒` (U+0952) and 4 dīrgha svarita `᳚` (U+1CDA) — standing in units 7, 11, 12, 15, 17 and 22. Those six passages are Vedic mantras that are printed with svara in the accented Taittirīya pāṭha, and **every one of them was lifted into this file by machine from that pāṭha and never typed**: `idam pitṛbhyo namo astvadya`, `udīratām avara utparāsaḥ` and `aṅgiraso naḥ pitaro navagvāḥ` from Taittirīya Saṁhitā 2.6; the three madhu verses from Taittirīya Saṁhitā 4.2; `namo vaḥ pitaro rasāya` from Taittirīya Saṁhitā 3.2; and `pitṛbhyaḥ svadhāvibhyaḥ svadhā namaḥ` from Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa 2.6. The source pages are vignanam.org's accented Devanāgarī Taittirīya pāṭha, which is a same-śākhā witness in an editorial lineage of its own. Two changes were made to the lifted strings and both were counted: **2 bracketed pada-repetitions and 2 anuvāka numerals** were removed, being that page's recitational apparatus and not text; and **1 site where it spells the nasal before a spirant with a zero-width space** was written `ग्ं`, which is the spelling the sibling files in this folder use. Nothing else in those six passages was altered by a character.

**No accent was supplied anywhere else, and the places where it is missing were checked rather than assumed.** All 80 of vignanam.org's accented Taittirīya Saṁhitā, Brāhmaṇa and Āraṇyaka pāṭha pages were fetched and searched for every mantra in this rite. Four of them are not printed there at all: `āyantu naḥ pitaraḥ somyāsaḥ`, `āyāta pitaraḥ somyā gambhīraiḥ`, `ūrjaṁ vahantīr amṛtam` and `ye ceha pitaro ye ca neha`. A domestic rite of this sūtra would take them from the Āpastamba Mantrapāṭha, but no copy of that collection, accented or not, could be reached, so that attribution is not confirmed. **They are therefore given here without marks.** Accented texts of other śākhās do print them, and every such occurrence found on a fetched page is listed below. None of those marks was used, because importing another recension's marking into a Taittirīya file is exactly what the cross-recension rule forbids. (Corrected 2026-09-16: this paragraph first said that the accented Ṛgveda carries three of the four. When the Ṛgveda was actually searched, it carries only one, `ye ceha pitaro`.) One related finding is recorded rather than acted on: where this rite says `āyāta pitaraḥ somyā gambhīraiḥ pathibhiḥ pūrvyaiḥ`, the Taittirīya Saṁhitā at 1.8 prints the dismissal form `pareta pitaraḥ somyā gambhīraiḥ pathibhiḥ pūrvyaiḥ` with a different second half. The two are not the same verse and the Saṁhitā's marks were not carried over.

**Where the four unaccented mantras stand in other accented collections (added 2026-09-16).** Every citation below was read off a page fetched on that date. Nothing is cited from memory. The Ṛgveda was searched in two independent accented texts: sa.wikisource's Ṛgveda sūkta pages, and GRETIL's accented Aufrecht edition (`sa_Rgveda-edAufrecht.xml`, cached at `stotras/bin/cache/`), searched verse by verse across all ten maṇḍalas. vignanam's Ṛgveda pages were not used, because they are machine-converted and broken. The Taittirīya Saṁhitā, Brāhmaṇa and Āraṇyaka were searched in all 80 of vignanam's accented pāṭha pages. The Atharvaveda was searched in sa.wikisource's accented Śaunaka text `शौनकसंहिता (सस्वरा)` (pageid 93253). **The Āpastamba Mantrapāṭha could not be reached**: GRETIL does not carry it, and a sa.wikisource search returned no page for it.

1. `āyantu naḥ pitaraḥ somyāsaḥ` (units 7 and 13). **Ṛgveda: not found** in either Ṛgveda text. **Vājasaneyi (Mādhyandina) Saṁhitā 19.58**, sa.wikisource `शुक्लयजुर्वेदः/अध्यायः १९`, pageid 25313, in its accented Uvaṭa–Mahīdhara block: `आय॑न्तु नः पि॒तर॑: सो॒म्यासो॑ऽग्निष्वा॒त्ताः प॒थिभि॑र्देव॒यानै॑: । अ॒स्मिन्य॒ज्ञे स्व॒धया॒ मद॒न्तोऽधि॑ ब्रुवन्तु॒ ते॒ऽवन्त्व॒स्मान् ।। ५८ ।।`. That page's unaccented block labels the same verse `१९.५९`; its verse labels in this chapter are off by one. Not found in the Taittirīya pāṭha or in the Śaunaka Atharvaveda. **Wording:** this file prints `मदं त्वधि बृवन्तु ते अवन्त्व स्मान्` where the Vājasaneyi text reads `मदन्तो ऽधि ब्रुवन्तु ते ऽवन्त्व् अस्मान्`. The file keeps its own base text's words and word-division.
2. `āyāta pitaraḥ somyā gambhīraiḥ` (units 8 and 23). **Ṛgveda: not found** in either Ṛgveda text. **Śaunaka Atharvaveda 18.4.62**, pageid 93253: `आ या॑त पितरः सो॒म्यासो॑ गम्भी॒रैः प॒थिभिः॑ पितृ॒याणैः॑। आयु॑र॒स्मभ्यं॒ दध॑तः प्र॒जां च॑ रा॒यश्च॒ पोषै॑र॒भि नः॑ सचध्वम् ।।६२।।` The unaccented page `अथर्ववेदः/काण्डं १८/सूक्तम् ०४` (pageid 64593) prints the same verse as 62. **Paippalāda Saṁhitā kāṇḍa 18**, pageid 38237, unaccented, numbered 8 in its sūkta: `आ यात पितर: सोम्यासो गम्भीरैः पथिभिः पितृयाणैः । प्रजामस्मभ्यं दधत रयिं च दीर्घायुत्वाय शतशारदाय`. Of all the texts found, this is the closest to this file. The **dismissal** form stands in three places. Taittirīya Saṁhitā 1.8 (vignanam) reads `परे॑त पितर-स्सोम्या गम्भी॒रैः प॒थिभिः॑ पू॒र्व्यैः`. The Āpastamba Śrauta prayoga for the piṇḍapitṛyajña, sa.wikisource `आपस्तम्बीय श्रौतप्रयोगः/पिण्डपितृयज्ञप्रयोगः` (pageid 124866, unaccented), reads `परेत पितरस्सोम्या गम्भीरैः पथिभिः पूर्व्यैः`. Maitrāyaṇī Saṁhitā 1.10, pageid 64914, reads `परेतन पितरः सोम्यासो गम्भीरेभिः पथिभिः पूर्वेभिः`. **Wording:** this file reads `आयात` with `पूर्व्यैः`, the invitation of the Atharvan texts with the path-word of the Taittirīya dismissal. Its second half, `प्रजामस्मभ्यं ददतो रयिं च दीर्घायुत्वं च शतसारदं च`, is close to the Paippalāda reading and not to the Śaunaka one.
3. `ūrjaṁ vahantīr amṛtaṁ ghṛtaṁ payaḥ` (units 14 and 21). **Ṛgveda: not found.** Ṛgveda 6.65.3 (GRETIL, `iṣa̱m ūrja̱ṁ vaha̍ntī̱r`) contains the same two words, but they are said of the dawns and it is a different verse. **Vājasaneyi Saṁhitā 2.34**, sa.wikisource `शुक्लयजुर्वेदः/अध्यायः २`, pageid 54646: `ऊर्जं॒ वह॑न्तीर॒मृतं॑ घृ॒तं पय॑: की॒लालं॑ परि॒स्रुत॑म् । स्व॒धा स्थ॑ त॒र्पय॑त मे पि॒तॄन् ।। ३४।।`. The Āpastamba Śrauta prayoga, pageid 124866, prints it unaccented as the water poured in the piṇḍapitṛyajña: `ऊर्जं वहन्तीरमृतं घृतं पयः कीलालं परिस्रुतम् । स्वधा स्थ तर्पयत मे पितॄन्`. Not found in the Taittirīya pāṭha or in the Śaunaka Atharvaveda. **Wording:** the same words; this file writes `स्वधास्थ` as one word.
4. `ye ceha pitaro ye ca neha` (unit 16). **Ṛgveda 10.15.13**, sa.wikisource `ऋग्वेदः सूक्तं १०.१५`, pageid 305: `ये चे॒ह पि॒तरो॒ ये च॒ नेह यांश्च॑ वि॒द्म याँ उ॑ च॒ न प्र॑वि॒द्म । त्वं वे॑त्थ॒ यति॒ ते जा॑तवेदः स्व॒धाभि॑र्य॒ज्ञं सुकृ॑तं जुषस्व ॥१३`. GRETIL's accented Aufrecht text has it at 10.015.13. **Vājasaneyi Saṁhitā 19.67**, pageid 25313, accented block. **Paippalāda Saṁhitā kāṇḍa 18**, pageid 38237, unaccented, numbered 8 in its sūkta, ending `त्वं वेत्थ यदि ते जातवेदः स्वधाभिर्यज्ञं सुकृतं जुषन्ताम्`. Not found in the Taittirīya pāṭha or in the Śaunaka Atharvaveda. **Wording:** this file reads `अग्ने तान् वेत्थ यदि ते जातवेदः` where the Ṛgveda and the Vājasaneyi read `त्वं वेत्थ यति ते जातवेदः`. The file's `यदि` agrees with the Paippalāda reading; its `अग्ने तान्` was found in no Vedic text searched.

Source / recension: **base text is the printed booklet Āpastamba Smārta Amāvāsyai Tarppaṇam, Bhavani Book Centre, 19 Station Road, West Mambalam, Madras 33**, archive.org item `hzzj_amavasya-tarpanam-apastamba-bhavani-book-center-tamil` (36 pages; the amāvāsyā rite runs from page 12 to page 23, and pages 6 to 8 carry the compiler's preface and his rules on who is to perform the rite). **The whole book was read as page images.** Its `_djvu.txt` OCR is unusable — it returns broken Latin fragments and no Sanskrit at all — so nothing in this file comes from it.

**A limitation of the base text, stated plainly because it bears on every line.** The booklet prints its Sanskrit **in Tamil script**, which does not distinguish the voiced, unvoiced and aspirated stops from one another. A Devanāgarī spelling cannot be read off such a page the way it can be read off a Telugu or Kannada one. So wherever the base text and the collating witness carry the same item, the Devanāgarī here is the machine transliteration of the **Telugu** witness by `bin/tel2dev.py`, and the base text is used for the order of service, the wording and the ritual direction. Where an item is in the base text alone — the saṅkalpa of unit 5, the invocation and seat of units 7 to 10, the tarpaṇa formulae, the dismissal of unit 23 — the Devanāgarī is the standard Sanskrit orthography of formulae the witness prints in a script that under-determines them, and the `vidhi:` field of each such unit says that the material comes from the base text. This is recorded as a weakness of the file, not passed over.

Independent collation: **one collating witness, and it is one lineage and not two.**

`stotranidhi.com/pitru-tila-tarpanam-in-telugu/`, Telugu script only, fetched 2026-09-15. Unlike the Taittirīya sandhyāvandana page of `01_krishna_yajurveda_sandhyavandanam.txt`, **this page is clean: zero Latin-`o`-for-anusvāra defects and zero zero-width joiners**, so nothing had to be repaired before transliterating it.

**vignanam.org's `devanagari/shraddha-pitru-tarpana-vidhi.html` is not a second witness to this rite and must not be counted as one.** It is a machine transliteration of that same stotranidhi page: it carries the page's Telugu ritual instructions transliterated letter for letter into Devanāgarī, so that `తలమీద నీళ్ళను జల్లుకోండి` stands in its Devanāgarī column as `तलमीद नील्लनु जल्लुकोण्डि`, which is not Sanskrit and not an instruction anyone could follow. The two sites are one lineage. This is worth knowing in advance, because vignanam is a genuine accented Devanāgarī source for the Taittirīya pāṭha and it is easy to assume the same of its paddhati pages.

**Seven differences between the two witnesses, itemised, and none of them reconciled.** They are large, and a reader should know the rite has two shapes.

**(1) The saṅkalpa.** The base text names all twelve of the two lines and says `amāvāsyā puṇya kāle tilatarpaṇaṁ kariṣye`. The Telugu witness says only `asmat pitṝn uddiśya asmat pitṝṇāṁ puṇyalokāvāptyarthaṁ pitṛ tarpaṇaṁ kariṣye`. The base text's form is at unit 5; the other is recorded here and not carried into the line, because a bracketed variant needs to be the same clause and these are not.

**(2) The invocation on the kūrca, and the seat.** The base text sets a kūrca for each line and invokes the forefathers onto it with `āyāta pitaraḥ somyā`, then gives them a seat with `śakṛdācchinnaṁ barhiḥ`. **The Telugu witness has neither.** Units 7 to 10 are from the base text.

**(3) The nine ṛcs.** The base text says three ṛcs before the offering to each of the three men of a line — `udīratām`, `aṅgiraso naḥ` and `āyantu naḥ` for the father; `ūrjaṁ vahantīḥ`, `pitṛbhyaḥ svadhāvibhyaḥ` and `ye ceha pitaro` for the grandfather; the three madhu verses for the great-grandfather — and repeats the same nine for the mother's father's line. **The Telugu witness has none of them.** This is the largest difference between the two, and it is what makes the base text the fuller rite.

**(4) The pitṛ-upasthāna.** The base text stands the worshipper up at the end and has him say `namo vaḥ pitaro rasāya`. **The Telugu witness has no upasthāna.** Unit 22 is from the base text.

**(5) The dismissal.** The base text returns the forefathers to their place from the kūrca. **The Telugu witness has no dismissal.** Unit 23 is from the base text.

**(6) The kāruṇya tarpaṇa.** The Telugu witness offers water, after the forefathers, to thirty-four further relations by name — wife, son, brother, daughter, sister, father's brother, mother's brother, son-in-law, daughter-in-law, father-in-law, mother-in-law, teacher and the rest. **The base text has no such list**, and confines the rite to the twelve of the two lines and then to those who are known and not known. That list is not in this file. It already stands, from two printed Telugu witnesses of its own, at `../samanya/10_pushkara_pitru_tarpanam.txt`, and putting it here would duplicate that file.

**(7) The water pressed from the cloth.** The Telugu witness closes with `ye ke cāsmat kule jātāḥ`, for those of the family who left no son. **The base text does not have it.** It is kept here, at unit 25, and the `vidhi:` field says whose it is.

Two smaller differences are noted in the `vidhi:` fields rather than here: the base text abbreviates the ācamana to three names where the Telugu witness gives twenty-four, and the Telugu witness offers to a stepmother where the base text does not.

**No Gītā Press witness applies to this file.** Gītā Press publishes the northern Vājasaneyi rite, and an edition in the wrong śākhā is not a weaker witness but no witness at all. Its text is in this repository already, in its own folder, at `../madhyandina/03_tarpana_vidhi.txt`, and the two were not collated against each other at any point.

Other sources searched and set aside, so that a later session need not search them again. `ebook-upayogiapps-com-read-deva-rishi-pitri-tarpana-vidhi` on archive.org has the right title but prints the northern saṅkalpa word for word and is the Gītā Press text. `apastamba-yajur-veda-trikaala-sandhyaavandanam` is genuinely Taittirīya but its tarpaṇa is only a Keśavādi one, unaccented. `vavilla_vaari_sandhya_vandanam` (Vavilla Ramaswamy Sastrulu and Sons) and `SandhyavandanaBhashyam_201704` (1916) are both in the right śākhā, but their Telugu OCR is destroyed and each carries only the short sandhyāṅga tarpaṇa. `apastambiya-purvaprayoga` is a real Āpastamba prayoga, but its tarpaṇa is the kāṇḍarṣi tarpaṇa of the upanayana. `ypoe-bodhayana-amavasya-tarpanam-by-s-sambandha-gurukka` is the same rite in the **Bodhāyana** sūtra, which is a different sūtra of the same śākhā and so belongs in a file of its own, not in this one. The two documents at parasakthifamily.org are headed for this very sūtra and confirm the order of service, but they are in phonetic English only and are a community compilation, so they were not used for any reading.

Adhikāra — what the witnesses actually say, and what they do not say. This is recorded because the question is asked often and is usually answered from hearsay.

**The living father is the restriction both witnesses state, and they state it first.** The base text heads a section on page 7 तர்ப்பணம் செய்ய வேண்டியவர்கள், those who are to perform the tarpaṇa, and its first sentence is that **only those whose father is no longer living are qualified to perform it**. The Telugu witness opens with the same rule as its first line on the page. The base text adds, on pages 7 and 8, that the mother's father's line is not offered to while the mother's father is living, and that when the father's father is living the offering moves up a generation, to the father, the grandfather's father and his father. `../02_brahma_yajna_vidhi.txt` unit 47 records from its own second witness that a man whose father is living does not wear the sacred thread over the right shoulder and holds it with the right thumb instead — that is, he makes the deva and ṛṣi offerings and not the pitṛ offering.

**The Veda ties the rite to the sacred thread, which is to say to the upanayana.** Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 2, the svādhyāya-brāhmaṇa, was read for this file at vignanam.org's accented pāṭha page. Its first anuvāka says `प्रसृतो ह वै यज्ञोपवीतिनो यज्ञोऽप्रसृतोऽनुपवीतिनः` — the sacrifice of one who wears the yajñopavīta is carried through; that of one who does not wear it is not. The same anuvāka defines the three positions of the thread, upavīta, prācīnāvīta and nivīta, on which this whole rite turns. That is the ground of the restriction to the initiated, and it is a Vedic statement and not a later one.

**Varṇa appears only as a change of one word, and not as a bar.** Both witnesses give the same rule: a brāhmaṇa says `śarmāṇam` in the formula, a kṣatriya `varmāṇam`, a vaiśya `guptam`. The same rule stands in the northern manual at `../madhyandina/03_tarpana_vidhi.txt` unit 1.

**Nothing read for this file prescribes anything for women, for boys before the upanayana, or for śūdras, and the absence is reported as an absence.** Neither witness mentions them. The Āpastamba Smārta Prayoga (archive.org `ApastamaSmartaPrayoga`) was fetched and searched, and its discussion of tarpaṇa concerns only the tarpaṇa that is a limb of the śrāddha. No nibandha was reached on the question. A community document at parasakthifamily.org does make a general statement about who should perform the rite, and it is **deliberately not used here**, because it is not an edition and this file does not fill a gap in its witnesses from a source it would not otherwise cite.

Cross-references, in both directions, because the Taittirīya tarpaṇa is in three places in this corpus and they are three different rites.

- **The daily deva-ṛṣi-pitṛ tarpaṇa of this tradition is at `02_brahma_yajna_vidhi.txt`, units 42 to 49.** In the Āpastamba tradition that rite is the second half of the brahmayajña and is not a separate work, which is why this folder has no separate daily tarpaṇa file. That is the file which answers to `../madhyandina/03_tarpana_vidhi.txt` for the daily rite. **This file does not repeat it and does not supersede it.**
- **The short tarpaṇa that is a limb of the sandhyā — Sandhyā, Gāyatrī, Brāhmī and Nimṛjī — is at `01_krishna_yajurveda_sandhyavandanam.txt`, unit 23.**
- **`../samanya/10_pushkara_pitru_tarpanam.txt`** is the offering of water to the forefathers on the bank of a river in her Puṣkara, from two printed Telugu witnesses. It holds the long list of further relations that the Telugu witness of this file also carries and this file does not.
- **`../samanya/01_gayatri_tarpanam.txt`** and **`../samanya/02_bhishmashtami_tarpanam.txt`** are other tarpaṇas that are not śākhā-specific.

Blurb: On the new moon day a man offers water to his forefathers. This file gives what is said. The rite is done after the bath and after the midday sandhyā. He sits facing south, wears a ring of darbha grass on his ring finger, does ācamana and prāṇāyāma, and makes his resolve. He then moves the sacred thread from the left shoulder to the right, which is the position kept through the whole offering. He sets a bundle of darbha grass on the ground for his father's line and another for his mother's father's line, and invites his forefathers to come and be seated there. He offers water mixed with black sesame seeds, three times to each forefather in turn, naming the gotra and the name of each. Six forefathers are named in his father's line and six in his mother's father's line. Before the offering to each of the men, a Vedic verse is said. At the end he offers to those of both lines whom he knows and those whom he does not know, salutes them standing, and returns them to their place. The last offering is water on the kuśa grass, for those who have no mother, no father and no kinsman to offer for them. **Only a man whose father is no longer living performs this rite.**
