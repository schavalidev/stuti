Author: Anonymous (traditional Taittirīya smārta rite). No single compiler. The order of service is the received Āpastamba/Bodhāyana sequence. The rite itself is laid down in the Svādhyāya Brāhmaṇa, which is prapāṭhaka 2 of the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka, and the mantras are taken from the Taittirīya Saṁhitā, Brāhmaṇa and Āraṇyaka, with the opening verse of each of the other three Vedas and the opening lines of the Vedāṅgas and the Śāstras.

Type: vidhi (ritual manual) — not a stotra. Uses the one-extra-field vidhi format documented in `../madhyandina/01_sandhyopasana_vidhi.txt`: a `vidhi:` field carrying the ritual instruction, then deva / iast / en / tel / hi.

Recension / śākhā: **Taittirīya (Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda) — the South Indian form.** This is not a formality. The brahmayajña is śākhā-specific, and this file is NOT the universal or only form of the rite. Northern practice follows the Mādhyandina / Vājasaneyi (Śukla-Yajurveda) form, where Gītā Press book 210 discharges the brahmayajña in a single instruction — face east, wear the yajñopavīta savya, recite the Gāyatrī at least three times — and gives no separate mantra for it. That stands at `../madhyandina/04_balivaisvadeva_vidhi.txt` unit 9. **That is not a shorter version of this file. It is what that volume prints for its own tradition, and the two must never be merged, harmonised or collated against each other.** A user who follows the Mādhyandina rite should not use this file. This rite follows the sandhyāvandana of the same tradition, which is at `01_krishna_yajurveda_sandhyavandanam.txt` in this folder.

Accent: **present throughout**, as in the sibling sandhyāvandana file — accented recitation is normal in this tradition and the source prints it. Udātta `॑` (U+0951) and anudātta `॒` (U+0952) as printed. The dīrgha/double svarita, which South Indian printing renders with a double-quote glyph `”`, is given as `᳚` (U+1CDA VEDIC TONE DOUBLE SVARITA). The nasal before a spirant is given in Devanāgarī as `ग्ं` (U+0917 + U+094D + U+0902), which is the spelling used in `01_krishna_yajurveda_sandhyavandanam.txt` and is romanised `gṁ`. The Telugu source writes that nasal in three ways, and all three are mapped to the one Devanāgarī spelling: `గ్ం` (ga + virama + anusvāra), `గ్` + accent + anusvāra, and — in the twenty-eight tarpaṇa formulae — `గ్‍` before a spirant with no anusvāra written at all (`దేవాగ్‍స్తర్పయామి`). Where an accent mark stood between `గ్` and the anusvāra in the source, it is placed after the cluster in the Devanāgarī, which is how the sibling file spells it (`सर्वाग्ं॑`, `यासा॒ग्ं॒`). **No accent was added, inferred, moved between syllables or reconstructed at any point.** Four zero-width joiners remain in this file, all of them in the prose above, where the source's own Telugu spelling `గ్‍ం` is quoted; there are none in any `deva:`, `iast:`, `en:`, `tel:` or `hi:` field, so the corpus-wide zero-ZWJ check needs no exemption for the text itself.

Corrections applied to the base text, with the count, because stotranidhi is not a critical edition. **(1) Twenty-four occurrences of Latin `o` (U+006F) standing where the Telugu anusvāra `ం` (U+0C02) belongs** — the same systematic encoding defect already recorded for that site in `01_krishna_yajurveda_sandhyavandanam.txt`, which found twenty-nine of them on its own page. They sit in `వరే”ణ్య॒o`, `సంధ॑త్త॒o`, `భూయాస॒o`, `ధా॒రణ॑o`, `దాన॒o`, `ఆత్మాన॑o` and the like. A Latin letter cannot occur in Telugu text and every instance sits on an anusvāra-final word after an accent mark, so the repair is unambiguous; all twenty-four were corrected. One of them, in `శ్ర॒ద్ధాయా”o`, sits behind the svarita glyph `”` and is the case the sibling file warned would be missed by a rule keyed only on Telugu characters; the rule used here converts `”` first for that reason. **(2) One asterisk removed.** The Māheśvara sūtra line prints `ఋ ళు* క్`; the asterisk is the source page's own footnote marker and not a letter, and the footnote itself is not on the page. **(3) One doubled letter read as single.** At `బ్రహ్మాణగ్గ్ స్వయంభువం` in the kāṇḍarṣi tarpaṇa, both Telugu witnesses print `గ్` twice. The doubling spells no sound, the word is the accusative `brahmāṇaṁ`, and the recitation is `brahmāṇagṁ svayambhuvaṁ`; it is therefore written here with the single nasal used in every other line of the same list. This is the one place in the file where a letter was not transcribed exactly as printed, and it is recorded rather than passed over. **No other change was made to the source's readings.**

Recension note: **every difference found between the witnesses is listed here, and none of them has been reconciled away.**

**(1) `hastāvavanijya` — three accent marks, and the only place where two independent editions agree against the base text.** vignanam and sanskritdocuments both read `हस्ता॑वव॒निज्य॒ त्रिराचा॑मे॒द्द्विः प॑रि॒मृज्य॑`; the base text reads `हस्ता॑वव॒निज्य॑ | त्रि॒राचा॑मेत् | द्विः परि॒मृ॑ज्य`. The base text's printing is kept, because it is a ritual manual quoting the passage in its own pāṭha and with its own daṇḍas, and because it also carries `यत्स॒व्यं पा॒णिं पा॒दौ प्रोक्ष॑ति`, which the Āraṇyaka pāṭha places elsewhere and which the second manual also prints here. **This is a real disagreement about accent and it is deliberately not reconciled.** A reciter who follows the Āraṇyaka pāṭha should use the marks given above.

**(2) The Gāyatrī — one mark placed differently.** Three Taittirīya witnesses read `तत्स॑वि॒तुर्वरे᳚ण्यम्`; the base text reads `तत्स॑वितु॒र्वरे᳚ण्य॒म्`, and adds `धी॒महि` where they write `धीमहि`. Not reconciled.

**(3) The Sāmavedic verse `agna ā yāhi` — four marks.** The Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa 3.5 carries the same verse with the Yajurvedic accent: `अग्न॒ आया॑हि वी॒तये᳚ । गृ॒णा॒नो ह॒व्यदा॑तये । नि होता॑ सथ्सि`. The source prints the Sāmavedic recitation, `अग्न॒ आ या᳚हि वी॒तये᳚ गृणा॒नो ह॒व्यदा᳚तये | नि होता᳚ सथ्सि`. **The Brāhmaṇa marking was not imported.** Taking it would be exactly the cross-recension transplant the svara rule forbids, and the verse is recited here as Sāmaveda.

**(4) The Atharvavedic verse `śaṁ no devīr` — two marks.** The Taittirīya corpus carries the same verse in three places with `शं नो॑ ... आपो॑`; the source prints `शं नो᳚ ... आपो᳚`, and writes `शं यो` where the Taittirīya pāṭha writes the nasalised `शं-योँ`. Not imported, for the same reason.

**(5) Ṛgveda 1.1.1 — three marks written with a different sign.** sa.wikisource reads `अ॒ग्निमी॑ळे ... ऋ॒त्विजं॑ | होता॑रं`; the source writes `᳚` at those three places. The accented syllables are identical in both; only the sign differs, and the sign is the South Indian one. The source's form is kept. The same hymn stands accented in the corpus at `../../veda/rigveda/01_agni_suktam.txt`, from Śākala sources, and unaccented at `../../misc_vedic/01_agni_suktam.txt`.

**(6) `ऋचो अक्षरे` — one mark.** The Brāhmaṇa reads `पर॒मे व्यो॑मन्न्`, the source `पर॒मे व्यो॑म॒न्`.

**(7) `paro rajasi` — one mark.** The Brāhmaṇa quotes `प॒रोर॑ज॒सीति`, with an anudātta on `ज` that the source does not print. `sāvadom` is the manual's own closing word and stands in both manuals; it is not in the Brāhmaṇa.

**(8) Taittirīya Saṁhitā 1.1 — two readings.** The pāṭha edition reads `आ प्या॑यद्ध्वम` and `अय॒क्ष्मा मा`; the source reads `आ प्या॑यध्वम` and `अय॒क्ष्माः मा`. The second manual agrees with the source. Both stand.

**(9) Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa 1.1 — two spellings.** The pāṭha edition writes `क्ष॒त्रग्ं` and `सन्ध॑त्तं`; the source writes `क्ष॒त्त्रग्ं` and `संध॑त्तं`. The second manual agrees with the source on both.

**(10) Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10.11 — two defects in the pāṭha page, neither adopted.** It prints `स॒पुंष्पि॑तस्य` for `स॒ंपुष्पि॑तस्य` and `जु॒गुफ्से᳚त्` for `जु॒गुप्से᳚त्`. The source and the second manual agree against it.

**(11) The `थ्स` cluster is left exactly as printed** — `प॒शून्थ्संध॑त्तं`, `सथ्सि` — and is not normalised to `त्स`. The independent Devanāgarī pāṭha edition spells it the same way (`प॒शू-न्थ्सन्ध॑त्तं`, `नि होता॑ सथ्सि`), so this is the tradition's own spelling of that sound and not a Telugu-only convention to undo. The second manual prints `సత్సి` and `పశూన్ధ్స`, which is OCR noise on a page whose other letters are sound.

**(12) `शीक्षा` with a long ī** is the source's spelling in the Vedāṅga section and is kept. So is `कंडर्षि` in the heading of the kāṇḍarṣi tarpaṇa, where the nine formulae under it all read `कांडर्षि`; the second manual writes `కాండర్షి` in both places. The heading is transcribed as the base text prints it.

**(13) The second manual writes `ఓగ్‍ం సువః`** — the praṇava with the guttural nasal — in the Gāyatrī vyāhṛti lines where the base text writes plain `ఓం సువః`. The base text's reading is kept, and the difference is recorded because it is a real difference in recitation, not in spelling.

**(14) The two manuals differ in how much of the Vedāṅga and Śāstra recitation they print.** The base text gives the opening lines of śikṣā, vyākaraṇa, chandas, nirukta, jyotiṣa, the two Mīmāṁsās, Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika, Yoga, Sāṁkhya, Manusmṛti, the Rāmāyaṇa, the Mahābhārata, the Bhagavadgītā, the Bhāgavata and Tantra. The second manual reduces the whole of that to four lines. This is a difference in the extent of the daily recitation between two manuals of one tradition, not a variant reading, and nothing was filled in from either side.

**(15) The second manual adds material the base text does not print** — `uttiṣṭhantu` at the opening, `svadhā namaḥ` in the first pitṛ-tarpaṇa line, and the closing `sarvaṁ śrīparameśvarārpaṇamastu`. **None of it was added to this file.** Two of its ritual directions are carried in `vidhi:` fields and are marked there as coming from the second witness.

Blurb: The brahmayajña is the daily study of the Veda. It is the fifth of the five great sacrifices that a householder performs every day, and it is the one that is owed to the sages. This file gives the rite as it is followed in the South Indian Taittirīya tradition, where it is done after the morning sandhyāvandana. The worshipper does not recite the whole Veda. He recites the opening words of each of the four Vedas, and then the opening words of the Vedāṅgas and of the Śāstras. In this way the whole of sacred learning is honoured every day in a few minutes. The Yajurveda portion is the longest, because it is his own Veda. After the recitation he offers water to the gods, to the sages, to the sages of the kāṇḍas and to the forefathers. The northern tradition does this rite in a much shorter form, and that form is kept in a separate file.
