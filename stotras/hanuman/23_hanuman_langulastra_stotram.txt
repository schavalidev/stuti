Title: Śrī Hanumān Lāṁgūlāstra Stotram
Devanāgarī: श्री हनुमान् लाङ्गूलास्त्र स्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ హనుమాన్ లాంగూలాస్త్ర స్తోత్రం

Author: Traditional; no author is named in the text or the colophon (iti śrī hanumāllāṅgūlāstra stotram). The closing verse gives the work a second title, Śatruñjaya ("conqueror of enemies").

Source / recension: stotranidhi.com — IAST recension at https://stotranidhi.com/en/sri-hanuman-langoolastra-stotram-in-english/, cross-checked verse for verse against the Devanāgarī recension at https://stotranidhi.com/hi/sri-hanuman-langoolastra-stotram-in-sanskrit/ (both retrieved with a browser User-Agent via curl, as direct fetches to the live site return HTTP 403). The two script recensions agree exactly across all twenty-three verses, with no variant readings. Stotranidhi publishes only the Sanskrit text for this stotra, without a prose meaning; the English, Telugu, and Hindi verse-meanings below are original translations prepared for this corpus from the Sanskrit.

Verse count: 23 (22 anuṣṭubh verses on a single refrain + 1 closing upajāti verse)

Sections:
Stotram — vv. 1–22, each a single line of vocatives capped by the lāṅgūlāstra refrain
Phalaśruti — the manner and fruit of recitation (v. 23)

Blurb: This is the tail-weapon stotra. It has twenty-two verses, and the entire second line never changes: 'lolallāṅgūlapātena mamārātīn nipātaya', meaning 'with one blow of your swinging tail, strike down my enemies'. The first line does nothing but add a fresh pair of epithets each time. The effect is that of a war-drum. The epithets run through the whole Rāmāyaṇa in fragments: Akṣa killed, the sun swallowed, Sugrīva freed, the ocean leapt, Laṅkā burnt, the Droṇa mountain lifted, and Sītā's blessing received. A few reach past the Rāmāyaṇa. Hanumān is an incarnation of Rudra. He is the single sun that cuts through the night of the night-rangers. He is the heroic force that enters great warriors such as Bhīma. The last verse tells the reciter where to sit, at the foot of an aśvattha tree, and gives the hymn its other name, Śatruñjaya.

lolallāṅgūlapātena mamārātīnnipātaya ||
en: O Hanumān, son of Añjanī, of mighty strength and prowess — with one blow of your swinging tail, strike down my enemies.
tel: ఓ హనుమంతా, అంజనీసూనుడా, మహాబలపరాక్రమము కలవాడా — కదులుచున్న నీ తోక దెబ్బతో నా శత్రువులను కూల్చివేయుము.
hi: हे हनुमान, अंजनीसुत, महाबल-पराक्रमी — अपनी लहराती पूँछ के एक प्रहार से मेरे शत्रुओं को गिरा दीजिए।

Recension note: stotranidhi's Devanāgarī and IAST pages carry the same twenty-three verses in the same order with identical wording, and were compared verse by verse without finding a variant. The IAST as published there uses the Telugu-tradition long vowels ē/ō, normalised here to plain e/o per corpus convention. The Devanāgarī page heads the stotra लांगूलास्त्र with an anusvāra but writes लाङ्गूल with the full nasal in the colophon and in the refrain of every verse; the fully spelled form has been used for the title line here. Verses 1–22 are anuṣṭubh and share an identical second line; the closing verse changes metre to upajāti, which is the usual signal in this genre that the stotra proper has ended and the phalaśruti has begun.
