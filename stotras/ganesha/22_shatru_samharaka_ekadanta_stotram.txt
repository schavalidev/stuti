Title: Śatrusaṁhāraka Ekadanta Stotram
Devanāgarī: शत्रुसंहारक एकदन्त स्तोत्रम्

Author: The gods and sages ("devarṣaya ūcuḥ"), Śrī Mudgala Mahāpurāṇa, second khaṇḍa, Ekadantacarita, chapter 52

Source / recension: stotranidhi.com — https://stotranidhi.com/en/shatru-samharaka-ekadanta-stotram-in-english/ (IAST), https://stotranidhi.com/hi/shatru-samharaka-ekadanta-stotram-in-sanskrit/ (Devanāgarī). Both pages were compared verse by verse and carry identical content with no variant readings. Neither page supplies a rendered prose meaning, so the en/tel/hi renderings below are this corpus's own translation of the fetched Sanskrit. The closing colophon ("iti śrīmanmudgalapurāṇe dvitīyekhaṇḍe ekadantacarite dvipañcāśattamo'dhyāye ekadantastotraṁ sampūrṇam") is not reproduced as a verse block. This is a separate hymn from the longer Ekadanta Stotram already in this folder (06_ekadanta_stotram.txt), which comes from an earlier chapter of the same Ekadantacarita and is narrated by Gṛtsamada; the two share no verses.

Verse count: 14

Sections:
స్తోత్రం — Stotram, the praise of the gods and sages (vv. 1–10)
వరప్రదానం — Varapradāna, Ekadanta's answer and the fruit of recitation (vv. 11–14)

Blurb: This hymn is known for the harshest of its promised fruits, which is the destruction of enemies. Its theological centre, however, is verse 7, which reads Gaṇeśa's own body as a doctrine. The trunk and the torso are joined with the three guṇas. The elephant head is of the nature of that which is without guṇas. The single tusk stands for the non-difference of the two. Around this the gods and the sages pile up statements that appear to contradict one another: he is within the reach of Vedānta and beyond it; he is the support of all existence and yet without qualities. In verse 10 they break off into simple astonishment, saying 'blessed are we, blessed are we', and then dance with tears and with gooseflesh. Only after all this does Ekadanta name the power of destroying enemies, and he places svānanda, his own bliss, after it.

en: The gods and sages said: Salutations to you, the elephant-faced, to Gaṇeśa — salutations, salutations; to the enjoyer of endless bliss, to Brahman, whose very form is Brahman.
tel: దేవర్షులు పలికిరి: గజవక్త్రుడవైన నీకు నమస్కారము, గణేశునికి నమస్కారము, నమస్కారము; అనంతానందమును అనుభవించువానికి, బ్రహ్మస్వరూపుడైన బ్రహ్మమునకు నమస్కారము.
hi: देवों और ऋषियों ने कहा: गजवक्त्र तुम्हें नमस्कार, गणेश को नमस्कार, नमस्कार; अनंत आनंद के भोक्ता, ब्रह्मरूप ब्रह्म को नमस्कार।

en: It destroys enemies, and at the very end it grants Svānanda, the bliss that is his own abode. By reciting it a person obtains everything — sons, grandsons and all the rest.
tel: ఇది శత్రువులను నశింపజేయును, చివరకు స్వానందమును ప్రసాదించును. దీనిని పఠించుటవలన మనుష్యుడు పుత్రపౌత్రాదులను, సమస్తమును పొందును.
hi: यह शत्रुओं का नाश करता है, और अंत में स्वानंद प्रदान करता है। इसके पाठ से मनुष्य पुत्र-पौत्र आदि सब कुछ प्राप्त करता है।
