Title: Śrī Gaṇapati Aṣṭottaraśatanāma Stotram
Devanāgarī: श्रीगणपति-अष्टोत्तरशतनामस्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ గణపతి అష్టోత్తరశతనామ స్తోత్రం

Author: Anonymous; Gaṇeśa Purāṇa, Upāsanā-khaṇḍa. The closing verse states that the hundred and eight were drawn out (आकृष्य) from the thousand names.

Source / recension: Primary text: **Gītā Press, Gorakhpur — Śatanāma Stotra Saṅgraha**, code 1850, pp. 11–14 (stotra pp. 11–12, nāmāvalī pp. 13–14), transcribed from the page images of the printed edition rather than from OCR. This is the corpus's first text taken directly from a Gītā Press volume, and the first aṣṭottaraśatanāma file. The volume prints the stotra and its nāmāvalī together for each deity, and that pairing is preserved here: verse blocks carry the stotra, the names block carries the 108 names as the same volume numbers them. Cross-checked against ganesha/29 (the Gaṇeśa Purāṇa sahasranāma), whose opening names run gaṇeśvara–gaṇakrīḍa in the same order, consistent with this text's own claim to be an extract from the thousand; that is a related-text check, not an independent witness. **No second witness to this aṣṭottara exists to collate.** stotranidhi.com was searched on 2026-09-08 and carries five Gaṇeśa aṣṭottaraśatanāmāvalis — the plain, the two gakāra forms, the varada and the vidyā — none of which is this text: its plain list opens `गजानन, गणाध्यक्ष, विघ्नराज, विनायक` (the widely circulated South Indian sequence) against this text's `गणेश्वर, गणक्रीड, महागणपति, विश्वकर्ता`, and the two lists share **two names out of 108**. They are different compositions, not variant recensions of one, so there is nothing here to collate rather than a collation left undone. sanskritdocuments.org has not been searched. The readings rest on Gītā Press alone, which the sourcing rule makes the authority where an edition exists; revisit if a second printed witness to *this* hymn turns up.

Recension note: **Prefix.** Gītā Press prints `ॐ` before each name in the nāmāvalī and notes (Prayoga-vidhi, p. 5) that women and the anupanīta substitute `श्री`. This corpus prints `श्री` for every nāmāvalī without exception, as the universally admissible form; the divergence from the printed text is deliberate and is recorded here rather than in the rows. **Case and the four rites.** Every name is given in the caturthī (dative), which is the form `नमः` governs; the volume prints the `नमः` (namana) form throughout. The same names serve three further rites — `पूजयामि` (pūjana), `तर्पयामि` (tarpaṇa) and `स्वाहा` (havana). `स्वाहा` also governs the dative, so those forms carry over unchanged; `पूजयामि` and `तर्पयामि` are transitive and require the dvitīyā, which would re-inflect the names. Twenty-seven of the 108 are irregular in that change (ṛ-, an-, in-, vat-, as-, us-, i- and u-stems, all of which fall together in the dative). Only the printed dative is recorded here; the accusative forms are not attested by the volume and have not been written into the file. **Two readings retained as printed, against grammatical expectation:** no. 92 `सहस्रशीर्षे पुरुषाय` — the stem is `सहस्रशीर्षन्` (cf. v. 15, `सहस्रशीर्षा`), whose dative is `सहस्रशीर्ष्णे`, so the printed `सहस्रशीर्षे` is grammatically anomalous. **Corrected 2026-09-08:** this file first called it a probable compositor's error. It is not. The identical form stands at `vishnu/26` no. 48, in a separately typeset nāmāvalī of a different text in the same volume, and was verified there at 500 dpi — there is no `ण्`. Two independent occurrences make it the volume's consistent practice with this stem, and it is retained on that ground rather than as a tolerated slip. No. 94 `सहस्रपादे` is correct as printed — the dative of the root-noun `सहस्रपाद्` (cf. v. 15, `सहस्रपात्`). Both were verified at 400 dpi against the page image before being retained. **Numbering.** The printed stotra numbers its verses 1–18; those are blocks 2–19 here, block 1 being the unnumbered dhyāna verse. **Name/verse mismatch:** the stotra's own name-sequence yields the 108 of the nāmāvalī, but `विश्वकर्ता` occurs twice (nos. 4 and 79, from vv. 2 and 13), so the list holds 107 distinct names in 108 slots — as the volume prints it.

Blurb: This is the shortest of the great Gaṇeśa name-hymns, and by its own account it is an extract. Its closing verse says that the hundred and eight were drawn out of the thousand and set in this more manageable form, to be recited at the brāhma muhūrta on rising. What survives the compression is the physical portrait. Verses 4 to 6 are almost entirely anatomical: shoulders like mountain ranges, a navel that is the sky, then a deep navel, a broad belly, a full chest, thick shoulders, a conch throat, heavy lips and a long nose. They close with the summary claim that he is complete in every limb and marked with every auspicious sign. Against that solidity the hymn sets a run of statements that seem to contradict one another. He is the support of all and yet free of both support and supported. He is the witness of action, the doer of action, and the giver of the fruit of action and of inaction alike. It ends where the Puruṣa Sūkta ends, with the thousand-headed, thousand-eyed, thousand-footed Person. Two lines later it ends with a fondness for laḍḍus.
