Title: Śrī Gaṇeśa Mahimnaḥ Stotram
Devanāgarī: श्री गणेश महिम्नः स्तोत्रम्
Telugu: శ్రీ గణేశ మహిమ్నః స్తోత్రం

Author: Traditionally attributed to Puṣpadanta (the same traditional author credited with the Śiva Mahimnaḥ Stotram)

Source / recension: stotranidhi.com — https://stotranidhi.com/en/sri-ganesha-mahimna-stotram-in-english/ (IAST), https://stotranidhi.com/hi/sri-ganesha-mahimna-stotram-in-sanskrit/ (Devanāgarī). Neither page offers a rendered prose meaning, so the English/Telugu/Hindi below are this corpus's own translation of the fetched Sanskrit. Several verses (notably vv. 7, 9, 15–16) are written in the compressed idiom of mantra-śāstra and syllable-symbolism or in list-of-equivalences style (identifying Gaṇapati with each of the six darśanas' views of the divine, with the letters of his own bīja mantra, or with the Navagraha and elements) — these are translated as faithfully as the compressed Sanskrit allows, favoring sense over word-for-word literalism.

Verse count: 31

Blurb: This is a philosophical hymn of praise, built on the model of Puṣpadanta's Śiva Mahimna Stotra. It opens by admitting that Gaṇapati's true form cannot be described. It then surveys how each school of thought names him: Gaṇeśa to his own worshippers, Śiva to the Śaivas, Ravi to the worshippers of the sun, Viṣṇu to the Vaiṣṇavas, the root-goddess to the Śāktas, Brahman to the Vedāntins, the unborn to the Sāṅkhyas, and pure awareness to the Buddhists. It moves through the symbolism of his bīja mantra, and of the three syllables of his very name. It gives a court scene, with Siddhi and Buddhi at his sides. It lists his many epithets, and identifies him with the elements and the planets. It tells how a wayside or doorway image of him came to be worshipped, with one verse describing the Bhādrapada Gaṇeśa-caturthī image directly. It closes with a personal appeal and a phalaśruti.

I do not know what to call him — only "salutations to him," the whole and supreme Brahman.

en: The syllable "ga" is said to be Heramba possessed of qualities; the syllable "ṇa" is the male principle beyond qualities. Though thus born twofold, he is one — Prakṛti and Puruṣa together — for "gaṇa" is indeed Brahman itself. And he, this first one, is the Lord who brings about creation, preservation and dissolution; from him all that has been and all that shall be comes forth — the lord, the ruler, Gaṇapati.
tel: "గ" అను అక్షరము గుణసహితమైన హేరంబుడు; "ణ" అను అక్షరము గుణాతీతమైన పురుషతత్త్వము. ఇట్లు రెండువిధములుగా జనించినను ఒక్కటే - ప్రకృతిపురుషులు కలిసినదే; "గణ" అనగా బ్రహ్మమే. ఈ ఆదిపురుషుడే సృష్టిస్థితిలయములను చేయు ఈశుడు; అతనినుండియే భూతభవిష్యత్తులు కలుగును - ఆ పతి, ఈశుడు, గణపతి.
