The note testers receive

Sent with the invite, by WhatsApp or mail. Warm, short, honest — it says what works, what is deliberately absent, why to install, what is counted, and how to write back. Fill the link before sending; the passcode and address are read from the build.

Work item B3. The two copies differ only in formatting — WhatsApp gets bold markers and shorter line breaks.

Namaste,

Thank you for agreeing to try Stuti before anyone else does. It is a small app for reading stotras — in Telugu, Devanāgarī or Roman letters, with the day's tithi and the vratas of the month alongside. It was built for the elders in our own family first, so if something is hard to read or hard to reach, that is a fault in the app, not in you. Please say so.

Getting in

  1. Open https://‹link› on your phone.
  2. When asked for a word, type matsya — it is the same for everyone invited, and asked only once.
  3. Answer the few questions it asks: your script, the deities you keep, your town for the almanac.

Please install it

Stuti works in the browser, but it is meant to sit on your home screen like any other app — it opens faster, fills the screen, and reads without a network once it has loaded once.

  • iPhone / iPad — in Safari, tap the Share button (the square with an arrow), then Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  • Android — in Chrome, tap the three dots at the top right, then Add to Home screen or Install app.

What is there

  • A library of about seventy stotras, each readable in three scripts, with meanings where we have them.
  • A saṅkalpa and nitya page for the day, the pañcāṅga for your town, and guides to the vratas, nomus and months.
  • Your reading place, saved texts and practice are kept on this phone only — nothing leaves it.

What is not there, on purpose

  • No audio yet. Recordings will come when we have ones worth your ears.
  • No account or sign-in. Practice stays on the device; if you change phones, Settings has a way to carry it across.
  • Reminders ring only while Stuti is open. Proper notifications need a server we have not built.
  • Many texts still carry a note saying they await proof against a printed source. If you know a text well and spot an error, that is the most valuable thing you can tell us.

What we count

Only which screens are opened and which features are used — never which text you read, never your name, never a verse. If your browser sends a do-not-track signal, we count nothing at all.

Three small asks this week

  1. Read one text a day, in your own script.
  2. Open the guide for the nearest vrata.
  3. Search for a text you already know by heart, and tell us if it is wrong.

Writing back

The easiest way is the Write to us door in Settings — it attaches the build and your screen size so we can reproduce what you saw. Or mail feedback@stuti.app directly. We reply within three days. Praise is nice; a screenshot of something broken is better.
