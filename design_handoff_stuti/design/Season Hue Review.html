Season hues — the last cool colours

Four of the six ṛtu hues are cool: two blues, a blue-grey, and a hot pink. They were picked as weather, not as pigment, and they sit on parchment beside a gold-and-red chrome. The question is whether representational exemption covers them — rain really is blue — or whether they should be ground finer like the deity hues were.

A · Current

As shipped. Śiśira and Varṣā are near-identical steel blues; Hemanta's grey is the only truly neutral thing in the app; Vasanta's pink is the highest-chroma hue anywhere outside a deity page.

B · Pigment

Same six identities, moved onto dyestuffs the manuscripts actually used: indigo instead of steel blue, madder rose instead of fuchsia, ash instead of blue-grey. Chroma down about a third — the same treatment the deity hues got.

C · Warm

One family. The season is told by the glyph and the motion; hue only says how hot or how dim. Nothing cool survives — including the rain.

Side by side — Varṣā, the hard case

Monsoon is where the three schemes disagree most, because falling water is the one motif whose colour is not decorative.

What I'd do

B. The exemption we wrote covers what colour means — sky, sun, moon, rain — not how loud it is. Rain stays blue in B; it just stops being a UI blue. C loses the one thing the ṛtu strip is for: you should know the season from across the room, and an olive monsoon and an ash winter are the same card.

Two fixes to make either way, independent of the hue question: Śiśira and Varṣā are too close to tell apart at a glance — late winter should be the pale one and monsoon the deep one, not two mid blues. And every hue is written twice, once in the JSX table and once in the ambient CSS, so a change today means editing two files and hoping they match. They should be six tokens next to the deity hues.
