What the app does, and what the website carries

Every surface in the app, read against the site as it stands. Four verdicts: on the site, partial (there but thinner than the app), missing (should be there and is not), and app only (needs a device, an account or a background process — the site should name it, not fake it).

The order to build it in

Ranked by what a visitor loses without it, not by what is easiest.

Word-by-word, on the web

The reader gives verse, meaning and the lit line. The app also splits every line into padas with a lexicon gloss — the single feature that most distinguishes this from a PDF, and it is pure text: stuti-lexicon.js is 46KB.

The seventeen vratams, on the grid

The month now marks what the engine derives from the tithi — Ekādaśī, Saṅkaṣṭī, the two moons — but not the vratams the library lists with their rules, because STUTI_VRATA is not loaded on the calendar. upcoming() and inMonth() hand them over as they are; each mark should open the vratam page the site already has.

Muhūrta, beyond the two windows

Abhijit, varjyam and amṛta, from stuti-muhurta.js, on the sun times the sheet already computes. The reckoning behind them is now a choice on the site, so the numbers would be the reciter's own.

The practice sequences

Sandhyā Vandanam and Nitya Pūjā Vidhānam are step sequences in stuti-library-data.js, with japa counts and pointers into the pool. The site describes practice without showing a single step of it — and now that the site names the juncture standing, the steps are the obvious next thing to name.

Then the honest boundary

Japa counts, vows, plans, records, reminders and the saṅkalpa flyleaf are the app's, because they are a person's history. The site's job is to say so on one page, with a screenshot each — not to reimplement them in a browser that forgets.
